## 5.1 What are life processes?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **LIFE PROCESSES are the maintenance jobs a body must keep doing to stay alive** — they go on even while you are asleep.
> ```
> NUTRITION       — taking in food for energy and materials
> RESPIRATION     — releasing energy from that food
> TRANSPORTATION  — moving materials around the body
> EXCRETION       — removing harmful metabolic wastes
> ```
> **Why maintenance is needed at all: a living body is a state of ORDER**, and order breaks down by itself. **Molecules are constantly being damaged and must be repaired or replaced** — which takes energy, whether you are running or asleep.

**Movement of some kind is usually taken as the sign of life** — though not always visible movement.

## 5.2 Nutrition

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **AUTOTROPHIC — the organism makes its own food from simple INORGANIC materials (CO2 and water), using an outside energy source such as the Sun.** Green plants and some bacteria.
> - **HETEROTROPHIC — the organism takes in COMPLEX substances made by other organisms.** Animals and fungi.
>
> **Heterotrophs must break those complex substances down into simpler ones before use, with bio-catalysts called ENZYMES.**
>
> **Every heterotroph depends, directly or indirectly, on an autotroph.** The chain always starts with something that made its own food.

### Photosynthesis

```
                      Sunlight
6CO2 + 12H2O   ------------------→  C6H12O6 + 6O2 + 6H2O
                     Chlorophyll
```
**Three events, which need NOT happen one after the other:**
```
(i)   ABSORPTION of light energy by chlorophyll
(ii)  CONVERSION of light energy to chemical energy, and SPLITTING of
      water into hydrogen and oxygen
(iii) REDUCTION of carbon dioxide to carbohydrates
```

> [!NOTE]
> **Desert plants prove the steps can be separated in time.** They take in carbon dioxide **at night** and make an intermediate, which the chlorophyll's absorbed energy acts on **during the day** — an adaptation that lets them keep their stomata shut in the heat.

**Carbohydrates not used at once are stored as STARCH — the plant's energy reserve.** (We do the same with **glycogen**.)

### The three experiments that prove what photosynthesis needs

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Testing for starch is how each of these works** — starch means photosynthesis happened. **All start by keeping the plant in the DARK for three days, so any starch already there gets used up.** Otherwise old starch would confuse the result.
>
> **1. CHLOROPHYLL is essential — the variegated leaf.** A leaf with green and white patches is destarched, exposed to sunlight, boiled in water, then in alcohol (to remove the green), and dipped in iodine. **Only the parts that were GREEN turn blue-black.**
>
> **2. CARBON DIOXIDE is essential — the bell-jar with potassium hydroxide.** Two destarched plants under sealed bell jars; one has KOH beside it, which **absorbs carbon dioxide**. Only the leaf from the plant **without** KOH shows starch.
>
> **3. SUNLIGHT is essential** — cover part of a destarched leaf with black paper, expose the plant to light, then test. **Only the uncovered part turns blue-black.**
>
> **The design principle in all three: change ONE factor and keep everything else identical.** That is what lets you name the cause.

### Stomata

```
STOMATA are tiny pores on the leaf surface, through which massive
gaseous exchange happens.

Each pore is bounded by two GUARD CELLS.
   water flows IN  → guard cells SWELL  → pore OPENS
   water flows OUT → guard cells SHRINK → pore CLOSES
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why plants close their stomata: a great deal of WATER is lost through them.**
>
> **So the plant faces a trade-off.** Open pores let carbon dioxide in but let water out. **When CO2 is not needed for photosynthesis, the pores are shut** and water is saved.
>
> **Gas exchange also happens across stems and roots**, not only leaves.

**Other raw materials — nitrogen, phosphorus, iron, magnesium — come from the SOIL through the roots.** Nitrogen, essential for making proteins, is taken up as **nitrates or nitrites**, or as organic compounds prepared by bacteria from atmospheric nitrogen.

### Kinds of heterotrophic nutrition

```
SAPROPHYTIC  — break food down OUTSIDE the body, then absorb it
               bread moulds, yeast, mushrooms
HOLOZOIC     — take in whole material and break it down INSIDE
               Amoeba, humans
PARASITIC    — take nutrition from a plant or animal WITHOUT killing it
               cuscuta (amar-bel), ticks, lice, leeches, tapeworms
```

**In AMOEBA, temporary finger-like extensions of the cell surface fuse over the food particle to make a FOOD VACUOLE, where it is digested.**

## 5.3 Nutrition in human beings

**The ALIMENTARY CANAL is one long tube from mouth to anus, with each region specialised for a different job.**

| Region | What is added | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Mouth** | saliva with **salivary amylase** | breaks **starch** into simple sugar; teeth crush the food; saliva wets it |
| **Oesophagus** | — | **peristaltic movements** push food down |
| **Stomach** | gastric juice: **HCl, pepsin, mucus** | **pepsin digests protein** |
| **Small intestine** | **bile** (liver), **pancreatic juice**, **intestinal juice** | **complete digestion**, then **absorption** |
| **Large intestine** | — | absorbs more **water** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why the food must be crushed and wetted:** every kind of food travels the same tube, so it must be reduced to **small particles of uniform texture**, and the soft lining needs the passage made smooth.
>
> **PERISTALSIS — rhythmic muscular contractions of the canal lining — moves food in a REGULATED way**, so that each region has time to do its work.

### The stomach — three secretions, three jobs

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **HYDROCHLORIC ACID** creates the **acidic medium that pepsin needs to work**, and **kills bacteria** in the food.
> - **PEPSIN** is the protein-digesting enzyme.
> - **MUCUS PROTECTS the stomach's own lining** from the acid.
>
> **"Acidity" is what happens when this balance fails** — too much acid, or too little mucus, and the acid attacks the stomach wall itself.

**A SPHINCTER muscle releases food from the stomach into the small intestine in small amounts.**

### The small intestine — where digestion is completed

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The food arriving from the stomach is ACIDIC, but pancreatic enzymes need an ALKALINE medium. BILE from the liver does both jobs:**
> 1. **makes the food alkaline**, so the pancreatic enzymes can act
> 2. **EMULSIFIES fats** — breaks large fat globules into small ones
>
> **Why emulsification matters: enzymes can only act on a SURFACE.** Large globules present very little surface for their volume, so digestion is slow. **Breaking them into many small globules multiplies the surface area enormously.** *(Exactly the action of soap on oily dirt, from Chapter 4.)*

```
PANCREATIC JUICE   trypsin  — digests proteins
                   lipase   — breaks down emulsified fats

INTESTINAL JUICE   completes everything:
   proteins      → amino acids
   carbohydrates → glucose
   fats          → fatty acids and glycerol
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **VILLI are numerous finger-like projections on the inner lining of the small intestine.**
>
> **They INCREASE THE SURFACE AREA for absorption**, and are **richly supplied with blood vessels** that carry the absorbed food to every cell in the body.
>
> **A recurring design principle: wherever a body needs to exchange something quickly, it grows folds, projections or branches.** Villi in the intestine, alveoli in the lungs, capillary clusters in the kidney — all the same trick.

> [!NOTE]
> **Why herbivores have a longer small intestine than carnivores:** grass contains **cellulose**, which is slow and difficult to digest, so it needs a longer journey. **Meat is easier to digest, so a tiger's small intestine is shorter.**

**Unabsorbed food passes to the large intestine, where more water is absorbed; the rest leaves through the anus, controlled by the ANAL SPHINCTER.**

## 5.4 Respiration

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **BREATHING is taking air in and out. RESPIRATION is the breakdown of food inside cells to release energy.** They are not the same thing.

**Every pathway starts the same way, in the CYTOPLASM:**
```
GLUCOSE (6 carbons) → PYRUVATE (3 carbons)
```
**Then the routes divide:**
```
(a) ANAEROBIC — in YEAST, no oxygen, in the cytoplasm
    pyruvate → ETHANOL + CO2 + a little energy       (FERMENTATION)

(b) AEROBIC — with oxygen, in the MITOCHONDRIA
    pyruvate → CO2 + WATER + a LOT of energy

(c) LACK OF OXYGEN in our MUSCLES
    pyruvate → LACTIC ACID (3 carbons) + a little energy
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **AEROBIC respiration releases FAR MORE energy than anaerobic respiration** — which is why oxygen is worth the trouble of breathing.
>
> **MUSCLE CRAMPS during sudden strenuous activity are caused by the BUILD-UP OF LACTIC ACID**, when the muscles are working faster than oxygen can be delivered.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ATP IS THE ENERGY CURRENCY OF THE CELL.**
>
> **The energy released in respiration is immediately used to make ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate. Breaking the terminal phosphate link of ATP releases 30.5 kJ/mol**, which drives the endothermic reactions of the cell.
>
> **Why the cell uses a currency instead of using the energy directly: the SAME molecule can power ANY job** — muscle contraction, protein synthesis, nerve impulses. **Like a battery, which can be turned into mechanical, light or electrical energy as required.**

### Gas exchange in plants

**Through STOMATA, by DIFFUSION. Large intercellular spaces keep every cell in contact with air.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The direction of exchange changes between day and night — and this is often misunderstood.**
> ```
> NIGHT — no photosynthesis, so CO2 RELEASE is the major activity
> DAY   — the CO2 made by respiration is USED UP by photosynthesis,
>         so there is no CO2 release; OXYGEN RELEASE is the major event
> ```
> **Plants respire all the time.** During the day photosynthesis simply outruns respiration and hides it.

### Gas exchange in animals

```
AQUATIC animals — use oxygen DISSOLVED in water, taken up at the GILLS
TERRESTRIAL animals — use oxygen from the ATMOSPHERE
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Fish breathe MUCH FASTER than land animals.**
>
> **Why: the amount of oxygen DISSOLVED in water is very small compared with the oxygen in air.** A fish must therefore pass a great deal of water over its gills to obtain what a land animal gets from a few breaths. **Terrestrial animals have the easier deal.**

**The human respiratory system:**
```
nostrils → throat → lungs

FINE HAIRS and MUCUS in the nostrils FILTER the air
RINGS OF CARTILAGE in the throat keep the air passage from collapsing
inside the lungs the passage divides into smaller and smaller tubes,
   ending in balloon-like ALVEOLI
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **How the lungs maximise the exchange area: ALVEOLI.**
>
> **Spread flat, the alveolar surface would cover about 80 m² — far larger than your whole body's surface.** Their walls carry an extensive network of blood vessels.
>
> **Why the surface must be inside the body: it has to be very FINE and DELICATE to let gases across** — and something that fine must be protected, which is why there are passages to carry air in to it, and a mechanism to move the air in and out.

**Breathing mechanism: ribs lift and the diaphragm flattens → the chest cavity enlarges → air is sucked in and fills the alveoli.**

> [!NOTE]
> **The lungs always keep a RESIDUAL VOLUME of air** — they never empty. **Why: it gives enough time for oxygen to be absorbed and carbon dioxide released**, instead of the exchange being interrupted with every breath out.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **HAEMOGLOBIN — the respiratory pigment in red blood corpuscles — has a very high affinity for oxygen.**
>
> **Why a pigment is needed at all: in a large body, DIFFUSION ALONE IS FAR TOO SLOW.** It is estimated that a molecule of oxygen would take **three years** to reach your toes from your lungs by diffusion.
>
> **Carbon dioxide needs no such carrier** — it is much more soluble in water than oxygen, so it is mostly transported **dissolved** in the blood.

## 5.5 Transportation in human beings

**BLOOD is a fluid connective tissue: cells suspended in a fluid called PLASMA.**
```
PLASMA carries       food, carbon dioxide and nitrogenous wastes — dissolved
RED BLOOD CORPUSCLES carry oxygen (bound to haemoglobin)
PLATELETS            plug leaks by clotting the blood at an injury
```
> [!TIP]
> **Why clotting matters twice over.** It stops the **loss of blood** — and just as importantly it prevents a **loss of PRESSURE**, which would make the whole pumping system less efficient.

### The heart

```
              RIGHT side                    LEFT side
           (de-oxygenated)                (oxygenated)

RIGHT ATRIUM  ← blood from body     LEFT ATRIUM  ← blood from lungs
     ↓                                    ↓
RIGHT VENTRICLE → to the LUNGS      LEFT VENTRICLE → to the BODY
```
**The cycle: an atrium relaxes and collects blood; it contracts while the ventricle below relaxes, transferring the blood; then the ventricle contracts and pumps it out.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why VENTRICLES have thicker muscular walls than atria: ventricles must pump blood right out to the organs**, while atria only pass it to the chamber below. **Thicker muscle for the harder push.**
>
> **VALVES ensure blood does not flow BACKWARDS** when the chambers contract.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why oxygenated and de-oxygenated blood must be kept apart: it allows a HIGHLY EFFICIENT supply of oxygen to the body.**
>
> **Which animals need that efficiency: birds and mammals, which constantly use energy to MAINTAIN THEIR BODY TEMPERATURE.**
>
> **Animals that do not — amphibians and many reptiles — take their temperature from the environment, so they can afford a THREE-chambered heart and some mixing.**
>
> **Fish have only TWO chambers.** Blood is pumped to the gills, oxygenated, and goes **straight to the body** — so it passes through the heart **once** per circuit. **In other vertebrates it passes through TWICE: DOUBLE CIRCULATION.**

> [!NOTE]
> **BLOOD PRESSURE is the force blood exerts on a vessel wall — much greater in arteries than in veins.**
> ```
> SYSTOLIC  — during ventricular CONTRACTION — normally about 120 mm Hg
> DIASTOLIC — during ventricular RELAXATION  — normally about  80 mm Hg
> ```
> **Measured with a SPHYGMOMANOMETER. HYPERTENSION (high blood pressure) is caused by constriction of the arterioles**, which raises the resistance to flow, and can rupture an artery and cause internal bleeding.

### Blood vessels

| | ARTERIES | VEINS | CAPILLARIES |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direction** | **away from** the heart | **towards** the heart | within a tissue |
| **Wall** | **thick and elastic** | thin | **ONE CELL thick** |
| **Valves** | no | **yes** | no |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Every difference follows from the pressure.**
>
> **Arteries have thick elastic walls because blood leaves the heart under HIGH PRESSURE.** Veins do not need thick walls because the blood is **no longer under pressure** — but for that same reason it might flow backwards, so **veins have VALVES** to keep it going one way.
>
> **Capillaries are ONE cell thick so that materials can be EXCHANGED across the wall** with the surrounding cells. **That is the whole point of the circulation** — everything else is plumbing.

### Lymph

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **LYMPH (tissue fluid) is formed when some plasma, protein and blood cells ESCAPE through the pores in capillary walls into the spaces between cells.**
>
> **It is like plasma but COLOURLESS and with LESS PROTEIN.** It drains into lymphatic capillaries, then larger lymph vessels, and finally into the veins.
>
> **Its jobs: carries DIGESTED AND ABSORBED FAT from the intestine, and drains EXCESS FLUID from the extracellular space back into the blood.**

## 5.6 Transportation in plants

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Plants have LOW energy needs — they do not move, and a large proportion of their tissue is dead cells. So they can use a SLOW transport system.**
>
> **But the DISTANCES can be enormous** in a tall tree, which is why a transport system is needed at all.

```
XYLEM   — carries WATER and MINERALS  — roots UPWARDS
PHLOEM  — carries the products of photosynthesis — from leaves to
          everywhere else, UPWARDS and DOWNWARDS
```

### How water rises

**Two forces, and they matter at different times:**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **1. ROOT PRESSURE.** Root cells in contact with the soil **actively take up ions**, creating a concentration difference between root and soil. **Water then moves into the root to even out that difference**, pushing a column of water steadily upward.
>
> **But root pressure alone cannot lift water to the top of a tall tree.**
>
> **2. TRANSPIRATION PULL.** Water evaporates from leaf cells through the stomata; the water lost is replaced from the xylem. **That evaporation creates a SUCTION which pulls water up from the roots.**
>
> **When each one dominates:**
> - **at NIGHT, the stomata are shut, so ROOT PRESSURE is the main effect**
> - **during the DAY, with stomata open, TRANSPIRATION PULL is the major driving force**

**TRANSPIRATION is the loss of water as vapour from the aerial parts of a plant.** Besides driving water upwards, **it helps regulate temperature** — evaporation cools the leaf.

### Translocation in the phloem

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **TRANSLOCATION is the transport of the soluble products of photosynthesis, in the PHLOEM**, through **sieve tubes with the help of companion cells** — in both directions.
>
> **Unlike xylem transport, it USES ENERGY:**
> ```
> sucrose is moved INTO the phloem using energy from ATP
>    → this raises the OSMOTIC PRESSURE of the tissue
>    → water moves in
>    → the resulting pressure pushes material towards tissues of LOWER pressure
> ```
> **Why the energy is worth spending: it lets the plant send material WHERE IT IS NEEDED**, rather than only in one fixed direction. **In spring, sugar stored in the root or stem is sent up to the buds**, which need energy to grow.

## 5.7 Excretion

**EXCRETION is the removal of harmful metabolic wastes from the body.** Unicellular organisms simply let them **diffuse** out from the body surface.

**The human excretory system: a pair of KIDNEYS, a pair of URETERS, a URINARY BLADDER and the URETHRA.**

### The nephron

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A NEPHRON is the basic filtration unit of the kidney; each kidney has large numbers of them packed close together.**
> ```
> 1. A cluster of very thin-walled blood CAPILLARIES sits inside the
>    cup-shaped BOWMAN'S CAPSULE, which collects the filtrate.
>
> 2. As the filtrate flows along the coiled tube, useful substances are
>    SELECTIVELY RE-ABSORBED: glucose, amino acids, salts and most of
>    the water.
>
> 3. The remaining fluid — urine — passes into the URETER, is stored in
>    the URINARY BLADDER, and leaves through the URETHRA.
> ```
> **How much re-absorption happens depends on how much excess water the body has, and how much dissolved waste must be excreted.**
>
> **The scale of it: about 180 L of initial filtrate is made daily, but only a LITRE OR TWO is passed out.** Nearly all of it is taken back.

> [!TIP]
> **Why filter almost everything and then take most of it back, instead of filtering only the waste?**
>
> **Because it is far simpler to filter by SIZE than by identity.** The capillary cluster lets through anything small — waste and useful alike — and the tubule then reclaims what is worth keeping. **One crude step plus one selective step beats trying to be selective at the start.**

**The bladder is muscular and under nervous control, which is why the urge to urinate can usually be held.**

> [!NOTE]
> **HEMODIALYSIS — the artificial kidney.** Tubes with a semi-permeable lining sit in a tank of **dialysing fluid**, which has the **same osmotic pressure as blood but contains NO nitrogenous wastes**. The patient's blood passes through the tubes and the wastes **diffuse out into the fluid**; the purified blood is pumped back.
>
> **How it differs from a real kidney: there is NO RE-ABSORPTION.** The machine can only remove; it cannot selectively reclaim.

### Excretion in plants

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Plants use completely different strategies from animals — and they can afford to, because much of a plant's tissue is DEAD CELLS and it can even LOSE parts of itself.**
> ```
> OXYGEN itself is a waste product of photosynthesis
> excess WATER goes by TRANSPIRATION
> many wastes are stored in CELLULAR VACUOLES
> some are stored in LEAVES, which then FALL OFF
> others are stored as RESINS and GUMS, especially in old xylem
> some are excreted into the SURROUNDING SOIL
> ```
> **An animal cannot drop a limb to get rid of waste; a plant can drop a leaf.**

## Summary

```
LIFE PROCESSES — nutrition, respiration, transportation, excretion
   needed because a living body is a state of ORDER that must be maintained

NUTRITION   AUTOTROPHIC — makes food from CO2 and water using sunlight
            HETEROTROPHIC — takes in complex food; needs ENZYMES
               saprophytic · holozoic · parasitic
   PHOTOSYNTHESIS: absorb light → convert energy and SPLIT water →
      reduce CO2 to carbohydrate; stored as STARCH
   the three experiments: variegated leaf (chlorophyll), KOH bell jar (CO2),
      covered leaf (sunlight) — always DESTARCH first, change ONE factor
   STOMATA — guard cells swell to open, shrink to close; shut to save water

HUMAN DIGESTION
   mouth    salivary amylase → starch to sugar; peristalsis moves food
   stomach  HCl (acidic medium for pepsin + kills bacteria), pepsin
            (protein), mucus (protects the lining)
   small intestine  BILE makes it alkaline AND emulsifies fat (more surface
            for enzymes); pancreatic trypsin and lipase; intestinal juice
            finishes: amino acids, glucose, fatty acids + glycerol
   VILLI increase surface area for absorption; herbivores need a longer
            intestine because cellulose is slow to digest
   large intestine absorbs water

RESPIRATION   glucose → PYRUVATE in the cytoplasm, then
   anaerobic (yeast)  → ethanol + CO2       little energy
   aerobic (mitochondria) → CO2 + water     MUCH more energy
   no oxygen in muscle → LACTIC ACID        → cramps
   ATP is the energy CURRENCY — one molecule powers any job
   fish breathe faster because dissolved oxygen is scarce
   ALVEOLI give ~80 m² of exchange surface; HAEMOGLOBIN carries oxygen
      because diffusion alone would take 3 years to reach your toes

CIRCULATION   plasma (food, CO2, wastes) · RBCs (oxygen) · platelets (clotting)
   four-chambered heart keeps oxygenated and de-oxygenated blood apart —
      needed by birds and mammals, which maintain body temperature
   fish 2 chambers (single circulation); amphibians/reptiles 3;
      birds and mammals 4 with DOUBLE CIRCULATION
   arteries thick and elastic (high pressure) · veins have VALVES ·
      capillaries ONE cell thick, where exchange actually happens
   LYMPH — colourless, less protein; carries absorbed FAT, drains excess fluid

PLANT TRANSPORT   XYLEM water and minerals up · PHLOEM food both ways
   ROOT PRESSURE dominates at night; TRANSPIRATION PULL during the day
   translocation USES ATP, so the plant can send food where it is needed

EXCRETION   NEPHRON — capillary cluster in Bowman's capsule filters,
   then the tubule SELECTIVELY RE-ABSORBS glucose, salts and most water
   180 L filtered daily, 1–2 L excreted
   dialysis removes wastes but cannot re-absorb
   PLANTS — waste in vacuoles, in falling leaves, as resins and gums in old
   xylem, or excreted into the soil
```
