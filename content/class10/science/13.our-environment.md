## 13.1 The ecosystem

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **An ECOSYSTEM is all the interacting organisms in an area TOGETHER WITH the non-living constituents of the environment.**
> ```
> BIOTIC components   — living organisms: plants, animals, microorganisms
> ABIOTIC components  — physical factors: temperature, rainfall, wind,
>                       soil, minerals
> ```
> **A garden is an ecosystem: grasses, trees, roses, jasmine, sunflowers, frogs, insects and birds, all interacting — and all affected in their growth and reproduction by the abiotic factors.**

```
NATURAL ecosystems      — forests, ponds, lakes
HUMAN-MADE (artificial) — gardens, crop fields, an AQUARIUM
```

### Producers, consumers, decomposers

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **PRODUCERS make organic compounds like sugar and starch from INORGANIC substances**, using the radiant energy of the Sun in the presence of chlorophyll. **All green plants and certain bacteria.**
> - **CONSUMERS eat the food that producers made** — directly, or indirectly by feeding on other consumers. They may be **herbivores, carnivores, omnivores or parasites.**
> - **DECOMPOSERS — bacteria and fungi — BREAK DOWN the dead remains and waste products of organisms.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why decomposers are indispensable: they RETURN the nutrients locked in dead bodies back to the soil, where producers can use them again.**
>
> **Without them, every nutrient on Earth would eventually end up locked inside corpses and waste**, and the producers would run out of raw materials. **They are what makes the cycle a cycle.**

> [!TIP]
> **Why an aquarium must be cleaned but a pond need not.**
>
> **A natural pond has a COMPLETE set of decomposers and a balance between producers and consumers, so wastes are broken down and reused as fast as they are made.**
>
> **A small artificial aquarium does not.** Its decomposer population cannot cope with the food we add and the wastes produced, so material accumulates and we must remove it by hand. **The cleaning is us doing the job the missing part of the ecosystem would otherwise do.**

## 13.2 Food chains, food webs and trophic levels

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A TROPHIC LEVEL is a step in a food chain.**
> ```
> grass  →  grasshopper  →  frog  →  snake  →  eagle
>   T1          T2          T3       T4        T5
> producer   primary     secondary  tertiary
>           consumer     consumer   consumer
> ```
> **A FOOD WEB is what you get when you stop pretending food chains are straight lines.** Each organism is generally eaten by **two or more** kinds of organism, which in turn are eaten by several others — so the relationships form **a series of branching lines**, not a single row.

## 13.3 Energy flow — the 10% law

**Autotrophs capture the energy in sunlight and convert it to chemical energy. From them it passes to heterotrophs and decomposers. But at every conversion, SOME ENERGY IS LOST to the environment in forms that cannot be used again.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE NUMBERS THAT MATTER:**
> ```
> green plants capture about 1% of the sunlight falling on their leaves
>
> at each following step, an average of 10% of the food eaten is turned
>    into the eater's own body and made available to the next level
> ```
> **Where the other 90% goes: a great deal is LOST AS HEAT to the environment, some goes into DIGESTION and into DOING WORK, and only the remainder goes towards GROWTH AND REPRODUCTION.**
>
> **It is only the part converted into body substance that the next consumer can ever get.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY FOOD CHAINS HAVE ONLY THREE OR FOUR STEPS.**
>
> **Because the loss at each step is so great that after four trophic levels VERY LITTLE USABLE ENERGY REMAINS.**
> ```
> 10,000 units at the producers
>  1,000 units at the primary consumers
>    100 units at the secondary consumers
>     10 units at the tertiary consumers
>      1 unit  at a fifth level — not enough to support a population
> ```
> **This is not a rule someone made up. It is arithmetic** — and it explains a fact about every ecosystem on Earth.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE TWO FACTS ABOUT ENERGY FLOW:**
>
> **1. IT IS UNIDIRECTIONAL.** Energy captured by autotrophs never returns to the Sun, and energy passed to herbivores never returns to the plants. **As it moves up the levels, it is no longer available to the level below.**
>
> **2. IT DIMINISHES PROGRESSIVELY** at each trophic level, because of the loss at every step.
>
> **Contrast this with NUTRIENTS, which CYCLE round and round.** Energy flows through an ecosystem and leaves; matter goes round inside it. **That is why sunlight must keep arriving, but new atoms need not.**

> [!TIP]
> **Why there are more individuals at the lower trophic levels — the greatest number being producers.**
>
> **Each level can only support what the 10% can feed.** A field of grass supports many grasshoppers; those grasshoppers support fewer frogs; those frogs support fewer snakes still. **The pyramid shape of a food chain is a direct consequence of the 10% law.**

## 13.4 Biological magnification

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **BIOLOGICAL MAGNIFICATION: harmful chemicals become PROGRESSIVELY MORE CONCENTRATED at each higher trophic level.**
>
> **How the chemicals get in:** pesticides and other chemicals used to protect crops are **washed down into the soil or into water bodies.** From the soil they are **absorbed by plants along with water and minerals**; from water they are taken up by aquatic plants and animals.
>
> **Why they build up: THESE CHEMICALS ARE NOT DEGRADABLE.** They are not broken down and not excreted, so they simply **accumulate** in the body.
>
> **Why the concentration RISES at each level: an animal eats many organisms from the level below, and inherits the pesticide from ALL of them — but only about 10% of the food's mass becomes its own body.** The poison concentrates while the biomass shrinks.

> [!WARNING]
> **HUMAN BEINGS OCCUPY THE TOP LEVEL OF ALMOST EVERY FOOD CHAIN — so the MAXIMUM concentration of these chemicals accumulates in OUR bodies.**
>
> **This is why wheat and rice, vegetables and fruits, and even meat contain varying amounts of pesticide residue. They cannot always be removed by washing.**
>
> **The chemicals are INSIDE the tissue, not on the surface** — which is exactly why washing fails.

## 13.5 Ozone depletion

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **OZONE (O3) is a molecule of THREE oxygen atoms.**
> ```
> O2 — ordinary oxygen — is ESSENTIAL for all aerobic life
> O3 — ozone — is a DEADLY POISON
> ```
> **Yet high in the atmosphere, ozone performs an essential function: IT SHIELDS THE EARTH'S SURFACE FROM ULTRAVIOLET (UV) RADIATION from the Sun** — radiation highly damaging to organisms, known to cause **skin cancer** in human beings.
>
> **The same molecule is a poison at ground level and a protector 30 km up.** Where it is matters as much as what it is.

**How ozone is made — by UV radiation acting on oxygen:**
```
             UV
      O2  ------→  O + O          high-energy UV splits molecular oxygen

      O + O2  →  O3               free atoms combine with O2 to make OZONE
```
> [!TIP]
> **Notice the elegance of this: THE UV RADIATION ITSELF MAKES THE SHIELD THAT BLOCKS IT.** Ozone is continuously created and destroyed high in the atmosphere, and the layer persists because the two processes balance.

### The damage, and the response

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The amount of ozone began to drop SHARPLY IN THE 1980s.**
>
> **The cause: synthetic chemicals called CHLOROFLUOROCARBONS (CFCs)**, used as refrigerants and in fire extinguishers.
>
> **The response: in 1987 the UNITED NATIONS ENVIRONMENT PROGRAMME (UNEP) succeeded in forging an agreement to FREEZE CFC PRODUCTION AT 1986 LEVELS.**
>
> **It is now MANDATORY for manufacturing companies throughout the world to make CFC-FREE REFRIGERATORS.**
>
> **This is the encouraging part of the story: a global environmental problem was identified, agreed upon, and acted on** — which is why it is worth knowing as more than a list of facts.

## 13.6 Managing our waste

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **BIODEGRADABLE substances ARE broken down by biological processes.**
> - **NON-BIODEGRADABLE substances are NOT.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY SOME THINGS ARE BIODEGRADABLE AND OTHERS ARE NOT — the reason is ENZYMES.**
>
> **Enzymes are SPECIFIC in their action: a particular substance needs a particular enzyme to break it down.**
>
> **That is why the same enzymes that digest your food cannot digest coal — you would get no energy from eating it.**
>
> **And it is why many human-made materials, such as PLASTICS, are not broken down by bacteria or other saprophytes: NO ORGANISM HAS AN ENZYME THAT FITS THEM.** They are too new for any to have evolved.
>
> **Such materials can be broken down by PHYSICAL processes — heat and pressure — but under the ordinary conditions of our environment they PERSIST FOR A LONG TIME.**

### How each kind affects the environment

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **BIODEGRADABLE waste:**
> - it is broken down and its nutrients **RETURN to the soil**, which is useful
> - but in large quantities it produces **FOUL SMELLS and BREEDS DISEASE-CARRYING organisms** such as flies
> - decomposing waste in water **uses up dissolved OXYGEN**, harming aquatic life
>
> **NON-BIODEGRADABLE waste:**
> - it **PERSISTS in the environment for a very long time**, accumulating year after year
> - it may be **INERT and simply clutter the land and water**, or actively **HARM members of the ecosystem** — animals choking on plastic, drains blocked, soil made infertile
> - **non-degradable pesticides undergo BIOLOGICAL MAGNIFICATION** and reach us in our food

> [!TIP]
> **The practical conclusion: BIODEGRADABLE AND NON-BIODEGRADABLE WASTE MUST BE SEPARATED at source.**
>
> **Why separating matters: they need completely different treatment.** Biodegradable waste can be **composted** and returned to the soil; non-biodegradable waste must be **recycled** or safely contained.
>
> **Mixed together, neither can be dealt with properly** — the compost is contaminated with plastic, and the plastic is too soiled to recycle.
