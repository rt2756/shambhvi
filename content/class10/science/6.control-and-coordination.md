## 6.1 Why control and coordination are needed

**We treat visible movement as a sign of life — but not all movement is the same. A germinating seedling moves because it GROWS; a running cat does not.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **What all these movements have in common: they are RESPONSES to a change in the environment, made to the organism's ADVANTAGE.**
>
> A cat runs **because it saw a mouse**. Plants grow **out into the sunshine**. You pull your hand back **to avoid a burn**.
>
> **And the response must be CONTROLLED — each change calls for a DIFFERENT and appropriate action.** You whisper to a friend in class rather than shouting. **So the organism must first RECOGNISE the event, and then produce only the CORRECT response.** That requires a system of control and coordination.

## 6.2 The nervous system

### The neuron

```
information detected at the DENDRITIC TIP
   → sets off a CHEMICAL REACTION that creates an ELECTRICAL IMPULSE
   → travels along the dendrite to the CELL BODY
   → then along the AXON to its end
   → at the axon's end, the impulse triggers the release of CHEMICALS
   → these cross the SYNAPSE and start a similar impulse in the dendrite
     of the NEXT neuron
```
**A similar synapse delivers the impulse from a neuron to a muscle cell or gland.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The signal changes form twice on its journey: chemical → electrical → chemical.**
>
> **Why: the SYNAPSE is a GAP.** An electrical impulse cannot leap across it, so the message is converted into a chemical that diffuses over and restarts the impulse on the other side.
>
> **This also makes the signal ONE-WAY.** Chemicals are released on one side and detected on the other, so the impulse cannot travel backwards.

**RECEPTORS are the specialised tips of nerve cells, located in the sense organs:**
```
GUSTATORY receptors — TASTE, in the tongue
OLFACTORY receptors — SMELL, in the nose
   also the inner ear, the eye, and the skin
```

> [!TIP]
> **Why food tastes bland when your nose is blocked — or when you have a cold.**
>
> **Much of what we call "taste" is really SMELL.** The gustatory receptors detect only a few basic tastes; the rich flavour comes from **olfactory** receptors picking up the food's vapours. **Block the nose and half the information never arrives.**

### Reflex action

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A REFLEX ACTION is a rapid, automatic response that does not involve thinking.**
>
> **Why reflexes exist: THINKING IS TOO SLOW.**
>
> **Thinking involves a complicated interaction of many impulses among many neurons in the brain.** If touching a flame had to be thought about — signal up to the brain, interpreted, decision made, instruction sent back down — **you would be burnt before your hand moved.**

**THE REFLEX ARC:**
```
receptor (skin) → SENSORY neuron → SPINAL CORD → MOTOR neuron → muscle
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why the connection is made in the SPINAL CORD: it is the point where the input and output nerves FIRST MEET.**
>
> Nerves from all over the body gather in a bundle in the spinal cord on their way to the brain. **Making the shortcut there is the earliest possible place — and therefore the fastest.**
>
> **The information still goes ON to the brain** — which is why you feel the pain a moment *after* your hand has already moved.

> [!NOTE]
> **Reflex arcs evolved because the brain's thinking process is not fast enough — and many animals have little or no thinking network at all.** So reflexes are an efficient way to function **in the absence of true thought**. **Even after complex brains evolved, reflex arcs remained the better option for quick responses.**

### The human brain

**The BRAIN and SPINAL CORD make up the CENTRAL NERVOUS SYSTEM. The PERIPHERAL NERVOUS SYSTEM — cranial nerves from the brain and spinal nerves from the spinal cord — connects it to the rest of the body.**

| Part | Region | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **FORE-BRAIN** | — | the **main thinking part**; separate areas for hearing, smell, sight; **association areas** interpret; **motor areas** control voluntary muscles; also the **hunger centre** |
| **MID-BRAIN** | — | involuntary actions |
| **HIND-BRAIN** | **CEREBELLUM** | **precision of voluntary actions; posture and BALANCE** |
| | **MEDULLA** | involuntary actions — **blood pressure, salivation, vomiting** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Three grades of action, not two:**
> ```
> REFLEX      — no thinking at all; spinal cord   (pupil size, hand off a flame)
> INVOLUNTARY — cannot be controlled by thinking; mid- and hind-brain
>               (heartbeat, breathing, digestion, blood pressure)
> VOLUNTARY   — decided by thinking; fore-brain   (writing, talking, clapping)
> ```
> **The involuntary group sits BETWEEN the other two** — not thought about, but not a sudden reflex either. **You cannot stop your heart by deciding to.**

> [!TIP]
> **What the cerebellum does becomes obvious when you imagine it failing.** Walking in a straight line, riding a bicycle, picking up a pencil — **all need constant fine adjustment that you never consciously make.** Without it, you could decide to move but not move accurately.

### Protection

```
the BRAIN sits inside a BONY BOX — the skull
   and within a FLUID-FILLED balloon, for further SHOCK ABSORPTION

the SPINAL CORD is protected by the VERTEBRAL COLUMN — the backbone
   (the hard, bumpy structure down the middle of your back)
```
> [!TIP]
> **Why fluid as well as bone: bone stops a puncture, but a hard box alone would let the brain SLAM against its walls in a knock.** The fluid cushions that shock — the same reason a fragile object is packed in padding inside a rigid carton.

### How muscle actually moves

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Muscle cells contain SPECIAL PROTEINS that change both their SHAPE and their ARRANGEMENT in response to a nervous electrical impulse.**
>
> **The new arrangement gives the cell a SHORTER form — the cell contracts.**
>
> **So the chain is complete: environment → receptor → electrical impulse → chemical at the synapse → muscle protein rearranges → movement.** Every step has a physical mechanism.

## 6.3 Coordination in plants

**Plants have NEITHER a nervous system NOR muscles — yet they respond. They do it in two quite different ways.**

### Immediate response — the sensitive plant

**Touch the leaves of chhui-mui (touch-me-not, Mimosa) and they fold up at once. NO GROWTH is involved.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Two things must still happen, as in an animal:**
>
> **1. The information must TRAVEL** — the movement occurs at a point **different** from the point touched. **Plants use electrical-chemical means to pass the signal from cell to cell — but they have NO SPECIALISED TISSUE for conducting it.**
>
> **2. Cells must CHANGE SHAPE. Instead of muscle proteins, plant cells change shape by changing the AMOUNT OF WATER in them** — swelling or shrinking.
>
> **The same two problems, solved with completely different equipment.**

### Movement due to growth — tropisms

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A TROPISM is a DIRECTIONAL growth movement in response to a stimulus** — either **towards** it or **away** from it.
> ```
> PHOTOTROPISM  — response to LIGHT
>    shoots bend TOWARDS light      (positive)
>    roots bend AWAY from light     (negative)
>
> GEOTROPISM    — response to GRAVITY
>    roots grow DOWNWARDS           (positive)
>    shoots grow UPWARDS            (negative)
>
> HYDROTROPISM  — response to WATER; roots grow towards it
> CHEMOTROPISM  — response to CHEMICALS
>    e.g. the growth of the POLLEN TUBE towards the ovule
> ```
> **Why the directions make sense: shoots need LIGHT and roots need WATER AND ANCHORAGE.** Each grows towards what it needs.

**The bean-seedling experiment:** seeds on a wire mesh over a flask, in a box open on one side facing a window. **After two or three days the shoots bend towards the light and the roots away from it.** Turn the flask around and **the NEW growth changes direction while the old parts do not** — proving the response is in the growing region.

**TENDRILS:** the part of the tendril **touching** the support **grows more SLOWLY** than the part away from it. **The faster-growing side therefore curves the tendril AROUND the object**, and it clings.

### Plant hormones

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **PLANT HORMONES are chemicals that coordinate growth, development and responses to the environment. They are made at places AWAY from where they act, and simply DIFFUSE to the area of action.**

| Hormone | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Auxin** | **cell elongation** at the shoot tip — behind phototropism |
| **Gibberellin** | helps in **growth of the stem** |
| **Cytokinin** | promotes **cell division** — abundant in fruits and seeds |
| **Abscisic acid** | **INHIBITS growth**; causes wilting of leaves |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **How auxin makes a shoot bend towards light — the mechanism, not just the fact:**
> ```
> 1. Auxin is made at the SHOOT TIP and helps cells GROW LONGER.
> 2. When light comes from one side, auxin DIFFUSES TOWARDS THE SHADY SIDE.
> 3. So the SHADY side has more auxin, and its cells grow LONGER.
> 4. One side longer than the other MAKES THE SHOOT BEND — towards the light.
> ```
> **Note what is actually happening: the plant does not "turn". Uneven growth on the two sides bends it.**

> [!TIP]
> **Why plants need growth-INHIBITING hormones too.** Growth cannot simply run on forever — a plant must also **stop**, shed leaves, and go dormant in a bad season. **Abscisic acid is the brake to the others' accelerator.**

## 6.4 Hormones in animals

### Why chemical signalling is needed at all

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Electrical impulses are fast, but they have TWO LIMITATIONS:**
>
> **1. They reach only the cells CONNECTED BY NERVOUS TISSUE** — not every cell in the body.
>
> **2. A cell needs TIME TO RESET after firing** before it can send another impulse — so it cannot signal continuously.
>
> **A CHEMICAL released by a cell diffuses all around it, and any cell with the right receptor molecules on its surface can detect it.**
>
> **This is SLOWER, but it can reach EVERY cell regardless of nerve connections, and it can be kept up STEADILY AND PERSISTENTLY.** The two systems are complementary, not rivals.

### Adrenaline — the fight-or-flight hormone

**A frightened squirrel must be ready to either FIGHT or RUN. Both are complicated and demand a great deal of energy — and the preparations they share can usefully be made in advance.**

```
ADRENALINE is secreted by the ADRENAL GLANDS, DIRECTLY INTO THE BLOOD

 → the HEART beats faster        → more oxygen to the muscles
 → small arteries in the DIGESTIVE SYSTEM and SKIN contract
                                 → blood is DIVERTED to the skeletal muscles
 → the diaphragm and rib muscles contract more
                                 → BREATHING RATE increases
```
> [!TIP]
> **Every effect serves the same goal: get oxygen and blood to the muscles that will do the fighting or running.**
>
> **And notice what gets shut down — digestion and the skin.** In an emergency the body **borrows** blood from jobs that can wait. This is why you feel your heart pound and your mouth go dry when you are frightened.

### The endocrine glands

| Hormone | Gland | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Growth hormone** | **Pituitary** | stimulates growth in all organs |
| **Thyroxin** | **Thyroid** | regulates **carbohydrate, protein and fat metabolism** for balanced growth |
| **Insulin** | **Pancreas** | regulates **blood sugar** level |
| **Testosterone** | **Testes** | male sex organs and puberty changes |
| **Oestrogen** | **Ovaries** | female sex organs, regulates the menstrual cycle |
| **Adrenaline** | **Adrenal** | prepares the body for emergency |
| **Releasing hormones** | **Hypothalamus** | stimulate the pituitary to release its hormones |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why iodised salt matters: IODINE IS ESSENTIAL for the thyroid gland to make THYROXIN.**
>
> **Too little iodine → too little thyroxin → GOITRE, whose symptom is a SWOLLEN NECK** — swollen exactly where the thyroid sits.
>
> **The chain is worth holding in mind: a mineral in the diet → a hormone → the regulation of metabolism.** A trace element missing from food shows up as a disease of growth.

```
DEFICIENCY of growth hormone in CHILDHOOD  → DWARFISM
EXCESS of growth hormone                    → gigantism

PUBERTY changes at 10–12 years — testosterone in males, oestrogen in females

DIABETES — too little insulin → blood sugar rises → many harmful effects
   treated with INSULIN INJECTIONS
```

### The feedback mechanism

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Hormones must be secreted in PRECISE quantities — so their timing and amount are regulated by FEEDBACK.**
> ```
> blood sugar RISES
>    → detected by the cells of the PANCREAS
>    → they produce MORE insulin
>    → blood sugar FALLS
>    → insulin secretion is REDUCED
> ```
> **Why this is so elegant: the RESULT of the hormone's action controls how much more is made.** Nothing has to decide in advance; the system corrects itself.
>
> **The hypothalamus does the same for the pituitary** — when growth hormone is low, it releases a **growth-hormone releasing factor** that stimulates the pituitary.

## Summary

```
CONTROL AND COORDINATION — needed so that each environmental change gets
the RIGHT response, not just any response

NERVOUS SYSTEM
   receptors (gustatory, olfactory, and those of the ear, eye, skin)
   NEURON: dendrite → cell body → axon → SYNAPSE (chemical) → next cell
      the signal is chemical → electrical → chemical, because a synapse
      is a GAP; this also makes transmission ONE-WAY
   REFLEX ARC — receptor → sensory neuron → SPINAL CORD → motor neuron
      → muscle;  made in the spinal cord because that is where input and
      output nerves FIRST MEET — thinking would be too slow
   BRAIN  fore-brain = thinking, sensory areas, hunger centre
          mid-brain and hind-brain = involuntary actions
          CEREBELLUM = precision, posture, BALANCE
          MEDULLA = blood pressure, salivation, vomiting
   protected by SKULL + fluid, and by the VERTEBRAL COLUMN
   muscles move because special PROTEINS rearrange into a shorter form

PLANTS — no nerves, no muscles
   IMMEDIATE (sensitive plant) — cells change shape by changing WATER content
   GROWTH movements = TROPISMS
      phototropism (shoot +, root −) · geotropism (root +, shoot −)
      hydrotropism · chemotropism (pollen tube to ovule)
   HORMONES  AUXIN — elongation; diffuses to the SHADY side, so that side
                     grows longer and the shoot BENDS towards light
             GIBBERELLIN — stem growth · CYTOKININ — cell division
             ABSCISIC ACID — INHIBITS growth, causes wilting

ANIMALS — hormones are needed because electrical impulses reach only
   connected cells and a cell must RESET before firing again;
   chemicals are slower but reach EVERY cell, steadily

   ADRENALINE (adrenal) — heart faster, blood diverted from skin and gut
      to skeletal muscle, breathing faster: fight or flight
   THYROXIN (thyroid) — metabolism; needs IODINE, else GOITRE
   GROWTH HORMONE (pituitary) — deficiency in childhood → dwarfism
   INSULIN (pancreas) — blood sugar; too little → diabetes
   TESTOSTERONE / OESTROGEN — puberty and sex organs

   FEEDBACK — the RESULT of a hormone's action controls how much more is
   made, so the system corrects itself
```
