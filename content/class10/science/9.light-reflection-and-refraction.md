## 9.1 Reflection

```
LAWS OF REFLECTION
   (i)  the angle of incidence = the angle of reflection
   (ii) the incident ray, the reflected ray and the NORMAL to the reflecting
        surface at the point of incidence all lie in the SAME PLANE
```
**These laws hold for EVERY reflecting surface — flat or curved, polished or rough.**

## 9.2 Spherical mirrors — the vocabulary

```
POLE  P            — the centre of the mirror's reflecting surface
CENTRE OF          — the centre of the sphere the mirror is part of
   CURVATURE  C
RADIUS OF          — the distance PC
   CURVATURE  R
PRINCIPAL AXIS     — the straight line through P and C
PRINCIPAL FOCUS F  — where rays parallel to the principal axis meet
                     (concave) or appear to come from (convex)
FOCAL LENGTH f     — the distance PF
APERTURE           — the diameter of the reflecting surface
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **For a spherical mirror of SMALL APERTURE, the principal focus lies MIDWAY between the pole and the centre of curvature:**
> ```
> f = R/2
> ```
> **A CONCAVE mirror CONVERGES light — it is a converging mirror.**
> **A CONVEX mirror DIVERGES light — it is a diverging mirror.**

> [!TIP]
> **How to find a concave mirror's focal length in one minute: hold it facing the Sun and catch the bright spot on a sheet of paper.**
>
> **That spot is a real, inverted, tiny IMAGE OF THE SUN**, and the distance from mirror to paper is the focal length. **It works because the Sun is so far away that its rays arrive effectively PARALLEL** — and parallel rays meet at the focus.

## 9.3 Image formation by a concave mirror

| Object position | Image position | Size | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **At infinity** | at the focus F | highly diminished, point-sized | real, inverted |
| **Beyond C** | between F and C | diminished | real, inverted |
| **At C** | at C | same size | real, inverted |
| **Between C and F** | beyond C | enlarged | real, inverted |
| **At F** | at infinity | highly enlarged | real, inverted |
| **Between P and F** | **behind the mirror** | enlarged | **virtual, erect** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The one case that is different: object BETWEEN P AND F gives a VIRTUAL, ERECT, ENLARGED image behind the mirror.**
>
> **Every other position gives a REAL, INVERTED image in front of the mirror.**
>
> **Why: for a real image the reflected rays must actually MEET. Bring the object closer than the focus and the reflected rays DIVERGE** — they never meet, so they only *appear* to come from a point behind the mirror. That apparent meeting point is a virtual image.

**A CONVEX mirror always gives a VIRTUAL, ERECT, DIMINISHED image, between P and F, wherever the object is.**

## 9.4 The three ray rules

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **To locate an image you need only TWO rays whose paths you know. Any two of these three will do:**
>
> **1. A ray PARALLEL to the principal axis**
> ```
> concave — passes THROUGH the principal focus after reflection
> convex  — appears to DIVERGE FROM the principal focus
> ```
> **2. A ray through (or directed towards) the PRINCIPAL FOCUS**
> ```
> emerges PARALLEL to the principal axis
> ```
> **3. A ray through (or directed towards) the CENTRE OF CURVATURE**
> ```
> is reflected straight BACK along the same path
> ```

> [!TIP]
> **Why rules 1 and 2 are the same rule read backwards.** Light paths are **reversible** — so if a parallel ray comes back through the focus, then a ray through the focus must come back parallel.
>
> **Why rule 3 works: a ray aimed at the centre of curvature strikes the mirror along a RADIUS, which is perpendicular to the surface.** It hits at 0°, so it reflects at 0° and returns the way it came.
>
> **The image is located where the two reflected rays INTERSECT** — or where they appear to intersect, if they diverge.

## 9.5 The New Cartesian Sign Convention

> [!IMPORTANT]
> ```
> (i)   The OBJECT is always placed to the LEFT of the mirror
>       — light falls on the mirror from the left.
> (ii)  All distances parallel to the principal axis are measured
>       FROM THE POLE.
> (iii) Distances measured to the RIGHT of the pole (+x) are POSITIVE;
>       to the LEFT (−x) are NEGATIVE.
> (iv)  Distances perpendicular to and ABOVE the principal axis (+y)
>       are POSITIVE.
> (v)   Distances perpendicular to and BELOW the axis (−y) are NEGATIVE.
> ```
> **Consequences you can then just read off:**
> ```
> object distance u — ALWAYS NEGATIVE (the object is always on the left)
> concave mirror  f — NEGATIVE (its focus is in front, on the left)
> convex mirror   f — POSITIVE (its focus is behind, on the right)
> real image      v — NEGATIVE (formed in front)
> virtual image   v — POSITIVE (formed behind)
> ```

> [!WARNING]
> **The signs are not decoration — the formula gives wrong answers without them.** Put in `u` as a positive number and every result that follows will be wrong.
>
> **A habit worth forming: write down u, v, f WITH THEIR SIGNS before touching the formula.**

## 9.6 The mirror formula and magnification

```
MIRROR FORMULA        1/v + 1/u = 1/f

MAGNIFICATION         m = h'/h = −v/u

   h  = height of the OBJECT   (positive — objects are above the axis)
   h' = height of the IMAGE    (positive if virtual, negative if real)
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **How to READ the answer, not just calculate it:**
> ```
> m NEGATIVE  →  the image is REAL and INVERTED
> m POSITIVE  →  the image is VIRTUAL and ERECT
> |m| > 1     →  ENLARGED
> |m| < 1     →  DIMINISHED
> ```
> **So a single number tells you the nature, the orientation and the size.** Getting `m = −0.23` means: real, inverted, and about a quarter of the object's size.

**Worked — a convex rear-view mirror, R = +3.00 m, bus at 5.00 m:**
```
f = R/2 = +1.50 m          (a convex mirror's focus is behind it)
u = −5.00 m

1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/1.50 − 1/(−5.00) = 1/1.50 + 1/5.00
    = (5.00 + 1.50)/7.50 = 6.50/7.50

v = 7.50/6.50 = +1.15 m

m = −v/u = −(1.15)/(−5.00) = +0.23
```
**Reading it: v is POSITIVE, so the image is behind the mirror — VIRTUAL and ERECT. m = +0.23, so it is DIMINISHED, about a quarter of the bus's size, and 1.15 m behind the mirror.**

> [!TIP]
> **Why rear-view mirrors are convex and not flat, despite shrinking everything.**
>
> **A convex mirror always gives an erect, diminished, virtual image — and a diminished image means a WIDER FIELD OF VIEW.** The driver sees far more of the road behind than a flat mirror of the same size could show.
>
> **The trade-off is real: objects appear smaller, and therefore FARTHER AWAY than they are** — which is why such mirrors carry a warning.

## 9.7 Refraction

**Everyday observations that all have one cause:**
```
the bottom of a pond appears RAISED
letters under a thick glass slab appear RAISED
a pencil half in water appears DISPLACED at the surface
a lemon in a glass of water looks BIGGER from the side
a coin in a bowl becomes visible again when water is poured in
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **REFRACTION is the change in the direction of light when it travels obliquely from one transparent medium into another.**
>
> **The pencil looks bent because the light reaching your eye from the part UNDER water arrives from a DIFFERENT DIRECTION than the light from the part above** — so your brain places the two parts in different lines.
>
> **The extent of the effect DIFFERS for different pairs of media** — water, kerosene and turpentine do not displace a pencil equally. **That is the clue that light travels at different speeds in different media.**

```
LAWS OF REFRACTION
   (i)  the incident ray, the refracted ray and the NORMAL at the point of
        incidence all lie in the SAME PLANE
   (ii) SNELL'S LAW —  sin i / sin r  =  a constant for the pair of media
                                      =  the refractive index
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHICH WAY THE RAY BENDS:**
> ```
> RARER  → DENSER   the ray SLOWS DOWN and bends TOWARDS the normal
> DENSER → RARER    the ray SPEEDS UP  and bends AWAY FROM the normal
> ```
> **A ray falling along the NORMAL (perpendicular to the surface) does NOT bend at all** — there is no oblique crossing, so there is nothing to bend.

**Through a RECTANGULAR GLASS SLAB, refraction happens TWICE — at air-to-glass and again at glass-to-air. The EMERGENT RAY IS PARALLEL to the incident ray, merely shifted sideways.**

> [!TIP]
> **Why the emergent ray comes out parallel: the two bends are EQUAL AND OPPOSITE.**
>
> Going in, the ray bends **towards** the normal; coming out, it bends **away** by the same amount, because the two surfaces are parallel. **The direction is restored; only the position has shifted — this is called LATERAL DISPLACEMENT.**

## 9.8 Refractive index

```
n21 = (speed of light in medium 1) / (speed of light in medium 2)
    = v1 / v2

ABSOLUTE refractive index (medium compared with air or vacuum):
    n = c / v      c = speed of light in air ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s
                   v = speed of light in the medium
```

| Medium | n | Medium | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| Air | 1.0003 | Crown glass | 1.52 |
| Ice | 1.31 | Rock salt | 1.54 |
| **Water** | **1.33** | Carbon disulphide | 1.63 |
| Alcohol | 1.36 | Dense flint glass | 1.65 |
| **Kerosene** | **1.44** | Ruby | 1.71 |
| Turpentine oil | 1.47 | Sapphire | 1.77 |
| Benzene | 1.50 | **Diamond** | **2.42** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **"n of water = 1.33" MEANS: light travels 1.33 times FASTER in air than in water.** It is a ratio of speeds, nothing more mysterious.
>
> **Light travels FASTEST in vacuum — 3 × 10⁸ m/s.** In air it is only marginally slower; in glass or water, considerably slower.

> [!WARNING]
> **OPTICAL density is NOT the same as MASS density.**
>
> **Optically denser simply means LARGER REFRACTIVE INDEX** — and therefore **slower** light.
>
> **The proof that they are different: KEROSENE (n = 1.44) is optically DENSER than WATER (n = 1.33) — even though kerosene FLOATS on water**, so its mass density is lower.
>
> **Whenever this chapter says "denser medium", it means OPTICALLY denser.**

## 9.9 Lenses

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A LENS is a transparent material bounded by two surfaces, of which at least ONE is spherical.**
> ```
> CONVEX (double convex)  — thicker in the MIDDLE   — CONVERGING
> CONCAVE (double concave)— thicker at the EDGES    — DIVERGING
> ```
> **A lens has TWO centres of curvature (C1 and C2), because it has two spherical surfaces.**
> **The line through them is the PRINCIPAL AXIS; the central point of the lens is the OPTICAL CENTRE, O.**
>
> **A ray through the OPTICAL CENTRE passes without any deviation** — which is what makes it the third ray rule for lenses.

### Image formation by a convex lens

| Object position | Image position | Size | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **At infinity** | at focus F2 | highly diminished, point-sized | real, inverted |
| **Beyond 2F1** | between F2 and 2F2 | diminished | real, inverted |
| **At 2F1** | at 2F2 | same size | real, inverted |
| **Between F1 and 2F1** | beyond 2F2 | enlarged | real, inverted |
| **At F1** | at infinity | highly enlarged | real, inverted |
| **Between F1 and O** | **same side as the object** | enlarged | **virtual, erect** |

**A CONCAVE lens always gives a VIRTUAL, ERECT, DIMINISHED image, between F1 and the optical centre, wherever the object is.**

> [!TIP]
> **Compare this table with the concave-mirror table and you will notice they are the same story.** In both, the image is real and inverted everywhere **except** when the object comes closer than the focus — where it turns virtual, erect and enlarged. **That is the magnifying-glass position.**

### Ray rules for lenses

```
1. A ray PARALLEL to the principal axis
      convex  — passes through the principal focus F2 after refraction
      concave — appears to diverge from the principal focus F1

2. A ray through the PRINCIPAL FOCUS
      emerges PARALLEL to the principal axis

3. A ray through the OPTICAL CENTRE
      passes WITHOUT any deviation
```

## 9.10 Lens formula, magnification and power

```
LENS FORMULA        1/v − 1/u = 1/f

MAGNIFICATION       m = h'/h = v/u
```

> [!WARNING]
> **Note the two differences from the mirror equations — and get them right:**
> ```
> mirror   1/v + 1/u = 1/f        m = −v/u
> lens     1/v − 1/u = 1/f        m = +v/u
> ```
> **Mixing these up is the single commonest source of wrong answers in this chapter.**

**Sign convention for lenses: the same rules, but all distances are measured from the OPTICAL CENTRE.**
```
CONVEX lens   f is POSITIVE
CONCAVE lens  f is NEGATIVE
```

### Power of a lens

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **POWER is the degree of convergence or divergence a lens produces:**
> ```
> P = 1/f          (f in METRES)
>
> SI unit: the DIOPTRE, D.      1 D = 1 m⁻¹
> 1 dioptre is the power of a lens of focal length 1 metre.
>
> CONVEX lens  → POSITIVE power
> CONCAVE lens → NEGATIVE power
> ```
> **Why power is the RECIPROCAL of focal length: a SHORT focal length means the lens bends rays through a LARGE angle**, focusing them close to the optical centre. **Stronger bending, shorter focus — so power must go UP as f goes DOWN.**

```
P = +2.0 D  → convex, f = +0.50 m
P = −2.5 D  → concave, f = −0.40 m
```

> [!TIP]
> **Why opticians use power rather than focal length: powers simply ADD.**
> ```
> P = P1 + P2 + P3 + …      for lenses placed in contact
> ```
> **During an eye test the optician slots several lenses of known power into the frame and finds the total by ADDITION** — which would be far messier with focal lengths, where you would have to combine reciprocals every time.

## Summary

```
REFLECTION  angle of incidence = angle of reflection; incident ray,
   reflected ray and normal lie in one plane

SPHERICAL MIRRORS   f = R/2 for a small aperture
   concave CONVERGES · convex DIVERGES
   RAY RULES — parallel ray → through F; ray through F → parallel;
      ray through C → straight back (it strikes along a radius, at 0°)
   CONCAVE mirror: real and inverted for every object position EXCEPT
      between P and F, where the image is VIRTUAL, ERECT and ENLARGED
   CONVEX mirror: ALWAYS virtual, erect, diminished — wider field of view,
      which is why it is used for rear-view

SIGN CONVENTION  object always on the LEFT; distances from the POLE;
   right positive, left negative; above the axis positive
   u always NEGATIVE · concave f negative · convex f positive
   real image v negative · virtual image v positive

MIRROR   1/v + 1/u = 1/f       m = −v/u
   m negative → real and inverted;  m positive → virtual and erect
   |m| > 1 enlarged;  |m| < 1 diminished

REFRACTION   rarer → denser: SLOWS, bends TOWARDS the normal
             denser → rarer: SPEEDS UP, bends AWAY from the normal
             a ray along the normal does not bend at all
   SNELL'S LAW   sin i / sin r = constant = refractive index
   a rectangular slab refracts TWICE; the emergent ray is PARALLEL to the
      incident ray, merely displaced sideways — the two bends cancel

REFRACTIVE INDEX   n = c/v ;  n(water) = 1.33 means light is 1.33 times
   faster in air than in water
   OPTICAL density ≠ MASS density — kerosene is optically denser than water
   yet floats on it

LENSES   convex thicker in the middle, CONVERGING, f POSITIVE
         concave thicker at the edges, DIVERGING, f NEGATIVE
   RAY RULES — parallel ray → through F; ray through F → parallel;
      ray through the OPTICAL CENTRE → undeviated
   convex lens: real and inverted except when the object is between F1 and O
      (virtual, erect, enlarged — the magnifying glass)
   concave lens: ALWAYS virtual, erect, diminished

LENS   1/v − 1/u = 1/f       m = v/u      (note the signs differ from mirrors)

POWER  P = 1/f in metres; unit the DIOPTRE (D), 1 D = 1 m⁻¹
   convex positive, concave negative; powers of lenses in contact ADD
```
