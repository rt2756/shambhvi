## 12.1 Magnetic field and field lines

**A COMPASS NEEDLE IS A SMALL MAGNET.** The end that points north is its **north pole**; the other is its **south pole**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A MAGNETIC FIELD is the region around a magnet in which the magnet's force can be detected.**
>
> **It is a quantity with both DIRECTION and MAGNITUDE.**

**MAGNETIC FIELD LINES are drawn by moving a compass needle step by step: mark both ends, then move the needle so its SOUTH pole occupies the place its NORTH pole just left, and repeat all the way to the magnet's south pole. Joining the marks with a smooth curve gives one field line.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The properties of magnetic field lines — with the reason for each:**
>
> **1. A field line is the path along which a hypothetical free NORTH POLE would move.** Its direction at any point is the direction a compass needle's north pole takes there.
>
> **2. OUTSIDE the magnet they run from NORTH to SOUTH; INSIDE, from SOUTH to NORTH.** So field lines are **CLOSED CURVES** — they never simply stop.
>
> **3. Where the lines are CROWDED, the field is STRONGER.** The compass deflection increases as you move towards the poles, which is exactly where the lines bunch together.
>
> **4. NO TWO FIELD LINES EVER CROSS.**
> **Why: if they did, then at the crossing point a compass needle would have to point in TWO directions at once** — which is impossible. The field has one definite direction at every point.

## 12.2 Magnetic field due to a current

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A CURRENT-CARRYING WIRE HAS A MAGNETIC FIELD AROUND IT.** This was Oersted's discovery, and it is the foundation of the whole chapter.
>
> **The demonstration: place a straight wire parallel to and above a compass needle.**
> ```
> current flowing NORTH to SOUTH  → the needle's north pole moves EAST
> REVERSE the current             → the needle moves WEST instead
> ```
> **So REVERSING THE CURRENT REVERSES THE FIELD.** The field's direction is tied to the current's direction.

### The right-hand thumb rule

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **RIGHT-HAND THUMB RULE: hold the current-carrying straight conductor in your RIGHT hand with the THUMB POINTING IN THE DIRECTION OF THE CURRENT. Your FINGERS then wrap around the wire in the direction of the MAGNETIC FIELD LINES.**
>
> **The field lines around a straight wire are a series of CONCENTRIC CIRCLES**, in a plane perpendicular to the wire.
>
> **This is also called MAXWELL'S CORKSCREW RULE:** if you imagine driving a corkscrew in the direction of the current, the direction the corkscrew **turns** is the direction of the field.

```
The field is STRONGER nearer the wire and WEAKER further away —
the concentric circles get larger and the lines more spread out.
```

**Worked:** current in a horizontal power line flows **east to west**. **Viewed from the EAST end, the field circles CLOCKWISE; viewed from the WEST end, ANTI-CLOCKWISE** — in a plane perpendicular to the wire, both above and below it.

### Field due to a circular loop

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **At every point of the loop the field lines are concentric circles that grow larger as you move away from the wire. BY THE TIME THEY REACH THE CENTRE OF THE LOOP, the arcs of those big circles appear as STRAIGHT LINES.**
>
> **Applying the right-hand rule to each section of the wire shows that EVERY part contributes to the field at the centre IN THE SAME DIRECTION** — so the contributions add instead of cancelling.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A coil of n TURNS produces a field n TIMES as large as a single turn.**
>
> **Why: the current in each turn flows in the SAME direction, so the field due to each turn simply ADDS to the others.**
>
> **This is the whole trick of building a strong magnetic field from a modest current** — use many turns rather than a bigger battery.

### The solenoid

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A SOLENOID is a coil of many circular turns of insulated copper wire wound closely in the shape of a cylinder.**
>
> **The field pattern around a current-carrying solenoid is SIMILAR TO THAT OF A BAR MAGNET** — one end behaves as a north pole, the other as a south pole.
>
> **INSIDE the solenoid the field lines are PARALLEL STRAIGHT LINES.** This means the field there is **THE SAME AT ALL POINTS — a UNIFORM field.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **AN ELECTROMAGNET is made by placing a core of SOFT IRON inside a current-carrying solenoid.** The strong uniform field inside magnetises the core.
>
> **Why SOFT iron and not steel: soft iron magnetises strongly but loses its magnetism as soon as the current stops** — which is exactly what an electromagnet is for. **Steel would keep its magnetism and could not be switched off.**
>
> **A parallel-line field is the sign of a UNIFORM field** — evenly spaced parallel lines mean the same strength and direction everywhere.

## 12.3 Force on a current-carrying conductor

**A current-carrying wire exerts a force on a nearby magnet. Ampère reasoned that THE MAGNET MUST EXERT AN EQUAL AND OPPOSITE FORCE ON THE WIRE — and it does.**

**The experiment:** a small aluminium rod AB hangs horizontally between the poles of a strong horseshoe magnet, connected in series with a battery, key and rheostat.
```
current from B to A              → the rod is displaced to the LEFT
REVERSE the current              → it is displaced to the RIGHT
REVERSE the field (swap poles)   → the direction of the force reverses again
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **So the direction of the force depends on BOTH the direction of the current AND the direction of the magnetic field.**
>
> **The force is LARGEST when the current is at RIGHT ANGLES to the magnetic field**, and it acts **perpendicular to both**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **FLEMING'S LEFT-HAND RULE. Stretch the THUMB, FOREFINGER and MIDDLE FINGER of your LEFT hand mutually perpendicular:**
> ```
> FOREFINGER   → direction of the magnetic FIELD
> MIDDLE (second) finger → direction of the CURRENT
> THUMB        → direction of the FORCE (the motion of the conductor)
> ```
> **A memory hook: F-F-C — Fore = Field, Centre = Current, Thumb = Thrust.**

> [!WARNING]
> **Note it is the LEFT hand here, but the RIGHT hand for the thumb rule of §12.2.** They answer different questions:
> ```
> RIGHT-HAND THUMB RULE — what FIELD does a current produce?
> FLEMING'S LEFT-HAND RULE — what FORCE does a field exert on a current?
> ```

**Worked:** an electron entering a magnetic field at right angles. **Remember that the direction of CURRENT is opposite to the direction of electron motion**, and then apply Fleming's left-hand rule to get the force.

**Devices that use current-carrying conductors in magnetic fields: ELECTRIC MOTORS, ELECTRIC GENERATORS, LOUDSPEAKERS, MICROPHONES and MEASURING INSTRUMENTS.**

> [!NOTE]
> **MAGNETISM IN MEDICINE.** Even the weak ion currents travelling along nerve cells produce magnetic fields — about **one-billionth** of the earth's field. **The heart and the brain produce the most significant ones.** Magnetic fields inside the body are the basis of **MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)**, whose images help in medical diagnosis.

## 12.4 Domestic electric circuits

```
LIVE wire     — RED insulation      (also called positive)
NEUTRAL wire  — BLACK insulation    (also called negative)
   the potential difference between them is 220 V in India, at 50 Hz

EARTH wire    — GREEN insulation
   connected to a metal plate buried deep in the earth near the house
```

**The path: mains → main fuse → electricity meter → main switch → the line wires of the house.**

```
TWO separate circuits are usually run:
   15 A rating — for high-power appliances: geysers, air coolers …
    5 A rating — for bulbs, fans …
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **APPLIANCES ARE CONNECTED IN PARALLEL, each with its own switch.**
>
> **Why: so that EACH GETS THE SAME FULL POTENTIAL DIFFERENCE of 220 V**, draws the current it needs, and can be switched on or off without affecting the others.

### The earth wire

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The EARTH WIRE is a SAFETY MEASURE, especially for appliances with a METALLIC BODY** — an electric press, toaster, table fan, refrigerator.
>
> **How it works: the metallic body is connected to the earth wire, which provides a LOW-RESISTANCE conducting path to the ground.**
>
> **So if current leaks to the body, it flows away to earth instead of through you — the body's potential stays at earth potential, and the user does not get a severe shock.**
>
> **Why LOW resistance is the essential point: current takes the easiest path.** The earth wire is deliberately made far easier than a human body, so almost none goes through the person.

### Short-circuiting and overloading

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SHORT-CIRCUITING: the LIVE and NEUTRAL wires come into DIRECT CONTACT** — because the insulation is damaged, or there is a fault in the appliance. **The current then increases abruptly.**
>
> **OVERLOADING can also arise from:**
> - **an accidental hike in the supply voltage**
> - **connecting TOO MANY APPLIANCES to a single socket**
>
> **THE FUSE prevents damage in both cases.** The **Joule heating** in the fuse wire melts it, breaking the circuit and stopping the unduly high current.
>
> **The precaution against overloading: do not connect too many appliances to one socket, and use the right circuit rating for the job.**

> [!TIP]
> **What happens if you run a 2 kW oven on a 5 A circuit at 220 V?**
> ```
> I = P/V = 2000/220 = 9.09 A
> ```
> **That is well above the 5 A rating, so the FUSE WILL BLOW** — which is the system working exactly as intended. **The oven belongs on the 15 A circuit.**

**The two commonest safety measures in domestic circuits: the ELECTRIC FUSE and the EARTH WIRE.**

## Summary

```
MAGNETIC FIELD LINES
   a field line is the path a free NORTH pole would take
   OUTSIDE a magnet: north → south;  INSIDE: south → north; CLOSED curves
   crowded lines = STRONGER field
   NO two field lines cross — a compass cannot point two ways at once

FIELD DUE TO A CURRENT
   a current-carrying wire always has a magnetic field around it
   REVERSING the current REVERSES the field
   STRAIGHT WIRE — concentric circles; direction by the RIGHT-HAND THUMB
      RULE (thumb along the current, fingers wrap along the field)
      the field weakens with distance from the wire
   CIRCULAR LOOP — near the centre the arcs look like straight lines, and
      every part of the loop contributes IN THE SAME DIRECTION
      a coil of n turns gives n times the field, because the turns ADD
   SOLENOID — field like a BAR MAGNET; inside, parallel straight lines
      → a UNIFORM field
   ELECTROMAGNET — soft iron core inside a solenoid; soft iron is chosen
      because it LOSES its magnetism when the current stops

FORCE ON A CONDUCTOR
   a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field EXPERIENCES A FORCE
   the force is largest when current and field are at RIGHT ANGLES,
      and acts PERPENDICULAR to both
   FLEMING'S LEFT-HAND RULE — Forefinger = FIELD, Centre finger = CURRENT,
      Thumb = FORCE
   right hand answers "what field does a current make?";
   left hand answers "what force does a field exert on a current?"
   used in motors, generators, loudspeakers, microphones, meters

DOMESTIC CIRCUITS
   LIVE red · NEUTRAL black · EARTH green;  220 V at 50 Hz
   separate 15 A and 5 A circuits; appliances in PARALLEL, each with a switch,
      so each gets the full 220 V and works independently
   EARTH WIRE gives a LOW-RESISTANCE path to the ground, so leaked current
      goes to earth instead of through the user
   SHORT-CIRCUIT — live and neutral touch, and the current jumps
   OVERLOADING — too many appliances on one socket, or a voltage surge
   the FUSE melts by Joule heating and breaks the circuit in both cases
```
