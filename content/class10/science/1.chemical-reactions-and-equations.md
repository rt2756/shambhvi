## 1.1 How do you know a reaction has happened?

**Milk turning sour. An iron pan rusting. Grapes fermenting. Food cooking, and food being digested. Every one of these changes the IDENTITY of the substance you started with — that is a CHEMICAL REACTION.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Four visible clues that a chemical reaction has taken place:**
> - **change of state**
> - **change of colour**
> - **evolution of a gas**
> - **change of temperature**
>
> **Why these are the clues: a new substance has new properties.** Reactions work by **breaking bonds between atoms and making new ones**, and the new arrangement has its own colour, its own state, and its own energy — so something you can see or feel has to change.

**Burning magnesium ribbon:** it burns with a dazzling white flame and leaves a **white powder** — magnesium oxide, made from magnesium and the oxygen in the air.

## 1.2 Writing a chemical equation

**A sentence is too long. A WORD-EQUATION is shorter:**
```
Magnesium + Oxygen  →  Magnesium oxide
 (reactants)              (product)
```
- **REACTANTS on the LEFT**, joined by `+`
- **PRODUCTS on the RIGHT**, joined by `+`
- **the arrowhead points towards the products** — it shows the direction of change

**Chemical formulae make it shorter still:**
```
Mg + O2 → MgO          this is a SKELETAL equation — not yet balanced
```

## Balancing — and why it is compulsory

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The LAW OF CONSERVATION OF MASS: mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.**
>
> **So the number of atoms of each element must be the SAME before and after.** Atoms are only rearranged — none appears from nowhere and none vanishes.
>
> **An unbalanced equation therefore claims something impossible**, which is why every equation must be balanced before it means anything.

**Balancing Fe + H2O → Fe3O4 + H2, step by step (the "hit-and-trial" method):**
```
Step 1  Count the atoms on each side
        Fe:  1 → 3      H:  2 → 2      O:  1 → 4

Step 2  Start with the compound holding the MOST atoms — Fe3O4 —
        and the element with most atoms in it, oxygen

Step 3  Balance O:  4 H2O on the left
        Fe + 4H2O → Fe3O4 + H2

Step 4  Balance H:  now 8 on the left, so 4H2 on the right
        Fe + 4H2O → Fe3O4 + 4H2

Step 5  Balance Fe:  3 on the right, so 3Fe on the left
        3Fe + 4H2O → Fe3O4 + 4H2                        BALANCED ✔

Step 6  Recount everything to check
        Fe: 3 = 3     H: 8 = 8     O: 4 = 4  ✔
```

> [!WARNING]
> **You may change the COEFFICIENT in front of a formula. You may NEVER change the formula itself.**
>
> To get four oxygens you write **4H2O** — never `H2O4` or `(H2O)4`.
>
> **Why: the formula IS the substance.** H2O is water; H2O4 is not a different amount of water, it is a different (and imaginary) chemical. **Changing a subscript changes what the reaction is about; changing a coefficient only changes how much of it there is.**

> [!TIP]
> **Start with the biggest, messiest compound and leave free elements (Fe, O2, H2) till last.** A free element can be adjusted freely without disturbing anything else, so it is the easiest thing to fix at the end.

### Making an equation more informative

```
(s) solid     (l) liquid     (g) gas     (aq) aqueous — dissolved in water

3Fe(s) + 4H2O(g) → Fe3O4(s) + 4H2(g)
```
**Conditions go above or below the arrow:**
```
                    340 atm
CO(g) + 2H2(g)  ------------→  CH3OH(l)

                    Sunlight
6CO2 + 12H2O   ---------------→  C6H12O6 + 6O2 + 6H2O
                   Chlorophyll
```

## 1.3 The five types of reaction

### Combination — two or more become one

```
CaO(s) + H2O(l) → Ca(OH)2(aq) + Heat
(quick lime)        (slaked lime)

C(s) + O2(g) → CO2(g)                     burning of coal
2H2(g) + O2(g) → 2H2O(l)
```

> [!NOTE]
> **Whitewashing uses this twice over.** Slaked lime is painted on the wall, and then it **reacts slowly with the carbon dioxide in the air**:
> ```
> Ca(OH)2(aq) + CO2(g) → CaCO3(s) + H2O(l)
> ```
> **The thin layer of calcium carbonate that forms over two or three days is what gives the shiny white finish** — and CaCO3 is also the formula of marble.

### Decomposition — one becomes many

**The exact opposite of combination.**
```
                   Heat
2FeSO4(s)      ---------→  Fe2O3(s) + SO2(g) + SO3(g)
(green crystals)           (ferric oxide, and the smell of burning sulphur)

                   Heat
CaCO3(s)       ---------→  CaO(s) + CO2(g)         THERMAL decomposition
(limestone)                (quick lime — used to make cement)

                   Heat
2Pb(NO3)2(s)   ---------→  2PbO(s) + 4NO2(g) + O2(g)     brown fumes of NO2

                 Sunlight
2AgCl(s)       ---------→  2Ag(s) + Cl2(g)      white turns grey
2AgBr(s)       ---------→  2Ag(s) + Br2(g)      used in black-and-white photography
```

**ELECTROLYSIS OF WATER** — decomposition driven by electricity:
```
2H2O(l) → 2H2(g) + O2(g)
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The hydrogen test tube collects TWICE the volume of the oxygen one.**
>
> **Why: the balanced equation says so — 2 molecules of H2 for every 1 of O2.** The volumes of gases stand in the same ratio as the number of molecules, so the 2 : 1 in the equation shows up directly as 2 : 1 in the test tubes.
>
> **This is the equation becoming visible.** Hydrogen burns with a pop; oxygen makes a glowing splint burn brighter.

### Exothermic and endothermic

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **EXOTHERMIC — heat is RELEASED** along with the products. The vessel gets **warm**.
> - **ENDOTHERMIC — energy is ABSORBED** (as heat, light or electricity). The vessel gets **cold**, or the reaction needs a supply.
>
> ```
> exothermic:  CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2 + heat
>              CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O          burning natural gas
>              C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy      RESPIRATION
>              vegetable matter decomposing into compost
>
> endothermic: thermal decomposition, electrolysis, photolysis by sunlight
>              barium hydroxide + ammonium chloride — the tube turns COLD
> ```

> [!TIP]
> **Combination reactions are usually exothermic and decomposition reactions usually endothermic — and the reason is the same in both cases.**
>
> **Making bonds RELEASES energy; breaking bonds ABSORBS it.** Combination mostly makes bonds, so heat comes out. Decomposition mostly breaks them, so energy must be put in — which is exactly why you have to heat, shine light on, or pass current through the reactant.

> [!NOTE]
> **Respiration is an exothermic reaction, and this is why you are warm.** Glucose from digested food combines with oxygen inside your cells, and the energy released is what keeps you alive and at 37 °C.

### Displacement — one element pushes another out

```
Fe(s) + CuSO4(aq) → FeSO4(aq) + Cu(s)
                    the blue colour FADES and the nail turns brownish

Zn(s) + CuSO4(aq) → ZnSO4(aq) + Cu(s)
Pb(s) + CuCl2(aq) → PbCl2(aq) + Cu(s)
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A MORE REACTIVE element displaces a LESS reactive one from its compound.**
>
> Iron, zinc and lead are all more reactive than copper, so they can throw copper out. **Copper cannot do the reverse** — it is not reactive enough to displace iron from iron sulphate.
>
> **The fading blue and the brown coating are the two halves of one event**: copper leaving the solution and settling on the nail.

### Double displacement — partners are exchanged

```
Na2SO4(aq) + BaCl2(aq) → BaSO4(s) + 2NaCl(aq)
                          a WHITE PRECIPITATE
```
**The ions swap partners: Ba²⁺ pairs with SO4²⁻, and Na⁺ with Cl⁻.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A PRECIPITATE is an insoluble solid formed in solution. Any reaction producing one is a PRECIPITATION reaction.**
>
> **Why the reaction happens at all: the precipitate leaves the solution.** BaSO4 is insoluble, so once formed it drops out and cannot go back — which drags the exchange forwards.
>
> The other product, NaCl, is soluble and simply stays dissolved.

**Lead nitrate + potassium iodide** gives a **yellow** precipitate of lead iodide:
```
Pb(NO3)2(aq) + 2KI(aq) → PbI2(s) + 2KNO3(aq)
```

## 1.4 Oxidation and reduction

```
                    Heat
2Cu + O2        ---------→  2CuO        copper gains oxygen — OXIDISED
                                        (a black coating forms)
                    Heat
CuO + H2        ---------→  Cu + H2O    CuO loses oxygen  — REDUCED
                                        H2 gains oxygen   — OXIDISED
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **OXIDATION — gain of oxygen, OR loss of hydrogen.**
> **REDUCTION — loss of oxygen, OR gain of hydrogen.**
>
> **The two ALWAYS happen together, in the same reaction** — such reactions are called **REDOX** reactions.
>
> **Why they must go together: the oxygen has to come from somewhere and go somewhere.** If one substance gains oxygen, whatever supplied it must have lost it. **There is no oxidation without a matching reduction.**

```
ZnO + C → Zn + CO                  C is oxidised, ZnO is reduced
MnO2 + 4HCl → MnCl2 + 2H2O + Cl2   HCl is oxidised, MnO2 is reduced
```

> [!TIP]
> **A quick way to spot the pair:** find the substance that ends up with more oxygen — that one was oxidised — and the substance that ended up with less — that one was reduced. **In burning magnesium, Mg gains oxygen, so magnesium is oxidised.**

## 1.5 Oxidation in everyday life

### Corrosion

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **CORROSION is a metal being eaten away by substances around it — moisture, air, acids.**
>
> ```
> iron    →  reddish-brown RUST
> silver  →  a BLACK coating
> copper  →  a GREEN coating
> ```
> **Why it matters: it destroys car bodies, bridges, railings and ships**, and enormous sums are spent every year replacing corroded iron.

### Rancidity

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **RANCIDITY: fats and oils in food are OXIDISED over time, and their taste and smell turn unpleasant.**
>
> **How it is slowed down — and why each method works:**
> - **ANTIOXIDANTS are added** — they are oxidised in place of the food, so the oxygen is used up on them instead
> - **AIR-TIGHT containers** — less oxygen can reach the food
> - **flushing packets with NITROGEN** — nitrogen is unreactive, so it displaces the oxygen without harming the food (this is why a chips packet is puffed up)
> - **REFRIGERATION** — cold slows every reaction down, including this one
>
> **Every method attacks the same thing: contact between the fat and oxygen.**

## Summary

```
A chemical reaction changes the IDENTITY of a substance; the clues are a
change of state, colour, temperature, or a gas coming off

BALANCING — required by the LAW OF CONSERVATION OF MASS
   same number of atoms of each element on both sides
   change COEFFICIENTS only, never the formula itself
   start with the biggest compound; leave free elements to the end
   state symbols (s) (l) (g) (aq); conditions above the arrow

THE FIVE TYPES
   COMBINATION           A + B → AB              usually EXOTHERMIC
   DECOMPOSITION         AB → A + B              usually ENDOTHERMIC
                         thermal / electrolytic / by light
   DISPLACEMENT          A + BC → AC + B         A must be MORE reactive
   DOUBLE DISPLACEMENT   AB + CD → AD + CB       ions swap partners;
                         a precipitate drives it forward
   REDOX                 oxidation and reduction together

EXO vs ENDO — making bonds RELEASES energy, breaking them ABSORBS it
   respiration is exothermic; that is why you are warm

OXIDATION  gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen
REDUCTION  loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen
   they always occur together — the oxygen must come from somewhere

electrolysis of water gives TWICE as much hydrogen as oxygen — because
the balanced equation says 2H2 : 1O2

CORROSION — iron rusts reddish-brown, silver blackens, copper greens
RANCIDITY — fats oxidise; stopped by antioxidants, air-tight packing,
            nitrogen flushing and refrigeration — all of which cut off oxygen
```
