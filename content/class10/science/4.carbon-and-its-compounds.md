## 4.1 Why carbon shares instead of transferring

**Carbon has ATOMIC NUMBER 6, so its configuration is (2,4) — FOUR valence electrons. To reach a noble-gas arrangement it needs to gain or lose four. Neither works:**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **Gain 4 electrons → C⁴⁻.** A nucleus with only **six protons could not hold on to ten electrons**.
> - **Lose 4 electrons → C⁴⁺.** It would take **an enormous amount of energy** to strip four electrons off, leaving six protons holding just two electrons.
>
> **So carbon does neither. It SHARES its valence electrons instead** — and the shared pairs belong to the outermost shell of **both** atoms, so both reach the noble-gas count.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A COVALENT BOND is a SHARED PAIR of electrons.**
> ```
> single bond   one shared pair    H–H
> double bond   two shared pairs   O=O
> triple bond   three shared pairs N≡N
> ```
> **Hydrogen (K shell needs 2): two H atoms share one pair each — that is H2.**
> **Oxygen has 6 valence electrons and needs 2 more, so two oxygen atoms share TWO pairs — a double bond.**

### Why covalent compounds behave so differently from ionic ones

| | Ionic compounds | Covalent compounds |
|---|---|---|
| **Melting/boiling point** | **high** (NaCl melts at 1074 K) | **low** (methane melts at 90 K) |
| **Conduct electricity** | yes, when molten or dissolved | **generally NOT** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Both differences have one explanation: covalent bonding produces NO IONS.**
>
> - **No ions means nothing to carry charge** — hence poor conductors.
> - **The bond INSIDE a molecule is strong, but the force BETWEEN separate molecules is weak** — and melting only has to overcome the forces *between* molecules. So little energy is needed, and the melting point is low.
>
> **A common confusion worth clearing: a low melting point does NOT mean a weak bond.** The C–H bonds in methane are strong; it is the pull between one methane molecule and the next that is feeble.

## 4.2 Why there are millions of carbon compounds

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **TWO properties explain the entire chapter — and all of organic chemistry.**
>
> **1. CATENATION — carbon's ability to bond with other carbon atoms**, forming long chains, branched chains and rings, joined by single, double or triple bonds.
>
> **2. TETRAVALENCY — carbon has a valency of FOUR**, so it can bond to four other atoms: more carbons, or hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, sulphur, chlorine and many more.

> [!TIP]
> **Why only carbon does this so well: the CARBON–CARBON BOND IS EXCEPTIONALLY STRONG AND STABLE.**
>
> **Silicon also catenates — but only up to seven or eight atoms, and those compounds are very reactive**, so they fall apart.
>
> **The reason is carbon's SMALL SIZE.** A small atom's nucleus is close to the shared pairs and holds them tightly. **Bigger atoms hold shared electrons more loosely, so their bonds are weaker.**
>
> **Millions of carbon compounds are known — more than all the compounds of every other element put together.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Organic chemistry got its name from a mistake.** These compounds were first obtained from living things, and it was believed a "vital force" from a living system was needed to make them. **Friedrich Wöhler disproved this in 1828 by making urea from ammonium cyanate** — a laboratory synthesis of something previously thought to require life.

### Saturated and unsaturated

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **SATURATED — only SINGLE bonds between carbon atoms.** These are the **ALKANES**.
> - **UNSATURATED — containing a DOUBLE or TRIPLE bond.** Double → **ALKENES**; triple → **ALKYNES**.
>
> **HYDROCARBONS are compounds of carbon and hydrogen only.**

**How to build a structure — a reliable two-step method:**
```
Step 1: link the CARBON atoms first
        C — C

Step 2: use HYDROGEN to satisfy every remaining valency
        each carbon must end up with exactly FOUR bonds

        H   H
        |   |
     H—C — C—H       ETHANE, C2H6
        |   |
        H   H
```

### Isomers, rings and benzene

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **STRUCTURAL ISOMERS have the SAME molecular formula but DIFFERENT structures.**
> ```
> C4H10 can be built two ways:
>
>    C—C—C—C            a straight chain (butane)
>
>    C—C—C              a branched chain (isobutane)
>      |
>      C
> ```
> **Same atoms, same count — different arrangement, and therefore different properties.** Isomerism is only possible from **four carbons onwards**; with three or fewer there is only one way to arrange them.

```
cyclohexane  C6H12  — six carbons in a RING, all single bonds
benzene      C6H6   — a six-carbon ring with alternate double bonds
```
**Straight-chain, branched and cyclic compounds may all be saturated or unsaturated.**

## 4.3 Functional groups

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A HETEROATOM is an atom other than carbon or hydrogen sitting in the chain. A FUNCTIONAL GROUP is a heteroatom or group of atoms that gives the compound its characteristic properties.**

| Heteroatom | Class | Functional group | Suffix / prefix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cl, Br | **Haloalkane** | `—Cl`, `—Br` | prefix **chloro-, bromo-** |
| O | **Alcohol** | `—OH` | suffix **-ol** |
| O | **Aldehyde** | `—CHO` | suffix **-al** |
| O | **Ketone** | `—CO—` | suffix **-one** |
| O | **Carboxylic acid** | `—COOH` | suffix **-oic acid** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE FUNCTIONAL GROUP DECIDES THE CHEMICAL PROPERTIES — regardless of how long the carbon chain is.**
>
> **CH3OH, C2H5OH, C3H7OH and C4H9OH all behave the same chemically**, because every one of them carries `—OH`. **The chain is a passenger; the group does the chemistry.**

## 4.4 Homologous series

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A HOMOLOGOUS SERIES is a family of compounds in which the SAME FUNCTIONAL GROUP substitutes for hydrogen in carbon chains of increasing length.**
>
> **Successive members differ by a `—CH2—` unit, which is 14 u of molecular mass.**
> ```
> CH4  → C2H6  → C3H8  → C4H10        differ by CH2 each time
> 16 u    30 u    44 u    58 u        differ by 14 u each time
> ```

**General formulas:**
```
alkanes   CnH2n+2       CH4, C2H6, C3H8, C4H10
alkenes   CnH2n         C2H4, C3H6, C4H8, C5H10        n = 2, 3, 4 …
alkynes   CnH2n−2       C2H2, C3H4, C4H6
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Within a homologous series:**
> - **PHYSICAL properties change GRADUALLY** — melting point, boiling point and solubility all shift steadily as the molecular mass rises.
> - **CHEMICAL properties stay THE SAME** — because they are decided solely by the functional group.
>
> **Why physical properties change: bigger molecules attract each other more strongly**, so more energy is needed to separate them and the boiling point climbs.
>
> **This is what makes a homologous series so useful: learn one member's chemistry and you know them all**, and you can predict the physical properties of a member you have never met.

## 4.5 Naming carbon compounds

```
1. Count the carbons and take the stem:
      1 meth-   2 eth-   3 prop-   4 but-   5 pent-   6 hex-

2. Add the ending for the family:
      saturated  -ane      double bond  -ene      triple bond  -yne

3. Add the functional group as a prefix or suffix.

4. If the suffix begins with a VOWEL, drop the final 'e' of the stem:
      propane − e + one  =  PROPANONE
      propane − e + ol   =  PROPANOL
      but chloro- is a PREFIX, so nothing is dropped: CHLOROPROPANE
```

**Worked names:**
```
CH3CH2Br            bromoethane
CH3CH2OH            ethanol
CH3COOH             ethanoic acid
CH3COCH3            propanone
CH3CH2CHO           propanal
CH2=CH2             ethene
CH≡CH               ethyne
```

## 4.6 Chemical properties

### Combustion

```
C + O2 → CO2 + heat and light
CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O + heat and light
CH3CH2OH + 3O2 → 2CO2 + 3H2O + heat and light
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SATURATED hydrocarbons burn with a CLEAN BLUE flame. UNSATURATED ones burn with a YELLOW SOOTY flame** that leaves a black deposit.
>
> **Why: the soot is unburnt CARBON.** An unsaturated compound has a higher proportion of carbon, and there is not enough oxygen to burn it all — so some escapes as smoke.
>
> **But limiting the AIR SUPPLY makes even a saturated hydrocarbon burn sooty**, for the same reason.

> [!TIP]
> **This is why a blackened cooking vessel means WASTED FUEL, not badly cooked food.** A gas stove has air inlets so that an oxygen-rich mixture burns cleanly and blue. **A blackened bottom means the air holes are blocked**, combustion is incomplete, and part of the fuel is leaving as soot instead of releasing its heat.

> [!NOTE]
> **Why some things burn with a flame and some just glow: a FLAME is only produced when GASEOUS substances burn.**
>
> Wood and charcoal give a flame at first, while the volatile substances in them vaporise and burn. **Once those are gone, the charcoal simply GLOWS red** — hot solid carbon reacting with oxygen, with no gas to feed a flame.
>
> **A LUMINOUS flame is glowing atoms**, and the colour is characteristic of the element. **The yellow of a candle flame is glowing soot particles.**

**Coal and petroleum contain some nitrogen and sulphur, and burning them produces oxides of sulphur and nitrogen — major air pollutants.**

### Oxidation

```
ethanol  --[alkaline KMnO4 or acidified K2Cr2O7]-->  ethanoic acid
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **An OXIDISING AGENT is a substance that ADDS OXYGEN to another substance.**
>
> **Alkaline potassium permanganate and acidified potassium dichromate turn ALCOHOLS into CARBOXYLIC ACIDS** — a controlled addition of oxygen, quite different from burning the compound away entirely.
>
> **The colour of KMnO4 disappears while there is still alcohol left to oxidise, and persists once the alcohol is used up** — so the colour itself tells you when the reaction is finished.

### Addition reaction — only for unsaturated compounds

```
                       Ni or Pd catalyst
CH2=CH2 + H2   ------------------------→   CH3—CH3
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **UNSATURATED hydrocarbons ADD hydrogen in the presence of a catalyst (nickel or palladium) to become SATURATED.**
>
> **A CATALYST changes the rate of a reaction without itself being changed.**
>
> **This is the HYDROGENATION of vegetable oils** — turning liquid oil into solid vanaspati with a nickel catalyst.
>
> **Vegetable OILS have long UNSATURATED chains; animal FATS are SATURATED.** Saturated fats are considered harmful to health, so **oils containing unsaturated fatty acids should be preferred for cooking** — and hydrogenating an oil moves it in the unhealthy direction.

### Substitution reaction — the reaction of saturated compounds

```
                Sunlight
CH4 + Cl2   ------------→   CH3Cl + HCl
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SUBSTITUTION: one atom or group takes the place of another.**
>
> **Saturated hydrocarbons are fairly UNREACTIVE and inert to most reagents** — but in sunlight, chlorine replaces their hydrogen atoms one by one, very fast.
>
> **Why substitution and not addition: there is no double bond to open up.** A saturated molecule has no spare capacity, so the only way in is to throw something out. **The type of reaction is decided by the structure.**

## 4.7 Ethanol — CH3CH2OH

**A liquid at room temperature, soluble in water in all proportions, and a good solvent — used in tincture iodine, cough syrups and tonics. It is the active ingredient of all alcoholic drinks.**

**Reaction with sodium:**
```
2Na + 2CH3CH2OH → 2CH3CH2O⁻Na⁺ + H2
                  (sodium ethoxide)
```
**Hydrogen is evolved — the same test you used for acids with metals.**

**Dehydration to an alkene:**
```
                  Hot conc. H2SO4, 443 K
CH3CH2OH   -----------------------------→   CH2=CH2 + H2O
```
**The concentrated sulphuric acid acts as a DEHYDRATING AGENT — it removes water from the ethanol.**

> [!WARNING]
> **Ethanol in quantity slows the metabolism and DEPRESSES THE CENTRAL NERVOUS SYSTEM** — causing lack of coordination, mental confusion, drowsiness and eventually stupor. **The drinker feels relaxed without realising that judgement, timing and muscular coordination are seriously impaired.**
>
> **METHANOL is far worse — a very small quantity can KILL.** In the liver it is oxidised to methanal, which **coagulates protoplasm** — much as cooking coagulates an egg — and **attacks the optic nerve, causing blindness.**
>
> **DENATURED ALCOHOL is industrial ethanol made undrinkable** by adding poisonous methanol, plus a blue dye so it can be recognised at a glance.

> [!NOTE]
> **Alcohol as a fuel: sugarcane is one of the most efficient converters of sunlight into chemical energy.** Its juice gives molasses, which is fermented to ethanol. **Some countries add it to petrol** because burning it in sufficient air gives only carbon dioxide and water.

## 4.8 Ethanoic acid — CH3COOH

**Commonly called ACETIC ACID; a 5–8% solution in water is VINEGAR, used as a preservative in pickles.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Why it is called GLACIAL acetic acid: pure ethanoic acid melts at 290 K (about 17 °C), so in a cold climate it FREEZES in winter** into ice-like crystals.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Carboxylic acids are WEAK acids** — unlike mineral acids such as HCl, which ionise completely.
>
> **Both turn blue litmus red**, so litmus cannot tell them apart. **But a universal indicator can**: dilute acetic acid shows a much higher pH than dilute HCl of the same concentration, revealing that fewer H⁺ ions were released.

**Esterification:**
```
                          acid catalyst
CH3COOH + CH3CH2OH   ------------------→   CH3COOC2H5 + H2O
(ethanoic acid) (ethanol)                  (an ESTER)
```
**ESTERS are sweet-smelling — used in perfumes and as flavouring agents.**

**Saponification — the reverse, with an alkali:**
```
CH3COOC2H5 + NaOH → C2H5OH + CH3COONa
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SAPONIFICATION is named for soap-making.** Treating an ester with sodium hydroxide gives back the alcohol plus the **sodium salt of the carboxylic acid** — and **SOAPS ARE EXACTLY THAT: sodium or potassium salts of LONG-CHAIN carboxylic acids.**

**With a base:**
```
NaOH + CH3COOH → CH3COONa + H2O
                 (sodium acetate)
```

**With carbonates and hydrogencarbonates:**
```
2CH3COOH + Na2CO3  → 2CH3COONa + H2O + CO2
 CH3COOH + NaHCO3  →  CH3COONa + H2O + CO2
```
> [!TIP]
> **This is how to tell an alcohol from a carboxylic acid experimentally: add sodium hydrogencarbonate.**
>
> **The acid gives BRISK EFFERVESCENCE of CO2 — which turns lime water milky. The alcohol does nothing at all.**

## 4.9 Soaps and detergents

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A soap molecule has TWO ENDS WITH OPPOSITE TASTES:**
> ```
> ——————————————————————— COO⁻ Na⁺
> the long HYDROCARBON tail        the IONIC head
> HYDROPHOBIC — dissolves in oil   HYDROPHILIC — dissolves in water
> ```
> **That split personality is the whole secret of cleaning.**

**How a MICELLE works:**
```
Most dirt is OILY, and oil does not dissolve in water.

The soap molecules surround an oil droplet:
   the hydrocarbon TAILS point INWARDS, into the oil
   the ionic HEADS point OUTWARDS, into the water

This cluster is a MICELLE. The dirt is trapped in the centre,
and the whole micelle floats away in the water when you rinse.
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why the micelles do not clump back together: their outsides are all NEGATIVELY charged, so they REPEL one another.** They stay suspended as a colloid — and are rinsed away carrying the dirt.
>
> **A soap solution looks CLOUDY because the micelles are large enough to SCATTER LIGHT.**

> [!TIP]
> **Why clothes must be beaten, scrubbed or agitated:** the soap surrounds the oily dirt, but the dirt is still **stuck to the fabric**. **Agitation supplies the mechanical energy to knock it loose** so the micelles can carry it off. **Soap alone does the chemistry; agitation does the physics.**

### Hard water, scum and detergents

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **HARD WATER contains calcium and magnesium salts. Soap reacts with them to form an insoluble white CURDY precipitate — SCUM.**
>
> **Consequences: little foam forms, a great deal more soap is needed, and scum is left on the clothes and on you.**
>
> **DETERGENTS solve this.** They are **sodium salts of sulphonic acids, or ammonium salts with chloride or bromide ions** — also with a long hydrocarbon chain.
>
> **Why they work in hard water: their charged ends do NOT form insoluble precipitates with calcium and magnesium ions.** So they lather and clean normally. Detergents are used in shampoos and washing products.

> [!TIP]
> **You can test whether water is hard using SOAP, not detergent.** Soap gives scum and poor foam in hard water; a detergent behaves the same in both, **so it would tell you nothing.** The test works precisely because soap fails.

## Summary

```
CARBON shares electrons rather than transferring them — gaining or losing
four would be impossible for a nucleus of only six protons
COVALENT BOND = a shared pair; double = two pairs; triple = three
   covalent compounds: LOW melting/boiling points, POOR conductors —
   because there are NO IONS, and the force BETWEEN molecules is weak

WHY MILLIONS OF CARBON COMPOUNDS
   CATENATION — carbon bonds to carbon in chains, branches and rings
   TETRAVALENCY — four bonds to four other atoms
   the C–C bond is strong because carbon is SMALL, so the nucleus grips
   the shared pairs tightly

SATURATED (single bonds) = ALKANES CnH2n+2
UNSATURATED: ALKENES CnH2n (double) · ALKYNES CnH2n−2 (triple)
STRUCTURAL ISOMERS — same formula, different structure (from C4 upwards)

FUNCTIONAL GROUPS decide the chemistry, whatever the chain length
   —OH alcohol (-ol) · —CHO aldehyde (-al) · —CO— ketone (-one)
   —COOH carboxylic acid (-oic acid) · —Cl/—Br haloalkane (chloro-/bromo-)

HOMOLOGOUS SERIES — same group, chains differing by —CH2— (14 u)
   physical properties change GRADUALLY; chemical properties stay the SAME

REACTIONS
   COMBUSTION — saturated burn blue and clean; unsaturated burn yellow
      and sooty; too little air makes even alkanes sooty (soot = unburnt carbon)
   OXIDATION — alkaline KMnO4 / acidified K2Cr2O7 turn ALCOHOLS into ACIDS
   ADDITION — unsaturated + H2 with Ni/Pd → saturated (hydrogenation of oils)
   SUBSTITUTION — saturated + Cl2 in sunlight; no double bond to open, so an
      atom must be thrown out instead

ETHANOL   + Na → sodium ethoxide + H2;  hot conc. H2SO4 dehydrates it to ethene
   methanol is lethal — oxidised to methanal, coagulates protoplasm, blinds
   denatured alcohol = industrial ethanol poisoned with methanol and dyed

ETHANOIC ACID  vinegar is 5–8%; a WEAK acid; "glacial" because it freezes at 290 K
   + alcohol → ESTER (sweet-smelling) ;  ester + NaOH → SAPONIFICATION
   + carbonate/hydrogencarbonate → CO2 — the test that distinguishes it
     from an alcohol

SOAPS — sodium/potassium salts of long-chain carboxylic acids
   hydrophobic tail in the oil, hydrophilic head in the water → MICELLE
   micelles repel each other (like charges), stay suspended, rinse away
   agitation is needed to knock the dirt off the fabric
   HARD WATER gives SCUM with soap; DETERGENTS work anyway because their
   charged ends form no precipitate with Ca and Mg ions
```
