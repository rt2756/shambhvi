## 8.1 How variation accumulates

**Inheritance gives the next generation TWO things: a common basic body design, AND subtle changes in it.**

```
generation 1  →  two individuals, similar design, subtle differences
generation 2  →  each of those gives rise to two more

Each individual in the bottom row differs from the others. SOME of those
differences are UNIQUE to it; OTHERS were INHERITED from its parents,
who were already different from each other.
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **So variation ACCUMULATES over generations** — new differences pile on top of inherited ones.
>
> **Asexual reproduction gives only minor differences**, caused by small inaccuracies in DNA copying. Four bacteria descended from one are very similar.
>
> **Sexual reproduction generates far greater diversity**, because it also **recombines** the variations two different parents already carried.
>
> **A field of sugarcane (propagated vegetatively) shows very little variation. A room of human beings shows a great deal.**

> [!TIP]
> **A useful consequence for reasoning about age of a trait:** in an asexually reproducing species, **a trait present in a LARGER fraction of the population probably AROSE EARLIER**.
>
> **Why: a new variation starts in ONE individual and can only spread as its descendants multiply — which takes generations.** A trait in 60% of the population has had far longer to spread than one in 10%.

**Variations do NOT have equal chances of surviving. Bacteria that can withstand heat survive a heat wave better. SELECTION OF VARIANTS BY ENVIRONMENTAL FACTORS is the basis of evolution.**

## 8.2 Mendel's experiments

> [!NOTE]
> **GREGOR JOHANN MENDEL (1822–1884)** was educated in a monastery and studied science and mathematics at Vienna. **He failed the examinations for a teaching certificate**, went back to his monastery, and started growing peas.
>
> **Others had studied inheritance before him. What made Mendel different: he blended science with MATHEMATICS and was the FIRST to KEEP COUNT of the individuals showing each trait in every generation.** Counting is what let him find the laws.

**He used contrasting visible characters of garden peas:** round/wrinkled seeds, tall/short plants, violet/white flowers, and others.

### The monohybrid cross — one trait

```
PARENTS   tall plant  ×  short plant
                TT          tt

F1        ALL TALL
                Tt

F1 self-pollinated

F2        TT : Tt : tt  =  1 : 2 : 1
          tall : short  =  3 : 1      (ONE QUARTER are short)
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Two findings, and what each proves.**
>
> **1. The F1 plants were ALL TALL — there were NO 'medium-height' plants.**
> **So the traits DO NOT BLEND.** Only one of the two parental traits appeared, not a mixture.
>
> **2. In F2, ONE QUARTER were SHORT.**
> **So the shortness trait was NEVER LOST — it was present in the F1 plants all along, merely NOT EXPRESSED.** It could not have reappeared out of nothing.
>
> **This led Mendel to propose that a sexually reproducing organism carries TWO COPIES of the factor (now called a GENE) controlling each trait.** The two may be identical or different, depending on parentage.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **DOMINANT and RECESSIVE:**
> ```
> TT  tall        a single copy of T is ENOUGH to make the plant tall
> Tt  tall        so T is DOMINANT
> tt  SHORT       both copies must be t for the plant to be short
>                 so t is RECESSIVE
> ```
> **A recessive trait appears only when NO dominant copy is present** — which is exactly why it can hide for a generation and return in the next.

> [!TIP]
> **How you would CONFIRM the 1 : 2 : 1 ratio in F2, since TT and Tt look identical.**
>
> **Self-pollinate each F2 tall plant and look at ITS offspring.**
> - **a TT plant gives ALL tall offspring**
> - **a Tt plant gives offspring in a 3 : 1 tall-to-short ratio**
>
> **So the hidden difference between the two kinds of tall plant shows up in the NEXT generation.** You cannot see a genotype directly — you deduce it from what it produces.

### The dihybrid cross — two traits at once

```
PARENTS   round, green seeds  ×  wrinkled, yellow seeds
                RRyy                    rrYY

F1        ALL ROUND and YELLOW
                RrYy
          → so ROUND and YELLOW are the DOMINANT traits

F1 × F1  self-pollinated

F2        round yellow    : round green : wrinkled yellow : wrinkled green
              9           :      3      :        3        :       1

Mendel's actual counts, out of 556 seeds:
   315 round yellow · 108 round green · 101 wrinkled yellow · 32 wrinkled green
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The decisive observation: NEW COMBINATIONS appeared in F2 that neither parent had.**
>
> **The parents were round-green and wrinkled-yellow. But F2 contained round-YELLOW and wrinkled-GREEN plants** — combinations that did not exist in the parent generation.
>
> **Conclusion: the two traits are INHERITED INDEPENDENTLY.** Seed shape and seed colour are not tied together; either version of one can travel with either version of the other.

## 8.3 How traits are actually expressed

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A GENE is a section of DNA that provides the information for making ONE protein.**

**Worked through, for tallness:**
```
plants have HORMONES that trigger growth
   → plant height depends on the AMOUNT of a particular hormone
   → that amount depends on the EFFICIENCY of the process that makes it
   → that process depends on an ENZYME

if the enzyme works efficiently  → a lot of hormone → TALL plant
if the gene for the enzyme is ALTERED so the enzyme is LESS EFFICIENT
                                 → less hormone   → SHORT plant
```
> [!TIP]
> **This is the chain that connects a gene to a visible trait, with nothing missing:**
> ```
> GENE → PROTEIN (enzyme) → amount of HORMONE → HEIGHT
> ```
> **It also explains why "T" is dominant.** A plant with one working copy still makes plenty of enzyme, so it is tall. **Only when BOTH copies are altered does the hormone run short** — which is why `tt` is needed for shortness.

### Why genes must be on chromosomes

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Both parents contribute EQUALLY to the DNA of the progeny — so both must contribute a copy of the SAME gene. Each pea plant therefore has TWO SETS of all genes, one from each parent, and each germ-cell must carry only ONE set.**
>
> **But here is the crucial argument: if a germ-cell inherited a single WHOLE gene set as one long thread, the dihybrid experiment COULD NOT WORK.**
>
> **Why: 'R' and 'y' came into the cross together on one parent's set. If a whole set travelled as a unit, they would be permanently LINKED and could never separate** — so round-yellow and wrinkled-green offspring would be impossible. **But they appeared.**
>
> **The explanation: each gene set exists NOT as one long thread of DNA but as SEPARATE INDEPENDENT PIECES — the CHROMOSOMES.**
> ```
> every cell has TWO copies of each chromosome — one from each parent
> every germ-cell takes ONE chromosome from each pair, of either
>    maternal or paternal origin
> when two germ-cells combine, the normal number is RESTORED
> ```
> **This ensures the stability of the species' DNA while still allowing traits to be shuffled independently.**

## 8.4 Sex determination

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Different species use very different strategies:**
> ```
> some REPTILES — the TEMPERATURE at which the fertilised eggs are kept
>                 decides whether the young are male or female
> SNAILS        — individuals can CHANGE SEX, so sex is not genetically fixed
> HUMAN BEINGS  — sex is largely GENETICALLY determined
> ```

**In humans:**
```
22 pairs of chromosomes have a maternal and a paternal copy — matched pairs

the 23rd pair — the SEX CHROMOSOMES — is not always a perfect pair

   WOMEN   XX   a perfect pair
   MEN     XY   a MISMATCHED pair: a normal-sized X and a short Y
```

```
              MOTHER  XX                 FATHER  XY
                 ↓                          ↓
             eggs: all X            sperms: half X, half Y

                    X (father)  →  XX  →  GIRL
                    Y (father)  →  XY  →  BOY

                    half the children boys, half girls
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE SEX OF THE CHILD IS DETERMINED BY WHAT IS INHERITED FROM THE FATHER.**
>
> **Why: EVERY child inherits an X chromosome from the mother, regardless of sex** — she has nothing else to give. **So the mother's contribution cannot make any difference.**
>
> **The father contributes either an X or a Y**, and that alone decides. **A child receiving his X is a GIRL; a child receiving his Y is a BOY.**

> [!WARNING]
> **This matters far beyond the exam. Women have been — and still are — blamed for the sex of their children.**
>
> **The biology says plainly that they cannot possibly be responsible**, since a mother can only ever contribute an X.

## Summary

```
VARIATION ACCUMULATES over generations — new differences add to inherited ones
   asexual reproduction → only minor differences from DNA-copying errors
   sexual reproduction  → far greater diversity, by recombining variations
   in an asexual species, a MORE COMMON trait probably arose EARLIER
   environmental SELECTION of variants is the basis of evolution

MENDEL — the first to COUNT individuals with each trait in each generation

MONOHYBRID CROSS   TT × tt → F1 all Tt (TALL) → F2 = 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt
   visible ratio 3 tall : 1 short
   traits do NOT blend — no medium-height plants appeared
   the short trait was never LOST, only not EXPRESSED — it came back in F2
   DOMINANT (T) needs only one copy; RECESSIVE (t) needs both

DIHYBRID CROSS   RRyy × rrYY → F1 all RrYy → F2 = 9 : 3 : 3 : 1
   NEW COMBINATIONS appeared that neither parent had
   → the two traits are INHERITED INDEPENDENTLY

GENES AND CHROMOSOMES
   a GENE is a section of DNA carrying the information for ONE protein
   gene → protein (enzyme) → amount of hormone → height
   both parents contribute equally, so each cell has TWO gene sets and each
      germ-cell carries ONE
   a gene set is NOT one long thread but SEPARATE pieces — CHROMOSOMES —
      which is the only way two traits could be inherited independently

SEX DETERMINATION
   some reptiles — by TEMPERATURE;  snails — can change sex
   humans — 22 matched pairs + one SEX pair;  women XX, men XY
   every child gets an X from the mother, so THE FATHER'S contribution
      decides:  his X → girl,  his Y → boy
```
