## 10.1 The human eye

**The eye is like a CAMERA: its lens system forms an image on a light-sensitive screen — the RETINA.**

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **CORNEA** | the thin transparent bulge at the front; **MOST OF THE REFRACTION happens here** |
| **IRIS** | a dark muscular diaphragm that **controls the size of the pupil** |
| **PUPIL** | the opening that **regulates how much light enters** |
| **EYE LENS** (crystalline lens) | provides the **finer adjustment** of focal length |
| **RETINA** | a delicate membrane with an enormous number of **light-sensitive cells** |
| **OPTIC NERVE** | carries the electrical signals to the **brain** |

**The eyeball is roughly spherical, about 2.3 cm across. The eye lens forms an INVERTED, REAL image on the retina.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A surprise worth noticing: MOST of the bending of light happens at the CORNEA, not at the lens.**
>
> **The lens only makes the fine adjustment** needed to focus objects at different distances.
>
> **Why the cornea does the heavy work: it is the boundary between AIR and the eye's material**, and that is the biggest jump in refractive index anywhere in the eye. The lens sits surrounded by fluid, so its surfaces produce far less bending.

**The light-sensitive cells generate ELECTRICAL SIGNALS on illumination; these travel to the brain along the optic nerves, and the BRAIN INTERPRETS them so that we perceive objects as they are.**

> [!TIP]
> **The image on the retina is INVERTED, yet the world looks the right way up. The correction happens in the BRAIN, not in the eye.** Seeing is not simply optics — it ends in interpretation.

## 10.2 Power of accommodation

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The eye lens is a fibrous, jelly-like material whose CURVATURE can be changed by the CILIARY MUSCLES — and changing curvature changes FOCAL LENGTH.**
> ```
> muscles RELAXED   → lens becomes THIN  → focal length INCREASES
>                   → DISTANT objects are seen clearly
>
> muscles CONTRACT  → lens becomes THICK → focal length DECREASES
>                   → NEARBY objects are seen clearly
> ```
> **ACCOMMODATION is the ability of the eye lens to adjust its focal length.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **NEAR POINT (least distance of distinct vision) — the MINIMUM distance at which an object can be seen distinctly WITHOUT STRAIN. For a young adult with normal vision it is about 25 cm.**
>
> **FAR POINT — the FARTHEST distance at which objects are seen clearly. For a normal eye it is INFINITY.**
>
> **So a normal eye sees clearly everything between 25 cm and infinity.**

> [!TIP]
> **Why you cannot read a page held 5 cm from your eye: THE FOCAL LENGTH OF THE EYE LENS CANNOT BE DECREASED BELOW A CERTAIN LIMIT.**
>
> The ciliary muscles can only squeeze the lens so far. **Beyond that limit the image cannot be brought onto the retina, so the print blurs — and the straining muscles are what make it uncomfortable.**

> [!NOTE]
> **CATARACT — in old age the crystalline lens sometimes becomes MILKY AND CLOUDY**, causing partial or complete loss of vision. **It can be treated by cataract surgery.**

## 10.3 Defects of vision

### Myopia — near-sightedness

```
CAN see nearby objects clearly · CANNOT see distant objects distinctly
the FAR POINT is nearer than infinity — perhaps only a few metres

CAUSE — the image of a distant object forms IN FRONT OF the retina
        because of (i) excessive curvature of the eye lens, or
                   (ii) elongation of the eyeball

CORRECTION — a CONCAVE lens of suitable power
```
> [!TIP]
> **Why a CONCAVE lens: the eye is already converging light too strongly, bringing it to a focus before the retina.**
>
> **A concave lens DIVERGES the incoming rays first**, so the over-strong eye then converges them exactly onto the retina. **You are handicapping the eye to compensate for its excess power** — which is why the corrective power is **negative**.

### Hypermetropia — far-sightedness

```
CAN see distant objects clearly · CANNOT see nearby objects distinctly
the NEAR POINT is FARTHER than the normal 25 cm — reading material must
be held well beyond arm's normal reading distance

CAUSE — light from a nearby object is focused BEHIND the retina
        because of (i) the focal length of the eye lens being too long, or
                   (ii) the eyeball having become too small

CORRECTION — a CONVEX lens of appropriate power
```
> [!TIP]
> **Why a CONVEX lens: the eye is not converging strongly enough, so the focus falls beyond the retina.**
>
> **Converging eye-glasses provide the ADDITIONAL FOCUSSING POWER** needed to pull the image forward onto the retina — hence a **positive** power.

### Presbyopia

```
CAUSE — the POWER OF ACCOMMODATION decreases with AGE, because of
        (i) gradual WEAKENING of the ciliary muscles, and
        (ii) diminishing FLEXIBILITY of the eye lens
EFFECT — the near point gradually recedes; nearby objects become hard to see
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **BI-FOCAL LENSES are for a person suffering from BOTH myopia and hypermetropia.**
> ```
> UPPER portion — a CONCAVE lens, for DISTANT vision
> LOWER portion — a CONVEX lens,  for NEAR vision
> ```
> **Why that arrangement and not the reverse: you look UP at distant things and DOWN at a book.** The lens is arranged to match how the eyes actually move.

**These days refractive defects can also be corrected with CONTACT LENSES or by SURGERY.**

### Working out the corrective power

**MYOPIA — the lens must bring an object at INFINITY to the eye's own FAR POINT.**
```
far point 80 cm:     u = infinity,  v = −80 cm = −0.80 m

1/f = 1/v − 1/u = 1/(−0.80) − 0 = −1.25

f = −0.80 m         P = 1/f = −1.25 D      a CONCAVE lens
```

**HYPERMETROPIA — the lens must bring an object at the NORMAL near point (25 cm) to the eye's own near point.**
```
near point 1 m:      u = −25 cm = −0.25 m,  v = −100 cm = −1.00 m

1/f = 1/v − 1/u = −1 − (−4) = 3

f = +0.33 m         P = +3 D               a CONVEX lens
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The same idea in both cases: the corrective lens forms an image of the object AT A PLACE THE EYE CAN ALREADY MANAGE — and the eye then does the rest.**
>
> **Note that v is NEGATIVE in both.** The corrective lens forms a **virtual** image on the same side as the object, and it is that virtual image the eye actually looks at.

## 10.4 Refraction through a prism

```
PE — incident ray        ∠i — angle of incidence
EF — refracted ray       ∠r — angle of refraction
FS — emergent ray        ∠e — angle of emergence
∠A — angle of the prism  ∠D — ANGLE OF DEVIATION
```
```
at the FIRST surface  — air to glass — the ray bends TOWARDS the normal
at the SECOND surface — glass to air — the ray bends AWAY from the normal
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Unlike a glass slab, a prism's emergent ray is NOT parallel to the incident ray. It is turned through the ANGLE OF DEVIATION, ∠D.**
>
> **Why: a slab's two surfaces are PARALLEL, so its two bends cancel. A prism's refracting surfaces are INCLINED to each other**, so the second bend adds to the first instead of undoing it. **The peculiar shape of the prism is the whole reason it deviates light.**

## 10.5 Dispersion of white light

**Sunlight through a narrow slit onto a prism produces a beautiful band of colours on a screen:**
```
VIBGYOR — Violet · Indigo · Blue · Green · Yellow · Orange · Red
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The band of coloured components is the SPECTRUM. The splitting of light into its component colours is DISPERSION.**
>
> **Why it happens: DIFFERENT COLOURS BEND THROUGH DIFFERENT ANGLES in the prism.**
> ```
> RED bends the LEAST
> VIOLET bends the MOST
> ```
> **So the rays of each colour emerge along different paths and become distinguishable.**

> [!NOTE]
> **ISAAC NEWTON'S decisive experiment.** He was the first to use a prism to obtain the spectrum of sunlight. **He then tried to split the colours FURTHER with a second prism — and could get no more colours.**
>
> **Next he placed a second identical prism INVERTED with respect to the first**, letting all the spectrum's colours pass through it. **A beam of WHITE LIGHT emerged from the other side.**
>
> **That recombination is what proved the point: the prism does not CREATE the colours — sunlight is MADE UP of them.** Any light giving a spectrum like sunlight's is called white light.

### The rainbow

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A RAINBOW is a natural spectrum, caused by dispersion of sunlight by tiny WATER DROPLETS in the atmosphere after a rain shower.**
>
> **Each droplet does THREE things in turn:**
> ```
> 1. REFRACTS and DISPERSES the sunlight entering it
> 2. REFLECTS it internally off the back of the drop
> 3. REFRACTS it again as it leaves
> ```
> **Why a rainbow is always OPPOSITE the Sun: the light is REFLECTED back out of the drop**, so it returns towards the side it came from. **You must have the Sun behind you to see one** — which is also why you can see a rainbow in a waterfall or fountain on a sunny day, with the Sun at your back.

## 10.6 Atmospheric refraction

**ATMOSPHERIC REFRACTION is refraction of light by the earth's atmosphere.**

**The small-scale version you can see any day: objects appear to WAVER and FLICKER when seen through the hot air rising above a fire or a radiator.**
```
the air just above the fire is HOTTER, so it is LIGHTER (less dense)
and has a slightly SMALLER refractive index than the cooler air above

the physical conditions of the refracting medium are NOT STATIONARY
   → the apparent position of the object FLUCTUATES
```

### Why stars twinkle

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Starlight entering the atmosphere is refracted CONTINUOUSLY, through a medium of GRADUALLY CHANGING refractive index.**
>
> **Since the atmosphere bends starlight TOWARDS the normal, a star appears slightly HIGHER than its actual position when seen near the horizon.**
>
> **And that apparent position is NOT STATIONARY — the atmosphere's physical conditions keep changing.**
>
> **Because stars are so distant they are effectively POINT-SIZED sources. As the light path varies, the amount of starlight entering the eye FLICKERS — brighter, then fainter. That is twinkling.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why PLANETS do NOT twinkle: they are much CLOSER, so they are EXTENDED sources rather than points.**
>
> **Think of a planet as a large collection of point sources.** Each point twinkles — but the variations of all of them **AVERAGE OUT TO ZERO** at any moment, since some are brightening while others are dimming.
>
> **So the total light arriving stays steady, and the twinkling is nullified.** This is a genuinely satisfying explanation: the effect is still happening, it just cancels itself out.

### Advance sunrise and delayed sunset

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Sun is visible about 2 MINUTES BEFORE actual sunrise and about 2 MINUTES AFTER actual sunset.**
>
> **"Actual" sunrise means the Sun's real crossing of the horizon.** Atmospheric refraction bends its light over the horizon, so we see it while it is still geometrically below.
>
> **The APPARENT FLATTENING of the Sun's disc at sunrise and sunset has the same cause** — light from the lower edge is bent more than light from the upper edge, so the disc is squashed vertically.

## 10.7 Scattering of light

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The TYNDALL EFFECT: when a beam of light strikes fine particles, the PATH OF THE BEAM ITSELF BECOMES VISIBLE**, because light is diffusely reflected off them towards us.
>
> **Where you see it: a beam of sunlight entering a smoke-filled room through a small hole, or sunlight passing through the canopy of a dense forest, where tiny water droplets in the mist scatter the light.**
>
> **The earth's atmosphere is a heterogeneous mixture of minute particles** — smoke, tiny water droplets, dust, and air molecules.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE COLOUR OF SCATTERED LIGHT DEPENDS ON THE SIZE OF THE SCATTERING PARTICLES.**
> ```
> VERY FINE particles  → scatter mainly BLUE light (short wavelengths)
> LARGER particles     → scatter longer wavelengths
> LARGE ENOUGH         → the scattered light may appear WHITE
> ```

### Why the clear sky is blue

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Air molecules and other fine particles are SMALLER than the wavelength of visible light, and such particles scatter SHORT wavelengths far more effectively than long ones.**
>
> **Red light has a wavelength about 1.8 TIMES greater than blue light.**
>
> **So as sunlight passes through the atmosphere, the fine particles scatter BLUE much more strongly than red — and it is that scattered blue light that reaches our eyes from all over the sky.**

> [!TIP]
> **The decisive test of this explanation: WITHOUT AN ATMOSPHERE THERE WOULD BE NO SCATTERING, AND THE SKY WOULD LOOK DARK.**
>
> **And that is exactly what is observed.** The sky appears dark to an astronaut, and dark to passengers flying at very high altitudes, **because scattering is not prominent where there is little or no air.**
>
> **A theory that predicts something surprising and is then confirmed is a theory worth trusting.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why DANGER SIGNALS are RED: red is SCATTERED THE LEAST by fog or smoke.**
>
> **Because it is scattered least, it travels furthest without being deflected — so it can be seen from a great distance, and still looks red rather than washed out.**
>
> **The same reasoning explains the reddening of the Sun at sunrise and sunset.** Near the horizon, sunlight travels through a much greater thickness of atmosphere; almost all the blue is scattered away along the path, and mostly red reaches the eye.

## Summary

```
THE EYE   cornea (MOST of the refraction) · iris (controls the pupil) ·
   pupil (regulates light) · eye lens (FINE adjustment) ·
   retina (light-sensitive cells) · optic nerve → brain
   the image on the retina is REAL and INVERTED; the BRAIN corrects it

ACCOMMODATION — the ciliary muscles change the lens's CURVATURE and so its
   focal length:  relaxed → thin lens, long f → DISTANT objects
                  contracted → thick lens, short f → NEAR objects
   NEAR POINT about 25 cm · FAR POINT infinity, for a normal eye
   f cannot fall below a limit, which is why very close print blurs
   CATARACT — the lens turns milky in old age; treated by surgery

DEFECTS
   MYOPIA (near-sighted) — image forms IN FRONT of the retina;
      excessive lens curvature or an elongated eyeball;
      corrected by a CONCAVE lens (negative power)
   HYPERMETROPIA (far-sighted) — image forms BEHIND the retina;
      focal length too long or the eyeball too small;
      corrected by a CONVEX lens (positive power)
   PRESBYOPIA — ciliary muscles weaken and the lens stiffens with age
   BI-FOCAL — concave above for distance, convex below for reading

PRISM   the emergent ray is deviated by ∠D — the refracting surfaces are
   INCLINED, so the two bends ADD instead of cancelling as in a slab

DISPERSION   VIBGYOR; RED bends LEAST, VIOLET bends MOST
   Newton's second, inverted prism RECOMBINED the colours into white light,
      proving the prism does not create the colours
   RAINBOW — droplets refract, disperse, reflect internally, refract again;
      always OPPOSITE the Sun, because the light is reflected back out

ATMOSPHERIC REFRACTION

      apparent position and brightness fluctuate

      their many points AVERAGE OUT to zero
   the Sun is seen about 2 min BEFORE actual sunrise and 2 min AFTER sunset;

SCATTERING
   TYNDALL EFFECT — the beam's path becomes visible off fine particles
   the colour scattered depends on PARTICLE SIZE; very fine particles
      scatter BLUE most (red's wavelength is about 1.8× blue's)
   THE SKY IS BLUE because air molecules scatter blue strongly —
      and it looks DARK to an astronaut, since without air there is no
      scattering
   DANGER SIGNALS ARE RED because red is scattered least by fog and smoke,
      so it carries furthest
```
