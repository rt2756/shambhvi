## 7.1 Why reproduction matters — and why copies are never exact

**Reproduction is unlike the other life processes: an individual does NOT need it to stay alive, and it costs a great deal of energy. So why do it?**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Reproducing organisms create new individuals that LOOK VERY MUCH LIKE THEMSELVES.**
>
> **Why: organisms look similar because their BODY DESIGNS are similar; and if body designs are similar, the BLUEPRINTS must be similar.**
>
> **The blueprint is DNA, in the chromosomes of the nucleus. DNA is the information source for making PROTEINS.** Change the information → different proteins → altered body design.
>
> **So the basic event in reproduction is THE CREATION OF A DNA COPY.**

### Why a cell must divide, rather than just push a copy out

```
1. The cell builds a copy of its DNA by chemical reactions — now there are two.
2. The two copies must be SEPARATED.
3. But simply pushing one out would fail — the ejected copy would have
   NO CELLULAR APPARATUS to maintain life processes.
4. So DNA copying is accompanied by the creation of ADDITIONAL cellular
   apparatus, and the copies separate each with its own.
   Effectively: THE CELL DIVIDES INTO TWO.
```

### Where variation comes from

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **NO BIOCHEMICAL REACTION IS ABSOLUTELY RELIABLE.**
>
> **So DNA copying carries some VARIATION every time.** The copies are similar to the original but **not identical**.
> ```
> some variations are so DRASTIC that the new DNA cannot work with the
>    cellular apparatus it inherits → the newborn cell simply DIES
> other variations are milder → the cell survives, subtly different
> ```
> **The surviving cells are therefore SIMILAR TO, BUT SUBTLY DIFFERENT FROM, each other.**
>
> **This inbuilt tendency for variation is the BASIS OF EVOLUTION.**

### Why variation is useful

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A NICHE is the well-defined place a population occupies in an ecosystem.**
>
> **Consistent DNA copying MAINTAINS the body-design features that suit the organism to its niche** — so reproduction is linked to the **stability** of populations.
>
> **But niches can change for reasons beyond any organism's control** — temperatures rise or fall, water levels change, meteorites strike.
> ```
> if a population perfectly suited to a niche has NO variation, and the
>    niche changes drastically → the whole population can be WIPED OUT
>
> if a FEW individuals carry variations → some may survive and grow further
> ```
> **Example: bacteria in temperate water when the temperature rises through global warming. Most die; the few HEAT-RESISTANT variants survive.**
>
> **The key distinction: variation may be no use at all to the INDIVIDUAL that carries it — a heat-resistant bacterium gains nothing while the water stays cool. It is the SPECIES that benefits, and only when conditions change.**

## 7.2 Asexual reproduction — one parent only

### Fission — for unicellular organisms

```
BINARY FISSION   — the cell splits into TWO
   bacteria and many protozoa — into two equal halves
   AMOEBA — the split can take place in ANY plane
   LEISHMANIA (causes kala-azar) — has a whip-like structure at one end,
      so fission occurs in a DEFINITE ORIENTATION relative to it

MULTIPLE FISSION — the cell divides into MANY daughter cells at once
   PLASMODIUM, the malarial parasite
```
> [!TIP]
> **Why Amoeba can split in any plane but Leishmania cannot.** Amoeba has **no fixed body organisation** — no front, no back — so no plane is special. **Leishmania has more organisation**, with a whip at one end, so the division must respect that structure. **More organisation means less freedom.**

### Fragmentation

**SPIROGYRA simply BREAKS UP into smaller pieces on maturing, and each fragment grows into a new individual.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why this only works for simply organised organisms.**
>
> **A complex multicellular body is NOT a random collection of cells.** Specialised cells are organised into **tissues**, tissues into **organs**, and the organs must sit at **definite positions**.
>
> **In such a carefully organised body, cell-by-cell division would be impractical** — a random fragment would not contain a working set of organs. **So complex organisms need more complex methods.**

### Regeneration

**HYDRA and PLANARIA can be cut into many pieces, and each piece grows into a complete organism.**

```
specialised cells PROLIFERATE into a large mass of cells
   → different cells change to become various cell types and tissues
   → in an organised sequence called DEVELOPMENT
```

> [!WARNING]
> **REGENERATION IS NOT REPRODUCTION.**
>
> **Why: most organisms would not normally DEPEND ON BEING CUT UP in order to reproduce.** Regeneration is a repair capacity that happens to be able to rebuild a whole animal — not a routine way of making offspring.

### Budding

**In HYDRA, repeated cell division at ONE SPECIFIC SITE produces a BUD as an outgrowth. The bud develops into a tiny individual, and when mature it DETACHES and becomes independent.**

**Yeast does the same — putting out small buds that separate and grow.**

### Vegetative propagation

**In many plants, ROOTS, STEMS and LEAVES develop into new plants under the right conditions.**
```
POTATO — the "eyes" or notches are buds; a piece with a bud sprouts,
         a piece without one does not
BRYOPHYLLUM — buds in the notches along the leaf margin fall onto the soil
              and develop into new plants
MONEY PLANT — a cutting with a node grows roots in water
methods used in agriculture: LAYERING and GRAFTING
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THREE reasons vegetative propagation is practised:**
> 1. **Plants raised this way bear FLOWERS AND FRUITS EARLIER** than plants grown from seed.
> 2. **It is the ONLY way to propagate plants that have LOST the ability to make seeds** — banana, orange, rose, jasmine.
> 3. **All the plants produced are GENETICALLY SIMILAR to the parent**, so they keep all its desirable characteristics.
>
> **The flip side, and it matters: NO VARIATION.** A whole field of genetically identical plants can be wiped out by a single disease, because what kills one kills all.

> [!NOTE]
> **TISSUE CULTURE:** tissue or cells are removed from the **growing tip** of a plant and placed in an artificial medium, where they divide rapidly into a small mass of cells called a **CALLUS**. The callus is moved to another medium with **hormones** for growth and differentiation, and the plantlets are finally planted in soil.
>
> **Its advantages: MANY plants from ONE parent, in DISEASE-FREE conditions.** Widely used for ornamental plants.

### Spore formation

**In RHIZOPUS (bread mould), the thread-like HYPHAE are NOT reproductive. The tiny "blob-on-a-stick" structures are — the blobs are SPORANGIA, containing SPORES that develop into new individuals.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Spores are covered by THICK WALLS that protect them until they meet a moist surface and can begin to grow.**
>
> **Why that is such an advantage: the spore can SURVIVE UNFAVOURABLE CONDITIONS — drought, heat, lack of food — and simply wait.** It also lets the organism **spread widely**, since spores are light and easily carried by air.
>
> **In effect the thick wall lets the organism travel through TIME as well as through space.**

## 7.3 Sexual reproduction — why bother with two parents?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The problem: DNA copying is accurate enough that variation accumulates only SLOWLY.**
>
> **And it cannot simply be made sloppier** — most badly copied DNA would fail to work with the cellular apparatus and the cell would die. **So making the copying less accurate would kill more than it varied.**
>
> **The solution: COMBINE variations from TWO different individuals.**
>
> **Why this works so well:**
> - **Each individual already carries variations accumulated over previous generations**, and two individuals will have **quite different patterns** of them.
> - **All of those variations are present in LIVING individuals — so they are guaranteed not to be lethal.**
> - **Combining them creates NOVEL COMBINATIONS**, each one new because it involves two different parents.
>
> **So sexual reproduction generates variation FAST without generating death.** That is its whole point.

### The chromosome-number problem, and meiosis

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A difficulty appears at once: if each generation combines DNA from two individuals, each generation would have TWICE the DNA of the last** — which would soon wreck the control of the cell by its DNA.
>
> **The solution: special lineages of cells in specialised organs contain only HALF the number of chromosomes and half the DNA of ordinary body cells. This halving is achieved by a type of cell division called MEIOSIS.**
>
> **When two such germ-cells combine, the full number is RE-ESTABLISHED** — and the count stays constant generation after generation.

### Why gametes are of two kinds

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **In very simple organisms the two germ-cells are barely different, or even identical. As body designs become complex, the germ-cells SPECIALISE:**
> ```
> FEMALE gamete — LARGE, contains the FOOD STORES
> MALE gamete   — SMALLER, and MOTILE
> ```
> **Why the division of labour: the zygote must grow into an organism with highly specialised tissues and organs, which needs SUFFICIENT STORES OF ENERGY.**
>
> **But a cell full of food is heavy and cannot travel.** So one gamete carries the supplies and stays put, while the other travels light and moves. **This single split explains almost every difference between male and female reproductive organs.**

## 7.4 Sexual reproduction in flowering plants

```
FLOWER PARTS
   SEPALS and PETALS — not reproductive; petals attract pollinators
   STAMEN — the MALE part; produces yellowish POLLEN GRAINS
   PISTIL — the FEMALE part, in the centre; three regions:
       STIGMA — the terminal part, often sticky
       STYLE  — the middle elongated part
       OVARY  — the swollen bottom part, containing OVULES,
                each ovule holding an EGG CELL
```
```
UNISEXUAL flower — has EITHER stamens OR pistil     papaya, watermelon
BISEXUAL flower  — has BOTH                          Hibiscus, mustard
```

### Pollination and fertilisation

```
POLLINATION — transfer of pollen from stamen to stigma
   SELF-pollination  — within the SAME flower
   CROSS-pollination — from ONE flower to ANOTHER
   agents: WIND, WATER or ANIMALS

FERTILISATION
   pollen lands on a suitable stigma
      → a POLLEN TUBE grows out and travels through the STYLE to the OVARY
      → the male germ-cell FUSES with the female gamete in the ovule
      → this fusion gives the ZYGOTE
```
> [!TIP]
> **Why the pollen tube is necessary: the male gamete has to reach the ovary, but pollen lands on the stigma — at the far end of the pistil.** The tube is the plant's answer to a motile sperm that cannot swim there.
>
> **And this is CHEMOTROPISM from Chapter 6 in action** — the tube grows towards the ovule guided by chemicals.

### After fertilisation

```
ZYGOTE  → divides many times → EMBRYO, inside the ovule
OVULE   → develops a TOUGH COAT → becomes the SEED
OVARY   → grows rapidly and ripens → becomes the FRUIT
petals, sepals, stamens, style and stigma → shrivel and FALL OFF

GERMINATION — the seed's embryo develops into a seedling under
              appropriate conditions
```
> [!TIP]
> **Why seed formation is such an advantage: the embryo is packaged with a FOOD STORE inside a TOUGH PROTECTIVE COAT.**
>
> **So the next generation can WAIT for good conditions and can be CARRIED far from the parent** — and it does not have to start from nothing when it germinates.

## 7.5 Reproduction in human beings

### Puberty

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why sexual maturation happens in the early teens and not earlier:**
>
> **While the body is growing to adult size, its RESOURCES ARE DIRECTED AT ACHIEVING THAT GROWTH.** Maturing the reproductive tissue is not a priority while that is going on.
>
> **So as the rate of general body growth SLOWS DOWN, the reproductive tissues begin to mature.** That period in adolescence is **PUBERTY**.

```
COMMON to both — thick hair in the armpits and genital area, and darker skin
   there; thinner hair on legs, arms and face; oily skin and pimples;
   a new awareness of one's own body and others'

IN GIRLS — breast size increases, nipples darken, MENSTRUATION begins
IN BOYS  — thick facial hair, the voice cracks, the penis occasionally
           becomes enlarged and erect
```
> [!NOTE]
> **These changes happen SLOWLY, over months and years, NOT all at once, and NOT at an exact age.** In some people they come early and quickly, in others slowly. **And they differ between people just as noses and fingers do** — there is no single correct pattern.

> [!TIP]
> **Why puberty produces visible signals at all.** For animals to mate, **their state of sexual maturity must be IDENTIFIABLE by other individuals.** Changes such as new hair-growth patterns are exactly such signals.

### Male reproductive system

```
TESTES — produce SPERMS; located OUTSIDE the abdominal cavity, in the SCROTUM
   also secrete TESTOSTERONE, which regulates sperm formation and causes
   the changes of puberty in boys

VAS DEFERENS — carries the sperms; unites with a tube from the urinary bladder

URETHRA — the COMMON passage for both sperms and urine

PROSTATE and SEMINAL VESICLES — add secretions along the way, so the sperms
   travel in a FLUID that makes transport easier and provides NUTRITION

SPERM — mainly genetic material, plus a LONG TAIL for swimming
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why the testes hang outside the body in the scrotum: SPERM FORMATION REQUIRES A LOWER TEMPERATURE than normal body temperature.**
>
> **A body cavity would simply be too warm.** This is a striking case of anatomy being dictated by a chemical requirement.

### Female reproductive system

```
OVARIES — make the EGGS, and also produce some HORMONES
   at BIRTH a girl's ovaries already contain THOUSANDS of immature eggs
   at PUBERTY some begin to mature — ONE egg per month, from one ovary

OVIDUCT (FALLOPIAN TUBE) — a thin tube carrying the egg from ovary to womb
UTERUS — the elastic bag-like structure where the two oviducts unite
CERVIX — the opening of the uterus into the VAGINA
```

### Fertilisation, implantation and the placenta

```
sperms enter through the vagina during sexual intercourse
   → they travel upwards and may meet the egg in the OVIDUCT
   → the fertilised egg (ZYGOTE) divides into a ball of cells, the EMBRYO
   → the embryo is IMPLANTED in the lining of the uterus
   → it grows and develops organs, becoming a FOETUS
   → development takes about NINE MONTHS
   → birth results from rhythmic CONTRACTIONS of the uterine muscles
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE PLACENTA — a disc embedded in the uterine wall.**
> ```
> on the EMBRYO's side  — VILLI
> on the MOTHER's side  — BLOOD SPACES surrounding those villi
> ```
> **Why villi again: they provide a LARGE SURFACE AREA** for glucose and oxygen to pass from mother to embryo — the same design solution as in the small intestine and the lungs.
>
> **It works in both directions: the embryo's WASTE substances are removed into the mother's blood** through the same structure.

### Menstruation

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Every month the uterus PREPARES to receive a fertilised egg — its lining becomes THICK and SPONGY, richly supplied with blood, ready to nourish an embryo.**
>
> **If the egg is NOT fertilised, it lives for about one day, and the preparation is no longer needed.** The lining slowly breaks down and comes out through the vagina as **blood and mucus**.
>
> **This is MENSTRUATION — roughly monthly, usually lasting about two to eight days.**
>
> **The logic is worth seeing clearly: the bleeding is not a failure or an illness. It is the DISMANTLING of a preparation that turned out not to be needed.**

## 7.6 Reproductive health

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Sexual maturation is GRADUAL and happens while general body growth is still going on. So some degree of sexual maturity does NOT mean the body or mind is ready for sexual acts, or for having and bringing up children.**
>
> **Pressures come from many directions** — from friends to join in activities whether one wants to or not, from families to marry and have children, from government agencies to avoid having them. **Making choices in that situation is genuinely difficult.**

### Sexually transmitted diseases

```
BACTERIAL — gonorrhoea, syphilis
VIRAL     — warts, HIV-AIDS
```
**Because the sexual act is a very intimate connection of bodies, many diseases can be transmitted through it. Using a CONDOM helps prevent transmission of many of these infections to some extent.**

### Contraception

| Category | How it works | Concern |
|---|---|---|
| **Barrier** — condoms and similar coverings | a **mechanical barrier** so sperm does not reach the egg | — |
| **Oral pills** | change the **hormonal balance** so eggs are not released | can cause **side effects**, since hormones are altered |
| **Loop or copper-T** | placed in the **uterus** | side effects from **irritation of the uterus** |
| **Surgical** — blocking the **vas deferens** in males or the **fallopian tube** in females | the gametes cannot meet | safe long-term, but **surgery itself can cause infection** if not properly done |

> [!WARNING]
> **Surgery can also be used to remove unwanted pregnancies — and this is MISUSED for ILLEGAL SEX-SELECTIVE ABORTION of female foetuses.**
>
> **PRENATAL SEX DETERMINATION IS PROHIBITED BY LAW.**
>
> **Why it matters: for a healthy society the female–male sex ratio must be MAINTAINED.** Because of reckless female foeticide, the child sex ratio has been **declining at an alarming rate** in some sections of society.

> [!NOTE]
> **On population: birth and death rates determine a population's size, and an expanding population makes it harder to improve everybody's standard of living.**
>
> **But the textbook raises a genuine counterpoint worth thinking about: if INEQUALITY in society is the main reason for poor living standards, then the SIZE of the population is relatively unimportant.**

## Summary

```
WHY COPIES ARE NOT EXACT
   DNA is the blueprint; reproduction = making a DNA COPY
   the copy needs its own cellular apparatus, so THE CELL DIVIDES
   no biochemical reaction is perfectly reliable → VARIATION every time
   drastic variations kill the new cell; milder ones survive
   variation is the basis of EVOLUTION
   consistency keeps a population suited to its NICHE; variation lets a few
   survive when the niche CHANGES — good for the SPECIES, not necessarily
   for the individual

ASEXUAL MODES — one parent
   BINARY FISSION   Amoeba (any plane), Leishmania (definite orientation)
   MULTIPLE FISSION Plasmodium
   BUDDING          Hydra, yeast
   FRAGMENTATION    Spirogyra — only works for simple body organisation
   REGENERATION     Hydra, Planaria — NOT reproduction; no organism
                    normally depends on being cut up
   VEGETATIVE PROPAGATION  potato eyes, Bryophyllum leaf buds, money plant,
                    layering, grafting — earlier flowering, works for
                    seedless plants, keeps the parent's characters,
                    but produces NO variation
   SPORE FORMATION  Rhizopus — thick walls let spores survive bad conditions
                    and spread widely

SEXUAL REPRODUCTION — combines DNA from TWO individuals, so variation is
   generated FAST without the deaths that sloppier copying would cause
   MEIOSIS halves the chromosome number, so it stays constant each generation
   gametes specialise: female LARGE with food stores, male SMALL and MOTILE

FLOWERING PLANTS
   stamen (male, pollen) · pistil = stigma + style + ovary (ovules, egg cells)
   unisexual (papaya) vs bisexual (Hibiscus)
   pollination self or cross, by wind, water or animals
   pollen tube grows through the style → fertilisation → ZYGOTE
   zygote → embryo · ovule → SEED · ovary → FRUIT

HUMANS
   PUBERTY comes when general growth slows, freeing resources for
      reproductive tissue; its visible changes are SIGNALS of maturity
   MALE   testes in the scrotum — sperm needs a LOWER temperature;
          vas deferens · urethra (shared with urine) · prostate and
          seminal vesicles supply fluid and nutrition
   FEMALE ovaries hold thousands of immature eggs from birth; one egg
          a month; oviduct → uterus → cervix → vagina
   fertilisation in the OVIDUCT; embryo IMPLANTS in the uterine lining
   PLACENTA — villi + maternal blood spaces = large surface area for
      glucose and oxygen in, wastes out; about nine months
   MENSTRUATION — the monthly dismantling of a uterine lining that was
      prepared for an embryo that did not arrive

REPRODUCTIVE HEALTH
   STDs — gonorrhoea, syphilis, warts, HIV-AIDS; condoms reduce transmission
   contraception — barrier · hormonal pills · loop or copper-T · surgical
   prenatal sex determination is PROHIBITED BY LAW; the sex ratio must be
   maintained for a healthy society
```
