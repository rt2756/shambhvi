Part A — Foundations you must already own Classes 6–9

Types of numbers

  • Natural (N): 1, 2, 3 …   Whole (W): 0, 1, 2 …
  • Integers (Z): … −2, −1, 0, 1, 2 …
  • Rational (Q): can be written [[p||q]], q ≠ 0. Decimal form terminates or repeats (0.75, 0.333…).
  • Irrational: decimal never ends, never repeats (√2, √3, π).
  • Real (R): rational + irrational — every point on the number line.
  • Prime: exactly 2 factors (2, 3, 5, 7, 11 …). 2 is the only even prime. 1 is neither prime nor composite.
  • Co-prime: HCF = 1 (e.g. 8 and 15).

Lines and angles

  • Complementary angles add to 90°; supplementary add to 180°.
  • Linear pair = 180°. Vertically opposite angles are equal.
  • Parallel lines cut by a transversal: corresponding angles equal · alternate interior angles equal · co-interior angles add to 180°.
  • Polygon with n sides: sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°. Regular polygon: each exterior angle = [[360°||n]].

Triangles — basic facts

  • Angle sum = 180°. Exterior angle = sum of the two interior opposite angles.
  • Sum of any two sides > third side. Bigger angle faces the longer side.
  • Isosceles: angles opposite equal sides are equal. Equilateral: each angle 60°.
  • Congruence rules: SSS · SAS · ASA · AAS · RHS. (No "SSA" or "AAA" for congruence.)
  • Pythagoras: in a right triangle, Hypotenuse² = Base² + Perpendicular². Converse: if c² = a² + b², the angle opposite c is 90°.
  • Mid-point theorem: the line joining mid-points of two sides is parallel to the third side and half of it.

Solving a linear equation in one variable

  • Whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Moving a term across "=" changes its sign; a multiplier becomes a divisor.
  • Clear fractions first by multiplying every term by the LCM of the denominators.
  • Cross-multiplication: [[a||b]] = [[c||d]] ⇒ ad = bc.

3x − 7 = 2x + 5 ⇒ 3x − 2x = 5 + 7 ⇒ x = 12

[[2x + 1||3]] = [[x − 1||2]] ⇒ 4x + 2 = 3x − 3 ⇒ x = −5

Put your answer back in the original equation. LHS must equal RHS.

Linear equations in two variables and graphs (Class 9)

  • ax + by + c = 0 has infinitely many solutions; its graph is a straight line. Every point on the line is a solution.
  • To plot: choose 3 values of x, find y, plot and join. Two points fix the line; the third is a check.
  • x-intercept: put y = 0. y-intercept: put x = 0.
  • x = a is a vertical line (parallel to the y-axis). y = b is a horizontal line (parallel to the x-axis).
  • x-axis is the line y = 0; y-axis is the line x = 0. The origin is (0, 0).
  • In (x, y): x is the abscissa (distance from the y-axis), y is the ordinate (distance from the x-axis).

Ungrouped data: mean, median, mode

The language of proofs

  • Axiom / postulate: accepted without proof. Theorem: a statement that has been proved.
  • Converse: swap "if" and "then". The converse of a true statement need not be true.
  • Proof by contradiction: assume the opposite, reach something impossible, conclude the original is true.
  • Counter-example: one example is enough to show a statement is false, but examples never prove it true.
  • Geometry proofs are written as: Given → To prove → Construction → Proof, with a reason for each step (CPCT, AA, alternate angles …).

1 · Real Numbers Unit: Number Systems · 6 marks

Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every composite number can be written as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique apart from the order of the factors. Example: 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5.

HCF = product of the smallest power of each common prime.
LCM = product of the greatest power of every prime involved.

HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b

This product rule works for two numbers only, not three.

"Largest number that divides…" → HCF. "Smallest number divisible by…" or "when will they meet / ring together again" → LCM.

Proving irrationality (√2, √3, √5)

Key theorem: if a prime p divides a², then p divides a.

Proof pattern (contradiction):

  • Assume √2 = [[a||b]], with a, b co-prime, b ≠ 0.
  • Square: a² = 2b² ⇒ 2 divides a² ⇒ 2 divides a. Let a = 2c.
  • Then 4c² = 2b² ⇒ b² = 2c² ⇒ 2 divides b.
  • So a and b share the factor 2 — contradicts co-prime. Hence √2 is irrational.

For 3 + 2√5 type: assume it is rational, isolate √5 = rational → contradiction.

Rational ± irrational = irrational. Non-zero rational × irrational = irrational.

Standard question types

  • Can 6n end in 0? A number ending in 0 needs prime factors 2 and 5. 6n = 2n × 3n has no 5 → never.
  • Show 7 × 11 × 13 + 13 is composite: take 13 common → 13 × (78), more than two factors.

EXTRA Euclid's lemma: a = bq + r, 0 ≤ r < b.  Decimals: [[p||q]] (lowest terms) terminates only if q = 2m × 5n.

Word problems

  • Meeting again: Sonia takes 18 min and Ravi 12 min for one round of a field, starting together. They meet at the start after LCM(18, 12) = 36 minutes.
  • Bells: bells ring every 9, 12 and 15 minutes. LCM = 180 minutes, so they ring together every 3 hours.
  • Remainder type: largest number that divides 245 and 1029 leaving remainder 5 in each case = HCF(245 − 5, 1029 − 5) = HCF(240, 1024) = 16.
  • Largest equal groups / longest tape / biggest tile: HCF.

5 − √3 is irrational

  1. Assume 5 − √3 is rational. Then 5 − √3 = [[a||b]], where a, b are co-prime integers and b ≠ 0.
  2. Rearranging: √3 = 5 − [[a||b]] = [[5b − a||b]].
  3. Since a and b are integers, [[5b − a||b]] is rational, so √3 is rational.
  4. But √3 is irrational. This is a contradiction.
  5. So the assumption is wrong, and 5 − √3 is irrational.

The same five lines work for 3 + 2√5, 7√5, [[1||√2]] and similar numbers. If asked, first prove √3 is irrational using the √2 pattern.

2 · Polynomials Unit: Algebra · 20 marks for chapters 2–5

Basics

  • Degree = highest power of x. Linear (1), quadratic (2), cubic (3).
  • Zero of p(x): a value k with p(k) = 0. On a graph, zeros are the x-coordinates where y = p(x) meets the x-axis.
  • A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeros.
  • Graph of ax² + bx + c is a parabola: opens up if a > 0, down if a < 0. It cuts the x-axis at 2, 1 or 0 points.

Zeros and coefficients — quadratic ax² + bx + c

α + β = −[[b||a]]      αβ = [[c||a]]

Build a polynomial from its zeros:

p(x) = k [ x² − (α + β)x + αβ ]

Useful rearrangements:

α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ
(α − β)² = (α + β)² − 4αβ
[[1||α]] + [[1||β]] = [[α + β||αβ]]
α³ + β³ = (α + β)³ − 3αβ(α + β)

EXTRA Cubic ax³ + bx² + cx + d:  α+β+γ = −[[b||a]],  αβ+βγ+γα = [[c||a]],  αβγ = −[[d||a]].

More vocabulary

  • p(x) = anxn + … + a₁x + a₀ with whole-number powers. [[1||x]], √x and x−2 are not polynomial terms.
  • Monomial (1 term), binomial (2), trinomial (3).
  • Constant polynomial (e.g. 7) has degree 0. The degree of the zero polynomial is not defined.
  • Value of p(x) at x = k is p(k). A linear polynomial ax + b has exactly one zero, −[[b||a]].

Reading a graph: number of zeros = number of points where the graph meets the x-axis. A graph that only touches the axis counts once (equal zeros).

Find the zeros and verify the relationship

Form a polynomial; use α and β

Sum √2, product [[1||3]]: x² − √2 x + [[1||3]]. Multiply by 3 → 3x² − 3√2 x + 1.

If α, β are zeros of x² − 5x + 6: α + β = 5, αβ = 6.

Quick facts for 1-mark questions

  • Zeros are equal in size, opposite in sign (α = −β) ⇒ sum = 0 ⇒ b = 0.
  • Zeros are reciprocals (αβ = 1) ⇒ c = a.
  • One zero is 0 ⇒ c = 0.
  • Polynomial with zeros [[1||α]], [[1||β]] is cx² + bx + a (coefficients reversed).
  • If k is a zero, substitute x = k and set p(k) = 0 to find an unknown coefficient.
  • Infinitely many polynomials share the same zeros: multiply by any non-zero k.

Solving methods

  • Substitution: from one equation write y in terms of x, put it in the other.
  • Elimination: multiply to make the coefficients of one variable equal, then add or subtract.

Setting up word problems

  • Two-digit number: tens digit x, units digit y → number 10x + y; reversed 10y + x.

4 · Quadratic Equations

Standard form and solving

ax² + bx + c = 0,   a ≠ 0

1. Factorisation (splitting the middle term): find two numbers whose sum is b and product is ac. Example: 2x² − 5x + 3 → sum −5, product 6 → −2 and −3 → (2x − 3)(x − 1) = 0 → x = [[3||2]], 1.

2. Quadratic formula:

x = [[−b ± √(b² − 4ac)||2a]]

Discriminant and nature of roots

D = b² − 4ac

DRoots
D > 0two distinct real roots
D = 0two equal real roots, each −[[b||2a]]
D < 0no real roots

"Real roots" means D ≥ 0. "Equal roots — find k" means set D = 0.

Sum of roots = −[[b||a]], product = [[c||a]].

Word problems

  • Consecutive integers: x, x + 1. Consecutive odd or even: x, x + 2.
  • Speed problems: [[d||x]] − [[d||x + k]] = time difference → take LCM → quadratic.
  • Rectangle: length × breadth = area with one side written in terms of the other.
  • Right triangle: use Pythagoras to form the equation.

Reject roots that make no sense (negative length, speed, age). Write "x ≠ −5 since speed cannot be negative".

Is it quadratic? Simplify first

  • (x + 1)² = 2(x − 3) ⇒ x² + 7 = 0. Quadratic.
  • x(x + 1) + 8 = (x + 2)(x − 2) ⇒ x + 12 = 0. Not quadratic — x² cancels.
  • x + [[1||x]] = 3 ⇒ x² − 3x + 1 = 0 (multiply by x). Quadratic.

Root given, find k: substitute the root. If x = 2 is a root of x² + kx − 10 = 0, then 4 + 2k − 10 = 0 ⇒ k = 3.

Roots given, form the equation: x² − (sum of roots)x + (product of roots) = 0.

Formula and discriminant

Solve 2x² − 7x + 3 = 0. a = 2, b = −7, c = 3.

D = 49 − 24 = 25    x = [[7 ± 5||4]] = 3 or [[1||2]]

Equal roots: kx(x − 2) + 6 = 0 ⇒ kx² − 2kx + 6 = 0.

D = 4k² − 24k = 0 ⇒ 4k(k − 6) = 0 ⇒ k = 6

k = 0 is rejected because the equation would stop being quadratic.

2x² + kx + 3 = 0, equal roots: k² = 24 ⇒ k = ±2√6.

  1. Make the coefficient of x² equal to 1.
  2. Move the constant to the right.
  3. Add (half the coefficient of x)² to both sides.
  4. Write the left side as a perfect square and take the square root.

x² + 6x − 7 = 0 ⇒ x² + 6x + 9 = 16
(x + 3)² = 16 ⇒ x + 3 = ±4 ⇒ x = 1 or −7

The quadratic formula is this method applied to ax² + bx + c = 0.

5 · Arithmetic Progressions

Core formulas

AP: a, a + d, a + 2d, …  where a = first term, d = common difference = a₂ − a₁.

nth term:  an = a + (n − 1)d

Sum:  Sn = [[n||2]] [2a + (n − 1)d] = [[n||2]] (a + l)

l = last term. Use the second form whenever the last term is known.

an = Sn − Sn−1     nth term from the end = l − (n − 1)d

Shortcuts

  • a, b, c are in AP ⇔ 2b = a + c.
  • Choose 3 terms as a − d, a, a + d; 4 terms as a − 3d, a − d, a + d, a + 3d (sum problems become easy).
  • Number of terms: n = [[l − a||d]] + 1.
  • Sum of first n natural numbers = [[n(n + 1)||2]].
  • Sum of first n odd numbers = n². Sum of first n even numbers = n(n + 1).
  • If an is of the form pn + q, the sequence is an AP with d = p.

n must be a positive integer. If you get n = 12.5, the term is not in the AP.

nth term problems

10th term of 2, 7, 12 … a = 2, d = 5 ⇒ a₁₀ = 2 + 9 × 5 = 47.

Which term of 21, 18, 15 … is −81?

21 + (n − 1)(−3) = −81 ⇒ n − 1 = 34 ⇒ n = 35

Given a₃ = 5 and a₇ = 9: subtract → 4d = 4 ⇒ d = 1, a = 3. AP: 3, 4, 5, 6 …

How many multiples of 4 lie between 10 and 250? 12, 16 … 248.

n = [[248 − 12||4]] + 1 = 60

Three-digit numbers divisible by 7: 105 … 994 ⇒ n = 128.

Sum problems

34 + 32 + 30 + … + 10: n = [[10 − 34||−2]] + 1 = 13.

S = [[13||2]] (34 + 10) = 286

How many terms of 24, 21, 18 … give a sum of 78?

[[n||2]] [48 − 3(n − 1)] = 78 ⇒ n² − 17n + 52 = 0
⇒ n = 4 or 13

Both are valid: terms 5 to 13 add up to zero.

Given Sn = 4n − n²: a₁ = S₁ = 3; S₂ = 4 so a₂ = 1; d = −2; an = 5 − 2n.

Real-life AP

200 logs are stacked with 20 in the bottom row, 19 in the next, 18 in the next … How many rows?

[[n||2]] [40 − (n − 1)] = 200 ⇒ n² − 41n + 400 = 0
⇒ n = 16 or 25

n = 25 would need a negative number of logs in the top row, so n = 16 rows and the top row has 20 − 15 = 5 logs.

Other AP situations: fixed yearly salary increment, equal monthly increase in savings, taxi fare per km after a fixed charge, rungs of a ladder. Compound interest is not an AP.

Properties for tricky questions

  • Adding, subtracting, multiplying or dividing every term by the same non-zero number still gives an AP.
  • Sum of terms equidistant from the two ends = a + l.
  • If Sn = An² + Bn, it is an AP with d = 2A.
  • If am = n and an = m, then d = −1 and am+n = 0.
  • If m × am = n × an, then am+n = 0.
  • Ratio of sums → ratio of terms: replace n by (2m − 1) in the ratio of Sn to get the ratio of the mth terms.
  • an − ak = (n − k)d — the fastest way to find d from two terms.

Similarity

Two triangles are similar (~) if corresponding angles are equal and corresponding sides are in the same ratio. Congruent = same shape and size; similar = same shape only.

ΔABC ~ ΔPQR ⇒ [[AB||PQ]] = [[BC||QR]] = [[AC||PR]]

Criteria:

  • AA (AAA): two pairs of angles equal.
  • SSS: all three pairs of sides proportional.
  • SAS: one angle equal and the sides including it proportional.

The order of letters matters. Write the correspondence correctly (A↔P, B↔Q, C↔R) before writing ratios.

Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales)

If DE || BC in ΔABC, then

[[AD||DB]] = [[AE||EC]]

Also [[AD||AB]] = [[AE||AC]] = [[DE||BC]].

Converse: if a line divides two sides in the same ratio, it is parallel to the third side.

The proof of BPT (using areas of triangles on the same base) is a frequently asked 5-mark question.

Results that follow from similarity

  • Ratio of corresponding sides = ratio of perimeters = ratio of corresponding altitudes = ratio of corresponding medians.
  • EXTRA Ratio of areas = (ratio of corresponding sides)².
  • In a right triangle with altitude to the hypotenuse, all three triangles formed are similar.
  • Shadow problems: object and shadow form similar triangles → [[height₁||shadow₁]] = [[height₂||shadow₂]].

Basic Proportionality Theorem

Given: ΔABC with DE || BC, D on AB, E on AC. To prove: [[AD||DB]] = [[AE||EC]].

Construction: join BE and CD. Draw EN ⟂ AB and DM ⟂ AC.

  1. ar(ADE) = ½ × AD × EN and ar(BDE) = ½ × DB × EN, so [[ar(ADE)||ar(BDE)]] = [[AD||DB]].
  2. ar(ADE) = ½ × AE × DM and ar(DEC) = ½ × EC × DM, so [[ar(ADE)||ar(DEC)]] = [[AE||EC]].
  3. ΔBDE and ΔDEC are on the same base DE and between the same parallels DE and BC, so ar(BDE) = ar(DEC).
  4. From 1, 2 and 3: [[AD||DB]] = [[AE||EC]].

Using BPT and similarity

BPT: DE || BC, AD = 1.5 cm, DB = 3 cm, AE = 1 cm. Then [[1.5||3]] = [[1||EC]] ⇒ EC = 2 cm.

Converse: if [[AD||DB]] = [[AE||EC]], state "by the converse of BPT, DE || BC".

[[h||28]] = [[6||4]] ⇒ h = 42 m

[[x||4.8 + x]] = [[0.9||3.6]] ⇒ 4x = 4.8 + x ⇒ x = 1.6 m

How to spot similar triangles

  • A common angle shared by both triangles.
  • Vertically opposite angles where two lines cross.
  • Parallel lines → alternate or corresponding angles equal.
  • Two right angles, plus one more equal angle.

Two equal angles are enough (AA). Then write the similarity in the correct order and pick the ratio you need.

Trapezium ABCD (AB || DC), diagonals meet at O: ΔAOB ~ ΔCOD by AA, so [[AO||OC]] = [[BO||OD]].

EXTRA Angle bisector theorem: if AD bisects ∠A, then [[BD||DC]] = [[AB||AC]].

Pythagoras theorem by similarity

Given: ΔABC right-angled at B. To prove: AC² = AB² + BC². Construction: BD ⟂ AC.

  1. ΔADB ~ ΔABC (∠A common, right angles) ⇒ [[AD||AB]] = [[AB||AC]] ⇒ AB² = AD × AC.
  2. ΔBDC ~ ΔABC (∠C common, right angles) ⇒ BC² = CD × AC.
  3. Add: AB² + BC² = AC(AD + CD) = AC².

The proof is outside the current syllabus EXTRA, but the theorem is used everywhere. Related results: in an equilateral triangle of side a and height h, 4h² = 3a². In a rhombus, 4 × side² = d₁² + d₂².

Tangent basics

  • Tangent: touches the circle at exactly one point. Secant: cuts it at two points.
  • Tangents from a point: inside the circle 0 · on the circle 1 · outside the circle 2.

Theorem 1: the tangent at any point is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.

Theorem 2: the lengths of the two tangents drawn from an external point are equal (PA = PB).

Both proofs are asked in boards. Theorem 2 uses RHS congruence of ΔOAP and ΔOBP.

Results used in problems

PA = PB = √(OP² − r²)

∠APB + ∠AOB = 180°

  • OP bisects ∠APB and ∠AOB; OP is the perpendicular bisector of chord AB.
  • Quadrilateral ABCD circumscribing a circle: AB + CD = AD + BC.
  • Tangents at the two ends of a diameter are parallel.
  • A parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus.
  • In-radius of a right triangle with legs a, b and hypotenuse c: r = [[a + b − c||2]].

Tangent is perpendicular to the radius

Given: circle with centre O, tangent XY at P. To prove: OP ⟂ XY.

  1. Take any point Q on XY other than P and join OQ.
  2. Q must lie outside the circle; otherwise XY would cut the circle at two points and be a secant.
  3. So OQ > radius, i.e. OQ > OP, for every such Q.
  4. OP is therefore the shortest distance from O to the line XY.
  5. The shortest distance from a point to a line is the perpendicular. Hence OP ⟂ XY.

Tangents from an external point are equal

Given: tangents PA and PB from external point P to a circle with centre O. To prove: PA = PB.

Construction: join OA, OB, OP.

  1. ∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90° (tangent ⟂ radius).
  2. OA = OB (radii).
  3. OP = OP (common hypotenuse).
  4. ΔOAP ≅ ΔOBP (RHS).
  5. PA = PB (CPCT). Also ∠APO = ∠BPO, so OP bisects ∠APB.

  • If the tangent lengths from the three vertices are x, y, z, the sides are x + y, y + z, z + x and s = x + y + z.

  • There is exactly one tangent at any point on a circle.

  • Solid dropped in water: volume of water displaced (or rise × base area) = volume of the solid.
  • Keep all lengths in the same unit before calculating. Diameter given? Halve it first.

More formulas and facts

  • Hollow cylinder (pipe): volume = π(R² − r²)h; curved surface (inner + outer) = 2π(R + r)h.
  • Cone : hemisphere : cylinder with the same radius and height = 1 : 2 : 3 in volume.
  • A cone has one-third the volume of a cylinder with the same base and height.
  • Scaling: if every length is multiplied by k, surface area × k², volume × k³. Doubling the radius of a sphere gives 4 times the area and 8 times the volume.
  • Largest sphere in a cube of edge a: diameter = a. Largest cone in a cube: r = [[a||2]], h = a.
  • Cube inside a sphere: a√3 = 2r.

Other standard situations

  • Well and embankment: earth dug out πr²h = volume of the ring π(R² − r²)H spread around it.
  • Wall and bricks: number of bricks = [[volume of wall||volume of one brick]] (subtract mortar if given).
  • Rain on a roof: roof area × rainfall depth = volume collected in the tank.
  • Ice-cream cone: cone + hemisphere; number of cones = [[volume of container||volume of one filled cone]].
  • Vessel = hemisphere + cylinder: inner surface = 2πr² + 2πrh.
  • Wooden article with scoops: the scooped surface is still exposed — add the curved area of each scoop, subtract the flat circles removed.
  • Cost questions: cost = rate × area (painting, canvas) or rate × volume (milk, digging).

Mean of grouped data

Class mark xi = [[upper limit + lower limit||2]]; fi = frequency.

Direct:  x̄ = [[Σ fixi||Σ fi]]

Assumed mean:  x̄ = a + [[Σ fidi||Σ fi]],   di = xi − a

Step deviation:  x̄ = a + ( [[Σ fiui||Σ fi]] ) × h,   ui = [[xi − a||h]]

a = assumed mean (pick a middle class mark), h = class size. All three methods give the same answer.

Mode of grouped data

Mode = l + ( [[f₁ − f₀||2f₁ − f₀ − f₂]] ) × h

Modal class = class with the highest frequency. l = its lower limit, f₁ = its frequency, f₀ = frequency of the class before, f₂ = frequency of the class after, h = class size.

Median of grouped data

Median = l + ( [[n/2 − cf||f]] ) × h

  • n = Σf. Make a cumulative frequency (cf) column.
  • Median class = first class whose cumulative frequency is ≥ [[n||2]].
  • l = lower limit of the median class, f = its frequency, cf = cumulative frequency of the class before it.

Empirical relation: 3 Median = Mode + 2 Mean

If classes are 10–19, 20–29 … (gaps), first make them continuous: 9.5–19.5, 19.5–29.5 … Needed for median and mode.

14 · Probability

Rules

P(E) = [[number of favourable outcomes||total number of equally likely outcomes]]

  • 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1. Impossible event: 0. Sure event: 1.
  • P(E) + P(not E) = 1.
  • Sum of probabilities of all elementary events = 1.
  • "At least one" = 1 − P(none).

A probability can never be negative or greater than 1. Always reduce the fraction.

Sample spaces to remember

  • Coins: 1 coin → 2 outcomes; 2 coins → 4 (HH, HT, TH, TT); 3 coins → 8.
  • One die: 6 outcomes. Primes: 2, 3, 5.
  • Two dice: 36 outcomes. Doublets: 6.

  • Cards (52): 4 suits × 13. Red 26 (hearts, diamonds), black 26 (spades, clubs). Face cards 12 (J, Q, K × 4). Aces 4 — an ace is not a face card.
  • 53 Sundays: leap year [[2||7]], ordinary year [[1||7]].
  • Numbers 1 to n: count carefully (multiples of 3 up to 50 → 16).

Vocabulary

  • Experiment / trial: an action with an uncertain result. Outcome: one possible result. Sample space: the set of all outcomes.
  • Event: a collection of outcomes. Elementary event: exactly one outcome.
  • Equally likely: each outcome has the same chance. "Fair", "unbiased", "at random" and "well-shuffled" all signal this.
  • Complementary event (not E): everything in the sample space outside E.
  • "A or B": count outcomes in either, each only once. "A and B": count outcomes in both.
  • Theoretical probability (this chapter) is calculated; experimental probability (Class 9) comes from actual trials.

Dice and coins

Two dice (36 outcomes):

  • P(sum 8) = [[5||36]]  ·  P(doublet) = [[6||36]] = [[1||6]]
  • P(sum ≥ 10) = [[3 + 2 + 1||36]] = [[1||6]]
  • P(product 6): (1,6), (6,1), (2,3), (3,2) ⇒ [[4||36]] = [[1||9]]
  • P(sum is prime): sums 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 ⇒ [[1 + 2 + 4 + 6 + 2||36]] = [[5||12]]

Coins: two coins, at least one head = [[3||4]]. Three coins: exactly two heads = [[3||8]]; at most two heads = 1 − P(three heads) = [[7||8]].

Cards and numbers

One card from 52:

  • P(red king) = [[2||52]] = [[1||26]]  ·  P(face card) = [[12||52]] = [[3||13]]
  • P(spade or ace) = [[13 + 3||52]] = [[4||13]] (the ace of spades counted once)
  • P(neither king nor queen) = [[52 − 8||52]] = [[11||13]]
  • If cards are removed first, reduce the total: all kings removed ⇒ 48 cards.

Card numbered 1 to 20: primes are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19 ⇒ P(prime) = [[8||20]] = [[2||5]].

Counting hints: two-digit numbers: 90 (10 to 99). Perfect squares from 1 to 100: 10. Numbers from a to b inclusive: b − a + 1.

Finding an unknown

Bag with 5 red and x blue balls; P(blue) = 2 × P(red):

[[x||5 + x]] = 2 × [[5||5 + x]] ⇒ x = 10

A box has 12 balls, x of them black. After adding 6 black balls the probability of black doubles:

[[x + 6||18]] = 2 × [[x||12]] ⇒ 12x + 72 = 36x ⇒ x = 3

Leap year with 53 Sundays: 366 days = 52 weeks + 2 extra days. The 7 possible pairs are (Sun, Mon), (Mon, Tue) … (Sat, Sun); 2 contain a Sunday ⇒ [[2||7]].

Area type: P(point lands in a region) = [[area of the region||total area]].

Defective items: P(good) = 1 − P(defective). Once an item is removed and not replaced, both the count and the total drop by 1.
