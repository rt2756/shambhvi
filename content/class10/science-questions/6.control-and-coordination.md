Why do living organisms need systems of control and coordination?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because movement in response to the environment must be CONTROLLED — each kind of change calls for a DIFFERENT and appropriate response.**
>
> You **whisper** to a friend in class rather than shouting. You **pull your hand back** from a flame but **reach out** for a warm cup. **The right movement depends entirely on which event triggered it.**
>
> **So the organism must do two things: RECOGNISE the event, and then produce ONLY the correct response.** That requires specialised tissues devoted to control and coordination.

---

Trace the path of a nervous impulse through a neuron and across a synapse.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> DENDRITIC TIP — information detected; a chemical reaction creates an
>                 ELECTRICAL IMPULSE
>       ↓
> CELL BODY     — the impulse travels through
>       ↓
> AXON          — the impulse travels along it to the end
>       ↓
> SYNAPSE       — the impulse triggers the RELEASE OF CHEMICALS, which cross
>                 the gap and start a NEW impulse in the next neuron's dendrite
> ```
> **A similar synapse delivers the impulse from a neuron to a MUSCLE CELL or a GLAND.**

---

What happens at the synapse between two neurons, and why does the signal change from electrical to chemical there?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The electrical impulse arriving at the end of the axon causes CHEMICALS TO BE RELEASED. These cross the gap and start a similar electrical impulse in the dendrite of the next neuron.**
>
> **Why the change of form is necessary: a SYNAPSE IS A GAP.** An electrical impulse cannot simply leap across empty space, so the message must be carried over by something that can **diffuse**.
>
> **A second consequence worth noticing: this makes transmission ONE-WAY.** Chemicals are released on one side and detected on the other, so an impulse can never travel backwards through a synapse.

---

How do we detect the smell of an incense stick?

> [!ANSWER]
> **OLFACTORY RECEPTORS in the nose detect it.**
>
> ```
> the receptors (specialised tips of nerve cells) detect the chemical
>    → an electrical impulse is generated
>    → it travels along the neuron to the FORE-BRAIN
>    → the fore-brain has a separate area specialised for SMELL
>    → association areas interpret it — we recognise the smell
> ```
> **Note that receptors merely DETECT; they do not identify.** Recognising *what* the smell is happens in the brain, by comparing the incoming signal with information already stored there.

---

Why does food taste bland when your nose is blocked or you have a cold?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because much of what we call "taste" is really SMELL.**
>
> **The GUSTATORY receptors on the tongue detect only a few basic tastes.** The rich flavour of food comes largely from **OLFACTORY receptors** picking up the vapours rising from it.
>
> **Block the nose and half the information never arrives** — so the brain has far less to work with and the food seems flat.
>
> **You can test it directly: eat some sugar, then pinch your nose shut and eat some more.** The difference is immediate.

---

What is a reflex action, and why did reflexes evolve?

> [!ANSWER]
> **A REFLEX ACTION is a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus that does not involve thinking.**
>
> **Why they exist: THINKING IS TOO SLOW.**
>
> **Thinking involves a complicated interaction of many impulses among many neurons in the brain.** If touching a flame had to be thought about — signal up to the brain, interpreted, decision made, instruction sent back — **you would already be burnt.**
>
> **And many animals have little or no thinking network at all**, so reflex arcs evolved as an efficient way to function **without** thought. **Even after complex brains appeared, reflexes remained better for quick responses.**

---

Describe the reflex arc and explain why the connection is made in the spinal cord rather than the brain.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> receptor (skin) → SENSORY neuron → SPINAL CORD → MOTOR neuron → muscle
> ```
> **Why the spinal cord: it is the point where the input and output nerves FIRST MEET.**
>
> **Nerves from all over the body gather in a bundle in the spinal cord on their way to the brain.** Making the shortcut there is **the earliest possible place**, and therefore the fastest.
>
> **The brain is not cut out of the loop — the information still travels ON to it.** That is why you *feel* the pain a moment **after** your hand has already moved. **The action happens first; the awareness catches up.**

---

What is the difference between a reflex action and walking?

> [!ANSWER]
> - **A REFLEX ACTION is automatic and involves NO thinking.** The connection is made in the **spinal cord**, and it happens whether you want it to or not.
> - **WALKING is a VOLUNTARY action, decided by the FORE-BRAIN.** You choose to walk, choose the direction, and can stop at will.
>
> **The reason for the difference is TIME.** A reflex answers a situation where a delay would be dangerous, so the pathway is made as short as possible. **Walking has no such urgency, so it can afford to go through the thinking part of the brain.**

Name the three major parts of the brain and give the function of each.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> FORE-BRAIN — the MAIN THINKING part
>    separate areas for hearing, smell, sight
>    ASSOCIATION areas interpret the sensory information by combining it
>       with other inputs and with what is already stored
>    MOTOR areas control the voluntary muscles
>    also contains the HUNGER centre — the feeling of being full
>
> MID-BRAIN  — involuntary actions
>
> HIND-BRAIN — CEREBELLUM: precision of voluntary actions, posture, BALANCE
>              MEDULLA:    blood pressure, salivation, vomiting
> ```

---

Which part of the brain maintains posture and balance? What would happen without it?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The CEREBELLUM, in the hind-brain.**
>
> **It is responsible for the PRECISION of voluntary actions and for maintaining POSTURE and BALANCE.**
>
> **What it does becomes obvious when you imagine it failing.** Walking in a straight line, riding a bicycle, picking up a pencil — **every one needs constant fine adjustment that you never consciously make.**
>
> **Without it you could still DECIDE to move, but you could not move ACCURATELY.** The intention would survive; the control would not.

---

Distinguish between reflex, involuntary and voluntary actions with examples.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> REFLEX      — no thinking at all; controlled by the SPINAL CORD
>               pupil narrowing in bright light, hand off a flame, mouth watering
>
> INVOLUNTARY — cannot be controlled by thinking; MID- and HIND-BRAIN
>               heartbeat, breathing, digestion, blood pressure
>
> VOLUNTARY   — decided by thinking; FORE-BRAIN
>               writing, talking, moving a chair, clapping
> ```
> **The involuntary group is the one usually forgotten — and it sits BETWEEN the other two.**
>
> **These are not thought about, but they are not sudden reflexes either.** You cannot stop your heart by deciding to, and you do not have to remember to digest your food.

How are the brain and spinal cord protected?

> [!ANSWER]
> - **The BRAIN sits inside a BONY BOX — the skull — and within it, in a FLUID-FILLED balloon that gives further SHOCK ABSORPTION.**
> - **The SPINAL CORD is protected by the VERTEBRAL COLUMN**, the hard bumpy structure you can feel down the middle of your back.
>
> **Why fluid AS WELL as bone: bone prevents a puncture, but a rigid box on its own would let the brain SLAM into its walls in a knock.**
>
> **The fluid cushions that impact** — the same reason a fragile object is packed in padding **inside** a rigid carton, rather than just in a strong box.

---

How does nervous tissue actually cause a muscle to move?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Muscle cells contain SPECIAL PROTEINS that change both their SHAPE and their ARRANGEMENT in response to a nervous electrical impulse.**
>
> **The new arrangement gives the muscle cell a SHORTER form — the cell contracts, and the muscle pulls.**
>
> **This completes the whole chain with a physical mechanism at every step:**
> ```
> environment → receptor → electrical impulse → chemical at the synapse
>             → muscle proteins rearrange → the cell shortens → MOVEMENT
> ```
> **Nothing in the sequence is left as magic** — each stage has a cause you can name.

---

How does the sensitive plant move its leaves, given that it has no nerves or muscles?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Two problems must still be solved, exactly as in an animal — but with completely different equipment.**
>
> **1. The information must TRAVEL.** The leaves move at a point **different from where they were touched**, so a signal has to get there. **Plants use electrical-chemical means to pass it from cell to cell — but they have NO SPECIALISED TISSUE for conducting information.**
>
> **2. Cells must CHANGE SHAPE.** Instead of muscle proteins, **plant cells change shape by changing the AMOUNT OF WATER in them** — swelling or shrinking.
>
> **NO GROWTH is involved**, which is why the movement is so fast.

---

How does the movement of the sensitive plant's leaves differ from the movement of a shoot towards light?

> [!ANSWER]
> | | Sensitive plant | Shoot bending to light |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Growth involved?** | **NO** | **YES** |
> | **Speed** | very **fast** | **slow** |
> | **Direction** | not related to the direction of touch | **towards the stimulus** — directional |
> | **Reversible?** | yes, the leaves reopen | no — the growth is permanent |
>
> **The essential difference: one is a movement OF the plant, the other is a movement caused BY GROWING.**
>
> **A test that separates them: stop the plant from growing.** The sensitive plant would still fold its leaves; the shoot would no longer bend.

---

Define tropism and name four kinds, giving the direction of response.

> [!ANSWER]
> **A TROPISM is a DIRECTIONAL growth movement in response to a stimulus — either towards it or away from it.**
> ```
> PHOTOTROPISM — response to LIGHT
>    shoots bend TOWARDS it   (positive)
>    roots bend AWAY from it  (negative)
>
> GEOTROPISM   — response to GRAVITY
>    roots grow DOWNWARDS     (positive)
>    shoots grow UPWARDS      (negative)
>
> HYDROTROPISM — response to WATER; roots grow towards it
>
> CHEMOTROPISM — response to CHEMICALS
>    e.g. the POLLEN TUBE growing towards the ovule
> ```
> **Why the directions make sense: shoots need LIGHT; roots need WATER and ANCHORAGE.** Each part grows towards what it needs, and that is the whole logic of the list.

---

Design an experiment to demonstrate hydrotropism.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 1. Take a wide trough and fill it with moist soil.
> 2. Plant a germinating seed on one side.
> 3. Bury a small porous clay pot filled with WATER on the OTHER side,
>    some distance from the seed.
> 4. Keep the soil in between only just damp — not wet.
> 5. After a few days, dig up the seedling carefully and look at its roots.
> ```
> **Result: the roots will have grown TOWARDS the pot of water**, not straight down.
>
> **What makes this a fair test: gravity acts equally in both directions sideways**, so any sideways bending can only be a response to water. **The one factor that differs across the trough is moisture** — which is what lets you name it as the cause.

---

Describe an experiment showing that shoots are positively phototropic and roots negatively phototropic.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 1. Fill a conical flask with water and cover the neck with a wire mesh.
> 2. Place two or three freshly germinated bean seeds on the mesh.
> 3. Put the flask inside a cardboard box open on ONE side, with the open
>    side facing a window.
> 4. After two or three days: the SHOOTS bend TOWARDS the light and the
>    ROOTS bend AWAY from it.
> 5. Now TURN THE FLASK so the shoots face away from the light. Leave it
>    a few days more.
> ```
> **The crucial observation on turning it: the OLD parts of the shoot and root do NOT change direction — but the NEW growth bends the other way.**
>
> **Why that matters: it proves the response happens in the GROWING REGION.** Growth already completed is fixed and cannot be undone; only what is still growing can respond.

---

How do auxins make a tendril curl around a support?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The part of the tendril TOUCHING the support grows MORE SLOWLY than the part away from it.**
>
> **Auxin moves away from the side in contact**, so the free side has more of it and its cells elongate faster.
>
> **One side growing faster than the other makes the tendril CURVE — and the curve wraps it around the object**, so the plant clings.
>
> **The general principle worth carrying forward: a plant "turns" only by growing UNEVENLY.** It has no muscles to bend with.

---

Explain how auxin makes a shoot bend towards light.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 1. AUXIN is made at the SHOOT TIP and helps cells GROW LONGER.
> 2. When light comes from one side, auxin DIFFUSES TOWARDS THE SHADY SIDE.
> 3. The SHADY side therefore has MORE auxin, and its cells grow LONGER.
> 4. One side longer than the other BENDS the shoot — towards the light.
> ```
> **Notice what is really going on: the plant does not "turn towards" the light in any active sense.**
>
> **It grows more on the dark side, and bending is the automatic geometric consequence.** A rod that gets longer on one side has no choice but to curve.

---

What are plant hormones? Name four and state what each does.

> [!ANSWER]
> **PLANT HORMONES are chemicals that coordinate growth, development and responses to the environment. They are made at places AWAY from where they act, and simply DIFFUSE to the area of action.**
>
> | Hormone | Effect |
> |---|---|
> | **Auxin** | **cell elongation** at the shoot tip — behind phototropism |
> | **Gibberellin** | helps in **growth of the stem** |
> | **Cytokinin** | promotes **cell division** — abundant in fruits and seeds |
> | **Abscisic acid** | **INHIBITS growth**; causes wilting of leaves |
>
> **Why cytokinin is concentrated in fruits and seeds: those are the regions of most rapid CELL DIVISION** — the hormone is found where its job is.

---

Why do plants need a growth-inhibiting hormone as well as growth-promoting ones?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because growth cannot simply run on forever.**
>
> **A plant must also STOP: shed its leaves, close its stomata in drought, and go dormant through a bad season.** Endless growth in an unfavourable season would be fatal.
>
> **ABSCISIC ACID is the brake to the other hormones' accelerator.** Its effects include the **wilting of leaves**.
>
> **The general point: control means being able to STOP as well as to START.** A system with only an accelerator is not controlled at all.

---

Why do animals need chemical (hormonal) coordination when they already have a fast nervous system?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because electrical impulses have TWO LIMITATIONS:**
>
> **1. They reach only the cells CONNECTED BY NERVOUS TISSUE** — not every cell in the body.
>
> **2. After firing, a cell needs TIME TO RESET** before it can generate another impulse — so it cannot signal continuously.
>
> **A chemical released by a cell DIFFUSES all around it, and any cell with the right receptor molecules can detect it.**
>
> **The trade-off: chemical signalling is SLOWER, but it reaches EVERY cell regardless of nerve connections, and can be sustained STEADILY and PERSISTENTLY.**
>
> **The two systems are complementary, not rivals** — one for speed, one for reach and duration.

---

How does the body respond when adrenaline is secreted, and why does each change help?

> [!ANSWER]
> **ADRENALINE is secreted by the ADRENAL GLANDS, directly into the BLOOD.**
> ```
> the HEART beats faster              → more oxygen delivered to the muscles
> small arteries in the DIGESTIVE
>   SYSTEM and SKIN contract          → blood is DIVERTED to skeletal muscle
> the diaphragm and rib muscles
>   contract more                     → BREATHING RATE increases
> ```
> **Every effect serves one goal: get oxygen and blood to the muscles that will fight or run.**
>
> **And notice what gets shut DOWN — digestion and the skin.** In an emergency the body **borrows** blood from jobs that can wait.
>
> **This is why you feel your heart pound and your mouth go dry when frightened** — you are feeling the diversion happen.

---

Why is the use of iodised salt advisable?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because IODINE IS ESSENTIAL for the THYROID gland to make THYROXIN.**
>
> **Thyroxin regulates carbohydrate, protein and fat metabolism**, striking the balance needed for proper growth.
>
> **If iodine is deficient in the diet, too little thyroxin can be made and one may suffer from GOITRE — whose symptom is a SWOLLEN NECK**, swollen exactly where the thyroid gland sits.
>
> **The chain is worth holding in mind: a trace mineral in the food → a hormone → the regulation of metabolism.** A tiny amount missing from the diet shows up as a disease of growth.

---

What causes dwarfism, and which gland is responsible?

> [!ANSWER]
> **A DEFICIENCY OF GROWTH HORMONE IN CHILDHOOD**, secreted by the **PITUITARY GLAND**.
>
> **Growth hormone regulates the growth and development of the body**, stimulating growth in all organs.
>
> **An EXCESS of the same hormone causes gigantism** — abnormally tall stature.
>
> **Why childhood is the critical period: that is when the body is actually growing.** The same deficiency arising later cannot produce dwarfism, because the growing has already been done.

---

Why are some diabetic patients treated with insulin injections?

> [!ANSWER]
> **INSULIN, produced by the PANCREAS, regulates the BLOOD SUGAR level.**
>
> **If it is not secreted in proper amounts, the sugar level in the blood RISES, causing many harmful effects.**
>
> **Injections supply the insulin the body is failing to make**, so that blood sugar can be brought back under control.
>
> **Why it must be injected rather than swallowed: insulin is a PROTEIN, and the digestive system would break it down into amino acids** before it could ever reach the blood.

---

What changes occur at puberty and which hormones cause them?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The dramatic changes in appearance around 10–12 years of age are caused by:**
> ```
> TESTOSTERONE — from the TESTES — in males
> OESTROGEN    — from the OVARIES — in females
> ```
> **OESTROGEN also controls the development of the female sex organs and regulates the MENSTRUAL CYCLE.**
>
> **Why hormones rather than nerves do this job: the changes affect the WHOLE BODY at once** — voice, hair, body shape, sex organs. **Only a chemical carried in the blood can reach every one of those tissues.**

---

Explain the feedback mechanism using insulin as an example.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> blood sugar RISES
>    → detected by the cells of the PANCREAS
>    → they produce MORE insulin
>    → blood sugar FALLS
>    → insulin secretion is REDUCED
> ```
> **Why this design is so elegant: the RESULT of the hormone's action controls how much MORE of it is made.**
>
> **Nothing has to decide in advance, and no external supervisor is needed** — the system corrects itself automatically, and settles at the right level.
>
> **Why it is necessary at all: hormones must be secreted in PRECISE quantities.** Too much insulin would be as dangerous as too little.

---

What role does the hypothalamus play in hormone release?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The HYPOTHALAMUS releases RELEASING HORMONES, which stimulate the PITUITARY GLAND to release its own hormones.**
>
> **An example: when the level of GROWTH HORMONE is low, the hypothalamus releases GROWTH-HORMONE RELEASING FACTOR**, which prompts the pituitary to release more.
>
> **This is feedback again, one level up.** The hypothalamus monitors the pituitary, just as the pancreas monitors blood sugar — **a chain of controllers, each one watching the result of the one below.**
