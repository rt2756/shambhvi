List the physical properties of metals, and give five exceptions that break the pattern.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Metals are LUSTROUS, MALLEABLE (beaten into sheets), DUCTILE (drawn into wires), SONOROUS (ring when struck), GOOD CONDUCTORS of heat and electricity, and generally hard solids.**
>
> **The exceptions:**
> - **MERCURY is a metal but a LIQUID** at room temperature
> - **BROMINE is a non-metal and also a liquid**
> - **IODINE is a non-metal but LUSTROUS**
> - **GRAPHITE is a non-metal that CONDUCTS electricity**
> - **Sodium, potassium and lithium are metals soft enough to cut with a knife**; gallium and caesium melt in your palm
>
> **Why this matters: because of these exceptions, elements CANNOT be reliably classified by physical properties.** The chemical properties — losing or gaining electrons, and whether the oxide is acidic or basic — have no such exceptions.

---

What are allotropes? Illustrate with carbon.

> [!ANSWER]
> **ALLOTROPES are different forms of the SAME element**, differing in how the atoms are arranged.
>
> ```
> DIAMOND  — the hardest natural substance known; very high melting
>            and boiling points; does NOT conduct
> GRAPHITE — soft, slippery, and CONDUCTS electricity
> ```
> **Both are pure carbon and nothing else.**
>
> **Why they differ so completely: properties come from ARRANGEMENT, not just from which atoms are present.** The same carbon atoms bonded in a different pattern give a substance that behaves nothing like the other.

---

Why is sodium stored immersed in kerosene oil?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because sodium reacts so vigorously with the oxygen and moisture of the air that it CATCHES FIRE on its own if left in the open.**
>
> **Kerosene keeps air and water away from the metal surface — and sodium does not react with kerosene**, so it is a safe liquid to store it under.
>
> **Potassium is stored the same way, and for the same reason.** Both sit at the very top of the reactivity series.

---

Why does aluminium not corrode away, even though it is quite high in the reactivity series?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because a THIN LAYER OF ALUMINIUM OXIDE forms on the surface as soon as it meets air, and that layer SEALS the metal underneath.**
>
> **Oxygen cannot get past it, so no further oxidation happens.** A reactive metal is protected by its own product.
>
> **ANODISING makes that layer deliberately thicker.** The article is made the **anode** and electrolysed in dilute sulphuric acid; oxygen released at the anode builds up a thicker oxide coat, which can then be dyed for an attractive finish.

---

What is an amphoteric oxide? Give an example with equations.

> [!ANSWER]
> **An AMPHOTERIC OXIDE reacts with BOTH acids and bases to give a salt and water.**
> ```
> Al2O3 + 6HCl  → 2AlCl3  + 3H2O            behaving as a BASE
> Al2O3 + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO2 +  H2O            behaving as an ACID
>                 (sodium aluminate)
> ```
> **Aluminium oxide and ZINC OXIDE are the two to know.**
>
> **Why they can do both: they sit on the boundary between metal and non-metal behaviour**, so they can play either role depending on what they meet.

---

Arrange these by their reaction with water and write the equations: potassium, calcium, iron.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> POTASSIUM — reacts VIOLENTLY with COLD water; the hydrogen catches fire
>    2K(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2KOH(aq) + H2(g) + heat
>
> CALCIUM — reacts with cold water, LESS violently
>    Ca(s) + 2H2O(l) → Ca(OH)2(aq) + H2(g)
>
> IRON — no reaction with cold or hot water; reacts only with STEAM
>    3Fe(s) + 4H2O(g) → Fe3O4(s) + 4H2(g)
> ```
> **Order of reactivity: K > Ca > Fe.**
>
> **The pattern to see: the MORE reactive the metal, the MILDER the form of water it can attack.** Potassium manages cold water; iron needs steam; gold cannot manage even steam.

---

Why does the hydrogen catch fire when potassium reacts with water, but not when calcium does?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Both reactions release heat, but potassium's releases FAR MORE.**
>
> **With potassium the reaction is so violent and so exothermic that the heat itself IGNITES the hydrogen** as it is formed.
>
> **With calcium the reaction is gentler and the heat evolved is NOT sufficient to set the hydrogen alight** — you get bubbles, but no flame.
>
> **The difference is a direct measure of the gap between them in the reactivity series.**

---

Why do calcium and magnesium start floating during their reaction with water?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because bubbles of HYDROGEN GAS stick to the surface of the metal and carry it up.**
>
> **The metal itself has not become lighter.** The metal plus the clinging bubbles is **less dense than water**, so the whole assembly floats.
>
> **A small but useful observation: it tells you a gas is being produced** even before you test it.

---

Why does nitric acid not give hydrogen gas with metals?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because HNO3 is a strong OXIDISING AGENT.**
>
> **The hydrogen IS produced — and then immediately destroyed.** Nitric acid oxidises it to water, while the acid itself is reduced to one of the nitrogen oxides (N2O, NO or NO2).
>
> **So you never collect any hydrogen**, even though the metal did react.
>
> **The exception: magnesium and manganese with VERY DILUTE nitric acid do evolve H2** — at that dilution the oxidising power is too weak to intercept it.

---

What is aqua regia, and why is it remarkable?

> [!ANSWER]
> **AQUA REGIA — Latin for "royal water" — is a FRESHLY PREPARED mixture of concentrated hydrochloric acid and concentrated nitric acid in the ratio 3 : 1.**
>
> **What makes it remarkable: it dissolves GOLD and PLATINUM, which NEITHER acid can dissolve on its own.**
>
> **It is a highly corrosive, fuming liquid** and one of very few reagents able to attack the least reactive metals.

---

Write out the activity series, and state the two rules that make it useful.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> K    potassium     MOST reactive
> Na   sodium
> Ca   calcium
> Mg   magnesium
> Al   aluminium
> Zn   zinc
> Fe   iron
> Pb   lead
> ----------------------  [H] hydrogen
> Cu   copper
> Hg   mercury
> Ag   silver
> Au   gold          LEAST reactive
> ```
> **Rule 1: metals ABOVE hydrogen displace hydrogen from dilute acids; those below cannot.** That is why copper gives no bubbles with dilute HCl.
>
> **Rule 2: a more reactive metal displaces a less reactive one from its salt solution.**
>
> **The series is not arbitrary — every position in it was decided by watching one metal displace another, or fail to.**

---

Four metals A, B, C and D were tested. A displaces copper but not iron. B displaces iron but not zinc. C displaces only silver. D displaces nothing. Arrange them and predict what happens when B is added to copper sulphate.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Order (most reactive first): B > A > C > D.**
>
> **The reasoning:**
> - **B displaces iron** — so B is above iron. **B does not displace zinc** — so B is below zinc. B sits between zinc and iron.
> - **A displaces copper but not iron** — so A is below iron and above copper.
> - **C displaces only silver** — so C is below copper and above silver.
> - **D displaces nothing** — the least reactive of all.
>
> **B added to copper sulphate: DISPLACEMENT happens.** B is above iron, and iron is above copper, so B is certainly above copper. **The blue colour would fade and reddish-brown copper would deposit.**

---

What would you see when zinc is added to iron(II) sulphate solution? Write the equation.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> Zn(s) + FeSO4(aq) → ZnSO4(aq) + Fe(s)
> ```
> **Observations: the PALE GREEN colour of the iron sulphate solution FADES**, and a **greyish deposit of iron** appears on the zinc.
>
> **Why it happens: zinc is ABOVE iron in the activity series**, so it can push iron out of its salt.
>
> **The reverse would not work** — iron added to zinc sulphate gives no reaction at all.

---

Explain how sodium chloride is formed, in terms of electrons.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> Na (2,8,1)  →  Na⁺ (2,8) + e⁻       LOSES one electron
> Cl (2,8,7) + e⁻ →  Cl⁻ (2,8,8)      GAINS one electron
> ```
> **Why they do this: both reach the stable, completely filled arrangement of a NOBLE GAS.** Sodium ends up like neon, chlorine like argon.
>
> **Where the charge comes from: the nucleus does not change.** Sodium still has 11 protons but now only 10 electrons — net **+1**. Chlorine still has 17 protons but 18 electrons — net **−1**.
>
> **The oppositely charged ions then attract by strong ELECTROSTATIC force**, and that attraction is the **IONIC BOND**.

---

Show the formation of MgCl2 and name the ions present.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> Mg (2,8,2)  →  Mg²⁺ (2,8) + 2e⁻
> Cl (2,8,7) + e⁻ →  Cl⁻ (2,8,8)          × 2
> ```
> **The ions are the MAGNESIUM cation Mg²⁺ and two CHLORIDE anions Cl⁻.**
>
> **Why TWO chlorides are needed: magnesium must give away two electrons to empty its outer shell, and each chlorine can accept only one.** So it takes two chlorine atoms to absorb what one magnesium gives — which is exactly why the formula is MgCl2 and not MgCl.

---

Why do ionic compounds have high melting and boiling points?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because a large amount of energy is needed to overcome the strong ELECTROSTATIC force of attraction between the oppositely charged ions.**
>
> **Melting means breaking that structure apart**, and the attraction being broken is powerful.
>
> **The numbers make it concrete: NaCl melts at 1074 K and boils at 1686 K; CaO melts at 2850 K.** Those temperatures are a direct measure of the strength of the bond.

---

Why do ionic compounds conduct electricity when molten or dissolved, but not as solids?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because conduction requires MOVING CHARGES — and ions can only move once they are free.**
>
> - **In the SOLID, the ions are locked in a rigid structure.** They carry charge but cannot travel, so no current flows.
> - **When MOLTEN, heat overcomes the electrostatic attraction** and the ions move freely.
> - **When DISSOLVED in water, the water pulls the ions apart** and they move to the opposite electrodes.
>
> **It is the same idea in both cases: the charges were always there; what changes is whether they can MOVE.**

---

Define mineral, ore and gangue, and explain how a mineral differs from an ore.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **MINERAL — any element or compound occurring naturally in the earth's crust.**
> - **ORE — a mineral that contains enough of a metal to be extracted PROFITABLY.**
> - **GANGUE — the soil, sand and other impurities mined along with the ore.**
>
> **Every ore is a mineral, but not every mineral is an ore.**
>
> **The difference is ECONOMIC, not chemical.** The same compound may be an ore where it is concentrated and merely a mineral where it is too dilute to be worth mining.

---

Why are gold and silver found free in nature while sodium and potassium never are?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because of where they sit in the activity series.**
>
> - **Gold, silver and platinum are at the BOTTOM — so unreactive that they do not combine with anything**, and are found in the free (native) state.
> - **Potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium and aluminium are at the TOP — so reactive that they combine with whatever is available** and are NEVER found free.
> - **Metals in the middle (Zn, Fe, Pb) are found as oxides, sulphides or carbonates.**
>
> **Most ores turn out to be OXIDES, because oxygen is both very abundant and very reactive.**

---

Distinguish roasting from calcination, with equations, and say why both are done.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> ROASTING — heating a SULPHIDE ore strongly in EXCESS AIR
>    2ZnS(s) + 3O2(g) → 2ZnO(s) + 2SO2(g)
>
> CALCINATION — heating a CARBONATE ore strongly in LIMITED AIR
>    ZnCO3(s) → ZnO(s) + CO2(g)
> ```
> **Why both are done: they produce the same thing — the metal OXIDE — and it is far EASIER to reduce an oxide than a sulphide or a carbonate.** Both are preparation steps, not the extraction itself.
>
> **How to keep them straight: ROASTING needs oxygen** (it adds oxygen to a sulphide); **CALCINATION is just heating** (it drives CO2 out of a carbonate).

---

Why can carbon reduce zinc oxide but not aluminium oxide?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because ALUMINIUM HAS A GREATER AFFINITY FOR OXYGEN THAN CARBON DOES.**
>
> **Reduction with carbon works only if carbon can take the oxygen away.** Zinc holds its oxygen loosely enough for carbon to win:
> ```
> ZnO(s) + C(s) → Zn(s) + CO(g)
> ```
> **Aluminium holds oxygen more tightly than carbon does**, so carbon simply cannot take it — the reaction does not go.
>
> **This is why metals at the TOP of the series are extracted by ELECTROLYTIC REDUCTION instead.** Electricity can force the change where chemistry cannot.

---

Describe how sodium is extracted, naming what happens at each electrode.

> [!ANSWER]
> **By the ELECTROLYSIS OF MOLTEN SODIUM CHLORIDE.**
> ```
> at the CATHODE (negative):   Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na
> at the ANODE (positive):     2Cl⁻ → Cl2 + 2e⁻
> ```
> **The metal is deposited at the CATHODE — because the sodium ion is POSITIVE and is attracted to the negative electrode.**
>
> **Chlorine is liberated at the anode.**
>
> **Why molten and not dissolved: in water, the water itself would be reduced instead of the sodium**, since sodium is so reactive. Melting the salt removes the water from the problem entirely.

---

What is the thermit reaction and why is it useful?

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> Fe2O3(s) + 2Al(s) → 2Fe(l) + Al2O3(s) + Heat
> ```
> **Aluminium reduces iron(III) oxide, since aluminium is more reactive than iron.**
>
> **Why it is useful: the reaction is SO exothermic that the iron is produced MOLTEN — note the (l).**
>
> **Molten iron poured straight into a gap is exactly what a weld needs**, which is why the thermit reaction is used to **join railway tracks and cracked machine parts** on site. **The reaction supplies both the metal and the heat to melt it** — no furnace required.

---

Describe electrolytic refining of copper. What is anode mud?

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> ANODE       = the IMPURE copper
> CATHODE     = a thin strip of PURE copper
> ELECTROLYTE = acidified copper sulphate solution
> ```
> **On passing current, pure copper DISSOLVES from the anode into the electrolyte, and an equal amount is DEPOSITED on the cathode.**
>
> **The impurities separate by themselves:**
> - **soluble impurities go into the solution**
> - **insoluble impurities settle below the anode as ANODE MUD**
>
> **The anode mud is not waste — it often contains SILVER, GOLD and PLATINUM**, and recovering them can pay for the whole refining operation.

Describe the three-test-tube experiment on rusting and state what it proves.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> A  nail + ordinary water, corked                  →  RUSTS
> B  nail + boiled distilled water + a layer of oil →  no rust
>       boiling drives out dissolved air; the oil keeps more from dissolving
> C  nail + dry air + anhydrous calcium chloride    →  no rust
>       the CaCl2 absorbs every trace of moisture
> ```
> **It proves that rusting needs BOTH AIR (oxygen) AND WATER. Either one alone does nothing.**
>
> **Why the design is good: each tube removes exactly ONE factor.** B has water but no air; C has air but no water; A has both. **Because only one thing differs at a time, the result points at the cause and nothing else.**

---

What is galvanisation, and why does it keep protecting iron even after the coating is scratched?

> [!ANSWER]
> **GALVANISATION is coating iron or steel with a thin layer of ZINC.**
>
> **The remarkable part: the article stays protected EVEN WHERE THE ZINC IS SCRATCHED THROUGH.**
>
> **Why: ZINC IS MORE REACTIVE THAN IRON.** Wherever both metals are exposed, the oxygen attacks the **zinc** in preference to the iron. **The zinc is sacrificed to save the iron.**
>
> **Contrast with paint: a scratch in paint exposes bare iron and rust starts there immediately.** A scratch in zinc exposes iron that is still being chemically defended.

---

Define an alloy and explain why iron is never used pure.

> [!ANSWER]
> **An ALLOY is a HOMOGENEOUS MIXTURE of two or more metals, or of a metal and a non-metal.** It is made by melting the main metal, dissolving the others in it in definite proportions, and cooling.
>
> **Pure iron is VERY SOFT and stretches easily when hot** — useless for building.
>
> **Add about 0.05% carbon and it becomes STEEL: hard and strong. Add nickel and chromium and it becomes STAINLESS STEEL: hard and rust-free.**
>
> **The whole point of alloying is that you can DESIGN the properties you need** rather than accept what the pure metal happens to offer.

---

Name the constituents of brass, bronze, solder and amalgam, and explain why solder is used for wires while brass is not.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> BRASS   = copper + zinc
> BRONZE  = copper + tin
> SOLDER  = lead + tin
> AMALGAM = any alloy containing MERCURY
> ```
> **An alloy has LOWER electrical conductivity and a LOWER melting point than the pure metals in it.**
>
> **So brass and bronze are NOT used for electrical circuits, although copper is** — alloying has spoilt the conductivity that made copper useful.
>
> **And solder IS used for wires precisely because of its LOW MELTING POINT** — it can be melted to join two wires without melting the wires themselves. **The same property that disqualifies one alloy qualifies the other.**

---

Why is pure gold not used for jewellery, and what does "22 carat" mean?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Pure gold is 24 carat and TOO SOFT to hold a shape** — an ornament made of it would bend and wear away.
>
> **It is alloyed with SILVER or COPPER to harden it.**
>
> **22 carat means 22 parts pure gold to 2 parts copper or silver** — the grade commonly used for ornaments in India.
>
> **So the carat number is simply the proportion of gold out of 24.**

---

Give three ways in which non-metals behave oppositely to metals in chemical reactions.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **Non-metals GAIN electrons to form NEGATIVE ions; metals LOSE them to form positive ions.**
> - **Non-metal oxides are ACIDIC or NEUTRAL; metal oxides are BASIC.**
> - **Non-metals do NOT displace hydrogen from dilute acids; reactive metals do.** Non-metals instead react with hydrogen to form **hydrides**.
>
> **The single idea underneath all three: metals are electron GIVERS and non-metals are electron TAKERS.**
>
> **That is also why a non-metal cannot displace hydrogen** — displacement means giving electrons away to take another element's place, and a non-metal is built to do the opposite.
