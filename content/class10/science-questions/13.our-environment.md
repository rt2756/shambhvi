What is an ecosystem? Name its two kinds of components with examples.

> [!ANSWER]
> **An ECOSYSTEM is all the interacting organisms in an area TOGETHER WITH the non-living constituents of the environment.**
> ```
> BIOTIC components  — living: plants, animals, microorganisms, humans
> ABIOTIC components — physical factors: temperature, rainfall, wind,
>                      soil, minerals
> ```
> **A garden is an ecosystem:** grasses, trees, roses, jasmine, sunflowers, frogs, insects and birds — all interacting, and all affected in their growth and reproduction by the abiotic factors.
>
> **Why the non-living part must be included in the definition: an organism's life is shaped as much by rainfall and soil as by what eats it.** Leaving out the abiotic components would describe only half the system.

---

Distinguish natural from artificial ecosystems and give two examples of each.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> NATURAL     — forests, ponds, lakes
> HUMAN-MADE  — gardens, crop fields, an aquarium
> ```
> **The difference is not in the kinds of organisms present but in who MAINTAINS the balance.**
>
> **A natural ecosystem is SELF-SUSTAINING** — it regulates itself without outside help.
>
> **An artificial ecosystem depends on continual human intervention** — watering, weeding, feeding, cleaning. Stop tending a crop field and it ceases to be a crop field.

---

Name the three functional groups of organisms in an ecosystem and state what each does.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **PRODUCERS** make organic compounds like sugar and starch from **INORGANIC** substances, using the radiant energy of the Sun in the presence of chlorophyll. **All green plants and certain bacteria.**
> - **CONSUMERS** eat the food that producers made — either **directly** from producers or **indirectly** by feeding on other consumers. They may be **herbivores, carnivores, omnivores or parasites.**
> - **DECOMPOSERS** — bacteria and fungi — **break down the dead remains and waste products** of organisms.
>
> **Notice how the three are defined: by HOW THEY OBTAIN THEIR SUSTENANCE**, not by what they look like or where they sit in the classification of life.

---

What is the role of decomposers, and what would happen without them?

> [!ANSWER]
> **DECOMPOSERS break down the dead remains and waste products of organisms — and in doing so, they RETURN the nutrients locked in those bodies back to the soil, where producers can use them again.**
>
> **Without them, every nutrient on Earth would eventually end up LOCKED INSIDE CORPSES AND WASTE.**
>
> **Dead bodies would pile up, and the producers would gradually run out of the raw materials they need to make food** — so the whole system would grind to a halt from the bottom up.
>
> **They are what makes the nutrient cycle a CYCLE rather than a one-way street.**

---

Why must an aquarium be cleaned regularly, while a pond or lake need not be?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because a natural pond is a COMPLETE, SELF-SUSTAINING ecosystem and a small aquarium is not.**
>
> **A pond has a full set of DECOMPOSERS and a balance between producers and consumers, so wastes are broken down and reused as fast as they are produced.**
>
> **An aquarium has far too few decomposers for the amount of food we add and the waste the fish produce**, so material accumulates faster than it can be broken down.
>
> **So the cleaning is US DOING THE JOB that the missing part of the ecosystem would otherwise do.** It is a good way to see what decomposers actually accomplish — by seeing what happens when they are outnumbered.

---

What are trophic levels? Give a food chain and identify the level of each organism.

> [!ANSWER]
> **A TROPHIC LEVEL is a STEP in a food chain.**
> ```
> grass  →  grasshopper  →  frog  →  snake  →  eagle
>   T1          T2          T3       T4        T5
>
> T1 — PRODUCER
> T2 — PRIMARY consumer   (herbivore)
> T3 — SECONDARY consumer (carnivore)
> T4 — TERTIARY consumer
> T5 — top carnivore
> ```
> **The producers are always at the FIRST trophic level** — every food chain must begin with something that can make its own food, since nothing else can bring energy into the system.

---

What is a food web, and why is it a better description of nature than a food chain?

> [!ANSWER]
> **A FOOD WEB is a series of BRANCHING lines showing all the feeding relationships in an ecosystem.**
>
> **Why it is more truthful: EACH ORGANISM IS GENERALLY EATEN BY TWO OR MORE KINDS OF ORGANISM, which are in turn eaten by several others.**
>
> **A straight-line food chain pretends each species has exactly one predator and one prey**, which is almost never the case.
>
> **The practical consequence: a food web is far more STABLE.** If one species disappears from a straight chain, everything above it starves. **In a web, its predators have other things to eat** — so the ecosystem can absorb the loss.

---

State the 10% law and explain where the other 90% of the energy goes.

> [!ANSWER]
> **THE 10% LAW: on average, only about 10% of the food eaten at one trophic level is turned into the eater's own body and made available to the next level.**
>
> **Where the remaining 90% goes:**
> ```
> a great deal is LOST AS HEAT to the environment
> some goes into DIGESTION
> some goes into DOING WORK — moving, hunting, breathing
> only the rest goes towards GROWTH AND REPRODUCTION
> ```
> **And only that last part — the food converted into body substance — can ever be passed on.**
>
> **A related figure worth knowing: green plants capture only about 1% of the sunlight falling on their leaves** — so even the entry point of energy into the living world is a small fraction of what arrives.

---

Why do food chains generally have only three or four steps?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because the LOSS OF ENERGY AT EACH STEP IS SO GREAT that after four trophic levels very little usable energy remains.**
> ```
> 10,000 units at the producers
>  1,000 units at the primary consumers
>    100 units at the secondary consumers
>     10 units at the tertiary consumers
>      1 unit  at a fifth level — not enough to support a population
> ```
> **A fifth-level predator would have to cover an enormous area to find enough food to stay alive** — so such populations cannot be sustained.
>
> **This is not a rule that biologists invented; it is arithmetic** — and it explains a fact true of every ecosystem on Earth.

---

State the two key features of energy flow through an ecosystem.

> [!ANSWER]
> **1. IT IS UNIDIRECTIONAL.**
>
> **The energy captured by autotrophs NEVER returns to the Sun, and the energy passed to herbivores NEVER returns to the plants.** As it moves up the trophic levels, it is **no longer available to the level below.**
>
> **2. IT DIMINISHES PROGRESSIVELY** at every trophic level, because of the loss at each step.
>
> **The contrast worth holding onto: ENERGY FLOWS THROUGH an ecosystem and leaves; NUTRIENTS CYCLE round and round inside it.**
>
> **That is exactly why sunlight must keep arriving every day, but new atoms need not.**

---

Why are there more individuals at the lower trophic levels of an ecosystem?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because EACH LEVEL CAN ONLY SUPPORT WHAT THE 10% CAN FEED.**
>
> **A field of grass supports many grasshoppers; those grasshoppers support fewer frogs; those frogs support fewer snakes still.**
>
> **With only a tenth of the energy passing up at each step, each level must be far smaller than the one below it.**
>
> **So the PYRAMID SHAPE of a food chain — greatest numbers at the producers, fewest at the top — is a DIRECT CONSEQUENCE of the 10% law**, not a separate fact.

---

What is biological magnification, and how do the chemicals get into the food chain in the first place?

> [!ANSWER]
> **BIOLOGICAL MAGNIFICATION is the progressive ACCUMULATION of harmful chemicals at each higher trophic level.**
>
> **How they get in:** pesticides and other chemicals used to protect crops are **washed down into the soil or into water bodies.**
> - **from the SOIL they are absorbed by plants along with water and minerals**
> - **from WATER they are taken up by aquatic plants and animals**
>
> **Why they build up rather than passing through: THESE CHEMICALS ARE NOT DEGRADABLE.** Nothing breaks them down and the body cannot excrete them, so they simply **accumulate** in the tissue.

---

Why does the concentration of such a chemical rise at each higher trophic level?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because an animal eats MANY organisms from the level below, and inherits the pesticide from ALL of them — but only about 10% of that food's mass becomes its own body.**
>
> ```
> the POISON is all retained (it cannot be broken down or excreted)
> the BIOMASS shrinks to a tenth at each step
> → so the CONCENTRATION multiplies
> ```
> **A frog that eats a hundred grasshoppers carries the pesticide of a hundred grasshoppers in a body far smaller than a hundred grasshoppers.**
>
> **The magnification is not caused by the chemical doing anything — it is caused by the ARITHMETIC of the food chain.**

---

Why do human beings accumulate the highest concentrations of pesticides, and why does washing not remove them?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because HUMAN BEINGS OCCUPY THE TOP LEVEL of almost every food chain** — so the maximum concentration of these chemicals accumulates in our bodies.
>
> **This is why our food grains such as wheat and rice, our vegetables and fruits, and even meat contain varying amounts of pesticide residue.**
>
> **Why washing fails: the chemicals are INSIDE THE TISSUE, not sitting on the surface.**
>
> **They were absorbed by the plant along with water and minerals, through the roots** — so they are distributed through the whole plant. **No amount of surface cleaning can reach them.**

---

Ozone is a poison, yet it is essential. Explain.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> O2 — ordinary oxygen — is ESSENTIAL for all aerobic life
> O3 — ozone, THREE oxygen atoms — is a DEADLY POISON
> ```
> **Yet at the HIGHER LEVELS of the atmosphere, ozone performs an essential function: IT SHIELDS THE EARTH'S SURFACE FROM ULTRAVIOLET (UV) RADIATION.**
>
> **UV radiation is highly damaging to organisms — it is known to cause SKIN CANCER in human beings.**
>
> **The resolution of the paradox: WHERE it is matters as much as WHAT it is.**
>
> **The same molecule is a pollutant at ground level, where we breathe it, and a protector 30 km up, where nothing breathes it and it can absorb UV before that radiation ever reaches us.**

---

How is ozone formed in the upper atmosphere?

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
>              UV
>       O2  ------→  O + O       high-energy UV splits molecular oxygen
>                                into free oxygen ATOMS
>
>       O + O2  →  O3            those atoms combine with O2 to form OZONE
> ```
> **Notice the elegance of this: THE UV RADIATION ITSELF MAKES THE SHIELD THAT BLOCKS IT.**
>
> **Ozone is continuously being created and destroyed high in the atmosphere, and the layer persists because the two processes BALANCE.**
>
> **Which is exactly why the layer is vulnerable: anything that speeds up the destruction, without changing the creation, shifts that balance** — and the layer thins.

---

What caused ozone depletion, and what was done about it?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The amount of ozone began to drop SHARPLY IN THE 1980s.**
>
> **The cause: synthetic chemicals called CHLOROFLUOROCARBONS (CFCs)**, used as **refrigerants and in fire extinguishers.**
>
> **The response:**
> ```
> 1987 — the UNITED NATIONS ENVIRONMENT PROGRAMME (UNEP) succeeded in
>        forging an agreement to FREEZE CFC PRODUCTION AT 1986 LEVELS
>
> it is now MANDATORY for manufacturing companies worldwide to make
>        CFC-FREE REFRIGERATORS
> ```
> **This is the encouraging part of the story, and worth knowing as more than a list of dates: a GLOBAL environmental problem was identified, agreed upon by nations, and acted on** — which shows such action is possible.

---

Why are some substances biodegradable and others not?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because ENZYMES ARE SPECIFIC IN THEIR ACTION — a particular substance needs a particular enzyme to break it down.**
>
> **This is why the same enzymes that digest your food cannot digest coal.** You would get no energy at all from eating it, however much you swallowed.
>
> **And it is why many human-made materials, such as PLASTICS, are not broken down by bacteria or other saprophytes: NO ORGANISM HAS AN ENZYME THAT FITS THEM.**
>
> **The deeper reason: these materials are TOO NEW.** Plastics have existed for a century; no enzyme has had time to evolve that can attack them.
>
> **Such materials CAN be broken down by PHYSICAL processes — heat and pressure — but under the ordinary ambient conditions of our environment they PERSIST FOR A VERY LONG TIME.**

---

Give two ways in which biodegradable substances affect the environment.

> [!ANSWER]
> **The good: they are broken down and their NUTRIENTS RETURN TO THE SOIL**, where producers can use them again. This is why kitchen waste makes good compost.
>
> **The bad, when the quantity is large:**
> - **FOUL SMELLS, and the BREEDING of disease-carrying organisms** such as flies and mosquitoes around rotting heaps
> - **decomposing waste in water USES UP THE DISSOLVED OXYGEN**, suffocating fish and other aquatic life
>
> **The point worth taking away: "biodegradable" does not mean "harmless".** It means the harm is temporary and the material eventually rejoins the nutrient cycle — provided it is not dumped faster than it can break down.

---

Give two ways in which non-biodegradable substances affect the environment.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **They PERSIST in the environment for a very long time, ACCUMULATING year after year.** Nothing removes them, so every year's waste adds to all the previous years'.
> - **They may be INERT and simply clutter land and water** — blocking drains, littering tourist sites, making soil infertile — **or they may actively HARM members of the ecosystem**, as when animals choke on plastic or are entangled in it.
>
> **And the most insidious effect: NON-DEGRADABLE PESTICIDES UNDERGO BIOLOGICAL MAGNIFICATION**, concentrating up the food chain until they reach us in our food.
>
> **That last one connects two halves of this chapter** — the property that makes a substance non-biodegradable is exactly the property that lets it magnify.

---

Why should biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste be separated at source?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because THEY NEED COMPLETELY DIFFERENT TREATMENT.**
> ```
> BIODEGRADABLE waste     → can be COMPOSTED and returned to the soil
> NON-BIODEGRADABLE waste → must be RECYCLED or safely contained
> ```
> **Mixed together, NEITHER can be dealt with properly:**
> - **the compost is CONTAMINATED with plastic fragments**, and is then unfit to put on a field
> - **the plastic is too SOILED to be worth recycling**, so it goes to landfill instead
>
> **And it must be done AT SOURCE, in the home, because separating a mixed heap afterwards is far harder, dirtier and more expensive** — often impossible.

---

Trace what happens to the energy in 10,000 units of grass as it passes up a food chain, and state what this shows.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 10,000 units in the GRASS                    (producers)
>  1,000 units in the GRASSHOPPERS             (primary consumers)
>    100 units in the FROGS                    (secondary consumers)
>     10 units in the SNAKES                   (tertiary consumers)
>      1 unit  would remain for an EAGLE       (fifth level)
> ```
> **What it shows:**
> - **the energy DIMINISHES drastically at each level** — 90% is lost every time
> - **so food chains cannot be long** — a fifth level has almost nothing to live on
> - **so there must be far MORE individuals at the bottom than at the top**
>
> **Three separate facts about ecosystems, all falling out of one chain of arithmetic.** That is what makes the 10% law worth understanding rather than memorising.

---

Energy flows but nutrients cycle. Explain the difference and why it matters.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> ENERGY    enters as SUNLIGHT, passes UP the trophic levels losing 90% at
>           each step, and LEAVES as heat — it never returns
>
> NUTRIENTS pass from soil → producers → consumers → decomposers → SOIL AGAIN
>           the same atoms go round and round indefinitely
> ```
> **Why it matters practically: THE SUN MUST KEEP SHINING, BUT NEW ATOMS NEED NOT ARRIVE.**
>
> **The Earth is essentially a CLOSED system for matter and an OPEN system for energy.** Almost no new matter arrives, and almost none leaves — but energy pours in from the Sun continuously and radiates away as heat.
>
> **This is also why decomposers are irreplaceable and sunlight is not.** Break the nutrient cycle and the atoms are lost to life; the energy supply, by contrast, renews itself every morning.
