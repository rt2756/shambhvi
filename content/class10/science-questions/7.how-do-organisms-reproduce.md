Why is DNA copying an essential part of reproduction?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because DNA is the BLUEPRINT of the body design.**
> ```
> organisms look similar → their BODY DESIGNS are similar
> body designs are similar → their BLUEPRINTS must be similar
> the blueprint is DNA, which is the information source for making PROTEINS
> change the information → different proteins → an ALTERED body design
> ```
> **So making an offspring that resembles the parent means, at bottom, making a COPY OF THE DNA.** Everything else in reproduction is arranged around that one requirement.

---

Why does a cell divide during reproduction, instead of simply pushing out the extra DNA copy?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because a copy of DNA on its own could not live.**
>
> **The ejected copy would have NO ORGANISED CELLULAR STRUCTURE for maintaining life processes** — no membrane, no cytoplasm, no machinery. It would be information with nothing to run on.
>
> **So DNA copying is accompanied by the creation of ADDITIONAL CELLULAR APPARATUS**, and only then do the copies separate, each with its own equipment.
>
> **The result of all this is simply that THE CELL DIVIDES INTO TWO.**

---

Where does variation come from during reproduction?

> [!ANSWER]
> **From errors in DNA copying — because NO BIOCHEMICAL REACTION IS ABSOLUTELY RELIABLE.**
>
> **Every time DNA is copied, some variation creeps in**, so the copies are similar to the original but **not identical**.
> ```
> DRASTIC variations — the new DNA cannot work with the cellular apparatus
>                      it inherits → the newborn cell simply DIES
> MILDER variations  — the cell survives, subtly different from its parent
> ```
> **So surviving cells are similar to, but subtly different from, one another — and this inbuilt tendency for variation is THE BASIS OF EVOLUTION.**

---

Why is variation beneficial to a species but not necessarily to the individual?

> [!ANSWER]
> **A NICHE is the well-defined place a population occupies in an ecosystem.**
>
> **Niches can change for reasons no organism controls** — temperatures rise or fall, water levels change, a meteorite strikes.
> ```
> a population perfectly suited to a niche, with NO variation
>    → if the niche changes drastically, the WHOLE population can be wiped out
>
> a population where a FEW individuals carry variations
>    → some of them may survive and grow further
> ```
> **Example: bacteria living in temperate water. If global warming raises the temperature, most die — but the few HEAT-RESISTANT variants survive.**
>
> **Why the individual gains nothing: a heat-resistant bacterium has no advantage at all while the water stays cool.** The variation only pays off **if and when conditions change** — so the benefit belongs to the species over time, not to the individual carrying it.

---

How does binary fission differ from multiple fission? Give an example of each.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **BINARY FISSION — the parent cell splits into TWO daughter cells.** Bacteria, **Amoeba**, Leishmania.
> - **MULTIPLE FISSION — the parent cell divides into MANY daughter cells simultaneously.** **Plasmodium**, the malarial parasite.
>
> **Why multiple fission is useful to a parasite: it produces a large number of individuals at once**, greatly increasing the chance that some will reach a new host.

---

Amoeba can split in any plane but Leishmania cannot. Why?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because of the difference in BODY ORGANISATION.**
>
> **Amoeba has NO fixed body organisation** — no front, no back, no permanent structures — so **no plane of division is special**. It can split any way.
>
> **Leishmania (which causes kala-azar) has MORE organisation, including a WHIP-LIKE STRUCTURE at one end.** Its binary fission therefore occurs in a **definite orientation** relative to that structure.
>
> **The general lesson: more organisation means LESS freedom.** A body with defined parts must divide in a way that preserves them.

---

Why can Spirogyra reproduce by fragmentation while a complex animal cannot?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because a complex multicellular body is NOT a random collection of cells.**
>
> **Specialised cells are organised into TISSUES, tissues into ORGANS, and the organs must sit at DEFINITE POSITIONS in the body.**
>
> **In such a carefully organised body, breaking into pieces would be useless** — a random fragment would not contain a working set of organs in the right arrangement.
>
> **Spirogyra is a simple filament with no such organisation**, so any piece is as complete as any other. **It simply breaks up on maturing, and each fragment grows into a new individual.**

---

What is regeneration, and why is it not the same as reproduction?

> [!ANSWER]
> **REGENERATION is the ability of a fully differentiated organism to grow a new individual from a body part.** **Hydra** and **Planaria** can be cut into many pieces, and each piece grows into a complete organism.
>
> **How it works:**
> ```
> specialised cells PROLIFERATE into a large mass of cells
>    → different cells change to become various cell types and tissues
>    → in an organised sequence called DEVELOPMENT
> ```
> **Why it is NOT reproduction: most organisms would not normally DEPEND ON BEING CUT UP in order to make offspring.**
>
> **It is a repair capacity that happens to be able to rebuild a whole animal — not a routine method of producing a new generation.**

---

Why can complex organisms not reproduce by regeneration?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because their bodies depend on a precise arrangement of specialised tissues and organs.**
>
> **Regeneration requires cells that can still PROLIFERATE and DIFFERENTIATE into every cell type.** In a highly specialised body, **most cells have lost that ability** — a nerve cell cannot turn back into a general-purpose cell and start building a liver.
>
> **And a fragment of a complex body would not contain a working set of organs** in the right positions to keep itself alive while it rebuilt the rest.
>
> **Simple animals like Hydra and Planaria keep large numbers of unspecialised cells**, which is precisely why they can do it.

---

Describe budding in Hydra.

> [!ANSWER]
> **REPEATED CELL DIVISION AT ONE SPECIFIC SITE produces a BUD as an outgrowth of the parent's body.**
>
> **The bud develops into a tiny individual, and when fully mature it DETACHES from the parent and becomes an independent organism.**
>
> **Hydra uses its REGENERATIVE cells for this** — the same unspecialised cells that let it regrow after being cut.
>
> **Yeast buds in a similar way**, putting out small buds that separate and grow further.

---

Give three reasons why vegetative propagation is used for growing certain plants.

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. **Plants raised this way bear FLOWERS AND FRUITS EARLIER** than plants grown from seed.
> 2. **It is the ONLY way to propagate plants that have LOST the capacity to produce seeds** — banana, orange, rose, jasmine.
> 3. **All the plants produced are GENETICALLY SIMILAR to the parent**, so every desirable characteristic is preserved exactly.
>
> **The flip side worth stating: NO VARIATION is produced.**
>
> **A whole orchard of genetically identical plants can be destroyed by a single disease**, because whatever kills one will kill all of them.

---

Explain the potato experiment and what it demonstrates.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 1. Observe a potato — NOTCHES ("eyes") can be seen on its surface.
> 2. Cut it into pieces; some containing a notch, some not.
> 3. Place all the pieces on wet cotton in a tray, keeping it moist.
> 4. Observe over the next few days.
> ```
> **Result: only the pieces WITH a notch give rise to fresh green shoots and roots.**
>
> **What it shows: the notches are BUDS**, and vegetative propagation requires a bud.
>
> **Why the piece without a notch fails: it has plenty of stored food but no growing point.** Food alone cannot start a plant — there must be a region of cells still able to divide and differentiate.

---

What is tissue culture, and what are its advantages?

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 1. Tissue or cells are removed from the GROWING TIP of a plant.
> 2. They are placed in an ARTIFICIAL MEDIUM, where they divide rapidly
>    into a small mass of cells called a CALLUS.
> 3. The callus is transferred to another medium containing HORMONES for
>    growth and differentiation.
> 4. The plantlets are placed in soil to grow into mature plants.
> ```
> **Advantages: MANY plants can be grown from ONE parent, in DISEASE-FREE conditions.** It is commonly used for ornamental plants.
>
> **Why the growing tip is used: those cells are still actively dividing and have not committed to becoming any one tissue** — so they can still be directed into whatever is needed.

---

How does Rhizopus reproduce, and how does an organism benefit from reproducing through spores?

> [!ANSWER]
> **In bread mould (Rhizopus), the thread-like HYPHAE are NOT reproductive. The tiny "blob-on-a-stick" structures are — the blobs are SPORANGIA, containing SPORES that develop into new individuals.**
>
> **The benefit lies in the spore's THICK WALL, which protects it until it meets a moist surface and can begin to grow:**
> - **it can SURVIVE UNFAVOURABLE CONDITIONS** — drought, heat, lack of food — and simply wait
> - **it can SPREAD WIDELY**, since spores are light and easily carried by air
> - **enormous NUMBERS are produced**, so at least some will land somewhere suitable
>
> **In effect the thick wall lets the organism travel through TIME as well as through space.**

---

Why did sexual reproduction evolve, when asexual reproduction is simpler?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because asexual reproduction generates variation only SLOWLY.**
>
> **DNA copying is accurate enough that variations accumulate at a crawl. And the copying cannot simply be made sloppier — most badly copied DNA would fail to work with the cellular apparatus and the cell would die.** Sloppier copying would kill more than it varied.
>
> **The solution: COMBINE variations from TWO different individuals.**
> - **Each individual already carries variations accumulated over previous generations**, and two individuals have **quite different patterns** of them.
> - **All those variations exist in LIVING individuals — so they are guaranteed not to be lethal.**
> - **Combining them creates NOVEL COMBINATIONS**, each new because it involves two different parents.
>
> **So sexual reproduction generates variation FAST without generating death.** That is precisely its advantage.

---

If each generation combines DNA from two parents, why does the DNA content not double every generation?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because of MEIOSIS.**
>
> **Special lineages of cells in specialised organs contain only HALF the number of chromosomes and half the amount of DNA of ordinary body cells.** This halving is achieved by the type of cell division called **MEIOSIS**.
>
> **When two such germ-cells combine during fertilisation, the full number is RE-ESTABLISHED** — so it stays constant generation after generation.
>
> **Why doubling would be disastrous: it would soon wreck the control of the cellular apparatus by the DNA.** Halving first is what makes combining possible at all.

---

Why are the male and female gametes so different in size?

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> FEMALE gamete — LARGE, containing the FOOD STORES
> MALE gamete   — SMALLER, and MOTILE
> ```
> **Why: the zygote must grow into an organism with highly specialised tissues and organs, which needs SUFFICIENT STORES OF ENERGY.**
>
> **But a cell packed with food is HEAVY and cannot travel.**
>
> **So the work is divided: one gamete carries the supplies and stays put, the other travels light and moves.**
>
> **In very simple organisms the two germ-cells are barely different, or even identical** — the specialisation appears only as body designs become complex. **And this one split explains almost every difference between male and female reproductive organs.**

Name the parts of a flower and state which are reproductive.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> SEPALS and PETALS — NOT reproductive; the petals attract pollinators
>
> STAMEN — the MALE reproductive part; produces yellowish POLLEN GRAINS
>
> PISTIL — the FEMALE reproductive part, in the centre, with three regions:
>    STIGMA — the terminal part, often sticky
>    STYLE  — the middle elongated part
>    OVARY  — the swollen bottom part, containing OVULES, each with an EGG CELL
> ```
> **Why the stigma is sticky: so that pollen landing on it STAYS** rather than being blown away — a small detail with an obvious purpose.

---

Distinguish unisexual from bisexual flowers, and self- from cross-pollination.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **UNISEXUAL — the flower has EITHER stamens OR pistil.** Papaya, watermelon.
> - **BISEXUAL — the flower has BOTH.** Hibiscus, mustard.
>
> - **SELF-POLLINATION — pollen is transferred within the SAME flower.**
> - **CROSS-POLLINATION — pollen is transferred from ONE flower to ANOTHER**, by **wind, water or animals**.
>
> **The connection between the two ideas: a UNISEXUAL flower CANNOT self-pollinate**, since it has only one kind of reproductive part. **Being unisexual is therefore a way of guaranteeing cross-pollination — and with it, more variation.**

---

Describe what happens from pollination to fertilisation in a flower.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 1. POLLINATION — pollen is transferred from the stamen to the stigma.
> 2. A POLLEN TUBE grows out of the pollen grain.
> 3. The tube travels down through the STYLE to reach the OVARY.
> 4. The male germ-cell FUSES with the female gamete inside the ovule.
> 5. This fusion — FERTILISATION — produces the ZYGOTE.
> ```
> **Why the pollen tube is needed: the male gamete must reach the ovary, but pollen lands on the stigma at the far end of the pistil.**
>
> **The tube is the plant's answer to having a gamete that cannot swim there.** And its growth towards the ovule is **CHEMOTROPISM** from Chapter 6 in action — guided by chemicals.

---

What happens to each part of the flower after fertilisation?

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> ZYGOTE → divides many times → EMBRYO, inside the ovule
> OVULE  → develops a TOUGH COAT → becomes the SEED
> OVARY  → grows rapidly and ripens → becomes the FRUIT
>
> petals, sepals, stamens, style and stigma → SHRIVEL and FALL OFF
> ```
> **GERMINATION is the process by which the seed's embryo develops into a seedling under appropriate conditions.**
>
> **Why the other parts fall off: their jobs are done.** Petals attracted pollinators, the stigma caught pollen, the style guided the tube — **none of that is needed once the zygote exists**, so the plant stops spending resources on them.

---

What is the advantage of seed formation for a plant?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The embryo is packaged with a FOOD STORE inside a TOUGH PROTECTIVE COAT.**
>
> **Three consequences follow:**
> - **the next generation can WAIT** for favourable conditions instead of having to grow immediately
> - **it can be CARRIED FAR from the parent**, so the offspring do not compete with it for light and water
> - **it does not have to start from nothing** — the stored food supports the seedling until it can photosynthesise
>
> **All three come from the same package: protection plus provisions.**

---

Why does sexual maturation happen in the early teens rather than in childhood?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because while the body is growing to its adult size, ITS RESOURCES ARE DIRECTED AT ACHIEVING THAT GROWTH.**
>
> **Maturing the reproductive tissue is not a priority while general body growth is in full swing** — there is only so much to go round.
>
> **So as the RATE OF GENERAL BODY GROWTH SLOWS DOWN, the reproductive tissues begin to mature.** That period during adolescence is called **PUBERTY**.
>
> **The logic is one of scheduling: build the body first, then equip it to reproduce.**

---

List the changes at puberty that are common to both sexes and those that differ.

> [!ANSWER]
> **COMMON to both:**
> ```
> thick hair in the armpits and the genital area, where the skin also darkens
> thinner hair on legs, arms and face
> the skin often becomes oily; pimples may develop
> a new awareness of one's own body and of others'
> ```
> **IN GIRLS:** breast size increases, the skin of the nipples darkens, and **menstruation begins**.
>
> **IN BOYS:** thick facial hair appears, the voice cracks, and the penis occasionally becomes enlarged and erect.
>
> **These changes happen SLOWLY over months and years, NOT all at once and NOT at an exact age.** In some people they come early and quickly, in others slowly — **and they vary between people just as noses and fingers do.**

---

Why does puberty produce such visible changes?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because for animals to mate, their STATE OF SEXUAL MATURITY MUST BE IDENTIFIABLE BY OTHER INDIVIDUALS.**
>
> **Many of the changes at puberty — new hair-growth patterns, changes in body proportions, a deepening voice — are SIGNALS that sexual maturation is taking place.**
>
> **Alongside the signals, the actual apparatus develops too:** the organs needed for the sexual act, and in humans the female reproductive organs and breasts must mature to carry a baby for a long period and to breast-feed it afterwards.

---

Why are the testes located outside the abdominal cavity?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because SPERM FORMATION REQUIRES A LOWER TEMPERATURE than normal body temperature.**
>
> **Inside the abdominal cavity it would simply be too warm** for sperm to form properly. Hanging in the **SCROTUM**, outside the body, keeps the testes a few degrees cooler.
>
> **This is a striking case of ANATOMY BEING DICTATED BY A CHEMICAL REQUIREMENT** — an otherwise strange arrangement, explained entirely by the temperature at which one process works.

---

Trace the path of a sperm from formation to release, naming the glands that contribute.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> TESTES (in the scrotum) — sperms are formed
>    ↓
> VAS DEFERENS — carries them; unites with a tube from the urinary bladder
>    ↓
> URETHRA — the COMMON passage for both sperms and urine
> ```
> **Along the path of the vas deferens, the PROSTATE and the SEMINAL VESICLES add their secretions**, so the sperms travel in a **FLUID**.
>
> **What that fluid does: it makes TRANSPORT easier and provides NUTRITION.**
>
> **A sperm itself is a tiny body of mainly GENETIC MATERIAL plus a LONG TAIL** that lets it swim towards the female germ-cell — it carries almost no supplies of its own, which is exactly why the glands must provide them.

Describe the human female reproductive system and the journey of the egg.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> OVARIES — make the EGGS and produce some HORMONES
>    at BIRTH the ovaries already contain THOUSANDS of immature eggs
>    at PUBERTY some begin maturing — ONE egg per month, from one ovary
>    ↓
> OVIDUCT (FALLOPIAN TUBE) — a thin tube carrying the egg towards the womb
>    ↓
> UTERUS — the elastic bag-like structure where the two oviducts unite
>    ↓
> CERVIX — the uterus opens into the VAGINA through it
> ```
> **Sperms enter through the vagina and travel upwards to the OVIDUCT, where they may meet the egg — so FERTILISATION happens in the oviduct, not the uterus.**

---

What is the placenta and how is it structured for its job?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The PLACENTA is a disc embedded in the uterine wall.**
> ```
> on the EMBRYO's side  — VILLI
> on the MOTHER's side  — BLOOD SPACES surrounding those villi
> ```
> **Why villi again: they provide a LARGE SURFACE AREA** for glucose and oxygen to pass from the mother to the embryo.
>
> **This is the same design solution seen in the small intestine and the lungs** — wherever the body must exchange something quickly, it grows projections.
>
> **And it works in BOTH directions: the embryo's WASTE substances are transferred into the mother's blood** through the same structure.

---

What is menstruation, and why does it happen?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Every month the uterus PREPARES to receive a fertilised egg — its lining becomes THICK and SPONGY, richly supplied with blood, ready to nourish an embryo.**
>
> **If the egg is NOT fertilised, it lives for only about one day, and the preparation is no longer needed.**
>
> **The lining then slowly breaks down and comes out through the vagina as BLOOD AND MUCUS.** The cycle repeats roughly every month and usually lasts about **two to eight days**.
>
> **The point worth understanding clearly: the bleeding is not a failure or an illness. It is the DISMANTLING of a preparation that turned out not to be needed** — and the uterus begins preparing again straight away.

Name two bacterial and two viral sexually transmitted diseases, and state how transmission can be reduced.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> BACTERIAL — GONORRHOEA, SYPHILIS
> VIRAL     — WARTS, HIV-AIDS
> ```
> **Why they spread this way: the sexual act is a VERY INTIMATE CONNECTION OF BODIES**, so it is unsurprising that many diseases can be transmitted through it.
>
> **Using a CONDOM during sex helps prevent the transmission of many of these infections to some extent** — it puts a physical barrier between the two bodies.

Describe the categories of contraceptive methods and the concern attached to each.

> [!ANSWER]
> | Category | How it works | Concern |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Barrier** — condoms and similar coverings | a **mechanical barrier** so sperm cannot reach the egg | — |
> | **Oral pills** | change the **hormonal balance** so eggs are not released | **side effects**, because hormones are being altered |
> | **Loop or copper-T** | placed inside the **uterus** | side effects from **irritation of the uterus** |
> | **Surgical** — blocking the **vas deferens** or the **fallopian tube** | the gametes cannot meet | safe long-term, but **surgery itself can cause infection** if not properly performed |
>
> **The categories differ in WHERE they intervene: before the gametes meet, before the egg is released, or by blocking the passage altogether.**

Why is prenatal sex determination prohibited by law in India?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because it has been MISUSED for ILLEGAL SEX-SELECTIVE ABORTION of female foetuses.**
>
> **Surgery that exists to remove unwanted pregnancies has been used by people who do not want a particular child** — specifically, a female child.
>
> **Why this matters for society: for a healthy society the FEMALE–MALE SEX RATIO MUST BE MAINTAINED.**
>
> **Because of reckless female foeticide, the child sex ratio has been DECLINING AT AN ALARMING RATE in some sections of society** — which is why the law prohibits determining a foetus's sex before birth.

Is population size the main reason for poor standards of living? Discuss both sides.

> [!ANSWER]
> **The argument that it is: birth and death rates determine a population's size, and an EXPANDING POPULATION makes it harder to improve everybody's standard of living** — the same resources must be shared among more people.
>
> **The counter-argument the textbook itself raises: if INEQUALITY in society is the main reason for poor living standards, then the SIZE of the population is relatively unimportant.**
>
> **Resources badly distributed among few people can leave just as many in poverty as resources fairly distributed among many.**
>
> **The honest answer is that this is a genuine open question**, and the chapter invites you to look around and judge which factor matters more where you live.
