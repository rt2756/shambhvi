## What this chapter is about

A pizza cut into slices, the patch of glass a car wiper cleans, the part of the sea a lighthouse beam sweeps — each is a **piece of a circle**. This chapter shows how to find the **area** and **arc length** of such pieces, using one simple idea: **a piece of a circle is a fraction of the whole circle, and the fraction is set by the angle.**

## Quick recap

For a circle of radius r:

```
circumference (perimeter) = 2πr
area                      = πr²
π ≈ 22/7 or 3.14 (the question tells you which)
```

> [!NOTE]
> **Why 2πr and πr²?** π is *defined* as circumference ÷ diameter, so circumference = π × 2r. For the area, cut the circle into many thin slices and lay them head-to-tail: they form a near-rectangle with height r and length half the circumference, πr. Area = πr × r = πr².

## 11.1 Sectors and segments

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **SECTOR** — the region between **two radii** and the arc between them (a pizza slice). The angle between the radii, ∠AOB = θ, is the **angle of the sector**.
> - **SEGMENT** — the region between a **chord** and its arc (the slice with the triangular tip cut off).
>
> Each comes as a **minor** (smaller) and a **major** (larger) piece. The major sector has angle **360° − θ**. "Sector" or "segment" on its own means the **minor** one.

### The area of a sector — by the unitary method

**Start with easy cases.** Half a circle is a sector with angle 180°, and its area is ½πr². A quarter (a **quadrant**, 90°) has area ¼πr². So the area is the same fraction of πr² as the angle is of 360°.

Think of it step by step:

```
angle 360°  →  area πr²       (the whole circle)
angle 1°    →  area πr²/360
angle θ     →  area (θ/360) × πr²
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **AREA OF A SECTOR** of angle θ (in degrees):
> ```
> area = (θ/360) × πr²
> ```
> **LENGTH OF ITS ARC:**
> ```
> arc length = (θ/360) × 2πr
> ```
> **Why the same fraction for both:** the angles at the centre split the whole circle evenly. A 60° sector is exactly 1/6 of the circle, so it gets 1/6 of the area **and** 1/6 of the circumference.

> [!TIP]
> **Simplify θ/360 first.** 30° → 1/12, 45° → 1/8, 60° → 1/6, 72° → 1/5, 90° → 1/4, 120° → 1/3, 180° → 1/2. The arithmetic gets much easier.

**Perimeter of a sector** = arc length + **2r** (don't forget the two straight radii).

### The area of a segment

A minor segment is the sector **with the triangle OAB removed**:

> [!IMPORTANT]
> ```
> area of minor segment = area of sector − area of △OAB
>                       = (θ/360)πr² − area of △OAB
>
> area of major sector  = πr² − area of minor sector
> area of major segment = πr² − area of minor segment
> ```

### Area of the triangle OAB

OA = OB = r, so △OAB is **isosceles**. Three angles come up all the time:

| θ | △OAB is … | Area |
|---|---|---|
| **60°** | **equilateral** (base angles are (180 − 60)/2 = 60° too) | (√3/4) r² |
| **90°** | **right isosceles** | ½ r² |
| **120°** | isosceles with height r/2 | (√3/4) r² |

> [!NOTE]
> **Why 120° gives (√3/4)r²:** drop OM ⊥ AB. It bisects the angle, so ∠AOM = 60°. Then OM = r cos 60° = r/2 and AM = r sin 60° = (√3/2)r, so AB = √3 r. Area = ½ × AB × OM = ½ × √3 r × r/2 = (√3/4) r². (In general, area = ½ r² sin θ — the same as for 60°, because sin 60° = sin 120°.)

## Worked examples from the book

**Example 1 — sector of radius 4 cm and angle 30°; also the major sector (π = 3.14).**

```
minor sector = (30/360) × 3.14 × 16 = 12.56/3 ≈ 4.19 cm²
major sector = 3.14 × 16 − 4.19 = 50.24 − 4.19 = 46.05 ≈ 46.1 cm²
check: (330/360) × 3.14 × 16 = 46.05 ✔
```

**Example 2 — the segment AYB, r = 21 cm, ∠AOB = 120° (π = 22/7).**

```
sector OAYB = (120/360) × (22/7) × 21 × 21 = (1/3) × 1386 = 462 cm²

triangle OAB: drop OM ⊥ AB; ∠AOM = 60°
OM = 21 cos 60° = 21/2         AM = 21 sin 60° = 21√3/2   →   AB = 21√3
area = ½ × 21√3 × 21/2 = (441/4)√3 cm²

segment = 462 − (441√3)/4 = (21/4)(88 − 21√3) cm²
```

## Exercise 11.1, worked (π = 22/7 unless told otherwise)

**1. Sector, r = 6 cm, 60°.** (1/6) × (22/7) × 36 = **132/7 cm²** (≈ 18.86 cm²).

**2. Quadrant of a circle with circumference 22 cm.** First find r: 2 × (22/7) × r = 22 → r = 7/2 cm.

```
quadrant = ¼ × (22/7) × (7/2)² = ¼ × (22/7) × 49/4 = 77/8 cm²
```

**3. Minute hand 14 cm; area swept in 5 minutes.** The minute hand turns 360° in 60 minutes, so **6° per minute** → 30° in 5 minutes.

```
(30/360) × (22/7) × 14 × 14 = (1/12) × 616 = 154/3 cm²
```

**4. r = 10 cm, chord makes a right angle at the centre (π = 3.14).**

```
minor sector = ¼ × 3.14 × 100 = 78.5
△OAB = ½ × 10 × 10 = 50              (right angle at O)
(i)  minor segment = 78.5 − 50 = 28.5 cm²
(ii) major sector  = 314 − 78.5 = 235.5 cm²
```

**5. r = 21 cm, 60° arc.**

```
(i)   arc    = (1/6) × 2 × (22/7) × 21 = 22 cm
(ii)  sector = (1/6) × (22/7) × 441    = 231 cm²
(iii) △OAB is equilateral: (√3/4) × 441
      segment = 231 − (441√3)/4 cm²
```

**6. r = 15 cm, 60° (π = 3.14, √3 = 1.73).**

```
sector  = (1/6) × 3.14 × 225 = 117.75
△ (equilateral) = (1.73/4) × 225 = 97.3125
minor segment = 117.75 − 97.3125 = 20.4375 cm²
major segment = 3.14 × 225 − 20.4375 = 706.5 − 20.4375 = 686.0625 cm²
```

**7. r = 12 cm, 120° (π = 3.14, √3 = 1.73).**

```
sector = (1/3) × 3.14 × 144 = 150.72
△ = (√3/4) × 144 = 36√3 = 36 × 1.73 = 62.28
segment = 150.72 − 62.28 = 88.44 cm²
```

**8. A horse tied at a corner of a 15 m square field with a 5 m rope (π = 3.14).** The corner of a square is 90°, so the horse grazes a **quadrant**.

```
(i)  ¼ × 3.14 × 5² = 19.625 m²
(ii) with a 10 m rope: ¼ × 3.14 × 10² = 78.5 m²
     increase = 78.5 − 19.625 = 58.875 m²
```

> [!TIP]
> **Doubling the radius makes the area 4 times as big** (r² → (2r)² = 4r²). So the increase is 3 × 19.625 = 58.875 — a quick check.

**9. A brooch: a circle of diameter 35 mm plus 5 diameters making 10 equal sectors.**

```
(i)  circumference = (22/7) × 35 = 110 mm;  5 diameters = 5 × 35 = 175 mm
     total wire = 285 mm
(ii) each sector = (1/10) × (22/7) × (35/2)² = (1/10) × (22/7) × 1225/4 = 385/4 mm²
```

**10. An umbrella, 8 equal ribs, radius 45 cm.** 8 sectors, each 360°/8 = 45°:

```
(1/8) × (22/7) × 45 × 45 = 44550/56 = 22275/28 cm²   (≈ 795.5 cm²)
```

**11. Two wipers, blade 25 cm, each sweeps 115°, no overlap.**

```
one wiper = (115/360) × (22/7) × 625
two wipers = 2 × that = (230/360) × (22/7) × 625 = 158125/126 cm²  (≈ 1255 cm²)
```

**12. A lighthouse beam over 80° to a distance of 16.5 km (π = 3.14).**

```
(80/360) × 3.14 × 16.5² = (2/9) × 3.14 × 272.25 ≈ 189.97 km²
```

**13. A round table cover, radius 28 cm, with six equal designs at ₹0.35 per cm² (√3 = 1.7).** The six designs are six **segments**, each on a 60° chord (360° ÷ 6).

```
one sector = (1/6) × (22/7) × 784 = 2464/6
one equilateral triangle = (√3/4) × 784 = 1.7 × 196 = 333.2
six segments = 6 × sector − 6 × triangle = 2464 − 1999.2 = 464.8 cm²
cost = 464.8 × 0.35 = ₹162.68
```

> [!TIP]
> **Six 60° sectors make the whole circle**, so 6 × sector = πr² = 2464. That's a shortcut: all six segments together = circle − hexagon.

**14. Area of a sector of angle p, radius R.** (p/360)πR² = (p/720) × 2πR² → **(D)**.

## More practice

**A. Perimeter of a sector of radius 7 cm and angle 90°.**

```
arc = ¼ × 2 × (22/7) × 7 = 11 cm;   perimeter = 11 + 7 + 7 = 25 cm
```

**B. A sector of radius 14 cm has area 154 cm². Its angle?**

```
(θ/360) × (22/7) × 196 = 154   →   (θ/360) × 616 = 154   →   θ/360 = 1/4   →   θ = 90°
```

**C. An arc of length 22 cm in a circle of radius 21 cm. Angle?**

```
(θ/360) × 2 × (22/7) × 21 = 22   →   (θ/360) × 132 = 22   →   θ = 60°
```

**D. Area of a sector from its arc length.** Since arc l = (θ/360)2πr and area = (θ/360)πr², dividing gives **area = ½ × l × r** — just like a triangle with base l and height r. (In C, area = ½ × 22 × 21 = 231 cm², matching Q5.)

**E. A square inscribed in a circle of radius 7 cm — the area outside the square.** The diagonal of the square is the diameter, 14 cm, so the square's area = ½ × 14 × 14 = 98. Circle = 154. **Outside = 56 cm².**

## Shaded-region problems

Exam questions often show a shaded shape made of squares, circles and sectors. The method is always the same: **shaded area = (area of the big shape) − (area of the unshaded parts)**, or the other way round, add up the shaded pieces.

**F. A square of side 14 cm has a quadrant drawn at each corner, radius 7 cm. Find the area left unshaded in the middle.**

```
square = 14 × 14 = 196 cm²
four quadrants of radius 7 = one full circle = (22/7) × 49 = 154 cm²
middle region = 196 − 154 = 42 cm²
```
**Why four quadrants make one circle:** each quadrant is ¼ of a circle of the same radius, so four of them add up to a whole circle.

**G. A ring (annulus) between circles of radii 7 cm and 3.5 cm.**

```
ring = π(R² − r²) = (22/7)(49 − 12.25) = (22/7)(36.75) = 115.5 cm²
```
**Why R² − r² and not (R − r)²:** you subtract the small disc's area (πr²) from the big disc's (πR²). (R − r)² would be the area of a small square, which has nothing to do with it.

**H. A semicircle is drawn on each side of a square of side 7 cm (outside it). Find the area of the whole figure.**

```
square = 49 cm²
four semicircles of radius 3.5 = two full circles = 2 × (22/7) × 12.25 = 77 cm²
total = 49 + 77 = 126 cm²
```

**I. A sector of a circle of radius 21 cm has angle 60°. Find the perimeter of the sector.**

```
arc = (1/6) × 2 × (22/7) × 21 = 22 cm
perimeter = 22 + 21 + 21 = 64 cm
```

**J. The minute hand of a clock is 7 cm long. What area does it sweep between 9:00 and 9:35?**

```
35 minutes × 6° per minute = 210°
area = (210/360) × (22/7) × 49 = (7/12) × 154 = 89.83 cm² (about)
```

> [!TIP]
> **Count the sectors before you calculate.** Four quadrants = one circle; two semicircles = one circle; six 60° sectors = one circle. Spotting this turns four sums into one.

## Method checklist

1. **Identify the shape:** sector (two radii), segment (chord), quadrant (90°), semicircle (180°).
2. **Find r** if only the circumference or diameter is given.
3. **Simplify θ/360.**
4. **For a segment, subtract the triangle** — equilateral at 60°, right at 90°, (√3/4)r² at 120°.
5. **Major = whole − minor.**
6. Use the **π (and √3)** the question gives, and give units (cm, cm²).

## In a nutshell

- **Sector:** area = (θ/360)πr²; arc = (θ/360)2πr; perimeter = arc + 2r.
- **Segment** = sector − triangle OAB.
- **Major sector/segment** = πr² − minor.
- Triangle OAB: **60° → equilateral (√3/4)r²**, **90° → ½r²**, **120° → (√3/4)r²**.
- Minute hand: 6° per minute. Corner of a square field: a quadrant.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** area uses πr², arc uses 2πr — don't swap them · the radius is half the diameter · a segment is NOT the whole sector — subtract the triangle · perimeter of a sector includes the two radii · area units are squared (cm²), arc length isn't · use the π value the question asks for · "increase in area" means new − old, not the new area.
