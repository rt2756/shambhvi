## 11.1 Sectors and segments

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A SECTOR is the part of a circular region enclosed by TWO RADII and the corresponding ARC.** The angle between the radii is the **ANGLE OF THE SECTOR**.
>
> **A SEGMENT is the part of a circular region enclosed between a CHORD and the corresponding ARC.**

**Both come in two sizes:**
- **MINOR SECTOR** (the smaller) and **MAJOR SECTOR** (the larger). **The angle of the major sector is `360° − θ`.**
- **MINOR SEGMENT** and **MAJOR SEGMENT**, formed by the same chord.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **CONVENTION: "sector" and "segment" mean the MINOR sector and MINOR segment, unless stated otherwise.**

> [!TIP]
> **The difference in one line: a SECTOR is cut by two RADII (like a slice of pizza); a SEGMENT is cut by a CHORD (like the piece sliced off the side of a roti).**
>
> **A sector always touches the centre; a segment never does.**

## Area of a sector

**Derivation by the UNITARY METHOD:**
```
the whole circle is a sector of angle 360°, with area  πr²

for an angle of 1°:        πr²/360

for an angle of θ°:        (θ/360) × πr²
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> ```
> area of a sector of angle θ  =  (θ/360) × πr²
> ```

## Length of an arc

**The same method, using the whole circumference:**
```
the whole circle (360°) has circumference  2πr

for an angle of θ°:        (θ/360) × 2πr
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> ```
> length of the arc of a sector of angle θ  =  (θ/360) × 2πr
> ```

> [!TIP]
> **Both formulas are the same idea: `(θ/360)` is the FRACTION of the whole circle you are taking.**
>
> **Take that fraction of the AREA to get the sector's area; take it of the CIRCUMFERENCE to get the arc's length.** There is nothing to memorise beyond "what fraction of the circle is this?"
>
> **Quick checks: θ = 360 gives the whole circle; θ = 180 gives a semicircle (half of each); θ = 90 gives a quadrant.**

## Area of a segment

> [!IMPORTANT]
> ```
> area of a segment  =  area of the corresponding SECTOR  −  area of the TRIANGLE
>
>                    =  (θ/360) × πr²  −  area of △OAB
> ```
> **Why: the sector consists of the segment PLUS the triangle formed by the two radii and the chord.** Remove the triangle and the segment is what remains.

**The major parts, without extra work:**
```
area of the MAJOR sector  = πr² − area of the minor sector
area of the MAJOR segment = πr² − area of the minor segment
```

## Finding the triangle's area

**In △OAB the two sides OA and OB are radii, and the angle between them is θ.** Drop `OM ⊥ AB`.

```
OA = OB (radii), so by RHS congruence △AMO ≅ △BMO

therefore M is the MIDPOINT of AB, and ∠AOM = ∠BOM = θ/2

then      OM = r cos(θ/2)          AM = r sin(θ/2)
          AB = 2 AM = 2r sin(θ/2)

area of △OAB = ½ × AB × OM = ½ × 2r sin(θ/2) × r cos(θ/2)
```

> [!TIP]
> **The perpendicular from the centre bisects BOTH the chord and the angle** — that is what turns an awkward isosceles triangle into two right triangles you can handle with the ratios of Chapter 8.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Two special angles are worth recognising instantly:**
> ```
> θ = 90°:   △OAB is right-angled at O, so its area is simply  ½ r²
>
> θ = 60°:   △OAB is EQUILATERAL (two radii and the angle between them
>            being 60° forces the third side to equal r too),
>            so its area is  (√3/4) r²
> ```

## Worked examples

**A sector of radius 4 cm and angle 30°. Find its area and the area of the major sector. (π = 3.14)**
```
area of the sector = (30/360) × 3.14 × 4 × 4
                   = (1/12) × 50.24
                   = 4.19 cm²  (approx.)

major sector = πr² − minor sector
             = 3.14 × 16 − 4.19
             = 50.24 − 4.19 = 46.05 ≈ 46.1 cm²
```
**Or directly:**
```
major sector = ((360 − 30)/360) × 3.14 × 16 = (330/360) × 50.24 ≈ 46.1 cm²
```

**A segment AYB with radius 21 cm and ∠AOB = 120°. Find its area. (π = 22/7)**
```
STEP 1 — the sector:
      area = (120/360) × (22/7) × 21 × 21 = (1/3) × 1386 = 462 cm²

STEP 2 — the triangle:
      drop OM ⊥ AB, so ∠AOM = 60°

      OM = 21 cos 60° = 21/2 cm
      AM = 21 sin 60° = 21√3/2 cm
      AB = 2 × AM = 21√3 cm

      area of △OAB = ½ × 21√3 × 21/2 = (441√3)/4 cm²

STEP 3 — subtract:
      area of the segment = 462 − (441√3)/4
                          = (21/4)(88 − 21√3) cm²
```

> [!WARNING]
> **The value of π to use.** Unless told otherwise, **take π = 22/7** — which is convenient whenever the radius is a multiple of 7. **Use π = 3.14 only when the question says so**, and never mix the two within one answer.

> [!TIP]
> **Work in stages and label them.** Sector first, triangle second, subtraction last. **Combining them into one long expression is where errors creep in**, and a wrong triangle area is impossible to spot inside a single line of algebra.

## Summary

```
SECTOR    enclosed by TWO RADII and an arc — always touches the centre
SEGMENT   enclosed by a CHORD and an arc  — never touches the centre
   "sector" and "segment" mean the MINOR ones unless stated otherwise
   the major sector's angle is 360° − θ

area of a sector       = (θ/360) × πr²
length of an arc       = (θ/360) × 2πr
   both are just "(θ/360) is the FRACTION of the circle you are taking"
   check: θ = 360 gives the whole circle, θ = 180 a semicircle, θ = 90 a quadrant

area of a segment      = area of the sector − area of △OAB

major sector  = πr² − minor sector
major segment = πr² − minor segment

FINDING △OAB   drop OM ⊥ AB; it BISECTS both the chord and the angle
      OM = r cos(θ/2)        AM = r sin(θ/2)        AB = 2r sin(θ/2)
      θ = 90°  → right-angled, area = ½ r²
      θ = 60°  → EQUILATERAL, area = (√3/4) r²

π = 22/7 unless the question says otherwise; use 3.14 only when told
work in stages — sector, then triangle, then subtract
```
