## What this chapter is about

Chapter 8 built the tools — sin, cos, tan and their values at 30°, 45°, 60°. This chapter puts them to work: finding the **height of a minar**, the **width of a river**, the **distance between two ships** — things you can't measure with a tape.

Every problem follows one pattern: **draw the picture → find a right triangle → pick the ratio that links what you know to what you want.**

## 9.1 Heights and distances

### The words you need

A student stands some distance from the **Qutub Minar** and looks at the top.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **Line of sight** — the line from the observer's **eye** to the point being looked at.
> - **Angle of elevation** — the angle between the line of sight and the **horizontal**, when the object is **above** eye level (you **raise** your head).
> - **Angle of depression** — the angle between the line of sight and the **horizontal**, when the object is **below** eye level (you **lower** your head). A girl on a balcony looking down at a flower pot sees it at an angle of depression.

> [!WARNING]
> **Both angles are measured from the HORIZONTAL**, never from the vertical wall or tower. The angle of depression is the angle *below* the observer's horizontal line, not the angle at the top of the tower inside the triangle.

> [!TIP]
> **Angle of depression from A to B = angle of elevation from B to A.** The horizontal at the top and the ground are **parallel**, and the line of sight cuts both, so the two angles are **alternate angles** — equal. This lets you put the depression angle at the bottom of the triangle, where it's easier to use.

### What do you need to know?

To find the minar's height CD you need: **(i)** the distance DE from its foot, **(ii)** the angle of elevation ∠BAC, **(iii)** the student's height AE. Then

```
CD = CB + BD,  where BD = AE (the student's eye height)
CB = AB × tan A     (tan links the opposite side CB to the adjacent side AB)
```

### Choosing the ratio

> [!IMPORTANT]
> | You know | You want | Use |
> |---|---|---|
> | horizontal distance | height | **tan θ** = height / distance |
> | slant length (rope, ladder, string) | height | **sin θ** = height / slant |
> | slant length | horizontal distance | **cos θ** = distance / slant |
> | height | slant length | **sin θ** again (slant = height / sin θ) |
>
> **Why tan is the favourite:** most problems give a ground distance and ask for a height (or the other way round) — those are the opposite and adjacent sides, which is exactly what tan connects.

**Values you'll use again and again:**

```
tan 30° = 1/√3     tan 45° = 1     tan 60° = √3
sin 30° = 1/2      sin 45° = 1/√2  sin 60° = √3/2
```

## Worked examples from the book

**Example 1 — from 15 m away, the angle of elevation of a tower's top is 60°. Height?**

```
tan 60° = AB/BC   →   √3 = AB/15   →   AB = 15√3 m
```

**Example 2 — an electrician must reach 1.3 m below the top of a 5 m pole using a ladder at 60°. Ladder length? How far from the pole is its foot?**

```
height to reach BD = 5 − 1.3 = 3.7 m
sin 60° = BD/ladder   →   ladder = 3.7 ÷ (√3/2) = 7.4/√3 ≈ 4.28 m
tan 60° = BD/DC       →   DC = 3.7/√3 ≈ 2.14 m
```
(The ladder is the slant side → **sin**; the foot's distance is the ground side → **tan**.)

**Example 3 — an observer 1.5 m tall, 28.5 m from a chimney, sees its top at 45°.**

```
tan 45° = AE/DE  →  AE = 28.5 m   (above her eyes)
chimney = 28.5 + 1.5 = 30 m
```
> [!WARNING]
> **Add the observer's height!** The triangle starts at her **eyes**, not the ground. Forgetting the 1.5 m is the most common slip in this chapter.

**Example 4 — from P, the top of a 10 m building is at 30° and the top of a flagstaff on it is at 45°.**

```
In △PAB:  tan 30° = 10/PA   →   PA = 10√3 ≈ 17.32 m     (distance to the building)
In △PAD:  tan 45° = (10 + x)/PA   →   10 + x = 10√3   →   x = 10(√3 − 1) ≈ 7.32 m
```
Flagstaff ≈ **7.32 m**, building ≈ **17.32 m** from P.

**Example 5 — a tower's shadow is 40 m longer when the sun's altitude is 30° than when it is 60°.** Let the height be h and the shorter shadow x.

```
tan 60° = h/x          →   x = h/√3
tan 30° = h/(x + 40)   →   x + 40 = h√3
subtract:  40 = h√3 − h/√3 = h(3 − 1)/√3 = 2h/√3   →   h = 20√3 m
```

> [!TIP]
> **Two angles, one height → write both triangles in terms of the same h, then subtract.** The unknown distance drops out.

**Example 6 — from the top of a tall building, the top and bottom of an 8 m building are seen at depressions of 30° and 45°.** Let the tall building be h and the gap between them d.

```
depression 45° to the bottom:  tan 45° = h/d        →   d = h
depression 30° to the top:     tan 30° = (h − 8)/d  →   h − 8 = h/√3
h(1 − 1/√3) = 8   →   h = 8√3/(√3 − 1) = 4√3(√3 + 1) = 4(3 + √3) ≈ 18.93 m
```
Height and distance are both **4(3 + √3) m**.

**Example 7 — from a bridge 3 m high, the two banks are at depressions of 30° and 45°. River width?**

```
bank on the 30° side:  AD = 3/tan 30° = 3√3 m
bank on the 45° side:  DB = 3/tan 45° = 3 m
width = 3√3 + 3 = 3(√3 + 1) m
```

## Exercise 9.1, worked

**1. A 20 m rope from the top of a pole to the ground makes 30° with the ground.** Rope = slant → **sin**:

```
sin 30° = h/20   →   h = 10 m
```

**2. A tree breaks; its top touches the ground 8 m from the foot, at 30°.** The standing part (AB), the broken part (AC, slant) and the ground (BC = 8) make a right triangle:

```
tan 30° = AB/8     →   AB = 8/√3
cos 30° = 8/AC     →   AC = 16/√3
tree = AB + AC = 24/√3 = 8√3 m
```

**3. Slides: 1.5 m high at 30°; 3 m high at 60°.** Slide = slant, height = opposite → **sin**:

```
1.5/sin 30° = 1.5 × 2 = 3 m         3/sin 60° = 6/√3 = 2√3 m
```

**4. From 30 m away the tower's top is at 30°.** h = 30 tan 30° = 30/√3 = **10√3 m**.

**5. Kite 60 m high, string at 60°.** string = 60/sin 60° = 120/√3 = **40√3 m**.

**6. A 1.5 m boy, a 30 m building; elevation goes from 30° to 60° as he walks.** Height above his eyes = 30 − 1.5 = **28.5 m**.

```
at 30°: distance = 28.5/tan 30° = 28.5√3
at 60°: distance = 28.5/tan 60° = 28.5/√3
walked = 28.5(√3 − 1/√3) = 28.5 × 2/√3 = 57/√3 = 19√3 m
```

**7. A tower on a 20 m building: elevations of its bottom and top are 45° and 60°.**

```
45° to the bottom: distance = 20/tan 45° = 20 m
60° to the top:    20 + t = 20 tan 60° = 20√3   →   t = 20(√3 − 1) m
```

**8. A 1.6 m statue on a pedestal: elevations 60° (statue top) and 45° (pedestal top).** Let the pedestal be h; at 45° the distance is also h.

```
tan 60° = (h + 1.6)/h   →   h√3 = h + 1.6   →   h = 1.6/(√3 − 1) = 0.8(√3 + 1) m
```

**9. From the foot of the tower, the building's top is at 30°; from the foot of the building, the 50 m tower's top is at 60°.**

```
gap d: tan 60° = 50/d   →   d = 50/√3
building = d tan 30° = (50/√3)(1/√3) = 50/3 = 16⅔ m
```

**10. Two equal poles on either side of an 80 m road; from a point between them, elevations 60° and 30°.** Let the point be x from the first pole:

```
h = x tan 60° = x√3        h = (80 − x) tan 30° = (80 − x)/√3
3x = 80 − x   →   x = 20,   h = 20√3 m
distances: 20 m and 60 m
```

**11. TV tower across a canal: 60° from the opposite bank, 30° from 20 m further back.** Width x:

```
h = x√3,   h = (x + 20)/√3   →   3x = x + 20   →   x = 10
tower = 10√3 m, canal = 10 m
```

**12. From a 7 m building: tower top at 60° elevation, tower foot at 45° depression.**

```
45° depression to the foot: distance = 7/tan 45° = 7 m
60° elevation to the top:   part above eye level = 7 tan 60° = 7√3
tower = 7 + 7√3 = 7(√3 + 1) m
```

**13. From a 75 m lighthouse, two ships (one behind the other) at depressions 30° and 45°.**

```
nearer ship (45°): 75/tan 45° = 75 m
farther ship (30°): 75/tan 30° = 75√3 m
gap = 75(√3 − 1) m
```

**14. A 1.2 m girl watches a balloon at 88.2 m; elevation falls from 60° to 30°.** Height above her eyes = 88.2 − 1.2 = **87 m**.

```
distance at 60° = 87/√3,   at 30° = 87√3
travelled = 87(√3 − 1/√3) = 87 × 2/√3 = 174/√3 = 58√3 m
```

**15. From a tower's top a car is at depression 30°; 6 s later at 60°. Time to reach the foot?** Let the tower be h.

```
at 30°: distance = h√3        at 60°: distance = h/√3
covered in 6 s: h√3 − h/√3 = 2h/√3
still to go:    h/√3   — exactly HALF of what it covered
same speed → half the time → 3 seconds
```

> [!TIP]
> **Q15 has no numbers for h — and doesn't need them.** It only asks about a ratio of distances, so h cancels. Watch for this.

## More practice

**A. A ladder 10 m long leans against a wall and makes 60° with the ground. How high up the wall does it reach, and how far is its foot from the wall?**

```
height = 10 sin 60° = 10 × √3/2 = 5√3 ≈ 8.66 m
foot   = 10 cos 60° = 10 × 1/2  = 5 m
```
The ladder is the slant side, so it's sin for the height and cos for the ground distance.

**B. The angle of elevation of the sun is 45°. A pole casts a shadow 12 m long. Height of the pole?** tan 45° = h/12 → **h = 12 m**. At 45° the height and the shadow are always equal, because tan 45° = 1.

**C. From the top of a 60 m cliff, the angle of depression of a boat is 30°. How far is the boat from the foot of the cliff?** Move the 30° to the boat (alternate angles): tan 30° = 60/d → d = 60√3 ≈ **103.9 m**.

**D. A tower casts a shadow √3 times its height. What is the sun's altitude?**

```
tan θ = height / shadow = h / (√3 h) = 1/√3   →   θ = 30°
```
**Why it works without numbers:** only the RATIO of height to shadow matters, and tan θ is exactly that ratio.

**E. Two men stand on opposite sides of a 75 m tower and see its top at 30° and 60°. How far apart are they?**

```
man at 30°: 75/tan 30° = 75√3
man at 60°: 75/tan 60° = 75/√3 = 25√3
distance apart = 75√3 + 25√3 = 100√3 ≈ 173.2 m
```
They're on **opposite sides**, so you **add** the distances. If they were on the same side, you would subtract (like Exercise 9.1 Q13).

## How to tackle any heights-and-distances problem

1. **Draw a neat diagram.** Mark the vertical (tower/building), the horizontal (ground), the observer.
2. **Mark the angle at the right place** — from the horizontal. Move a depression angle to the bottom using alternate angles.
3. **Spot the right triangle(s)** and label the unknowns (h, x).
4. **Pick the ratio** (tan for height ↔ distance, sin/cos with a slant).
5. **Two triangles? Write both in the same unknowns**, then eliminate.
6. **Adjust for eye height** at the end.
7. **Rationalise or approximate** as the question asks (√3 ≈ 1.732).

## In a nutshell

- **Line of sight:** eye to object. **Elevation:** looking up from the horizontal. **Depression:** looking down from the horizontal.
- **Depression from the top = elevation from the bottom** (alternate angles).
- **tan θ = height/distance** for most problems; **sin θ** when a rope, ladder, string or slide is the slant.
- **Two angles from two points:** express both distances with the same h and subtract.
- **Add the observer's eye height** to get the full height.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** measure angles from the horizontal, never from the vertical · forgetting the observer's height (subtract it first, add it back at the end) · using tan when the slant side is given — use sin/cos · for depression, the angle inside the triangle at the top is 90° − θ, not θ · tan 30° = 1/√3, not √3 · keep √3 exact unless the question says to approximate.
