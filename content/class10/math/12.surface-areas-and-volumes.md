## What this chapter is about

An oil tanker on a truck is a **cylinder with a hemisphere at each end**. A test tube is a **cylinder with a hemisphere at the bottom**. A spinning top is a **cone on a hemisphere**. None of these is a single basic solid — they're **combinations**.

The plan for every problem: **break the object into basic solids you already know, then put the answers back together carefully.** Volumes simply add. Surface areas need more care, because where two solids are glued, some surface disappears.

## The basic solids (from Class 9)

| Solid | Curved / lateral surface area | Total surface area | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cuboid** l × b × h | 2h(l + b) | 2(lb + bh + hl) | lbh |
| **Cube** side a | 4a² | 6a² | a³ |
| **Cylinder** radius r, height h | 2πrh | 2πr(r + h) | πr²h |
| **Cone** radius r, height h, slant l | πrl | πr(l + r) | ⅓πr²h |
| **Sphere** radius r | 4πr² | 4πr² | (4/3)πr³ |
| **Hemisphere** radius r | 2πr² | 3πr² | (2/3)πr³ |

**Slant height of a cone:** l = √(r² + h²) — Pythagoras in the right triangle made by the height, the radius and the slant side.

> [!NOTE]
> **Where these come from (short reasons):**
> - **Cylinder CSA 2πrh:** unroll the curved surface — it's a rectangle whose width is the circumference 2πr and whose height is h.
> - **Cylinder volume πr²h:** a stack of circular discs, each of area πr², piled to height h.
> - **Cone volume ⅓πr²h:** fill a cone with water and pour it into a cylinder with the same base and height — it takes exactly **three** cones to fill it.
> - **Cone CSA πrl:** cut and flatten the curved surface — it's a sector of radius l whose arc is the base circumference 2πr; its area is ½ × arc × radius = ½ × 2πr × l.
> - **Hemisphere CSA 2πr²** is half the sphere's 4πr²; its **TSA 3πr²** adds the flat circular face πr².

## 12.2 Surface area of a combination

### The key idea

Glue a cone onto a hemisphere to make a round-bottomed toy. Paint it. Which surfaces get paint?

- the **curved surface of the cone** ✔
- the **curved surface of the hemisphere** ✔
- the flat circle where they were glued ✘ — it's **inside** the toy now.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Surface area of a combined solid = the sum of the surfaces you can actually SEE (touch, paint).**
> It is **not** the sum of the total surface areas of the parts — the glued faces are hidden.
> ```
> tanker  = CSA(hemisphere) + CSA(cylinder) + CSA(hemisphere)
> top/toy = CSA(hemisphere) + CSA(cone)
> ```

> [!TIP]
> **Ask, for each face: "If I were an ant walking on the outside, could I stand here?"** Count it only if yes. And watch for **rings** — when a smaller solid sits on a bigger flat face, the part of the bigger face around it stays visible.

### Worked examples from the book

**Example 1 — Rasheed's top: a cone on a hemisphere, total height 5 cm, diameter 3.5 cm.**

```
r = 1.75 cm
CSA hemisphere = 2πr² = 2 × (22/7) × 1.75 × 1.75 = 19.25 cm²
cone height = 5 − 1.75 = 3.25 cm
slant l = √(1.75² + 3.25²) ≈ 3.7 cm
CSA cone = πrl = (22/7) × 1.75 × 3.7 ≈ 20.35 cm²
area to colour ≈ 39.6 cm²
```

> [!WARNING]
> **The height of the cone isn't 5 cm** — part of the 5 cm is the hemisphere, whose height is its radius. Always subtract.

**Example 2 — a 5 cm cube with a hemisphere (diameter 4.2 cm) on top.** The hemisphere covers a circle of the top face, then adds its curved dome:

```
TSA of cube = 6 × 25 = 150 cm²
block = 150 − πr² + 2πr² = 150 + πr²
      = 150 + (22/7) × 2.1 × 2.1 = 150 + 13.86 = 163.86 cm²
```

**Example 3 — a toy rocket: cone (diameter 5 cm, height 6 cm) on a cylinder (diameter 3 cm), total height 26 cm. Paint the cone orange, the cylinder yellow (π = 3.14).**

```
cone: r = 2.5, h = 6, l = √(6.25 + 36) = 6.5
cylinder: r′ = 1.5, h′ = 26 − 6 = 20

orange = CSA cone + (cone's base − cylinder's base)   ← the visible RING
       = π[2.5 × 6.5 + 2.5² − 1.5²] = 3.14 × 20.25 = 63.585 cm²
yellow = CSA cylinder + its bottom = πr′(2h′ + r′) = 3.14 × 1.5 × 41.5 = 195.465 cm²
```

**Example 4 — Mayank's bird-bath: a cylinder (height 1.45 m, radius 30 cm) with a hemispherical hollow at the top.** Outside: the curved cylinder. Inside the hollow: the curved hemisphere. (The top circle is removed by the hollow.)

```
TSA = 2πrh + 2πr² = 2πr(h + r) = 2 × (22/7) × 30 × (145 + 30) = 33000 cm² = 3.3 m²
```

### Exercise 12.1, worked (π = 22/7)

**1. Two cubes of 64 cm³ joined end to end.** Edge = ∛64 = 4 cm. The result is a 8 × 4 × 4 cuboid:

```
2(8×4 + 4×4 + 4×8) = 2(32 + 16 + 32) = 160 cm²
```
*(Check another way: 2 cubes have 12 faces, 2 are glued → 10 faces × 16 = 160 ✔.)*

**2. A hollow hemisphere (diameter 14 cm) under a hollow cylinder; total height 13 cm. Inner surface area?**

```
r = 7; cylinder height = 13 − 7 = 6
inner area = 2πr² + 2πrh = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × (7 + 6) = 44 × 13 = 572 cm²
```

**3. A cone (r = 3.5 cm) on a hemisphere of the same radius; total height 15.5 cm.**

```
cone height = 15.5 − 3.5 = 12;  l = √(12.25 + 144) = √156.25 = 12.5
TSA = πrl + 2πr² = (22/7)(3.5)(12.5) + 2(22/7)(3.5)² = 137.5 + 77 = 214.5 cm²
```

**4. A 7 cm cube with a hemisphere on top.** The hemisphere can't stick out past the face, so its **greatest diameter = 7 cm** (r = 3.5).

```
SA = 6 × 49 − πr² + 2πr² = 294 + πr² = 294 + 38.5 = 332.5 cm²
```

**5. A hemispherical hollow of diameter l cut from a cube of edge l.** Remove the circle, add the inside of the bowl:

```
SA = 6l² − π(l/2)² + 2π(l/2)² = 6l² + πl²/4 = (l²/4)(π + 24)
```

**6. A capsule: 14 mm long, diameter 5 mm.** r = 2.5; cylinder length = 14 − 2 × 2.5 = 9. Two hemispheres make one sphere:

```
SA = 2πr × 9 + 4πr² = 2πr(9 + 2r) = 2 × (22/7) × 2.5 × 14 = 220 mm²
```

**7. A tent: cylinder (height 2.1 m, diameter 4 m) with a conical top of slant 2.8 m. Canvas, and its cost at ₹500/m²?** No canvas on the floor, none where the cone meets the cylinder.

```
cylinder CSA = 2 × (22/7) × 2 × 2.1 = 26.4 m²
cone CSA     = (22/7) × 2 × 2.8      = 17.6 m²
canvas = 44 m²,   cost = 44 × 500 = ₹22000
```

**8. A cylinder (h = 2.4 cm, diameter 1.4 cm) with a cone of the same size hollowed out.** Remaining surface: outside curve + bottom + inside of the cone (the top circle has been removed).

```
r = 0.7, l = √(0.49 + 5.76) = 2.5
2πrh = 2 × (22/7) × 0.7 × 2.4 = 10.56
πr²  = 1.54
πrl  = (22/7) × 0.7 × 2.5 = 5.5
total = 17.6 ≈ 18 cm²
```

**9. A cylinder (h = 10 cm, r = 3.5 cm) with a hemisphere scooped from each end.**

```
SA = 2πrh + 2 × 2πr² = 220 + 154 = 374 cm²
```

## 12.3 Volume of a combination

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Volume of a combined solid = the sum of the volumes of its parts** (or the difference, if something is scooped out).
>
> **Why volumes behave better than areas:** gluing two solids doesn't make any space disappear — every bit of each part is still there. Only the *surfaces* at the join get hidden.

### Worked examples from the book

**Example 5 — Shanta's shed: a 15 m × 7 m × 8 m cuboid topped by a half-cylinder (diameter 7 m, length 15 m). Machinery takes 300 m³ and 20 workers take 0.08 m³ each.**

```
cuboid        = 15 × 7 × 8 = 840 m³
half cylinder = ½ × (22/7) × 3.5 × 3.5 × 15 = 288.75 m³
empty shed    = 1128.75 m³
with machinery and workers: 1128.75 − 300 − 1.6 = 827.15 m³
```

**Example 6 — a juice glass: inner diameter 5 cm, height 10 cm, with a raised hemisphere at the bottom (π = 3.14).**

```
apparent capacity = πr²h = 3.14 × 2.5² × 10 = 196.25 cm³
hemisphere        = (2/3) × 3.14 × 2.5³ ≈ 32.71 cm³
actual capacity   = 196.25 − 32.71 = 163.54 cm³
```

**Example 7 — a toy: cone (height 2 cm) on a hemisphere, base diameter 4 cm. Then the gap if a cylinder is fitted round it (π = 3.14).**

```
r = 2
toy = (2/3)πr³ + (1/3)πr²h = (2/3)(3.14)(8) + (1/3)(3.14)(4)(2) = 16.75 + 8.37 = 25.12 cm³
cylinder: r = 2, height = 2 + 2 = 4 → 3.14 × 4 × 4 = 50.24 cm³
difference = 50.24 − 25.12 = 25.12 cm³
```

> [!NOTE]
> The toy fills **exactly half** the cylinder here. That's because cone + hemisphere = (⅓ + ⅔)πr³ = πr³ when h = r, and the cylinder is πr² × 2r = 2πr³.

### Exercise 12.2, worked (π = 22/7)

**1. A cone on a hemisphere, both radius 1 cm, cone height = radius.**

```
(2/3)π(1)³ + (1/3)π(1)²(1) = (2/3)π + (1/3)π = π cm³
```

**2. Rachel's model: a cylinder with a cone at each end; diameter 3 cm, total length 12 cm, each cone 2 cm high.**

```
r = 1.5; cylinder length = 12 − 2 − 2 = 8
volume = πr² × 8 + 2 × (1/3)πr² × 2 = πr²(8 + 4/3) = (22/7)(2.25)(28/3) = 66 cm³
```

**3. 45 gulab jamuns, each a cylinder with hemispherical ends, length 5 cm, diameter 2.8 cm; syrup is 30% of the volume.**

```
r = 1.4; cylinder length = 5 − 2.8 = 2.2
one = πr² × 2.2 + (4/3)πr³ = (22/7)(1.96)(2.2) + (4/3)(22/7)(2.744)
    ≈ 13.55 + 11.50 = 25.05 cm³
45 of them ≈ 1127.3 cm³;  syrup = 30% ≈ 338 cm³
```

**4. A pen stand: a 15 × 10 × 3.5 cm cuboid with four conical holes (r = 0.5, depth 1.4 cm).**

```
cuboid = 525 cm³
four cones = 4 × (1/3)(22/7)(0.25)(1.4) ≈ 1.47 cm³
wood = 525 − 1.47 = 523.53 cm³
```

**5. An inverted cone (height 8 cm, radius 5 cm) full of water; lead shots of radius 0.5 cm make ¼ of the water overflow.** The shots' total volume = the water that flowed out.

```
cone = (1/3)(22/7)(25)(8) = 4400/21 cm³;   ¼ of it = 1100/21
one shot = (4/3)(22/7)(0.5)³ = 11/21 cm³
number = (1100/21) ÷ (11/21) = 100
```

**6. An iron pole: a cylinder (h = 220 cm, diameter 24 cm) topped by a cylinder (h = 60 cm, r = 8 cm); 1 cm³ weighs 8 g (π = 3.14).**

```
3.14 × 12² × 220 + 3.14 × 8² × 60 = 99475.2 + 12057.6 = 111532.8 cm³
mass = 111532.8 × 8 g = 892262.4 g ≈ 892.26 kg
```

**7. A cone (h = 120 cm, r = 60 cm) on a hemisphere (r = 60 cm) placed in a full cylinder (r = 60 cm, h = 180 cm). Water left?** The solid pushes out its own volume of water.

```
cylinder   = π × 60² × 180 = 648000π
cone       = (1/3)π × 60² × 120 = 144000π
hemisphere = (2/3)π × 60³ = 144000π
left = 648000π − 288000π = 360000π cm³ ≈ 1131428 cm³ ≈ 1.131 m³
```

**8. A spherical flask (diameter 8.5 cm) with a cylindrical neck (8 cm long, diameter 2 cm). A child says 345 cm³. Correct? (π = 3.14)**

```
sphere = (4/3)(3.14)(4.25)³ ≈ 321.39 cm³
neck   = 3.14 × 1² × 8 = 25.12 cm³
total  ≈ 346.51 cm³          → not quite: she's about 1.5 cm³ short
```

## Problem-solving checklist

1. **Sketch it** and label every part with its own r, h, l.
2. **Find hidden measurements:** cylinder length = total − ends; cone height = total − hemisphere radius; slant l = √(r² + h²).
3. **Surface area:** list only the faces you can see. Watch for glued faces (remove) and rings (add the visible part).
4. **Volume:** add the parts; subtract hollows.
5. **Convert units** at the end (1 m = 100 cm, so 1 m² = 10 000 cm² and 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³; 1 litre = 1000 cm³).

## In a nutshell

- Break a combined solid into **cuboids, cylinders, cones, spheres, hemispheres**.
- **Surface area:** add only the **exposed** surfaces — never just add the parts' TSAs.
- **Volume:** **add** the parts' volumes (subtract anything hollowed out).
- **Cone:** l = √(r² + h²), CSA πrl, V ⅓πr²h. **Hemisphere:** CSA 2πr², TSA 3πr², V ⅔πr³.
- A solid dropped in a full vessel pushes out its **own volume** of liquid.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** don't add TSAs — glued faces vanish · subtract the hemisphere's radius from the total height to get a cylinder's or cone's height · use the SLANT height l for the cone's surface, the vertical height h for its volume · radius = diameter ÷ 2 · a scooped-out hollow ADDS its inside curved surface but REMOVES the flat circle · units: cm² for area, cm³ for volume, and 1 m³ = 10⁶ cm³.
