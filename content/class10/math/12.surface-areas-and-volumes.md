## 12.1 Why this chapter exists

**From Class 9 you know the CUBOID, CONE, CYLINDER and SPHERE, and how to find their surface areas and volumes.**

**But real objects are usually COMBINATIONS:**
- an **oil tanker** on a truck — a **cylinder with two hemispheres** at the ends
- a **test tube** — a **cylinder with one hemisphere**
- many buildings and monuments

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE METHOD OF THE WHOLE CHAPTER: when you meet a new problem, break it into smaller problems you have already solved.**
>
> **Every combination solid is decomposed into basic solids, and the results are combined.** There is not one new formula in this chapter — only a new way of putting old ones together.

## The basic formulas

| Solid | Curved / lateral surface area | Total surface area | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cuboid** (l, b, h) | `2h(l + b)` | `2(lb + bh + hl)` | `lbh` |
| **Cube** (side a) | `4a²` | `6a²` | `a³` |
| **Cylinder** (r, h) | `2πrh` | `2πr(r + h)` | `πr²h` |
| **Cone** (r, h, slant l) | `πrl` | `πr(r + l)` | `(1/3)πr²h` |
| **Sphere** (r) | `4πr²` | `4πr²` | `(4/3)πr³` |
| **Hemisphere** (r) | `2πr²` | `3πr²` | `(2/3)πr³` |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **For a cone, `l² = r² + h²`** — the slant height is the hypotenuse of the right triangle formed by the radius and the height.
>
> **A hemisphere's TOTAL surface area is `3πr²`** — `2πr²` for the curved part **plus** `πr²` for the flat circular face.

## 12.2 Surface area of a combination

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE KEY PRINCIPLE: only the surfaces you can SEE are counted.**
>
> When two solids are joined, **the faces that get stuck together DISAPPEAR from the outside** — so they must not be included.

**The oil tanker — a cylinder with a hemisphere at each end:**
```
TSA of the new solid = CSA of one hemisphere
                     + CSA of the cylinder
                     + CSA of the other hemisphere

                     = 2πr² + 2πrh + 2πr²
```
**The cylinder's two flat circular ends are NOT counted** — they are exactly where the hemispheres are attached, so they are no longer part of the surface.

> [!WARNING]
> **Adding the TOTAL surface areas of the two pieces is the commonest error in this chapter.** That counts the joined faces **twice**, and they should be counted **zero** times.
>
> **The reliable habit: imagine painting the finished object.** Only what the brush can reach gets counted.

> [!TIP]
> **How to set up any surface-area problem:**
> 1. **Identify the basic solids** the object is made of.
> 2. **Find the joining faces** — those disappear.
> 3. **Add up only the EXPOSED surfaces.**
> 4. **Check the radii match** at each join — a hemisphere fits a cylinder only if their radii are equal.

## 12.3 Volume of a combination

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **VOLUMES SIMPLY ADD.**
> ```
> volume of a combination = sum of the volumes of the parts
> ```
> **And where material is REMOVED, subtract:**
> ```
> volume of the remaining solid = volume of the whole − volume removed
> ```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE CONTRAST WORTH HOLDING ONTO: SURFACE AREAS need care about which faces vanish, but VOLUMES never do.**
>
> **Why: joining two solids destroys surface but creates no space and destroys none.** The interior of each piece is still there, so the volumes add without adjustment.

## Worked examples

**A shed is a cuboid 15 m × 7 m × 8 m topped by a half cylinder of diameter 7 m and length 15 m. Find the air it holds; then subtract 300 m³ of machinery and 20 workers occupying 0.08 m³ each.**
```
cuboid:        15 × 7 × 8 = 840 m³

half cylinder: ½ × (22/7) × (7/2)² × 15
             = ½ × (22/7) × 12.25 × 15 = 288.75 m³

total volume = 840 + 288.75 = 1128.75 m³

machinery + workers = 300 + 20(0.08) = 300 + 1.6 = 301.6 m³

air remaining = 1128.75 − 301.6 = 827.15 m³
```

**A juice glass is a cylinder of inner diameter 5 cm and height 10 cm, with a hemispherical bump raised into the base. Find the apparent and actual capacities. (π = 3.14)**
```
APPARENT capacity = πr²h = 3.14 × 2.5² × 10 = 196.25 cm³

the raised hemisphere STEALS volume:
      (2/3)πr³ = (2/3) × 3.14 × 2.5³ = 32.71 cm³

ACTUAL capacity = 196.25 − 32.71 = 163.54 cm³
```

> [!TIP]
> **This is a case where the maths exposes a trick.** The glass **looks** as though it holds 196 cm³ but actually holds only 164 cm³ — **the raised bottom removes about a sixth of the juice** without changing the glass's appearance from outside.

**A toy is a hemisphere topped by a cone of height 2 cm, with base diameter 4 cm. Find its volume, and the difference from the volume of the cylinder that just circumscribes it. (π = 3.14)**
```
r = 2 cm for both the cone and the hemisphere

volume of toy = (2/3)πr³ + (1/3)πr²h
              = (2/3)(3.14)(8) + (1/3)(3.14)(4)(2)
              = 16.75 + 8.37 = 25.12 cm³

the CIRCUMSCRIBING cylinder has
      radius = 2 cm  (the widest part of the toy)
      height = 2 + 2 = 4 cm  (cone height + hemisphere radius)

volume = 3.14 × 4 × 4 = 50.24 cm³

difference = 50.24 − 25.12 = 25.12 cm³
```

> [!TIP]
> **Getting the circumscribing cylinder right is the whole difficulty.** Its **radius** is the radius of the widest cross-section, and its **height** is the total height of the toy — which here means **cone height PLUS hemisphere radius**, not the cone height alone.
>
> **The answer is striking: the toy occupies exactly HALF the cylinder**, and the empty space equals the toy's own volume.

## Working strategy

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **For any problem in this chapter:**
> 1. **DRAW and DECOMPOSE** — name the basic solids involved.
> 2. **List the dimensions of each part**, being careful with **diameter vs radius**.
> 3. **Decide: is this SURFACE AREA (which faces vanish?) or VOLUME (just add or subtract)?**
> 4. **Compute part by part**, keeping the working separate.
> 5. **Combine**, and **check the units** — cm² for areas, cm³ for volumes.

> [!WARNING]
> **The three errors that account for most lost marks:**
> - **Using the DIAMETER as the radius.** Halve it first, every time.
> - **Adding TOTAL surface areas instead of only the exposed ones.**
> - **Mixing units** — convert everything to the same unit before starting, not at the end.

## Summary

```
COMBINATION SOLIDS — break the new problem into problems already solved

basic formulas
   cuboid    CSA 2h(l+b)    TSA 2(lb+bh+hl)    V lbh
   cube      CSA 4a²        TSA 6a²            V a³
   cylinder  CSA 2πrh       TSA 2πr(r+h)       V πr²h
   cone      CSA πrl        TSA πr(r+l)        V (1/3)πr²h      l² = r² + h²
   sphere    SA 4πr²                           V (4/3)πr³
   hemisphere CSA 2πr²      TSA 3πr²           V (2/3)πr³

SURFACE AREA of a combination
   count ONLY the EXPOSED surfaces — joined faces DISAPPEAR
   imagine painting the object: only what the brush reaches counts
   never add the TOTAL surface areas of the pieces

VOLUME of a combination
   volumes simply ADD; subtract anything hollowed out or removed
   no adjustment is ever needed — joining destroys surface, not space

circumscribing cylinder: radius = widest radius,
                         height = TOTAL height of the object

common errors: using diameter as radius · adding total surface areas ·
               mixing units
```
