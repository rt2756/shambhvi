## 8.1 What trigonometry is

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The word comes from the Greek: *tri* (three) + *gon* (sides) + *metron* (measure).**
>
> **TRIGONOMETRY IS THE STUDY OF THE RELATIONSHIPS BETWEEN THE SIDES AND ANGLES OF A TRIANGLE.**

**The earliest known work on trigonometry was recorded in Egypt and Babylon.** Early astronomers used it to find **the distances of stars and planets from the Earth**, and today most advanced methods in engineering and the physical sciences rest on it.

**This chapter defines ratios of the sides of a RIGHT triangle with respect to its ACUTE angles**, works out their values for some specific angles, and establishes the **trigonometric identities**.

## 8.2 The six trigonometric ratios

**In a right triangle ABC, right-angled at B, consider the acute angle A:**

| Side | Relative to ∠A |
|---|---|
| **BC** | the side **OPPOSITE** to ∠A — it faces the angle |
| **AB** | the side **ADJACENT** to ∠A — it is part of the angle |
| **AC** | the **HYPOTENUSE** — always opposite the right angle |

> [!WARNING]
> **"Opposite" and "adjacent" depend on WHICH ANGLE you are looking at.** Switch attention from ∠A to ∠C and BC becomes the adjacent side while AB becomes the opposite one.
>
> **Only the HYPOTENUSE never changes** — it is always the side facing the right angle.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE THREE PRIMARY RATIOS**
> ```
> sin A = (side opposite to A) / hypotenuse            = BC/AC
> cos A = (side adjacent to A) / hypotenuse            = AB/AC
> tan A = (side opposite to A) / (side adjacent to A)  = BC/AB
> ```
> **THE THREE RECIPROCALS**
> ```
> cosec A = 1/sin A = hypotenuse / opposite            = AC/BC
> sec A   = 1/cos A = hypotenuse / adjacent            = AC/AB
> cot A   = 1/tan A = adjacent / opposite              = AB/BC
> ```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Two relations that follow immediately from the definitions:**
> ```
> tan A = sin A / cos A            cot A = cos A / sin A
> ```
> **Why:** `sin A / cos A = (BC/AC) ÷ (AB/AC) = BC/AB = tan A` — **the hypotenuse cancels.**

> [!TIP]
> **Watch which reciprocal goes with which.** It is **cosec** that pairs with **sin**, and **sec** with **cos** — the names cross over, which is exactly what makes them easy to muddle.

> [!NOTE]
> **Where "sine" comes from.** The first use of the idea as we use it today was in the **Aryabhatiyam by ARYABHATA, in 500 CE.** He used **ardha-jya** for the half-chord, shortened over time to **jya** or **jiva**.
>
> When the Aryabhatiyam was translated into Arabic, **jiva** was kept as it was. Translating the Arabic into Latin, **jiva** became **sinus**, meaning *curve* — and **sinus**, or **sine**, became standard in mathematical texts everywhere.
>
> **The modern word is an accident of a chain of translations from an Indian original.**

## 8.3 Ratios of specific angles

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE STANDARD TABLE**
>
> | ∠A | **0°** | **30°** | **45°** | **60°** | **90°** |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | **sin A** | 0 | 1/2 | 1/√2 | √3/2 | 1 |
> | **cos A** | 1 | √3/2 | 1/√2 | 1/2 | 0 |
> | **tan A** | 0 | 1/√3 | 1 | √3 | **not defined** |
> | **cosec A** | **not defined** | 2 | √2 | 2/√3 | 1 |
> | **sec A** | 1 | 2/√3 | √2 | 2 | **not defined** |
> | **cot A** | **not defined** | √3 | 1 | 1/√3 | 0 |

> [!TIP]
> **How to reconstruct the sine row instead of memorising it:**
> ```
> write   0, 1, 2, 3, 4
> divide each by 4:   0, 1/4, 2/4, 3/4, 1
> take the square root:  0, 1/2, 1/√2, √3/2, 1
> ```
> **That is the sin row exactly.** The **cos row is the same values REVERSED**, and **tan = sin ÷ cos**. The bottom three rows are the reciprocals of the top three.
>
> **Reconstructing beats memorising** — you can rebuild the whole table in under a minute and never wonder whether you have sin and cos the wrong way round.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why some entries are "not defined":** they are reciprocals of **zero**.
> ```
> tan 90° = sin 90°/cos 90° = 1/0        — undefined
> cosec 0° = 1/sin 0° = 1/0              — undefined
> cot 0° = cos 0°/sin 0° = 1/0           — undefined
> sec 90° = 1/cos 90° = 1/0              — undefined
> ```
> **Division by zero is not a large number; it is no number at all.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE KEY TREND: as ∠A increases from 0° to 90°,**
> ```
> sin A INCREASES from 0 to 1
> cos A DECREASES from 1 to 0
> ```
> **This gives an instant sanity check.** If a calculation yields `sin A = 1.4`, something is wrong — **sin and cos of an acute angle can never exceed 1**, because the opposite and adjacent sides are always shorter than the hypotenuse.

## 8.4 Trigonometric identities

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **An equation is an IDENTITY when it is true for ALL values of the variables involved.** A **TRIGONOMETRIC IDENTITY** is one involving trigonometric ratios that holds for **all** values of the angle.

**Everything below comes from ONE fact — Pythagoras' theorem in △ABC, right-angled at B:**
```
AB² + BC² = AC²                    …(1)
```

### Identity 1 — divide by AC²

```
AB²/AC² + BC²/AC² = AC²/AC²

(AB/AC)² + (BC/AC)² = 1

(cos A)² + (sin A)² = 1
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> ```
> sin²A + cos²A = 1
> ```
> **True for all A with 0° ≤ A ≤ 90°.**

### Identity 2 — divide by AB²

```
AB²/AB² + BC²/AB² = AC²/AB²

1 + (BC/AB)² = (AC/AB)²
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> ```
> 1 + tan²A = sec²A
> ```
> **True for 0° ≤ A `<` 90°** — **not at 90°, because tan A and sec A are undefined there.**

### Identity 3 — divide by BC²

```
AB²/BC² + BC²/BC² = AC²/BC²

(AB/BC)² + 1 = (AC/BC)²
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> ```
> cot²A + 1 = cosec²A
> ```
> **True for 0° `<` A ≤ 90°** — **not at 0°, because cot A and cosec A are undefined there.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The three identities are ONE theorem seen three ways.**
>
> **Take Pythagoras and divide by the square of each side in turn:**
> ```
> divide by hypotenuse²  →  sin²A + cos²A = 1
> divide by adjacent²    →  1 + tan²A = sec²A
> divide by opposite²    →  cot²A + 1 = cosec²A
> ```
> **You never need to memorise the second and third.** Divide `sin²A + cos²A = 1` by `cos²A` to get the second, and by `sin²A` to get the third — the same two lines you would do anyway.

> [!WARNING]
> **`sin²A` means `(sin A)²`, NOT `sin(A²)`.** The square applies to the ratio, not to the angle.

## Using the identities

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE MAIN USE: if ONE ratio is known, ALL the others can be found.**

**Worked — given `tan A = 1/√3`, find all six ratios.**
```
cot A = 1/tan A = √3

sec²A = 1 + tan²A = 1 + 1/3 = 4/3
sec A = 2/√3          so    cos A = √3/2

sin²A = 1 − cos²A = 1 − 3/4 = 1/4
sin A = 1/2           so    cosec A = 2
```
**(These are the ratios of 30°, as the table confirms.)**

**Worked — express cos A, tan A and sec A in terms of sin A.**
```
cos²A = 1 − sin²A       so      cos A = √(1 − sin²A)

tan A = sin A / cos A   =       sin A / √(1 − sin²A)

sec A = 1 / cos A       =       1 / √(1 − sin²A)
```

> [!TIP]
> **Why the POSITIVE square root only:** we are dealing with **acute angles**, and for an acute angle every ratio is a ratio of positive lengths — so all six are **positive**. The ± that algebra offers is settled by geometry.

## Summary

```
in a right triangle, with respect to an acute angle A
      sin A = opposite/hypotenuse       cosec A = 1/sin A
      cos A = adjacent/hypotenuse       sec A   = 1/cos A
      tan A = opposite/adjacent         cot A   = 1/tan A

      tan A = sin A/cos A               cot A = cos A/sin A

"opposite" and "adjacent" swap when you change which angle you look at;
only the HYPOTENUSE is fixed

THE TABLE
      A        0°     30°     45°     60°     90°
      sin      0      1/2    1/√2    √3/2     1
      cos      1     √3/2    1/√2     1/2     0
      tan      0     1/√3      1      √3    undefined
   rebuild the sin row from 0,1,2,3,4 → ÷4 → square root
   cos row is the sin row REVERSED;  tan = sin/cos
   "not defined" always means a reciprocal of ZERO

   as A goes 0° → 90°:  sin RISES 0 → 1,  cos FALLS 1 → 0
   sin and cos of an acute angle NEVER exceed 1

IDENTITIES — all three are Pythagoras divided three ways
      sin²A + cos²A = 1                 0° ≤ A ≤ 90°
      1 + tan²A = sec²A                 0° ≤ A < 90°
      cot²A + 1 = cosec²A               0° < A ≤ 90°

   divide the first by cos²A to get the second, by sin²A to get the third
   sin²A means (sin A)², never sin(A²)

   knowing ONE ratio determines ALL the others
   for ACUTE angles every ratio is positive, so take the positive root
```
