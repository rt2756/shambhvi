What is the central method of this chapter?

> [!ANSWER]
> **When you meet a new problem, BREAK IT DOWN into smaller problems you have already solved.**
>
> Real objects — an oil tanker, a test tube, a capsule — are **COMBINATIONS** of the basic solids from Class 9. **Decompose the object, use the old formulas on each part, and combine the results.**
>
> **There is not one new formula in this chapter.** Everything is the Class 9 formulas put together in a new way.

---

List the surface area and volume formulas for the basic solids.

> [!ANSWER]
> | Solid | Curved / lateral SA | Total SA | Volume |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **Cuboid** (l, b, h) | `2h(l + b)` | `2(lb + bh + hl)` | `lbh` |
> | **Cube** (side a) | `4a²` | `6a²` | `a³` |
> | **Cylinder** (r, h) | `2πrh` | `2πr(r + h)` | `πr²h` |
> | **Cone** (r, h, slant l) | `πrl` | `πr(r + l)` | `(1/3)πr²h` |
> | **Sphere** (r) | `4πr²` | `4πr²` | `(4/3)πr³` |
> | **Hemisphere** (r) | `2πr²` | `3πr²` | `(2/3)πr³` |
>
> **For a cone, `l² = r² + h²`** — the slant height is the hypotenuse of the triangle formed by the radius and the height.
>
> **A hemisphere's TOTAL surface area is `3πr²`** — `2πr²` curved **plus** `πr²` for the flat circular face.

---

What is the key principle for finding the surface area of a combination?

> [!ANSWER]
> **ONLY THE SURFACES YOU CAN SEE ARE COUNTED.**
>
> **When two solids are joined, the faces that get stuck together DISAPPEAR from the outside** — they must not be included at all.
>
> **The reliable test: imagine PAINTING the finished object.** Only what the brush can reach gets counted.
>
> **The commonest error is adding the TOTAL surface areas of the two pieces** — that counts the joined faces **twice**, when they should be counted **zero** times.

---

Why do volumes never need this care?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because joining two solids destroys SURFACE but creates and destroys no SPACE.**
>
> The interior of each piece is still exactly where it was, so
> ```
> volume of a combination = simply the SUM of the parts' volumes
> ```
> **And where material is removed — a cavity, a depression, a hollowed core — subtract it.**
>
> **This contrast is worth holding onto: surface areas need thought about which faces vanish; volumes never do.**

---

Two cubes of volume 64 cm³ each are joined end to end. Find the surface area of the resulting cuboid.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> each cube:  a³ = 64  →  a = 4 cm
>
> joined end to end, the cuboid is  8 × 4 × 4 cm
>
> SA = 2(lb + bh + hl) = 2(8×4 + 4×4 + 4×8)
>    = 2(32 + 16 + 32) = 2(80) = 160 cm²
> ```
> **Check against the naive answer:** two separate cubes have `2 × 6(16) = 192 cm²`. Joining hides **two faces of 16 cm² each**, and `192 − 32 = 160` ✔

---

A vessel is a hollow hemisphere topped by a hollow cylinder. The hemisphere's diameter is 14 cm and the total height is 13 cm. Find the inner surface area.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> r = 14/2 = 7 cm
>
> the hemisphere contributes 7 cm of the height (its radius),
> so the cylinder's height = 13 − 7 = 6 cm
>
> inner SA = CSA of hemisphere + CSA of cylinder
>          = 2πr² + 2πrh
>          = 2(22/7)(49) + 2(22/7)(7)(6)
>          = 308 + 264 = 572 cm²
> ```
> **The step that is easy to miss: the hemisphere's own depth is its RADIUS**, so it must be subtracted from the total height to get the cylinder's height.

---

A toy is a cone of radius 3.5 cm on a hemisphere of the same radius, with total height 15.5 cm. Find its total surface area.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> hemisphere depth = r = 3.5 cm,  so cone height = 15.5 − 3.5 = 12 cm
>
> slant height:  l = √(12² + 3.5²) = √(144 + 12.25) = √156.25 = 12.5 cm
>
> TSA = CSA of cone + CSA of hemisphere
>     = πrl + 2πr²
>     = (22/7)(3.5)(12.5) + 2(22/7)(12.25)
>     = 137.5 + 77 = 214.5 cm²
> ```
> **Neither flat circular face appears** — they are exactly where the two solids are joined.

---

A cubical block of side 7 cm is surmounted by a hemisphere. Find the greatest possible diameter and the surface area of the solid.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> the hemisphere must fit on a 7 cm square face, so the greatest diameter is 7 cm
>                                                     giving r = 3.5 cm
>
> SA = surface of the cube − the circle covered + curved surface of the hemisphere
>    = 6a² − πr² + 2πr²
>    = 6a² + πr²
>    = 6(49) + (22/7)(12.25)
>    = 294 + 38.5 = 332.5 cm²
> ```
> **Note what happens algebraically: `−πr² + 2πr² = +πr²`.** The circle hidden under the hemisphere is removed, and the hemisphere's curved surface — **twice** as large — is added.

---

A hemispherical depression of diameter l is cut from one face of a cube of edge l. Find the surface area of the remaining solid.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> radius of the depression = l/2
>
> SA = surface of the cube − the circle removed + the curved surface exposed inside
>    = 6l² − π(l/2)² + 2π(l/2)²
>    = 6l² + π(l/2)²
>    = 6l² + πl²/4
>    = (l²/4)(24 + π)
> ```
> **Exactly the same arithmetic as the previous question** — a depression *cut into* the cube exposes the same curved area that a hemisphere *sitting on top* would add. **Hollowing out and mounting on give identical surface areas here.**

---

A medicine capsule is a cylinder with a hemisphere at each end. The whole capsule is 14 mm long and 5 mm in diameter. Find its surface area.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> r = 2.5 mm
>
> the two hemispheres take up 2r = 5 mm of the length,
> so the cylinder's length = 14 − 5 = 9 mm
>
> SA = CSA of cylinder + 2 × CSA of hemisphere
>    = 2πrh + 2(2πr²)
>    = 2(22/7)(2.5)(9) + 4(22/7)(6.25)
>    = 141.43 + 78.57 = 220 mm²
> ```
> **Subtracting BOTH hemisphere radii from the total length is essential** — one at each end, so 5 mm in all.

---

A tent is a cylinder of height 2.1 m and diameter 4 m topped by a cone of slant height 2.8 m. Find the canvas area and its cost at ₹500 per m².

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> r = 2 m
>
> canvas = CSA of cylinder + CSA of cone
>        = 2πrh + πrl
>        = 2(22/7)(2)(2.1) + (22/7)(2)(2.8)
>        = 26.4 + 17.6 = 44 m²
>
> cost = 44 × 500 = ₹22,000
> ```
> **"The base of the tent will not be covered with canvas"** — so the circular floor is excluded, as are the joined faces between cylinder and cone.

---

From a solid cylinder of height 2.4 cm and diameter 1.4 cm, a conical cavity of the same height and diameter is hollowed out. Find the total surface area of what remains.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> r = 0.7 cm, h = 2.4 cm
>
> slant of the cone:  l = √(2.4² + 0.7²) = √(5.76 + 0.49) = √6.25 = 2.5 cm
>
> the remaining solid's surface consists of:
>       the cylinder's CURVED surface     2πrh   = 2(22/7)(0.7)(2.4) = 10.56
>       the cylinder's BOTTOM circle      πr²    = (22/7)(0.49)      =  1.54
>       the cone's CURVED surface (now on the inside)  πrl = (22/7)(0.7)(2.5) = 5.50
>
> TSA = 10.56 + 1.54 + 5.50 = 17.6 ≈ 18 cm²
> ```
> **The top circle is GONE** — that is where the cone was removed — but the cone's curved surface is now **exposed on the inside** and must be added. **The bottom circle survives untouched.**

---

A wooden article is made by scooping a hemisphere from each end of a solid cylinder of height 10 cm and radius 3.5 cm. Find its total surface area.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> TSA = CSA of the cylinder + 2 × CSA of a hemisphere
>     = 2πrh + 2(2πr²)
>     = 2(22/7)(3.5)(10) + 4(22/7)(12.25)
>     = 220 + 154 = 374 cm²
> ```
> **Both flat circular ends have been scooped away**, and each is replaced by the curved inner surface of a hemisphere — **which is twice the area of the circle it replaced.**

---

A solid is a cone standing on a hemisphere, both of radius 1 cm, with the cone's height equal to its radius. Find the volume in terms of π.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> V = volume of hemisphere + volume of cone
>   = (2/3)πr³ + (1/3)πr²h
>   = (2/3)π(1) + (1/3)π(1)(1)
>   = 2π/3 + π/3
>   = π cm³
> ```
> **Volumes simply add — no adjustment for the joined face.**

---

A model is a cylinder with a cone at each end. Its diameter is 3 cm, total length 12 cm, and each cone is 2 cm high. Find the volume of air it contains.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> r = 1.5 cm
>
> the two cones take 2 + 2 = 4 cm of the length,
> so the cylinder's height = 12 − 4 = 8 cm
>
> V = πr²h + 2 × (1/3)πr²h_cone
>   = π(2.25)(8) + (2/3)π(2.25)(2)
>   = 18π + 3π = 21π
>   = 21 × (22/7) = 66 cm³
> ```

---

A gulab jamun is a cylinder with hemispherical ends, 5 cm long and 2.8 cm in diameter. If it is 30% syrup, how much syrup is in 45 of them?

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> r = 1.4 cm
>
> the two hemispheres take 2r = 2.8 cm,
> so the cylinder's length = 5 − 2.8 = 2.2 cm
>
> volume of ONE:
>       cylinder      πr²h        = (22/7)(1.96)(2.2)     = 13.55
>       two hemispheres = a whole sphere:
>                     (4/3)πr³    = (4/3)(22/7)(2.744)    = 11.50
>       total ≈ 25.05 cm³
>
> 45 gulab jamuns:  45 × 25.05 ≈ 1127 cm³
>
> syrup = 30% of 1127 ≈ 338 cm³
> ```
> **Two hemispheres of equal radius make one whole SPHERE**, so `2 × (2/3)πr³ = (4/3)πr³` — a useful simplification whenever a solid has hemispherical ends.

---

A wooden pen stand is a cuboid 15 cm × 10 cm × 3.5 cm with four conical depressions of radius 0.5 cm and depth 1.4 cm. Find the volume of wood.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> cuboid:  15 × 10 × 3.5 = 525 cm³
>
> one conical depression:  (1/3)πr²h = (1/3)(22/7)(0.25)(1.4) = 0.367 cm³
>
> four of them:  4 × 0.367 = 1.47 cm³
>
> wood = 525 − 1.47 = 523.53 cm³
> ```
> **The depressions are REMOVED material, so they are subtracted.** Note how little difference they make — under 0.3% of the block.

---

A conical vessel of height 8 cm and top radius 5 cm is full of water. Dropping in lead shots of radius 0.5 cm makes one-quarter of the water overflow. How many shots were dropped?

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> vessel:  (1/3)πr²h = (1/3)(22/7)(25)(8) = 4400/21 ≈ 209.52 cm³
>
> water displaced = ¼ × 209.52 = 52.38 cm³
>
> one lead shot: (4/3)πr³ = (4/3)(22/7)(0.125) = 0.5238 cm³
>
> number = 52.38 / 0.5238 = 100
> ```
> **The principle: the volume of water that overflows EQUALS the total volume of the shots dropped in.** That is what lets a volume question be turned into a counting question.

---

An iron pole is a cylinder of height 220 cm and base diameter 24 cm topped by a cylinder of height 60 cm and radius 8 cm. Find its mass, given 1 cm³ of iron weighs about 8 g. (π = 3.14)

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> lower cylinder:  r = 12,  V = 3.14 × 144 × 220 = 99,475.2 cm³
> upper cylinder:  r = 8,   V = 3.14 × 64 × 60   = 12,057.6 cm³
>
> total volume = 111,532.8 cm³
>
> mass = 111,532.8 × 8 = 892,262.4 g ≈ 892.26 kg
> ```
> **The diameter is given for the first cylinder and the RADIUS for the second** — a deliberate trap. **Read each dimension's label, every time.**

---

A cone of height 120 cm and radius 60 cm stands on a hemisphere of radius 60 cm, placed in a cylinder of radius 60 cm and height 180 cm full of water. Find the water left.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> cylinder:   πr²h = (22/7)(3600)(180) = 2,036,571.43 cm³
>
> the solid:
>       cone       (1/3)πr²h = (1/3)(22/7)(3600)(120) = 452,571.43
>       hemisphere (2/3)πr³  = (2/3)(22/7)(216000)    = 452,571.43
>       total = 905,142.86 cm³
>
> water left = 2,036,571.43 − 905,142.86 = 1,131,428.57 cm³ ≈ 1.13 m³
> ```
> **A pleasing check: the cone and the hemisphere have EXACTLY equal volumes here**, because the cone's height is twice the radius — `(1/3)r²(2r) = (2/3)r³`.

---

A spherical vessel of diameter 8.5 cm has a cylindrical neck 8 cm long and 2 cm in diameter. A child measures its volume as 345 cm³. Is she right? (π = 3.14)

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> neck:    r = 1 cm,  V = πr²h = 3.14 × 1 × 8 = 25.12 cm³
>
> sphere:  r = 4.25 cm,  V = (4/3)πr³ = (4/3)(3.14)(76.766) ≈ 321.39 cm³
>
> total ≈ 346.51 cm³
> ```
> **She is very close but NOT exactly right — the true volume is about 346.5 cm³, not 345 cm³.**
>
> **A discrepancy of about 0.4% is entirely reasonable for a measurement made by pouring water**, so her method was sound even though her figure is slightly low.

---

A shed is a cuboid 15 m × 7 m × 8 m topped by a half cylinder of diameter 7 m. Find the air it holds after 300 m³ of machinery and 20 workers at 0.08 m³ each.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> cuboid:         15 × 7 × 8 = 840 m³
>
> half cylinder:  ½ × (22/7) × (3.5)² × 15 = ½ × 577.5 = 288.75 m³
>
> total = 1128.75 m³
>
> occupied = 300 + 20(0.08) = 300 + 1.6 = 301.6 m³
>
> air remaining = 1128.75 − 301.6 = 827.15 m³
> ```
> **The half cylinder's LENGTH is 15 m — the length of the shed — not its height.** Identifying which dimension plays which role is the main difficulty in combination problems.

---

A cylindrical glass of inner diameter 5 cm and height 10 cm has a hemispherical bump raised into its base. Find the apparent and actual capacities. (π = 3.14)

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> APPARENT:  πr²h = 3.14 × 2.5² × 10 = 196.25 cm³
>
> the raised hemisphere steals:
>            (2/3)πr³ = (2/3)(3.14)(15.625) = 32.71 cm³
>
> ACTUAL = 196.25 − 32.71 = 163.54 cm³
> ```
> **The maths exposes the trick.** The glass **looks** as though it holds 196 cm³ but really holds only about 164 cm³ — **the raised bottom removes roughly a sixth of the juice** with no change to the glass's outward appearance.

---

A toy is a hemisphere topped by a cone of height 2 cm and base diameter 4 cm. Find its volume and the volume of the space inside the cylinder that just circumscribes it. (π = 3.14)

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> r = 2 cm for both parts
>
> volume of toy = (2/3)πr³ + (1/3)πr²h
>               = (2/3)(3.14)(8) + (1/3)(3.14)(4)(2)
>               = 16.75 + 8.37 = 25.12 cm³
>
> the CIRCUMSCRIBING cylinder:
>       radius = 2 cm       (the widest part of the toy)
>       height = 2 + 2 = 4  (cone height + hemisphere radius)
>
> volume = 3.14 × 4 × 4 = 50.24 cm³
>
> empty space = 50.24 − 25.12 = 25.12 cm³
> ```
> **The toy fills exactly HALF the cylinder**, so the empty space equals the toy's own volume.
>
> **Getting the circumscribing cylinder right is the whole difficulty:** its **height is the TOTAL height of the toy**, which means the cone's height **plus the hemisphere's radius** — not the cone's height alone.

What are the three commonest errors in this chapter, and how do you avoid each?

> [!ANSWER]
> **1. Using the DIAMETER as the radius.**
> **Fix: halve it the moment you read it**, and write `r = …` explicitly before any formula.
>
> **2. Adding the TOTAL surface areas of the pieces.**
> **Fix: imagine painting the object.** Joined faces get no paint, so they get no area. **Ask "which faces have disappeared?" before writing anything down.**
>
> **3. Mixing units.**
> **Fix: convert everything to one unit at the START**, not at the end — and check that areas end in cm² and volumes in cm³.
>
> **A fourth worth naming: forgetting that a hemisphere's DEPTH is its RADIUS.** Whenever a total height is given for a combination, that radius must be subtracted to find the other part's height.
