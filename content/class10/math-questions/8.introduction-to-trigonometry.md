Where does the word "trigonometry" come from, and what does the subject study?

> [!ANSWER]
> **From the Greek: *tri* (three) + *gon* (sides) + *metron* (measure).**
>
> **TRIGONOMETRY IS THE STUDY OF THE RELATIONSHIPS BETWEEN THE SIDES AND ANGLES OF A TRIANGLE.**
>
> **The earliest known work was recorded in Egypt and Babylon**, and early astronomers used it to find **the distances of stars and planets from the Earth** — measurements no tape could ever make.

---

Define the six trigonometric ratios of an acute angle A in a right triangle.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> sin A = opposite / hypotenuse            cosec A = 1/sin A = hypotenuse/opposite
> cos A = adjacent / hypotenuse            sec A   = 1/cos A = hypotenuse/adjacent
> tan A = opposite / adjacent              cot A   = 1/tan A = adjacent/opposite
> ```
> **And two relations that follow immediately:**
> ```
> tan A = sin A / cos A            cot A = cos A / sin A
> ```
> **Why: `sin A/cos A = (opp/hyp) ÷ (adj/hyp) = opp/adj = tan A`** — the hypotenuse cancels.

---

In right triangle ABC (right angle at B), which side is opposite ∠A, and what changes if you consider ∠C instead?

> [!ANSWER]
> **With respect to ∠A:** **BC** is **opposite** (it faces the angle), **AB** is **adjacent** (it is part of the angle), and **AC** is the **hypotenuse**.
>
> **With respect to ∠C the first two SWAP:** **AB** becomes opposite and **BC** becomes adjacent.
>
> **Only the HYPOTENUSE never changes** — it is always the side facing the **right angle**, whichever acute angle you are looking at.
>
> **This is why `sin A = cos C` in a right triangle** — the same side is opposite one angle and adjacent to the other.

---

Which reciprocal goes with which ratio, and why is it easy to get wrong?

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> cosec pairs with sin        sec pairs with cos        cot pairs with tan
> ```
> **The names CROSS OVER:** you might expect "cos" and "cosec" to belong together from their spelling, but **cosec is the reciprocal of SIN**, and **sec is the reciprocal of COS**.
>
> **A memory aid: the reciprocal of `s`in starts with `c`, and the reciprocal of `c`os starts with `s`.**

In △ABC right-angled at B, AB = 24 cm and BC = 7 cm. Find sin A, cos A, sin C and cos C.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> AC = √(24² + 7²) = √(576 + 49) = √625 = 25 cm
>
> for ∠A:  opposite = BC = 7,  adjacent = AB = 24
>       sin A = 7/25        cos A = 24/25
>
> for ∠C:  opposite = AB = 24,  adjacent = BC = 7
>       sin C = 24/25       cos C = 7/25
> ```
> **Notice `sin A = cos C` and `cos A = sin C`** — because the sides swap roles between the two acute angles.

---

In △PQR right-angled at Q, PQ = 12 cm and PR = 13 cm. Find `tan P − cot R`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> QR = √(13² − 12²) = √(169 − 144) = √25 = 5 cm
>
> tan P = opposite/adjacent (for ∠P) = QR/PQ = 5/12
> cot R = adjacent/opposite (for ∠R) = QR/PQ = 5/12
>
> tan P − cot R = 5/12 − 5/12 = 0
> ```
> **The answer is zero, and not by accident:** in any right triangle **`tan P = cot R`** for the two acute angles, because the side that is opposite one is adjacent to the other.

---

If `sin A = 3/4`, find cos A and tan A.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> sin A = opposite/hypotenuse = 3/4, so take opposite = 3, hypotenuse = 4
>
> adjacent = √(4² − 3²) = √(16 − 9) = √7
>
> cos A = √7/4          tan A = 3/√7
> ```
> **Why we may "take" opposite = 3 and hypotenuse = 4: the ratios depend only on the SHAPE, not the size.** Any right triangle with those sides in the ratio 3 : 4 gives the same answers, so choosing the simplest is legitimate.

---

Given `15 cot A = 8`, find sin A and sec A.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> cot A = 8/15 = adjacent/opposite
> so take adjacent = 8, opposite = 15
>
> hypotenuse = √(8² + 15²) = √(64 + 225) = √289 = 17
>
> sin A = 15/17          sec A = 17/8
> ```
> **(8, 15, 17) is a Pythagorean triple worth recognising**, alongside (3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13) and (7, 24, 25).

---

Given `sec θ = 13/12`, find all the other trigonometric ratios.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> sec θ = hypotenuse/adjacent = 13/12
> so hypotenuse = 13, adjacent = 12
>
> opposite = √(13² − 12²) = √(169 − 144) = 5
>
> sin θ = 5/13       cos θ = 12/13       tan θ = 5/12
> cosec θ = 13/5     sec θ = 13/12       cot θ = 12/5
> ```
> **One ratio determines all six** — this is the single most useful fact in the chapter.

---

If ∠A and ∠B are acute and cos A = cos B, show that ∠A = ∠B.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Place the two angles in right triangles ABC and ABD sharing the side AB, right-angled at C and D.**
> ```
> cos A = AC/AB      and      cos B = BD/AB
>
> given cos A = cos B, and the hypotenuses are the same AB,
> so    AC = BD
> ```
> **Now the two triangles have equal hypotenuses and one pair of equal sides**, so by RHS they are congruent, and therefore **∠A = ∠B**.
>
> **The underlying reason: as an acute angle increases from 0° to 90°, cos DECREASES steadily from 1 to 0.** A steadily decreasing quantity never takes the same value twice — so equal cosines force equal angles.

---

If `cot θ = 7/8`, evaluate `(1 + sin θ)(1 − sin θ) / [(1 + cos θ)(1 − cos θ)]`.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Simplify first, using the difference of two squares — do NOT rush to find sin θ and cos θ.**
> ```
> (1 + sin θ)(1 − sin θ) = 1 − sin²θ = cos²θ
> (1 + cos θ)(1 − cos θ) = 1 − cos²θ = sin²θ
>
> so the whole expression = cos²θ/sin²θ = cot²θ = (7/8)² = 49/64
> ```
> **The identity `sin²θ + cos²θ = 1` turns four factors into one ratio in two lines** — and the answer needed only cot θ, which was given.

---

If `3 cot A = 4`, check whether `(1 − tan²A)/(1 + tan²A) = cos²A − sin²A`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> cot A = 4/3, so tan A = 3/4
>
> LHS = (1 − 9/16)/(1 + 9/16) = (7/16)/(25/16) = 7/25
>
> with adjacent = 4, opposite = 3, hypotenuse = 5:
>       cos A = 4/5,  sin A = 3/5
>
> RHS = 16/25 − 9/25 = 7/25
>
> LHS = RHS   ✔  the statement is TRUE
> ```
> **It is in fact an IDENTITY, true for every A** — dividing `cos²A − sin²A` by `cos²A + cos²A`... more directly, divide numerator and denominator of the LHS by `sec²A`.

---

In △ABC right-angled at B with `tan A = 1/√3`, find `sin A cos C + cos A sin C` and `cos A cos C − sin A sin C`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> tan A = 1/√3  →  ∠A = 30°,  and since ∠B = 90°,  ∠C = 60°
>
> (i)  sin A cos C + cos A sin C
>      = (1/2)(1/2) + (√3/2)(√3/2)
>      = 1/4 + 3/4 = 1
>
> (ii) cos A cos C − sin A sin C
>      = (√3/2)(1/2) − (1/2)(√3/2)
>      = 0
> ```
> **The answers are not coincidences.** These expressions are `sin(A + C)` and `cos(A + C)`, and `A + C = 90°` — so they equal `sin 90° = 1` and `cos 90° = 0`.

---

In △PQR right-angled at Q, `PR + QR = 25 cm` and `PQ = 5 cm`. Find sin P, cos P and tan P.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> by Pythagoras:   PR² = PQ² + QR²
>                  PR² − QR² = 25
>                  (PR − QR)(PR + QR) = 25
>                  (PR − QR)(25) = 25
>                  PR − QR = 1
>
> with PR + QR = 25:      PR = 13,  QR = 12
>
> sin P = QR/PR = 12/13
> cos P = PQ/PR = 5/13
> tan P = QR/PQ = 12/5
> ```
> **The clever step is factorising `PR² − QR²` as a difference of squares** — it turns one quadratic relation into a simple linear pair.

---

True or false, with reasons: (i) tan A is always less than 1; (ii) sec A = 12/5 for some A; (iii) cos A abbreviates cosecant; (iv) cot A is cot times A; (v) sin θ = 4/3 for some θ.

> [!ANSWER]
> **(i) FALSE.** `tan 60° = √3 ≈ 1.73`, and tan grows without limit as A approaches 90°. **tan can be any non-negative value.**
>
> **(ii) TRUE.** `sec A = 12/5 = 2.4`, and **sec A is always ≥ 1** since the hypotenuse is the longest side. 2.4 is a perfectly possible value.
>
> **(iii) FALSE.** `cos A` is the **cosine**; the cosecant is **cosec A**.
>
> **(iv) FALSE.** `cot A` is a **single symbol** meaning the cotangent of A — "cot" has no meaning standing alone, so there is nothing to multiply.
>
> **(v) FALSE.** `4/3 > 1`, and **sin θ can never exceed 1** — the opposite side is never longer than the hypotenuse.

Reproduce the table of trigonometric ratios for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°.

> [!ANSWER]
> | ∠A | **0°** | **30°** | **45°** | **60°** | **90°** |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | **sin A** | 0 | 1/2 | 1/√2 | √3/2 | 1 |
> | **cos A** | 1 | √3/2 | 1/√2 | 1/2 | 0 |
> | **tan A** | 0 | 1/√3 | 1 | √3 | **not defined** |
> | **cosec A** | **not defined** | 2 | √2 | 2/√3 | 1 |
> | **sec A** | 1 | 2/√3 | √2 | 2 | **not defined** |
> | **cot A** | **not defined** | √3 | 1 | 1/√3 | 0 |

---

Give a method for reconstructing the table rather than memorising it.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> write down     0,  1,  2,  3,  4
> divide by 4:   0, 1/4, 2/4, 3/4, 1
> take square roots:  0, 1/2, 1/√2, √3/2, 1        ← this is the SIN row
>
> the COS row is the sin row REVERSED:  1, √3/2, 1/√2, 1/2, 0
>
> the TAN row is sin ÷ cos, term by term
>
> the bottom three rows are the reciprocals of the top three
> ```
> **Rebuilding beats memorising:** it takes under a minute, and you can never end up with sin and cos the wrong way round — which is the mistake memorising invites.

---

Why are some entries in the table "not defined"?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because they are reciprocals of ZERO.**
> ```
> tan 90°  = sin 90°/cos 90° = 1/0        undefined
> sec 90°  = 1/cos 90°       = 1/0        undefined
> cosec 0° = 1/sin 0°        = 1/0        undefined
> cot 0°   = cos 0°/sin 0°   = 1/0        undefined
> ```
> **Division by zero is not "a very large number" — it is no number at all.**
>
> **Notice the pattern: the undefined entries occur exactly where the ratio in the denominator is 0** — sin at 0°, cos at 90°.

Evaluate `sin 60° cos 30° + sin 30° cos 60°`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> = (√3/2)(√3/2) + (1/2)(1/2)
> = 3/4 + 1/4
> = 1
> ```
> **(This is `sin(60° + 30°) = sin 90° = 1`, which is why it comes out so neatly.)**

Evaluate `2 tan²45° + cos²30° − sin²60°`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> = 2(1)² + (√3/2)² − (√3/2)²
> = 2 + 3/4 − 3/4
> = 2
> ```
> **`cos 30°` and `sin 60°` are equal**, so the last two terms cancel before you calculate anything — worth spotting first.

---

Evaluate `cos 45° / (sec 30° + cosec 30°)`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> = (1/√2) / (2/√3 + 2)
>
> combine the denominator:  2/√3 + 2 = (2 + 2√3)/√3
>
> = (1/√2) × √3/(2 + 2√3)
> = √3 / [√2 · 2(1 + √3)]
>
> multiply above and below by (√3 − 1), noting (1 + √3)(√3 − 1) = 2:
>
> = √3(√3 − 1) / [2√2 · 2] = (3 − √3)/(4√2)
>
> rationalise:  = (3 − √3)√2/8 = (3√2 − √6)/8
> ```

Evaluate `(5 cos²60° + 4 sec²30° − tan²45°) / (sin²30° + cos²30°)`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> the DENOMINATOR is sin²30° + cos²30° = 1        (the first identity!)
>
> numerator = 5(1/2)² + 4(2/√3)² − 1²
>           = 5(1/4) + 4(4/3) − 1
>           = 5/4 + 16/3 − 1
>           = (15 + 64 − 12)/12
>           = 67/12
>
> answer:  67/12
> ```
> **Spotting that the denominator is the Pythagorean identity saves all the arithmetic there** — it is 1 for **any** angle.

---

Simplify `2 tan 30° / (1 + tan²30°)` and identify it.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> tan 30° = 1/√3
>
> = 2(1/√3) / (1 + 1/3)
> = (2/√3) / (4/3)
> = (2/√3)(3/4)
> = 3/(2√3) = √3/2
>
> = sin 60°
> ```

---

Simplify `2 tan 30° / (1 − tan²30°)` and identify it.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> = (2/√3) / (1 − 1/3)
> = (2/√3) / (2/3)
> = (2/√3)(3/2)
> = 3/√3 = √3
>
> = tan 60°
> ```
> **Compare with the previous question — the ONLY difference is a plus or a minus in the denominator, and the answers are completely different** (`sin 60°` against `tan 60°`). **Read such expressions carefully.**

Simplify `(1 − tan²45°)/(1 + tan²45°)`.

For what value of A is `sin 2A = 2 sin A` true?

> [!ANSWER]
> **A = 0°.**
> ```
> at A = 0°:  sin 0° = 0  and  2 sin 0° = 0        ✔
>
> at A = 30°: sin 60° = √3/2 ≈ 0.866  but  2 sin 30° = 1        ✘
> at A = 45°: sin 90° = 1             but  2 sin 45° = √2 ≈ 1.41 ✘
> ```
> **The general lesson: `sin 2A ≠ 2 sin A`.** The "sin" is not a multiplier you can distribute — it is a function, and doubling the **angle** is not the same as doubling the **ratio**. **A = 0° works only because 0 doubled is still 0.**

If `tan(A + B) = √3` and `tan(A − B) = 1/√3`, with `0° < A + B ≤ 90°` and `A > B`, find A and B.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> tan(A + B) = √3   →   A + B = 60°
> tan(A − B) = 1/√3 →   A − B = 30°
>
> adding:      2A = 90°   →   A = 45°
> subtracting: 2B = 30°   →   B = 15°
> ```
> **Check: A > B ✔ and A + B = 60° ≤ 90° ✔**

True or false, with reasons: (i) sin(A + B) = sin A + sin B; (ii) sin θ increases as θ increases; (iii) cos θ increases as θ increases; (iv) sin θ = cos θ for all θ; (v) cot A is undefined at A = 0°.

> [!ANSWER]
> **(i) FALSE.** Take A = B = 30°: `sin 60° = √3/2 ≈ 0.866` but `sin 30° + sin 30° = 1`. **"sin" is not a multiplier and does not distribute over addition.**
>
> **(ii) TRUE.** As θ goes from 0° to 90°, **sin θ increases steadily from 0 to 1.**
>
> **(iii) FALSE.** **cos θ DECREASES from 1 to 0** over the same range.
>
> **(iv) FALSE.** `sin θ = cos θ` holds at **θ = 45° only**, where both equal 1/√2.
>
> **(v) TRUE.** `cot 0° = cos 0°/sin 0° = 1/0`, which is **not defined**.

---

Derive the three trigonometric identities from Pythagoras' theorem.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Start with `AB² + BC² = AC²` in △ABC right-angled at B, and divide by the square of each side in turn.**
> ```
> divide by AC² (the HYPOTENUSE squared):
>       (AB/AC)² + (BC/AC)² = 1
>       cos²A + sin²A = 1
>
> divide by AB² (the ADJACENT squared):
>       1 + (BC/AB)² = (AC/AB)²
>       1 + tan²A = sec²A
>
> divide by BC² (the OPPOSITE squared):
>       (AB/BC)² + 1 = (AC/BC)²
>       cot²A + 1 = cosec²A
> ```
> **All three are ONE theorem seen three ways** — which is why you need only remember Pythagoras and which side you divided by.

---

State the valid range of each identity and explain the restrictions.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> sin²A + cos²A = 1          for  0° ≤ A ≤ 90°     — no restriction
> 1 + tan²A = sec²A          for  0° ≤ A <  90°    — excludes 90°
> cot²A + 1 = cosec²A        for  0° <  A ≤ 90°    — excludes 0°
> ```
> **The restrictions come from the UNDEFINED entries in the table.**
> - `tan A` and `sec A` are **undefined at 90°**, so the second identity cannot even be stated there.
> - `cot A` and `cosec A` are **undefined at 0°**, so the third cannot be stated there.
>
> **An identity must be true for all values it is DEFINED for** — and where an expression has no value, the question of truth does not arise.

---

How can you get the second and third identities from the first, without going back to Pythagoras?

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> start from   sin²A + cos²A = 1
>
> divide throughout by cos²A:
>       sin²A/cos²A + 1 = 1/cos²A
>       tan²A + 1 = sec²A                    ✔
>
> divide throughout by sin²A:
>       1 + cos²A/sin²A = 1/sin²A
>       1 + cot²A = cosec²A                  ✔
> ```
> **So you only ever need to remember ONE identity.** The other two are two lines away, using `tan = sin/cos`, `sec = 1/cos` and `cot = cos/sin`.

---

Express sin A, sec A and tan A in terms of cot A.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> tan A = 1/cot A
>
> cosec²A = 1 + cot²A
> so  cosec A = √(1 + cot²A)     and     sin A = 1/√(1 + cot²A)
>
> sec²A = 1 + tan²A = 1 + 1/cot²A = (cot²A + 1)/cot²A
> so  sec A = √(1 + cot²A) / cot A
> ```
> **Positive roots only, since the angle is acute and every ratio is then positive.**

---

Write all the other trigonometric ratios in terms of sec A.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> cos A   = 1/sec A
>
> tan²A   = sec²A − 1      so    tan A = √(sec²A − 1)
>
> sin A   = tan A · cos A  =    √(sec²A − 1) / sec A
>
> cosec A = 1/sin A        =    sec A / √(sec²A − 1)
>
> cot A   = 1/tan A        =    1 / √(sec²A − 1)
> ```

Evaluate `9 sec²A − 9 tan²A`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> = 9(sec²A − tan²A)
> = 9(1)                        since sec²A − tan²A = 1
> = 9
> ```
> **The answer does not depend on A at all** — it is 9 for every angle, which is exactly what an identity guarantees.

---

Simplify `(sec A + tan A)(1 − sin A)`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> = (1/cos A + sin A/cos A)(1 − sin A)
> = [(1 + sin A)/cos A](1 − sin A)
> = (1 + sin A)(1 − sin A)/cos A
> = (1 − sin²A)/cos A
> = cos²A/cos A
> = cos A
> ```
> **The strategy that works nearly always: convert everything to sin and cos, then simplify.**

---

Simplify `(1 + tan²A)/(1 + cot²A)`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> = sec²A / cosec²A
> = (1/cos²A) ÷ (1/sin²A)
> = sin²A/cos²A
> = tan²A
> ```
> **Recognising the numerator and denominator as `sec²A` and `cosec²A` turns four terms into two in one step.**

---

Simplify `(1 + tan θ + sec θ)(1 + cot θ − cosec θ)`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> = (1 + sinθ/cosθ + 1/cosθ)(1 + cosθ/sinθ − 1/sinθ)
>
> = [(cosθ + sinθ + 1)/cosθ] · [(sinθ + cosθ − 1)/sinθ]
>
> = [(sinθ + cosθ) + 1][(sinθ + cosθ) − 1] / (sinθ cosθ)
>
> = [(sinθ + cosθ)² − 1] / (sinθ cosθ)                 (difference of squares)
>
> = [sin²θ + 2 sinθ cosθ + cos²θ − 1] / (sinθ cosθ)
>
> = [1 + 2 sinθ cosθ − 1] / (sinθ cosθ)
>
> = 2 sinθ cosθ / (sinθ cosθ) = 2
> ```
> **Quick check at θ = 45°:** `(1 + 1 + √2)(1 + 1 − √2) = (2 + √2)(2 − √2) = 4 − 2 = 2` ✔

---

Prove that `(cosec θ − cot θ)² = (1 − cos θ)/(1 + cos θ)`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> LHS = (1/sinθ − cosθ/sinθ)²
>     = [(1 − cosθ)/sinθ]²
>     = (1 − cosθ)² / sin²θ
>     = (1 − cosθ)² / (1 − cos²θ)                 using sin²θ = 1 − cos²θ
>     = (1 − cosθ)² / [(1 − cosθ)(1 + cosθ)]      difference of squares
>     = (1 − cosθ)/(1 + cosθ)
>     = RHS      ∎
> ```
> **The pivotal move is writing `sin²θ` as `1 − cos²θ` and then FACTORISING it** — that produces the `(1 − cos θ)` which cancels.

---

Prove that `cos A/(1 + sin A) + (1 + sin A)/cos A = 2 sec A`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> LHS = [cos²A + (1 + sinA)²] / [(1 + sinA) cosA]
>
>     = [cos²A + 1 + 2sinA + sin²A] / [(1 + sinA) cosA]
>
>     = [1 + 1 + 2sinA] / [(1 + sinA) cosA]         since sin²A + cos²A = 1
>
>     = [2(1 + sinA)] / [(1 + sinA) cosA]
>
>     = 2/cosA = 2 sec A          ∎
> ```
> **The `(1 + sin A)` cancels top and bottom** — which is what makes this identity work out so cleanly.

---

Prove that `√[(1 + sin A)/(1 − sin A)] = sec A + tan A`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> multiply inside the root by (1 + sinA)/(1 + sinA):
>
> LHS = √[ (1 + sinA)² / ((1 − sinA)(1 + sinA)) ]
>     = √[ (1 + sinA)² / (1 − sin²A) ]
>     = √[ (1 + sinA)² / cos²A ]
>     = (1 + sinA)/cosA                    (positive root; A is acute)
>     = 1/cosA + sinA/cosA
>     = sec A + tan A          ∎
> ```
> **The technique — multiplying by the CONJUGATE to remove the difference from the denominator — is the standard move whenever `1 ± sin` or `1 ± cos` appears under a root.**

---

Prove that `(sin A + cosec A)² + (cos A + sec A)² = 7 + tan²A + cot²A`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> LHS = sin²A + 2 sinA cosecA + cosec²A + cos²A + 2 cosA secA + sec²A
>
> now  sinA · cosecA = 1  and  cosA · secA = 1
>
>     = (sin²A + cos²A) + 2 + 2 + cosec²A + sec²A
>     = 1 + 4 + (1 + cot²A) + (1 + tan²A)
>     = 1 + 4 + 1 + 1 + cot²A + tan²A
>     = 7 + tan²A + cot²A          ∎
> ```
> **The two reciprocal products give the 2 + 2**, and the three identities supply the rest of the 7. **Expanding fully first, then substituting, is the reliable order.**

---

A student writes `sin²A` and reads it as "sine of A squared". Correct them.

> [!ANSWER]
> **`sin²A` means `(sin A)²` — the RATIO is squared, not the angle.**
>
> **`sin(A²)` would mean the sine of the squared angle**, which is something entirely different and almost never what is intended.
>
> **Check with a number:**
> ```
> sin²30° = (1/2)² = 1/4
> sin(30²) = sin 900°, which is not even in the acute range
> ```
> **The notation is a convention, but a strict one:** the exponent sits between the function name and the angle precisely to show it applies to the whole ratio.
