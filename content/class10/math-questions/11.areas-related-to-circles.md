Distinguish a sector from a segment.

> [!ANSWER]
> **A SECTOR is the part of a circular region enclosed by TWO RADII and the corresponding arc.**
> **A SEGMENT is the part enclosed between a CHORD and the corresponding arc.**
>
> **The quick distinction: a SECTOR is cut by two RADII (like a slice of pizza) and always TOUCHES THE CENTRE. A SEGMENT is cut by a CHORD (like a piece sliced off the side of a roti) and never touches the centre.**
>
> **Both come in two sizes** — minor and major. **By convention, "sector" and "segment" mean the MINOR ones unless stated otherwise**, and the major sector's angle is `360° − θ`.

---

Derive the formula for the area of a sector.

> [!ANSWER]
> **By the UNITARY METHOD:**
> ```
> the whole circle is a sector of angle 360°, with area πr²
>
> for an angle of 1°:      πr²/360
>
> for an angle of θ°:      (θ/360) × πr²
> ```
> ```
> area of a sector of angle θ  =  (θ/360) × πr²
> ```

---

Derive the formula for the length of an arc, and say what the two formulas have in common.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> the whole circle (360°) has circumference 2πr
>
> for an angle of θ°:      (θ/360) × 2πr
> ```
> **What they share: `(θ/360)` is simply the FRACTION of the whole circle you are taking.**
>
> **Take that fraction of the AREA to get the sector's area; take it of the CIRCUMFERENCE to get the arc's length.** There is nothing else to remember.
>
> **Check it: θ = 360 gives the whole circle, θ = 180 gives exactly half of each, θ = 90 gives a quarter.** ✔

---

State the formula for the area of a segment, and explain why it works.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> area of a segment = area of the corresponding SECTOR − area of the TRIANGLE
>                   = (θ/360) × πr² − area of △OAB
> ```
> **Why: the SECTOR consists of the SEGMENT plus the TRIANGLE** formed by the two radii and the chord. **Remove the triangle and what remains is exactly the segment.**
>
> **Draw it once and you never need to memorise it** — the picture does the work.

---

How do you find the area of the triangle OAB in a segment problem?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Drop `OM ⊥ AB` from the centre.**
> ```
> OA = OB (radii), so by RHS congruence △AMO ≅ △BMO
>
> therefore M is the MIDPOINT of AB, and ∠AOM = ∠BOM = θ/2
>
> OM = r cos(θ/2)        AM = r sin(θ/2)        AB = 2r sin(θ/2)
>
> area of △OAB = ½ × AB × OM
> ```
> **The perpendicular from the centre bisects BOTH the chord and the angle** — which turns an awkward isosceles triangle into two right triangles, handled by the ratios of Chapter 8.
>
> **Two special angles to recognise at once:**
> ```
> θ = 90°:  the triangle is right-angled at O, so its area is  ½ r²
> θ = 60°:  the triangle is EQUILATERAL, so its area is  (√3/4) r²
> ```

---

Why is the triangle equilateral when θ = 60°?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because two sides are radii, so they are EQUAL — making the triangle isosceles — and the angle between them is 60°.**
>
> In an isosceles triangle the two base angles are equal, so each is `(180° − 60°)/2 = 60°`. **All three angles are 60°, so the triangle is EQUILATERAL** and the chord equals the radius.
>
> **This saves real work:** instead of computing sines and cosines, you use `area = (√3/4) r²` directly.

---

Find the area of a sector of radius 6 cm and angle 60°.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> area = (60/360) × (22/7) × 6²
>      = (1/6) × (22/7) × 36
>      = (22 × 6)/7
>      = 132/7 ≈ 18.86 cm²
> ```

---

Find the area of a quadrant of a circle whose circumference is 22 cm.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> first find r:   2πr = 22
>                 2 × (22/7) × r = 22
>                 r = 7/2 = 3.5 cm
>
> a QUADRANT is a sector of 90°, i.e. one quarter:
>
> area = (1/4) × (22/7) × (3.5)²
>      = (1/4) × (22/7) × 12.25
>      = 9.625 cm²
> ```
> **The circumference had to be converted to a radius first** — the area formulas take r, never the circumference.

---

The minute hand of a clock is 14 cm long. Find the area it sweeps in 5 minutes.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> the minute hand sweeps 360° in 60 minutes,
> so in 5 minutes it sweeps  (5/60) × 360° = 30°
>
> area = (30/360) × (22/7) × 14²
>      = (1/12) × (22/7) × 196
>      = (1/12) × 616
>      = 154/3 ≈ 51.33 cm²
> ```
> **The hand's length IS the radius**, and converting minutes to degrees is the only extra step: **each minute is 6°.**

---

A chord of a circle of radius 10 cm subtends a right angle at the centre. Find the minor segment and the major sector. (π = 3.14)

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> SECTOR:    (90/360) × 3.14 × 100 = (1/4) × 314 = 78.5 cm²
>
> TRIANGLE:  right-angled at O with both legs = 10
>            area = ½ × 10 × 10 = 50 cm²
>
> MINOR SEGMENT = 78.5 − 50 = 28.5 cm²
>
> MAJOR SECTOR = (270/360) × 3.14 × 100 = (3/4) × 314 = 235.5 cm²
> ```
> **For θ = 90° the triangle is right-angled at the CENTRE, so its two legs are both radii** — no trigonometry needed at all.

---

In a circle of radius 21 cm an arc subtends 60° at the centre. Find the arc's length, the sector's area and the segment's area.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> (i) arc = (60/360) × 2 × (22/7) × 21
>         = (1/6) × 132 = 22 cm
>
> (ii) sector = (60/360) × (22/7) × 441
>             = (1/6) × 1386 = 231 cm²
>
> (iii) the triangle is EQUILATERAL with side 21:
>       area = (√3/4) × 441 = (441√3)/4 cm²
>
>       segment = 231 − (441√3)/4 cm²  ≈ 231 − 190.95 ≈ 40.05 cm²
> ```

---

A chord of a circle of radius 15 cm subtends 60° at the centre. Find the minor and major segments. (π = 3.14, √3 = 1.73)

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> SECTOR   = (60/360) × 3.14 × 225 = (1/6) × 706.5 = 117.75 cm²
>
> TRIANGLE (equilateral, side 15)
>          = (1.73/4) × 225 = 97.3125 cm²
>
> MINOR SEGMENT = 117.75 − 97.3125 ≈ 20.44 cm²
>
> MAJOR SEGMENT = πr² − minor segment
>               = 706.5 − 20.44 ≈ 686.06 cm²
> ```
> **Computing the major segment as `whole circle − minor segment` avoids repeating the whole calculation** for the 300° sector.

---

A chord of a circle of radius 12 cm subtends 120° at the centre. Find the segment's area. (π = 3.14, √3 = 1.73)

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> SECTOR = (120/360) × 3.14 × 144 = (1/3) × 452.16 = 150.72 cm²
>
> TRIANGLE:  half-angle = 60°
>       OM = 12 cos 60° = 6 cm
>       AM = 12 sin 60° = 6√3 cm,  so AB = 12√3 cm
>       area = ½ × 12√3 × 6 = 36√3 = 36 × 1.73 = 62.28 cm²
>
> SEGMENT = 150.72 − 62.28 = 88.44 cm²
> ```
> **With θ = 120° the triangle is obtuse at O**, so the perpendicular OM falls inside and the half-angle method is needed — the equilateral shortcut does not apply here.

---

A horse is tied by a 5 m rope to a peg at one corner of a square field of side 15 m. Find the grazing area, and the increase if the rope were 10 m. (π = 3.14)

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> at a CORNER of a square the horse can sweep a QUARTER circle (90°)
>
> with 5 m:   (1/4) × 3.14 × 25 = 19.625 m²
>
> with 10 m:  (1/4) × 3.14 × 100 = 78.5 m²
>
> increase = 78.5 − 19.625 = 58.875 m²
> ```
> **Why a quarter and not a half: the field's two sides at the corner form a 90° angle**, and the horse cannot graze outside the field.
>
> **Note that doubling the rope QUADRUPLES the area** — area depends on r², so the increase is far more than double.

A brooch is a circle of diameter 35 mm with 5 diameters dividing it into 10 equal sectors. Find the total wire length and the area of each sector.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> r = 35/2 = 17.5 mm
>
> (i) wire = circumference + 5 diameters
>          = 2 × (22/7) × 17.5 + 5 × 35
>          = 110 + 175 = 285 mm
>
> (ii) 10 equal sectors, so each has angle 360/10 = 36°
>
>      area = (36/360) × (22/7) × (17.5)²
>           = (1/10) × (22/7) × 306.25
>           = (1/10) × 962.5 = 96.25 mm²
> ```
> **Don't forget the 5 diameters in the wire total** — the circle alone is only 110 mm of the 285 mm needed.

---

An umbrella has 8 equally spaced ribs and is treated as a flat circle of radius 45 cm. Find the area between two consecutive ribs.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 8 ribs divide the circle into 8 equal sectors, each of angle 360/8 = 45°
>
> area = (45/360) × (22/7) × 45²
>      = (1/8) × (22/7) × 2025
>      = 22275/28 ≈ 795.54 cm²
> ```

---

A car has two non-overlapping wipers, each with a 25 cm blade sweeping through 115°. Find the total area cleaned per sweep.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> area cleaned by ONE wiper = (115/360) × (22/7) × 25²
>                           = (115/360) × (22/7) × 625
>                           = 158125/252 ≈ 627.48 cm²
>
> total for TWO wipers = 2 × 627.48 ≈ 1254.96 cm²
> ```
> **"Do not overlap" is essential** — it is what allows the two areas simply to be added, with no double-counting.

A lighthouse spreads red light over a sector of angle 80° to a distance of 16.5 km. Find the area warned. (π = 3.14)

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> area = (80/360) × 3.14 × (16.5)²
>      = (2/9) × 3.14 × 272.25
>      = (2/9) × 854.865
>      ≈ 189.97 km²
> ```

---

A round table cover of radius 28 cm has six equal segment-shaped designs. Find the cost of making them at ₹0.35 per cm². (√3 = 1.7)

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> six equal designs, so each corresponds to an angle of 360/6 = 60°
>
> SECTOR   = (60/360) × (22/7) × 784 = (1/6) × 2464 = 410.67 cm²
>
> TRIANGLE (equilateral, side 28)
>          = (1.7/4) × 784 = 333.2 cm²
>
> ONE design (segment) = 410.67 − 333.2 = 77.47 cm²
>
> SIX designs = 6 × 77.47 = 464.8 cm²
>
> cost = 464.8 × 0.35 ≈ ₹162.68
> ```
> **The designs are SEGMENTS, not sectors** — which is why the triangle must be subtracted. Reading the figure correctly is the whole difficulty.

---

The area of a sector of angle p degrees in a circle of radius R is: (A) (p/180)·2πR; (B) (p/180)·πR²; (C) (p/360)·2πR; (D) (p/720)·2πR².

> [!ANSWER]
> **(D).**
> ```
> (p/720) × 2πR² = (p × 2πR²)/720 = (p/360) × πR²        ✔
> ```
> **Why the others are wrong:**
> - **(A) and (C)** have `2πR`, not `R²` — those are **arc LENGTHS**, not areas. **(C) is in fact the correct arc-length formula.**
> - **(B)** uses 180 instead of 360, giving **twice** the correct area.
>
> **A quick way to check any such option: put p = 360 and see whether you get πR²** (for an area) or `2πR` (for a length).

---

A question gives a radius of 21 cm and does not state a value of π. Which value should you use, and why?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Use π = 22/7, unless the question says otherwise.**
>
> **Why here in particular: 21 is a multiple of 7**, so `(22/7) × 21²` cancels cleanly and leaves whole numbers — the question was designed for it.
>
> **Use π = 3.14 only when explicitly told to**, and **never mix the two within one answer** — the results will not agree and neither will be defensible.

---

Why is it better to compute a segment in labelled stages than in one long expression?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because a mistake in one stage becomes invisible inside a single line of algebra.**
>
> **The reliable order:**
> 1. **SECTOR** — one formula, easily checked
> 2. **TRIANGLE** — the part where errors actually happen
> 3. **SUBTRACT**
>
> **Written out separately, each intermediate value can be sanity-checked:** the triangle must be **smaller** than the sector, the segment must be **positive**, and for a small angle the segment should be a **thin sliver**. **None of those checks is possible if everything is combined.**
