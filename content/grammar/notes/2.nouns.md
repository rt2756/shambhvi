# 2. Nouns

*Shows up in: class 5, 6, 7 (countable/uncountable in class 6).*

A **noun** is a **naming word** — the name of a person, place, animal, thing, or idea.
*Riya, Delhi, tiger, pencil, happiness.*

**Test:** can you put *the*, *a*, or *my* in front of it? *the pencil, my happiness* → noun.

## Kinds of nouns

| Kind | Names … | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Proper** | one particular person/place/thing — **always a capital letter** | Riya, Ganga, Monday, India |
| **Common** | any one of a kind | girl, river, day, country |
| **Collective** | a group taken as one unit | a **team** of players, a **herd** of cows, a **flock** of sheep, a **bunch** of keys |
| **Material** | a substance things are made of | gold, wood, cotton, water |
| **Abstract** | an idea, quality or feeling — you can't touch it | honesty, joy, childhood, freedom |

**Why the capital letter:** a proper noun is a *name tag* for exactly one thing. *River* could
be any river; *Ganga* is that one — the capital marks it as a name.

> [!TIP]
> Abstract nouns are often built from other words: *brave → **bravery**, child → **childhood**,
> free → **freedom**, kind → **kindness**, know → **knowledge**, see lesson 19 (suffixes).*

## Countable and uncountable (class 6)

- **Countable:** you can count them, so they have a plural — *one apple, three apples*.
- **Uncountable:** you can't count them one by one — *water, rice, advice, furniture, information, luggage*.
  No plural, no *a/an*: say **some** advice, **a piece of** furniture, **a glass of** water.
- Use **many / few** with countable, **much / little** with uncountable: *many books*, *much water*.

**Why it works:** *rice* is made of grains too small to bother counting, so English treats it as one
mass. To count it you count the container: *two bags of rice*.

> [!WARNING]
> *advices, furnitures, informations, luggages* — all **wrong**. Say *pieces of advice*.

## Singular and plural

| Rule | Examples | Why |
|---|---|---|
| most nouns: **+ s** | book → books | the default |
| ends in **s, sh, ch, x, z**: **+ es** | bus → buses, box → boxes, church → churches | *boxs* can't be said; *-es* adds the extra sound |
| consonant + **y** → **ies** | baby → babies, city → cities | |
| vowel + **y**: just **+ s** | boy → boys, day → days | the vowel already keeps the sound |
| **f / fe → ves** (most) | leaf → leaves, knife → knives, wife → wives | but: roof → roofs, chief → chiefs |
| consonant + **o → oes** (many) | potato → potatoes, hero → heroes | but: photo → photos, piano → pianos |
| changes inside | man → men, foot → feet, tooth → teeth, mouse → mice, child → children | old English forms that survived |
| same in both | sheep, deer, fish, aircraft | |

> [!NOTE]
> Always plural: **scissors, trousers, spectacles, jeans** (two matching halves) — *a pair of scissors*.
> Look plural but take a singular verb: **news, mathematics, physics** — *The news **is** good.*

## Gender

- **Masculine** (boy, king, lion) · **Feminine** (girl, queen, lioness) · **Common** — either (child, teacher, friend, doctor) · **Neuter** — neither (table, book).
- Pairs to know: *actor–actress, host–hostess, prince–princess, nephew–niece, horse–mare, bull–cow, dog–bitch, monk–nun, hero–heroine, lord–lady.*

## Possessive nouns ('s)

- Shows **belonging**: *Riya's bag* = the bag that belongs to Riya.
- Singular → **'s**: *the girl's book* (one girl). Plural ending in s → just **'**: *the girls' books* (many girls).
- Plural not ending in s → **'s**: *children's park, men's shoes*.

**Why it works:** the apostrophe marks the *owner*. Look at the word **before** the apostrophe —
that is who owns it. *girl's* → owner is *girl* (one); *girls'* → owner is *girls* (many).

> [!WARNING]
> **its** (belonging) has **no** apostrophe: *The dog wagged **its** tail.* — *it's* means *it is*.
