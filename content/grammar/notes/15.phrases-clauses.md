# 15. Phrases & clauses

*Shows up in: class 7, 8, 9, 10 (relative clauses in class 10).*

## Phrase vs clause — the one test

- *in the morning* · *a very old man* · *running fast* — **phrases**: no subject + verb pair.
- *when the bell rang* · *who lives next door* · *I am hungry* — **clauses**: each has a **subject + verb**.

**Test:** find a *who/what* doing a verb. If there's a subject-verb pair, it's a clause; if not, a phrase.

## Phrases — named by the job they do

| Phrase | Does the job of | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun phrase | a noun | ***The tall old tree** fell.* |
| Adjective phrase | an adjective (describes a noun) | *a girl **with blue eyes*** (= a blue-eyed girl) |
| Adverb phrase | an adverb (how/when/where) | *He spoke **in a loud voice*** (= loudly) |
| Prepositional phrase | starts with a preposition; acts as adj. or adv. | ***on the table**, **after dinner*** |

## Clauses — main and subordinate

- **Main (independent) clause:** makes sense alone — *I stayed home*.
- **Subordinate (dependent) clause:** begins with a joiner and **cannot** stand alone — *because it rained*.

**Why "subordinate":** it *serves* the main clause, adding a reason, a time, a description — like a helper.

### 1. Noun clause — works as a noun

Replace it with *it/something* and the sentence still works.
- ***What he said** was true.* (*It* was true) — subject
- *I know **that she is honest**.* (I know *it*) — object
- Joiners: **that, what, who, where, when, why, how, whether, if**

### 2. Adjective (relative) clause — describes a noun

Comes **right after** the noun it describes, joined by a **relative pronoun**.
- *The boy **who won the prize** is my cousin.*
- *This is the house **where I was born**.*
- Joiners: **who, whom, whose, which, that, where, when**

**Defining vs non-defining:**
- *My brother **who lives in Pune** is a doctor.* — no commas: I have several brothers; this tells you *which one*.
- *My father, **who lives in Pune**, is a doctor.* — commas: I have only one father; the clause just adds extra info.

**Why the commas:** commas mark "you could remove this and still know who I mean". With *father* you can; with one of several *brothers* you can't.
(*that* is **never** used after a comma.)

### 3. Adverb clause — works as an adverb

Tells **when, where, why, how, on what condition, in spite of what**.
- ***When the rain stopped**, we went out.* (time)
- *She stayed in **because she was ill**.* (reason)
- ***If you work hard**, you will pass.* (condition)
- ***Although it was late**, he kept reading.* (contrast)
- Joiners: **when, while, as, since, because, if, unless, although, so that, where, wherever**

## Same joiner, different clause — look at the job

- *I know **when he will come*** → noun clause (I know *it*: object of *know*).
- *I'll tell him **when he comes*** → adverb clause (tells *when* I'll tell him).
- *I remember the day **when we met*** → adjective clause (describes *the day*).
- *This is the reason **why he left*** → adjective clause (which reason?).
- *The fact **that he lied** upset me* → noun clause: it **is** the fact (fact = that he lied), it doesn't describe it.

## Combining sentences (class 9–10 transformation)

- *This is the boy. He broke the window.* → *This is the boy **who** broke the window.* (adjective clause)
- *He was ill. He did not come.* → ***As** he was ill, he did not come.* (adverb clause)
- *He will come. I am sure of it.* → *I am sure **that** he will come.* (noun clause)
