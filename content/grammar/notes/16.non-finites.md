# 16. Gerunds, infinitives & participles

*Shows up in: class 8 (and in class 9–10 sentence transformation).*

A **finite** verb has a subject and shows tense: *She **swims**. She **swam**.*
A **non-finite** is a verb form that **doesn't show tense** and does another word's job:

| Form | Looks like | Works as | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gerund** | V-**ing** | a **noun** | ***Swimming** is good exercise.* |
| **Infinitive** | **to** + V1 | noun / adjective / adverb | *I want **to swim**.* |
| **Participle** | V-**ing** (present) / **V3** (past) | an **adjective** | *a **swimming** fish* · *a **broken** cup* |

**Why "non-finite":** *finite* means "limited" — limited to a subject and a time. *Swimming is fun* is true
whatever the time and whoever swims, so *swimming* isn't tied (limited) to either.

## Gerund (-ing as a noun)

Test: replace it with *it* or *this* — the sentence still works.
- Subject: ***Reading** improves the mind.* · Object: *I enjoy **reading**.* · After a preposition: *She is good at **drawing**.*

**Verbs followed by a gerund:** *enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, practise, suggest, keep, stop, deny, admit, can't help, look forward to.*
*I **avoid eating** junk food. Would you **mind opening** the door?*

> [!TIP]
> After **every preposition** → gerund: *interested **in learning**, tired **of waiting**, **before leaving**, **without saying***.
> Watch out — **to** is sometimes a preposition: *look forward **to meeting** you, be used **to getting** up early.*

## Infinitive (to + V1)

- As a noun: ***To err** is human.* · As an object: *She wants **to dance**.*
- As purpose (adverb): *He went to the market **to buy** vegetables.* (why did he go?)
- After an adjective: *happy **to help**, easy **to understand**.*
- **Verbs followed by to-infinitive:** *want, decide, hope, plan, promise, refuse, agree, learn, try, need.*

**Bare infinitive** (no *to*) after: **modals** (*can go*), **let, make** (*Let him **go**. She made me **laugh**.*), **had better, would rather**, and **see/hear/watch** + object (*I saw him **leave***).

**Why no *to* after *make* and *let***: these verbs already show the push (*cause* / *allow*), so the second verb comes straight after.

### Meaning changes: stop / remember / forget / try

- *He **stopped smoking*** (gave up the habit) · *He **stopped to smoke*** (paused in order to smoke).
- *I **remember locking** the door* (I locked it — I recall it) · ***Remember to lock** the door* (don't forget to do it).
- *I **tried to lift** it* (made an effort) · *Try **adding** salt* (do it as an experiment).
- *I **regret to inform** you…* (sorry about what I'm about to say) · *I **regret shouting*** (sorry about what I did).

**Why:** the gerund looks **back** at something already happening; the infinitive looks **forward** to something not yet done.

## Participles (verb used as an adjective)

- **Present participle (-ing)** — active, doing it: *a **barking** dog, **boiling** water, **falling** leaves.*
- **Past participle (V3)** — passive, done to it: *a **broken** window, **boiled** eggs, **fallen** leaves.*

**-ing vs -ed feelings:** *The film was **boring*** (it causes boredom) · *I was **bored*** (I feel it).
*an **exciting** match → **excited** fans.*

### Participle phrases — joining two sentences

- *He saw the police. He ran away.* → ***Seeing** the police, he ran away.*
- *She finished her work. She went home.* → ***Having finished** her work, she went home.* (one action complete before the next)
- *He is very weak. He cannot walk.* → *He is **too weak to walk**.* (infinitive)
- If the participle has a different subject, give it one: ***It being** a rainy day, we stayed in.*

> [!WARNING]
> **Dangling participle** — the participle must belong to the subject that follows:
> ✗ *Walking down the road, **a tree** fell on him.* (the tree wasn't walking!)
> ✓ *Walking down the road, **he** was hit by a falling tree.*
