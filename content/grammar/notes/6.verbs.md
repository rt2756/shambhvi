# 6. Verbs — kinds and forms

*Shows up in: class 6, 7 (and underneath every tense, voice and speech lesson after).*

A **verb** is a **doing or being word** — it tells what the subject *does* or *is*.
*She **runs**. He **is** tired. They **have** a dog.* Every sentence needs one.

## Main verbs and helping verbs

- **Main (lexical) verb** carries the meaning: *eat, write, think, go.*
- **Helping (auxiliary) verbs** help the main verb show tense, voice, question or negative:
  - **be:** am, is, are, was, were, be, being, been
  - **have:** has, have, had
  - **do:** do, does, did
  - **modals:** can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to (lesson 8)
- *She **is** writing. They **have** eaten. **Did** you go?*

> [!NOTE]
> *be, have, do* can also be **main** verbs: *She **is** a doctor. I **have** two pens. He **does** his homework.*
> Test: is there another verb after it doing the main work? If not, it's the main verb.

## Transitive and intransitive

- **Transitive:** the action passes to an **object** — ask *"what?" / "whom?"* after the verb and get an answer.
  *She **wrote** a letter.* (wrote what? a letter ✓)
- **Intransitive:** no object needed — *The baby **slept**. Birds **fly**.* (slept what? ✗)
- Some verbs can be both: *He **runs** fast* (intransitive) · *He **runs** a shop* (transitive — runs what? a shop).

**Why it matters:** only transitive verbs can become **passive** (lesson 13) — there has to be an
object to turn into the new subject. *The baby slept* has no passive form.

## Linking verbs

*be, seem, appear, become, look, feel, taste, smell, sound, grow* can **link** the subject to a
word that describes it: *The soup **tastes** salty. She **became** a pilot.*

**Why an adjective follows (not an adverb):** *salty* describes the soup, not the tasting. Compare
*She **tasted** the soup **carefully*** — now *tasted* is an action, so *carefully* (adverb) describes how.

## The three forms (V1 · V2 · V3)

| Base (V1) | Past (V2) | Past participle (V3) | -ing |
|---|---|---|---|
| walk | walked | walked | walking |
| go | went | gone | going |
| write | wrote | written | writing |
| eat | ate | eaten | eating |
| begin | began | begun | beginning |
| see | saw | seen | seeing |
| take | took | taken | taking |
| bring | brought | brought | bringing |
| put | put | put | putting |
| ring | rang | rung | ringing |
| shrink | shrank | shrunk | shrinking |
| catch | caught | caught | catching |
| hear | heard | heard | hearing |
| set · cut · hit | set · cut · hit | set · cut · hit | setting |
| lie (rest) | lay | lain | lying |
| lay (put down) | laid | laid | laying |
| lie (tell an untruth) | lied | lied | lying |

> [!TIP]
> **lie / lay** trap: *lay* always has an object — you lay **something** down (*lay the table, a hen lays eggs*).
> *lie* has none — you just lie there. The trick: the past of *lie* (rest) is *lay*. *Yesterday I **lay** in bed.*

- **Regular** verbs add **-ed** for both V2 and V3. **Irregular** ones change — they must be learnt (they're the oldest, most-used verbs in English, which is why they kept their old forms).
- **V2** stands alone: *She **wrote**.* **V3** always needs a helper: *She **has written**. It **was written**.*

**Why V3 needs a helper:** on its own, *written* doesn't say *when* — the helper (*has / had / was*) carries the tense.

> [!WARNING]
> *I have went* ✗ → *I have **gone*** · *He has wrote* ✗ → *He has **written*** · *She has ate* ✗ → *She has **eaten***.

## Spelling with -ing / -ed

- drop silent **e**: *make → making* · double a single final consonant after a short stressed vowel: *run → running, stop → stopped* · **ie → y**: *lie → lying, die → dying* · consonant + **y → ied**: *cry → cried* (but *play → played*).
