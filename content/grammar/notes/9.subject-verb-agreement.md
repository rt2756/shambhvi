# 9. Subject–verb agreement

*Shows up in: class 7, 8; tested in class 9–10 editing questions.*

- *The dog **barks**.* · *The dogs **bark**.*

The verb must **agree** with its subject in **number**: singular subject → singular verb,
plural subject → plural verb.

**The odd thing:** in the present tense a *singular* verb ends in **-s** (*barks*), while a
*plural* noun ends in -s (*dogs*). **Why:** English keeps one -s per pair — it goes on the noun
*or* the verb, almost never both. *The dog**s** bark* / *The dog bark**s***.

## Rule 1 — find the real subject

Ignore words between the subject and the verb.
- *The **box** of chocolates **is** on the table.* (box is, not chocolates are)
- *One **of** my friends **lives** in Pune.* (one lives)
- *The **teacher**, along with her students, **is** coming.* — *along with, as well as, together with, with, besides* do **not** add to the subject.

**Why:** the phrase *of chocolates* only describes the box. Cross it out and the agreement is obvious.

## Rule 2 — joining subjects

| Joined by | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **and** | plural | *Riya **and** Sam **are** friends.* |
| and — but one person/idea | singular | ***Bread and butter is** my breakfast.* · *The **secretary and treasurer has** come* (one person, both jobs) |
| **or, either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also** | agrees with the **nearer** subject | *Neither the teacher nor the **students were** late.* · *Either you or **he is** wrong.* |

**Why the nearer one:** with *or/nor* only one of them is actually doing it, so the verb simply
matches the one it can "hear" — the closest.

## Rule 3 — words that are always singular

- **each, every, either, neither, everyone, someone, anyone, nobody, everything** → singular.
  *Each of the boys **has** a bag. Everyone **is** here.*
- **Uncountable nouns:** *The furniture **is** new. Information **is** power.*
- **news, mathematics, physics, economics** (subjects/one idea): *The news **is** good.*
- **Amounts as one sum** (money, time, distance): *Ten kilometres **is** a long walk. Five hundred rupees **is** too much.*
- **Titles:** *"Little Women" **is** a great book.*

## Rule 4 — always plural

- **scissors, trousers, spectacles, jeans, cattle, police, people**: *The police **have** arrived. The cattle **are** grazing.*
- **both, few, many, several**: *Both **are** right.*
- But *a pair of* + noun → singular: *A pair of shoes **is** under the bed.*

## Rule 5 — collective nouns

- Acting **as one group** → singular: *The **team is** strong.*
- Members acting **separately** → plural: *The **jury are** divided in their opinions.*

## Rule 6 — tricky starts

- **There is / There are** — the real subject comes *after*: *There **is** a book. There **are** two books.*
- **The number of** + plural → singular (*The number of students **is** 40*); **A number of** (= many) → plural (*A number of students **were** absent*).
- **Many a** + singular noun → singular verb: *Many a man **has** tried.*
- **None** → singular in formal writing, plural is also accepted: *None of the answers **is** right.*
- **who / which / that** take the number of the word they refer to: *She is one of the girls who **sing** well* (girls sing).
