# 4. Adjectives & degrees of comparison

*Shows up in: class 5, 6, 7.*

An **adjective** describes a noun — it tells **what kind, how many, how much, or which one**.
*a **red** kite, **three** kites, **some** water, **this** kite.*

**Test:** ask *what kind of …?* about the noun. *a red kite* → what kind? red.

## Where adjectives sit

- **Before** the noun: *a **tall** tree.* · **After** a linking verb (is, seems, looks, feels, becomes): *The tree is **tall**.*

## Kinds

| Kind | Tells | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Quality | what kind | kind, red, huge, honest |
| Quantity | how much (uncountable) | some, much, little, enough, whole |
| Number | how many (countable) | one, first, few, many, several, every |
| Demonstrative | which one | this, that, these, those |
| Interrogative | asks which | which, what, whose (+ noun) |
| Possessive | whose | my, your, his, her, its, our, their (+ noun) |

## Degrees of comparison

| Positive (one thing) | Comparative (two things) | Superlative (three or more) |
|---|---|---|
| tall | taller | tallest |
| large | larger | largest |
| big | bigger | biggest |
| happy | happier | happiest |
| beautiful | more beautiful | most beautiful |
| good | better | best |
| bad | worse | worst |
| many / much | more | most |
| little | less | least |
| far | farther / further | farthest / furthest |

**The rules — and why:**
- **Short words (1 syllable):** add **-er / -est** — *tall, taller, tallest.* Ends in e → just **-r / -st** (*large*).
- **Short vowel + one consonant:** **double** it — *big → bigger, hot → hotter.* (*Why:* without the double g, *biger* would rhyme with *tiger*.)
- **Ending in consonant + y:** **y → i** — *happy → happier, easy → easiest.*
- **Long words (3+ syllables, most 2-syllable words):** use **more / most** — *more beautiful.* (*Why:* *beautifuller* is hard to say — English adds a separate word instead.)
- **good, bad, many, little** are irregular — learn them as pairs.

- Comparative takes **than**: *Ravi is **taller than** Amit.* Superlative takes **the**: *Ravi is **the tallest** in the class.*

**Why *the* with superlatives:** there is only **one** tallest — and *the* is how English points at the one particular thing.

> [!WARNING]
> **Never double up:** *more taller* ✗, *most tallest* ✗. *-er* already means "more".
> Comparing two: *the **better** of the two* (not *the best of the two*).

## Changing degree without changing meaning

- *Mount Everest is **the highest** peak in the world.*
  = *Mount Everest is **higher than any other** peak.* (comparative)
  = ***No other** peak is **as high as** Mount Everest.* (positive)

**Why "any other":** *higher than any peak* would include Everest itself — and Everest can't be higher than itself.

## few / a few / little / a little

- **a few** (countable) / **a little** (uncountable) = some, *enough* — positive. *I have **a few** friends* (I'm fine).
- **few / little** (no *a*) = almost none — negative. *I have **few** friends* (I'm lonely).

## Order of adjectives

**Opinion → size → age → shape → colour → origin → material → purpose + noun**
*a **lovely little old** **round** **blue** **Chinese** **china** **tea** cup* — natural sounding because
we put the most "personal" judgement furthest from the noun and the most permanent fact nearest to it.
