# 1. Sentences

*Shows up in: class 5, 6 — and every year after.*

## What makes a sentence

- **"The dog."** — not a sentence. You ask: *the dog what?*
- **"Barked loudly."** — not a sentence. You ask: *who barked?*
- **"The dog barked loudly."** — a sentence. Nothing is left hanging.

A **sentence** is a group of words that makes **complete sense**. It starts with a capital
letter and ends with `.` `?` or `!`.

**Why it works:** every complete thought answers two questions — *who or what is it about?*
and *what is said about it?* Take away either answer and the listener is left asking.

## Subject and predicate

- **Subject** = who or what the sentence is about. **Predicate** = what is said about it.
- *[The little girl]* *[sang a song.]* · *[Birds]* *[fly.]* · *[My elder brother]* *[lives in Pune.]*
- To find the subject, ask **"Who?"** or **"What?"** before the verb: *Who sang?* → the little girl.

> [!TIP]
> The subject is not always first. *Under the tree sat **an old man**.* Ask "who sat?" → an old man.
> In commands the subject **you** is hidden: *(You) Shut the door.*

## The four kinds of sentences

| Kind | Job | Ends with | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Assertive** (declarative) | states a fact or opinion | `.` | The sun rises in the east. · I don't like tea. |
| **Interrogative** | asks a question | `?` | Where do you live? · Is it raining? |
| **Imperative** | gives an order, request, advice | `.` (or `!`) | Close the window. · Please sit down. |
| **Exclamatory** | shows strong feeling | `!` | What a lovely day! · How tall he is! |

**Why it works:** the kind is decided by the **job** the sentence does, not by its length.
"Sit!" is one word but gives an order, so it is imperative.

> [!WARNING]
> *I wonder where he went.* is **assertive**, not interrogative — it *states* that I wonder;
> it does not ask you anything. So it ends with a full stop.
> A negative statement (*She is not here.*) is still assertive.

## Affirmative and negative

- **Affirmative:** says yes — *She plays.* **Negative:** says no — *She does not play.*
- Make a negative by adding **not** after the helping verb (is/are/was/do/does/did/has/can …).
  If there is no helping verb, bring in **do/does/did**: *He runs.* → *He **does not run**.*

**Why it works:** English puts *not* after a helping verb. When the sentence has none, *do*
is brought in just to carry the *not* (and the tense) — that is why *runs* drops its *s*:
the *does* has already taken it.

## Simple, compound, complex (class 7 onwards)

- **Simple:** one subject–verb pair. *The bell rang.*
- **Compound:** two simple sentences joined by **and, but, or, so**. *The bell rang **and** we ran out.*
- **Complex:** one main clause + one or more dependent clauses (joined by *because, when, if,
  who, which, that, although* …). *We ran out **when** the bell rang.*

**Why it works:** in a compound sentence both halves can stand alone as sentences. In a complex
sentence one half (*when the bell rang*) cannot — it *depends* on the main clause to make sense.
See lesson 15 for clauses.
