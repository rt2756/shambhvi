# 18. Punctuation

*Shows up in: class 5, 7, 8 (and in every piece of writing after).*

Punctuation marks are the **traffic signals** of writing — they tell the reader where to stop,
pause, and how to read. Change them and the meaning changes:
- *Let's eat, Grandma!* (inviting Grandma) · *Let's eat Grandma!* (eating her!)

## Capital letters

- First word of a sentence · the word **I** · proper nouns (*Riya, Delhi, Monday, July, Diwali, Hindi*) · titles (*The Jungle Book*) · first word inside quotation marks.
- **Not** for seasons (*summer*) or ordinary nouns (*my mother* — but *Mother* when used as a name: *Thank you, Mother*).

## End marks

| Mark | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **.** full stop | statements, commands; abbreviations | *I like mangoes. Dr. Rao* |
| **?** question mark | direct questions only | *Where is he?* (but: *I asked where he was.*) |
| **!** exclamation mark | strong feeling | *What a goal!* |

## Comma ( , ) — a short pause

1. **Lists:** *I bought pens, pencils, erasers and a ruler.*
2. **Before and / but / so** joining two full sentences: *It was late, so we left.*
3. **After an introductory word/phrase/clause:** *However, he came. When the bell rang, we ran out.*
4. **Around extra information:** *My father, who is a doctor, works late.* (lesson 15)
5. **Calling someone (direct address):** *Riya, come here.* · *Thank you, sir.*
6. **Before a quotation:** *She said, "I'm tired."*
7. **Dates & places:** *15 August, 1947* · *Pune, Maharashtra*

**Why commas matter:** *Riya come here* is unclear; *Riya, come here* shows you're *speaking to* Riya.

> [!WARNING]
> **Comma splice:** two full sentences joined by just a comma is wrong. ✗ *It was raining, we stayed home.*
> ✓ *It was raining, **so** we stayed home.* · ✓ *It was raining; we stayed home.* · ✓ *It was raining. We stayed home.*

## Apostrophe ( ' )

1. **Missing letters (contractions):** *do not → don't, it is → it's, I am → I'm, they are → they're, cannot → can't.*
2. **Belonging:** *Riya's book, the girls' room* (lesson 2).

**Why:** the apostrophe sits exactly where letters were removed — *do n**o**t → don't*.

> [!WARNING]
> **it's** = it is · **its** = belonging · **you're** = you are · **your** = belonging · **they're** = they are · **their** = belonging · **there** = place.
> Plurals never take an apostrophe: *mango**es*** (not *mango's*).

## Quotation marks ( " " )

- Exact spoken words: *"I'm hungry," said Ravi.* · *Ravi said, "I'm hungry."*
- The **end mark goes inside** the quotes: *She asked, "Are you coming?"*
- Titles of short works: *the poem "The Road Not Taken"*.
- Quote **inside** a quote → single marks: *She said, "Read 'The Road Not Taken' tonight."*
- Speech split in two: *"Yes," said the teacher, "you may go."* — *you* stays small because it's one sentence.

> [!NOTE]
> Odd contraction to remember: *will not → **won't***. And commas around a name show it's just extra:
> *My sister, Meena, is a nurse* (I have one sister) vs *My sister Meena is a nurse* (I have more than one — this tells you which).

## Other marks

| Mark | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **:** colon | introduces a list or explanation | *Bring three things: a pen, a book and water.* |
| **;** semicolon | joins two closely linked sentences | *I was tired; I went to bed.* |
| **-** hyphen | joins words into one idea | *well-known, ten-year-old, mother-in-law* |
| **—** dash | a sudden break or extra thought | *He was — I think — very angry.* |
| **( )** brackets | extra information | *Delhi (the capital) is crowded.* |
| **…** ellipsis | words left out / trailing off | *I wonder if …* |
