# 7. Tenses

*Shows up in: every class from 5 to 10; tense-filling is a fixed class 9–10 exam question.*

A **tense** tells **when** an action happens — and **how** we see it (finished? going on? a habit?).

## The big idea: 3 times × 4 aspects = 12 tenses

Every tense = **a time** (past / present / future) + **an aspect**:

| Aspect | What it shows | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | a fact, habit, or single complete action | V1 / V2 / will + V1 |
| **Continuous** | an action **in progress** | be + **V-ing** |
| **Perfect** | an action **completed before** a point | have + **V3** |
| **Perfect continuous** | in progress **for a length of time** up to a point | have been + **V-ing** |

**Why this helps:** you never memorise 12 separate formulas. Pick the **helper for the time**
(*is/was/will be*, *has/had/will have*) and the **verb form for the aspect** (-ing or V3).

## The full table (verb: *write*)

| | Past | Present | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | I **wrote** | I **write** / she **writes** | I **will write** |
| **Continuous** | I **was writing** | I **am writing** / they **are writing** | I **will be writing** |
| **Perfect** | I **had written** | I **have written** / she **has written** | I **will have written** |
| **Perfect continuous** | I **had been writing** | I **have been writing** | I **will have been writing** |

## When to use each — with the signal words

**Simple present** — habits, facts, timetables: *She **walks** to school every day. Water **boils** at 100 °C. The train **leaves** at 6.*
- Signals: *always, usually, often, every day, never.* **he/she/it → add -s.**

**Present continuous** — happening **now** or around now: *Look! It **is raining**. I **am reading** a novel these days.*
- Signals: *now, at the moment, look!, listen!, these days.*

**Present perfect** — past action, **result matters now** (time not stated): *I **have lost** my key* (so I can't get in). *She **has** just **left**.*
- Signals: *just, already, yet, ever, never, so far, recently, since, for.*

> [!WARNING]
> Never use present perfect with a **finished past time**: *I have seen him yesterday* ✗ → *I **saw** him yesterday* ✓.
> **Why:** present perfect links the past to *now*; *yesterday* cuts that link and fixes the action in a finished time.

**Present perfect continuous** — started in the past, **still going on**, stressing **how long**: *She **has been studying** for three hours.*

**since vs for:** **since** + a *point* in time (*since Monday, since 2020, since morning*); **for** + a *length* of time (*for two days, for an hour*).

**Simple past** — finished action at a known past time: *We **visited** Agra **last year**.*
- Signals: *yesterday, ago, last week, in 2019, when I was young.*

**Past continuous** — in progress at a past moment, often interrupted: *I **was sleeping** when the phone **rang**.*
- **Why both tenses:** the long action (*was sleeping*) is the background; the short one (*rang*) cuts into it.

**Past perfect** — the **earlier** of two past actions: *The train **had left** before we **reached** the station.*
- **Why:** both are past, so we need a way to say which came first — *had + V3* marks the earlier one.
- *By the time / before / Hardly … when / No sooner … than* all set up two past actions: *Hardly **had** he **sat** down **when** the bell **rang**.*

**Past perfect continuous** — in progress for a length of time **up to a past point**: *She **had been waiting** for an hour when the bus **came**.*

> [!TIP]
> *did / does / do* already carry the tense, so the main verb goes back to **V1**: *Did you **post** it?* · *He did not **go**.*

**Simple future** — *will + V1*: *I **will call** you tomorrow.* Also **going to** for plans/clear signs: *Look at the clouds — it **is going to rain**.*

**Future continuous** — in progress at a future moment: *At 8 pm tomorrow I **will be travelling**.*

**Future perfect** — completed before a future point: *By next June, I **will have finished** the course.* (Signal: **by**.)

## Two rules that catch everyone

1. **if / when / before / after / until** + a future idea → use **simple present**, not *will*:
   *If it **rains** tomorrow, we will stay in.* (not *if it will rain*). **Why:** the *if*-part is a condition, not a prediction — *will* already sits in the main part.
2. **Stative verbs** (know, like, love, want, believe, understand, belong, own) are **not** used in continuous: *I **know** him* (not *I am knowing*). **Why:** they describe states, not actions — a state isn't "in progress", it just is.
