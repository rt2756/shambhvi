# 3. Pronouns

*Shows up in: class 5, 6, 7.*

- *Riya lost Riya's bag, so Riya asked Riya's mother to help Riya.* — clumsy.
- *Riya lost **her** bag, so **she** asked **her** mother to help **her**.* — smooth.

A **pronoun** stands **in place of a noun** so we don't repeat it. *pro-* = "for" → a word *for* a noun.

## Personal pronouns — subject vs object

| | Subject (does the action) | Object (receives it) | Possessive + noun | Possessive alone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st person | I · we | me · us | my · our | mine · ours |
| 2nd person | you | you | your | yours |
| 3rd person | he · she · it · they | him · her · it · them | his · her · its · their | his · hers · — · theirs |

- **Subject** form before the verb: ***She** helped me.* **Object** form after a verb or preposition: *She helped **me**. Come with **us**.*

**Why it works — the cover-up test:** with two people, cover the other one.
*Riya and (I / me) went home* → cover *Riya and* → *I went home* ✓.
*He invited Riya and (I / me)* → *He invited me* ✓.

> [!WARNING]
> *between you and **me*** — not *between you and I*. *between* is a preposition, so it takes the object form.

> [!TIP]
> Polite order: put yourself **last** — *you, he and I* (not *I, you and he*).

## Possessive pronouns

- **my / your / her / our / their** sit **before** a noun: *This is **my** pen.*
- **mine / yours / hers / ours / theirs** stand **alone**: *This pen is **mine**.*
- **None** of them takes an apostrophe: *yours* (not *your's*), *theirs*, *its*.

**Why no apostrophe:** these words are *already* possessive — the apostrophe's whole job is done
inside the word. *it's / they're / you're* are short forms of *it is / they are / you are*.

## Reflexive and emphatic pronouns (-self / -selves)

**myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves**

- **Reflexive:** the action turns back on the subject — *She hurt **herself**.* (Remove it → sentence breaks.)
- **Emphatic:** adds force — *I **myself** saw it.* (Remove it → sentence still works.)

> [!WARNING]
> *hisself, theirselves* are not words. And don't use *myself* as a fancy *me*: *Call Riya or **me*** (not *or myself*).

## Other pronouns

| Kind | Words | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Demonstrative** (points) | this, that, these, those | ***This** is my house.* |
| **Interrogative** (asks) | who, whom, whose, which, what | ***Who** is there?* |
| **Relative** (joins + refers back) | who, whom, whose, which, that | *The boy **who** won is my friend.* |
| **Indefinite** (not one particular) | someone, anyone, everyone, nobody, each, none | ***Everyone** is here.* |

- **who** for people, **which** for things/animals, **that** for either.
- **who** = subject (*who came*), **whom** = object (*whom you met*), **whose** = belonging (*whose bag*).

**Why who/whom:** it's the same as he/him. Answer the question in your head: *Who/Whom did you call?*
→ *I called **him*** → *him* is object → **whom**.

> [!NOTE]
> *this/these* = near, *that/those* = far. *this/that* singular, *these/those* plural.
> When *this* is followed by a noun (*this book*) it is a determiner, not a pronoun — lesson 5.
