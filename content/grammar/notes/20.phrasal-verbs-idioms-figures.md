# 20. Phrasal verbs, idioms & figures of speech

*Shows up in: class 6–10 (phrasal verbs and idioms in class 7–10; simile and metaphor from class 6, more figures in 9–10 poems).*

All three share one idea: **the words mean more than they say**. *Give up* isn't "give" + "up";
*a piece of cake* isn't cake; *the classroom was a zoo* isn't about animals.

## Phrasal verbs — verb + small word = new meaning

| Phrasal verb | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| give up | stop trying / quit | *Never **give up**.* |
| look after | take care of | *She **looks after** her grandmother.* |
| look for | search | *I'm **looking for** my keys.* |
| look up | find in a dictionary/list | ***Look up** the word.* |
| look forward to | wait happily for | *I **look forward to** the trip.* |
| put off | postpone | *They **put off** the match.* |
| put out | extinguish | *They **put out** the fire.* |
| call off | cancel | *The trip was **called off**.* |
| turn down | refuse | *He **turned down** the offer.* |
| run out of | have none left | *We **ran out of** milk.* |
| get along (with) | be friendly | *I **get along with** my cousins.* |
| take after | look/behave like (a parent) | *She **takes after** her mother.* |
| set off | start a journey | *We **set off** at dawn.* |
| break down | stop working; start crying | *The car **broke down**.* |
| bring up | raise a child; mention | *She was **brought up** in Assam.* |
| carry on | continue | ***Carry on** with your work.* |
| come across | find by chance | *I **came across** an old photo.* |
| find out | discover | ***Find out** who did it.* |
| make up | invent (a story); become friends again | *Don't **make up** excuses.* |
| go through | examine carefully; suffer | *She **went through** the papers.* |

**Why you can't guess them:** the small word (*up, off, out*) adds a **direction or completeness** idea.
*Burn **up**, eat **up**, use **up*** = completely; *put **off**, call **off*** = away/cancelled. It helps, but the meanings are fixed by use — learn them in sentences.

## Idioms — fixed phrases with a hidden meaning

| Idiom | Means |
|---|---|
| a piece of cake | very easy |
| break the ice | start a conversation in an awkward situation |
| once in a blue moon | very rarely |
| under the weather | slightly ill |
| hit the nail on the head | say exactly the right thing |
| let the cat out of the bag | reveal a secret |
| cost an arm and a leg | very expensive |
| spill the beans | tell a secret |
| in hot water | in trouble |
| a blessing in disguise | something that seemed bad but turned out good |
| burn the midnight oil | study/work late at night |
| bite off more than you can chew | take on too much |
| see eye to eye | agree |
| when pigs fly | never |
| the apple of one's eye | someone loved very much |
| feel blue | feel sad |

## Figures of speech — the poet's tools

| Figure | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Simile** | compares using **like** or **as** | *as brave as a lion* · *She sings like a nightingale.* |
| **Metaphor** | says one thing **is** another (no like/as) | *The classroom **was a zoo**.* · *Time is money.* |
| **Personification** | gives human qualities to non-human things | *The wind **whispered**.* · *The sun **smiled** on us.* |
| **Alliteration** | repeats the first consonant sound | ***P**eter **P**iper **p**icked …* · *the **w**ild **w**est **w**ind* |
| **Hyperbole** | exaggerates for effect | *I've told you a **million** times.* |
| **Onomatopoeia** | word sounds like what it means | *buzz, hiss, splash, tick-tock* |
| **Oxymoron** | two opposite words together | *sweet sorrow, deafening silence* |
| **Repetition** | repeats words for emphasis | *Water, water, everywhere* |
| **Rhetorical question** | a question that expects no answer | *Who doesn't love holidays?* |

**Simile vs metaphor — why it matters:** a simile *admits* it's a comparison (*like a zoo*); a metaphor *claims*
it really is the thing (*was a zoo*) — so it's bolder and stronger. Test: look for **like / as**.
