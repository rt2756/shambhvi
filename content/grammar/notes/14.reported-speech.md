# 14. Reported speech (direct & indirect)

*Shows up in: class 7, 8, 9, 10; a fixed class 9–10 exam item.*

- **Direct:** Riya said, **"I am tired."** — her exact words, in quotation marks.
- **Indirect (reported):** Riya said **that she was tired.** — you retell it in your own words.

## Why things change: you are telling it later, as someone else

Think of what *must* change when **you** retell **Riya's** words **later**:
1. **Person** — *I* was Riya, so to you she is ***she***.
2. **Tense** — she said it in the past, so the verb usually steps **one step back** in time.
3. **Time & place words** — her *now / here / today* are not your now/here/today.

## Step 1 — the reporting verb and joiner

| Direct sentence is a … | Reporting verb | Joiner |
|---|---|---|
| statement | said, told (+ person) | **that** |
| yes/no question | asked, enquired | **if / whether** |
| wh- question | asked | the **wh-word** itself |
| order / request / advice | ordered, requested, advised, told | **to** + V1 (not to + V1) |
| exclamation | exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise | **that** |

- **said** has no person after it; **told** must have one: *She **said** that … / She **told me** that …* (never *said me*).

## Step 2 — backshift the tense (if the reporting verb is past)

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| am / is → | was |
| are → | were |
| do / does (simple present) → | did (simple past) |
| is doing → | was doing |
| has / have done → | had done |
| did (simple past) → | had done |
| will → | would |
| can → | could |
| may → | might |
| shall → | should |
| must → | had to (or must) |

**No change when:** the reporting verb is **present** (*She **says**, "I am tired"* → *She says that she **is** tired*),
or the words are a **universal truth / habit** (*The teacher said, "The earth is round"* → *…that the earth **is** round*).
**Why:** backshift only happens because time has passed. If the fact is still true for ever, there's nothing to shift.

## Step 3 — time & place words

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today / tonight | that day / that night |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| ago | before |
| here / this / these | there / that / those |
| last week | the week before |

## Questions — turn them back into statement order

- *He said to me, "**Where do you live?**"* → *He asked me **where I lived**.* (no *do*, no **?**)
- *She said, "**Are you ready?**"* → *She asked **if I was ready**.*

**Why statement order:** in indirect speech you're no longer *asking* — you're *reporting* that a question was asked, so it becomes a statement.

## Commands & requests

- *The teacher said, "**Sit down.**"* → *The teacher **ordered** us **to sit** down.*
- *She said, "**Please help me.**"* → *She **requested** me **to help** her.*
- *Mother said, "**Don't** play in the rain."* → *Mother **told** me **not to play** in the rain.*

## Exclamations & greetings

- *He said, "**What a lovely view!**"* → *He **exclaimed that** it was a very lovely view.*
- *She said, "**Alas!** I have failed."* → *She **exclaimed with sorrow** that she had failed.*
- *He said, "**Good morning**, sir."* → *He **wished** his teacher good morning.*
- *She said, "**Thank you.**"* → *She **thanked** me.*
- *The doctor said, "You **should** rest."* → *The doctor **advised** me **to rest**.*
- *He said, "**Let's** go."* → *He **suggested that we should** go.*
- *She said, "**Shall I** help?"* → *She **asked if she should** help.* (an offer)
- *He said, "**May** God bless you!"* → *He **prayed that** God **might** bless me.*
- *He said, "**Well done!**"* → *He **applauded / praised** me (saying that I had done well).*

> [!TIP]
> **Pick the reporting verb from the feeling, not the words.** That's why *Alas!* and *Thank you* disappear —
> *exclaimed with sorrow* and *thanked* already carry their meaning.
