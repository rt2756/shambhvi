## 5.1 Choosing the right unit

| To measure | Sensible unit |
|---|---|
| Height of India Gate (42) | **metres** |
| Length of a handkerchief (40) | **centimetres** |
| Depth of a well (50) | **metres** |
| Length of a mobile phone (13) | **centimetres** |
| Length of an elephant's trunk (2) | **metres** |
| Distance between two shirt buttons (5) | **centimetres** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE RULE FOR CHOOSING A UNIT: pick the one that gives a SENSIBLE-SIZED NUMBER.**
>
> **India Gate is 42 metres — which is also 4,200 centimetres.** Both are correct, but **4,200 is an awkward number to carry around** and 42 is not.
>
> **A handkerchief is 40 centimetres — which is also 0.4 metres.** Again both are true, but 40 is far easier to say and picture.
>
> **So the unit is chosen for CONVENIENCE, not correctness.** Small things in centimetres, room-sized things in metres, journeys in kilometres.

## 5.2 Converting between units

```
1 metre     = 100 centimetres          1 m  = 100 cm
1 kilometre = 1,000 metres             1 km = 1,000 m

"KILO" means THOUSAND
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Going from a BIG unit to a SMALL one, MULTIPLY. Going from a SMALL unit to a BIG one, DIVIDE.**
> ```
> 5 m   → cm:   5 × 100 = 500 cm
> 700 cm → m:   700 ÷ 100 = 7 m
> 3 km  → m:    3 × 1,000 = 3,000 m
> ```
> **Why: a centimetre is SMALLER than a metre, so you need MORE of them to make the same length.**
>
> **The check that never fails: the number should get BIGGER when the unit gets SMALLER.** If your answer went the wrong way, you multiplied when you should have divided.

**Mixed measures — split them at the unit:**
```
5 m 40 cm = 500 cm + 40 cm = 540 cm
2 m  4 cm = 200 cm +  4 cm = 204 cm
2 m 40 cm = 200 cm + 40 cm = 240 cm

and back the other way:
5,792 cm = 5,700 cm + 92 cm = 57 m 92 cm
```

> [!WARNING]
> **2 m 4 cm and 2 m 40 cm are NOT the same** — they are 204 cm and 240 cm.
>
> **The zero matters: 4 cm and 40 cm differ by 36 cm**, which is more than a school ruler.
>
> **When converting, always ask how many HUNDREDS of centimetres you have** — that number is the metres, and the rest is the leftover centimetres.

## 5.3 Comparing lengths

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **YOU CANNOT COMPARE UNTIL BOTH ARE IN THE SAME UNIT.**
> ```
> 456 cm  and  5 m
>    5 m = 500 cm
>    456 < 500   →  456 cm < 5 m
>
> 6 m 5 cm  and  6 m 50 cm
>    605 cm  and  650 cm
>    605 < 650   →  6 m 5 cm < 6 m 50 cm
>
> 238 cm  and  138 cm + 1 m
>    138 cm + 100 cm = 238 cm
>    238 = 238   →  they are EQUAL
> ```
> **Why converting first is not optional: "456" looks bigger than "5" but is not.** Comparing the numbers without the units is exactly the mistake this rule prevents.

> [!TIP]
> **Sometimes you can compare without converting at all.**
> ```
> 55 cm + 200 cm    versus    200 cm + 54 cm
> ```
> **Both have 200 cm, so ignore it and compare 55 with 54.** The left side is bigger.
>
> **Looking for what is the SAME on both sides can save all the arithmetic.**

## 5.4 Kilometres

```
1,000 m = 1 km       "kilo" means thousand
```

| Length of rope | Ropes needed to make 1 km |
|---|---|
| 1,000 m | **1** |
| 500 m | **2** |
| 250 m | **4** |
| 200 m | **5** |
| 100 m | **10** |
| 10 m | **100** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Notice the pattern: the SHORTER the rope, the MORE you need.**
>
> **This is division doing its job:** each answer is 1,000 ÷ (length of rope).
>
> **And it is the same relationship as choosing units — smaller unit, bigger number.** A 10 m rope needs 100 of it; a 1,000 m rope needs just one.

**Working out a 3-km race:**
```
3 km = 3,000 m

WATER STATIONS every 500 m
   3,000 ÷ 500 = 6 stations
   at 500, 1000, 1500, 2000, 2500, 3000 m

CHILDREN standing every 300 m
   3,000 ÷ 300 = 10 children
   at 300, 600, 900, 1200, 1500, 1800, 2100, 2400, 2700, 3000 m

FLAGS every 50 m
   3,000 ÷ 50 = 60 flags in all
   placed alternately red, blue, red, blue …
   → 30 red and 30 blue
```

> [!TIP]
> **The alternating-flags question has a hidden second step.** Dividing gives 60 **flags**, but the question asks for reds and blues separately — **so you must halve again**, giving 30 of each.
>
> **Always re-read what the question actually asked for** after the arithmetic is done.

## 5.5 Measuring long distances

```
LONG TAPE            — for a playground or a field
RODOMETER            — a measuring wheel you push along a road
RANGEFINDER          — measures a distance you cannot walk
LASER DISTANCE METER — for rooms and buildings
```

> [!TIP]
> **A method that needs no equipment at all: MEASURE YOUR OWN STEP.**
>
> **Mark out 100 m with a long tape, walk it, and count your steps.** If it takes 125 steps, then **one step is about 100 ÷ 125 = 0.8 m**.
>
> **Now you can measure any distance by walking it and counting** — because you have turned your own body into a measuring instrument.

   kilometres for journeys

CONVERTING
   BIG unit → SMALL unit : MULTIPLY      5 m = 500 cm
   SMALL unit → BIG unit : DIVIDE        700 cm = 7 m
   the check: the number gets BIGGER when the unit gets SMALLER

MIXED MEASURES — split at the unit
   5 m 40 cm = 500 + 40 = 540 cm
   2 m 4 cm = 204 cm, NOT 240 cm — the zero matters

COMPARING — convert to the SAME unit FIRST
   456 cm < 5 m, because 5 m is 500 cm

   500 m rope → 2 · 250 m rope → 4 · 100 m rope → 10 · 10 m rope → 100

   laser distance meter
   or measure your OWN STEP over a known 100 m, then walk and count
```
