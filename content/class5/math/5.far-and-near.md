## 5.1 Choosing the right unit

| To measure | Sensible unit |
|---|---|
| Height of India Gate (42) | **metres** |
| Length of a handkerchief (40) | **centimetres** |
| Depth of a well (50) | **metres** |
| Length of a mobile phone (13) | **centimetres** |
| Length of an elephant's trunk (2) | **metres** |
| Distance between two shirt buttons (5) | **centimetres** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE RULE FOR CHOOSING A UNIT: pick the one that gives a SENSIBLE-SIZED NUMBER.**
>
> **India Gate is 42 metres — which is also 4,200 centimetres.** Both are correct, but **4,200 is an awkward number to carry around** and 42 is not.
>
> **A handkerchief is 40 centimetres — which is also 0.4 metres.** Again both are true, but 40 is far easier to say and picture.
>
> **So the unit is chosen for CONVENIENCE, not correctness.** Small things in centimetres, room-sized things in metres, journeys in kilometres.

## 5.2 Converting between units

```
1 metre     = 100 centimetres          1 m  = 100 cm
1 kilometre = 1,000 metres             1 km = 1,000 m

"KILO" means THOUSAND
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Going from a BIG unit to a SMALL one, MULTIPLY. Going from a SMALL unit to a BIG one, DIVIDE.**
> ```
> 5 m   → cm:   5 × 100 = 500 cm
> 700 cm → m:   700 ÷ 100 = 7 m
> 3 km  → m:    3 × 1,000 = 3,000 m
> ```
> **Why: a centimetre is SMALLER than a metre, so you need MORE of them to make the same length.**
>
> **The check that never fails: the number should get BIGGER when the unit gets SMALLER.** If your answer went the wrong way, you multiplied when you should have divided.

**Mixed measures — split them at the unit:**
```
5 m 40 cm = 500 cm + 40 cm = 540 cm
2 m  4 cm = 200 cm +  4 cm = 204 cm
2 m 40 cm = 200 cm + 40 cm = 240 cm

and back the other way:
5,792 cm = 5,700 cm + 92 cm = 57 m 92 cm
```

> [!WARNING]
> **2 m 4 cm and 2 m 40 cm are NOT the same** — they are 204 cm and 240 cm.
>
> **The zero matters: 4 cm and 40 cm differ by 36 cm**, which is more than a school ruler.
>
> **When converting, always ask how many HUNDREDS of centimetres you have** — that number is the metres, and the rest is the leftover centimetres.

## 5.3 Comparing lengths

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **YOU CANNOT COMPARE UNTIL BOTH ARE IN THE SAME UNIT.**
> ```
> 456 cm  and  5 m
>    5 m = 500 cm
>    456 < 500   →  456 cm < 5 m
>
> 6 m 5 cm  and  6 m 50 cm
>    605 cm  and  650 cm
>    605 < 650   →  6 m 5 cm < 6 m 50 cm
>
> 238 cm  and  138 cm + 1 m
>    138 cm + 100 cm = 238 cm
>    238 = 238   →  they are EQUAL
> ```
> **Why converting first is not optional: "456" looks bigger than "5" but is not.** Comparing the numbers without the units is exactly the mistake this rule prevents.

> [!TIP]
> **Sometimes you can compare without converting at all.**
> ```
> 55 cm + 200 cm    versus    200 cm + 54 cm
> ```
> **Both have 200 cm, so ignore it and compare 55 with 54.** The left side is bigger.
>
> **Looking for what is the SAME on both sides can save all the arithmetic.**

## 5.4 Kilometres

```
1,000 m = 1 km       "kilo" means thousand
```

| Length of rope | Ropes needed to make 1 km |
|---|---|
| 1,000 m | **1** |
| 500 m | **2** |
| 250 m | **4** |
| 200 m | **5** |
| 100 m | **10** |
| 10 m | **100** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Notice the pattern: the SHORTER the rope, the MORE you need.**
>
> **This is division doing its job:** each answer is 1,000 ÷ (length of rope).
>
> **And it is the same relationship as choosing units — smaller unit, bigger number.** A 10 m rope needs 100 of it; a 1,000 m rope needs just one.

**Working out a 3-km race:**
```
3 km = 3,000 m

WATER STATIONS every 500 m
   3,000 ÷ 500 = 6 stations
   at 500, 1000, 1500, 2000, 2500, 3000 m

CHILDREN standing every 300 m
   3,000 ÷ 300 = 10 children
   at 300, 600, 900, 1200, 1500, 1800, 2100, 2400, 2700, 3000 m

FLAGS every 50 m
   3,000 ÷ 50 = 60 flags in all
   placed alternately red, blue, red, blue …
   → 30 red and 30 blue
```

> [!TIP]
> **The alternating-flags question has a hidden second step.** Dividing gives 60 **flags**, but the question asks for reds and blues separately — **so you must halve again**, giving 30 of each.
>
> **Always re-read what the question actually asked for** after the arithmetic is done.

## 5.5 Measuring long distances

```
LONG TAPE            — for a playground or a field
RODOMETER            — a measuring wheel you push along a road
RANGEFINDER          — measures a distance you cannot walk
LASER DISTANCE METER — for rooms and buildings
```

> [!TIP]
> **A method that needs no equipment at all: MEASURE YOUR OWN STEP.**
>
> **Mark out 100 m with a long tape, walk it, and count your steps.** If it takes 125 steps, then **one step is about 100 ÷ 125 = 0.8 m**.
>
> **Now you can measure any distance by walking it and counting** — because you have turned your own body into a measuring instrument.

## 5.6 The world of small things — millimetres

**Look closely at a ruler between 1 cm and 2 cm.** There are small marks splitting it into **10 equal parts**. Each part is **1 MILLIMETRE (mm)**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **10 mm = 1 cm · 100 cm = 1 m · 1,000 m = 1 km**
>
> **Why we need mm:** a screw or a sprouting seed is often less than a centimetre, or "3 cm and a bit". The mm marks measure that "bit" — they make the measurement more **precise**.

**Drawing 36 mm:** 36 mm = 30 mm + 6 mm = **3 cm 6 mm** — go to the 3 cm mark, then 6 small marks more. In the same way 67 mm = 6 cm 7 mm, and 5 cm 5 mm = 55 mm.

## 5.7 Converting — the one rule

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **BIG unit → SMALL unit: MULTIPLY. SMALL unit → BIG unit: DIVIDE (or split off the whole big units).**
> ```
> 4 cm 5 mm   = 40 mm + 5 mm         = 45 mm
> 89 mm       = 80 mm + 9 mm         = 8 cm 9 mm
> 234 cm      = 234 × 10             = 2,340 mm
> 514 mm      = 510 mm + 4 mm        = 51 cm 4 mm
> 6 m 34 cm   = 600 cm + 34 cm       = 634 cm
> 20 m 12 cm  = 2,000 cm + 12 cm     = 2,012 cm
> 397 m       = 397 × 100            = 39,700 cm
> 5,792 cm    = 5,700 cm + 92 cm     = 57 m 92 cm
> 9,108 cm    = 9,100 cm + 8 cm      = 91 m 8 cm
> 34 km       = 34 × 1,000           = 34,000 m
> 6,870 m     = 6,000 m + 870 m      = 6 km 870 m
> 10,552 m    = 10,000 m + 552 m     = 10 km 552 m
> 29 km 30 m  = 29,000 m + 30 m      = 29,030 m      (not 29,300!)
> 32 km 359 m = 32,000 m + 359 m     = 32,359 m
> ```
> **Why multiply going to the small unit:** each big unit holds MANY small ones, so the count of small units must be bigger. **Check yourself:** small unit → bigger number. If your number got smaller when changing to a smaller unit, you went the wrong way.

## 5.8 Adding and subtracting lengths

**Saji walked 3 km 450 m in the morning and 4 km 650 m in the evening.** Two ways:
```
WAY 1 — add like units:   3 km + 4 km = 7 km;  450 m + 650 m = 1,100 m = 1 km 100 m
                          total 7 km + 1 km 100 m = 8 km 100 m
WAY 2 — all in metres:    3,450 m + 4,650 m = 8,100 m = 8 km 100 m
```
**Electricians had 63 m of cable and used 16 m 75 cm.** In cm: 6,300 − 1,675 = 4,625 cm = **46 m 25 cm** left.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Add or subtract only LIKE units — km with km, m with m.** If the metres reach 1,000 (or cm reach 100), **regroup** into one more big unit, exactly like 10 ones becoming a ten.
>
> **Why:** 450 m + 650 m is 1,100 metres, and 1,000 of those metres ARE a kilometre — leaving it as "7 km 1,100 m" is correct but untidy.

## 5.9 Multiplying and dividing lengths

**A shirt needs 1 m 80 cm of cloth. For 20 shirts?** Multiply each part: 20 × 1 m = 20 m and 20 × 80 cm = 1,600 cm = 16 m → **36 m**.

**5 m of cloth costs ₹100. What does 1 m cost?** ₹100 ÷ 5 = **₹20**. Now the double number line is easy: 10 m → ₹200, 20 m → ₹400, 40 m → ₹800, and ₹2,000 buys 2,000 ÷ 20 = **100 m**.

> [!TIP]
> **Find the cost of ONE first (divide), then multiply up to any amount.** **Why:** once you know one unit's worth, every other amount is just copies of it.

## 5.10 Inches and feet

**Heights are often given in feet and inches.** Check your ruler — one edge may have inch marks.
```
1 inch ≈ 2 cm 5 mm      (a little more, really)
1 foot = 12 inches ≈ 30 cm
```
**So "4 feet 2 inches" is about 4 × 30 + 2 × 2½ = 120 + 5 = 125 cm.**

## From the book — activities worked, with reasons

### Choose the unit — metre or centimetre?
**India Gate's height — m. Handkerchief — cm. Depth of a well — m. Mobile phone — cm. Elephant's trunk — m (about 2 m; cm also fine). Distance between shirt buttons — cm.** **Why:** pick the unit that gives a sensible, not-too-big number — 42 m is easier to say than 4,200 cm.

### Same sari, different names
```
204 cm = 2 m 4 cm          540 cm = 5 m 40 cm          240 cm = 2 m 40 cm
404 cm = 2 m 204 cm        750 cm = 6 m 150 cm
```
**Why 2 m 204 cm is the same as 404 cm:** 2 m is 200 cm, and 200 + 204 = 404. It's an odd way to write it — but not wrong.

### Ritika's rods
```
456 cm < 5 m                    (5 m = 500 cm)
55 + 200 = 255 cm > 254 cm      (200 + 54)
6 m 5 cm < 6 m 50 cm            (5 cm is less than 50 cm)
2 m 150 cm = 3 m 50 cm          (both are 350 cm)
238 cm = 138 cm + 1 m           (138 + 100 = 238)
```
**Why convert first:** you can only compare numbers that count the SAME unit.

### World's tallest statues
Statue of Unity 182 m · Spring Temple Buddha 128 m · Guanyin of Nanshan 108 m · Statue of Liberty 93 m · The Motherland Calls 91 m · Christ the Redeemer 38 m.
- **(a) Tallest − Liberty:** 182 − 93 = **89 m**.
- **(b) Least difference:** Liberty 93 m and Motherland Calls 91 m — just **2 m**.
- **(c) Largest difference:** Unity − Christ the Redeemer = 182 − 38 = **144 m**.
- **(d) Doubled equals 182 m:** 91 × 2 = 182 → **The Motherland Calls**.

### How many ropes make 1 km?
```
1,000 m → 1      500 m → 2      250 m → 4
  200 m → 5      100 m → 10      10 m → 100
```
**Why:** 1 km = 1,000 m, so divide 1,000 by the rope length. Half the rope length → twice as many ropes.

### The 3-km race
- **Water every 500 m:** at 500, 1,000, 1,500, 2,000, 2,500 and 3,000 m → **6 stations** (5 if none is needed right at the finish).
- **Children every 300 m:** 300, 600, …, 3,000 → 3,000 ÷ 300 = **10 children**.
- **Flags every 50 m:** 3,000 ÷ 50 = **60 flags**, alternating → **30 red + 30 blue**. (If a flag also stands at the start line, that's 61.)

> [!NOTE]
> **The "fence-post" question:** counting gaps gives 3,000 ÷ 50 = 60, but a marker at BOTH ends needs one more than the number of gaps. Always ask "is there one at the start?"

### The Vivek Express (distances from Dibrugarh)
```
1. Whole route:                 4,187 km
2. Vijayawada − Jalpaiguri Rd:  2,800 − 983   = 1,817 km
3. Vijayawada − Visakhapatnam:  2,800 − 2,450 = 350 km
4. Guwahati − Dimapur = 556 − 306 = 250 km;  Bhubaneswar − Jalpaiguri = 2,007 − 983 = 1,024 km
   → Bhubaneswar and Jalpaiguri Road are farther apart
5. Guwahati − Coimbatore:       3,675 − 556   = 3,119 km
```
**Why subtract:** both distances are measured from the same starting point (Dibrugarh), so the gap between two stations is the difference.

### Ribbons, waterfalls and cable cars
- **Rani:** 3 m 75 cm + 2 m 25 cm = 5 m 100 cm = **6 m**.
- **Bhopal → Sanchi 48 km 700 m; waterfall at 17 km 900 m:** 48,700 − 17,900 = 30,800 m = **30 km 800 m**.
- **Gulmarg Gondola:** 2 km 300 m + 2 km 650 m = **4 km 950 m**.

### Circle the bigger length
```
11 mm vs 1 cm (10 mm)          → 11 mm, by 1 mm
26 mm vs 2 cm (20 mm)          → 26 mm, by 6 mm
20 cm (200 mm) vs 201 mm       → 201 mm, by 1 mm
1,020 mm vs 1 m (1,000 mm)     → 1,020 mm, by 20 mm
2 m (200 cm) vs 245 cm         → 245 cm, by 45 cm
5,678 m vs 6 km (6,000 m)      → 6 km, by 322 m
6 km 1,480 m (= 7 km 480 m) vs 7 km 479 m → 6 km 1,480 m, by 1 m
```

### Thread and road
- **Embroidery:** 1 m of thread per 50 cm of border = 2 m per 1 m. A 5 m border needs 5 × 2 = **10 m**, costing 10 × ₹50 = **₹500**.
- **Road of 12 km 600 m in 6 equal days:** 12 km ÷ 6 = 2 km and 600 m ÷ 6 = 100 m → **2 km 100 m** a day. **Check:** 6 × 2 km 100 m = 12 km 600 m ✔.

### Estimating heights
**Use something you know as a ruler.** A single-storey house is about 3 m tall; a building with 5 floors is about 5 × 3 = 15 m. **Why it works:** comparing with a known height is more reliable than guessing from nothing.
