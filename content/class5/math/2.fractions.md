## 2.1 The whole matters

**Tamanna has two chocolates of DIFFERENT sizes. She says 1/3 of one is bigger than 1/2 of the other. Is she right?**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **She CAN be right — if the chocolate she took a third of is much bigger.**
> ```
> 1/3 of a large chocolate  CAN be more than  1/2 of a tiny one
> ```
> **THE RULE THAT FOLLOWS: to compare two fractions, THE WHOLES THEY COME FROM MUST BE THE SAME.**
>
> **Why: a fraction is not a fixed amount — it is a fixed SHARE of something.** "One half" means nothing on its own; **half of WHAT** is the question that has to be answered first.
>
> **So when we say 1/2 is greater than 1/3, we always mean of the SAME whole.**

## 2.2 Equivalent fractions

**Shade one-third of a shape. Now draw lines dividing it into more equal parts, and look at what you have shaded:**
```
   1        2        3        4
   —   =    —   =    —   =    —      the SAME shaded region every time
   3        6        9       12
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **EQUIVALENT FRACTIONS are different names for the SAME part of a whole.**
>
> **How they are made: cut each piece into more equal pieces.**
> ```
> break each 1/2 piece into 2 equal parts → each is a 1/4 piece
> so 2 pieces of 1/4 make 1/2:      1/2 = 2/4
> ```
> **Why the value does not change: you have cut the SAME amount into MORE, SMALLER pieces.** The pieces are half the size but there are twice as many, so the total is untouched.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE PATTERN — multiply the top and bottom BY THE SAME NUMBER.**
> ```
>   1     ×2    2     ×3    3     ×4    4
>   —    ---→   —    ---→   —    ---→   —
>   3     ×2    6     ×3    9     ×4   12
>
>   2   =  4  =  6  =  10  =  20  =  40
>   —     —     —      —      —      —
>   5    10    15     25     50    100
> ```
> **Why the SAME number both times: the top counts the pieces you have and the bottom counts the pieces in the whole.** Cutting everything into twice as many pieces doubles **both** counts — so the share stays the same.

> [!WARNING]
> **You must multiply the top and bottom by the same number, not ADD to them.**
> ```
> 1/3 = 2/6   ✔   (both multiplied by 2)
> 1/3 = 2/4   ✘   (1 added to both — this is a DIFFERENT fraction)
> ```
> **Adding changes the ratio of pieces-you-have to pieces-in-the-whole; multiplying does not.**

**How to check whether two fractions are equivalent:** see whether you can get from one to the other by multiplying (or dividing) top and bottom by the same number.
```
3/5 and 6/10   →  3×2 = 6 and 5×2 = 10   ✔ EQUIVALENT
4/12 and 2/6   →  2×2 = 4 and 6×2 = 12   ✔ EQUIVALENT
6/9 and 1/3    →  1×3 = 3? no — 3×3 = 9 and 1×3 = 3, but 6 ≠ 3
                  try dividing: 6÷3 = 2 and 9÷3 = 3, giving 2/3, not 1/3
                  ✘ NOT equivalent
2/3 and 3/4    →  no whole number works   ✘ NOT equivalent
```

## 2.3 Comparing fractions with the SAME denominator

**Sevi ate 1/3 of a chikki and Shami ate 2/3. Who ate more?**
```
2 pieces of 1/3  is more than  1 piece of 1/3

so   2/3  >  1/3
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SAME BOTTOM NUMBER → the one with the BIGGER TOP NUMBER is bigger.**
> ```
> 1/4 < 3/4        3/5 < 4/5        5/7 > 2/7
> ```
> **Why it is that easy: the bottom number tells you the SIZE of each piece.** If the bottom numbers match, all the pieces are the same size — **so you are just counting how many pieces each person has.**

## 2.4 Comparing fractions with the SAME numerator

**Sevi ate 4/6 of a paratha; Shami ate 4/5 of one. Who ate more?**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SAME TOP NUMBER → the one with the SMALLER BOTTOM NUMBER is bigger.**
> ```
> 4/5  >  4/6        3/7 > 3/8        6/9 > 6/10
> ```
> **Why — and this is the part that feels backwards at first:**
>
> **The bottom number says HOW MANY PIECES THE WHOLE WAS CUT INTO.** Cut a paratha into 6 pieces and each piece is **smaller** than if you cut it into 5.
> ```
> a 1/6 piece is SMALLER than a 1/5 piece
> so 4 of the smaller pieces is LESS than 4 of the bigger ones
> ```
> **Both people took FOUR pieces — but Shami's pieces were bigger.**

> [!TIP]
> **The everyday version: would you rather share a cake with 5 people or with 10?**
>
> **More people sharing means a SMALLER slice each** — and that is exactly what a bigger bottom number means.

## 2.5 Fractions greater than 1

**Raman's father cuts parathas into halves. Maa takes 5 pieces of 1/2. How many parathas is that?**
```
1/2 + 1/2 + 1/2 + 1/2 + 1/2  =  5/2

2 halves make 1 whole, so:

   5/2  =  2 wholes + 1 half  =  2½ parathas
```
**On a number line:**
```
   0     1/2    2/2    3/2    4/2    5/2
   |------|------|------|------|------|
                =1            =2     =2½
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A fraction whose TOP is bigger than its BOTTOM is MORE THAN ONE WHOLE.**
> ```
> 6/2 = 3 wholes exactly
> 7/2 = 3 wholes and a half  = 3½
> 9/4 = 2 wholes and a quarter = 2¼
> ```
> **How to work it out: ASK HOW MANY PIECES MAKE ONE WHOLE, then see how many whole sets you have.**
> ```
> 9/4  —  4 quarters make a whole
>      —  9 = 4 + 4 + 1
>      —  so 2 wholes and 1 quarter left over  = 2¼
> ```

> [!TIP]
> **A quick check on any such answer: is the top a MULTIPLE of the bottom?**
> ```
> 6/2 — yes, 6 = 2 × 3, so the answer is a whole number, 3
> 7/2 — no, so there must be a leftover piece
> ```

## Summary

```
A FRACTION IS A SHARE OF SOMETHING — so two fractions can only be compared
   if they come from THE SAME WHOLE
   1/3 of a big chocolate can beat 1/2 of a tiny one

EQUIVALENT FRACTIONS — different names for the same part
   1/3 = 2/6 = 3/9 = 4/12       2/5 = 4/10 = 20/50 = 40/100
   made by MULTIPLYING the top and bottom by the SAME number
   the value is unchanged because you have cut the same amount into more,
      smaller pieces — both counts double together
   NEVER add to top and bottom; that gives a different fraction

COMPARING — SAME BOTTOM number
   bigger TOP wins:   2/3 > 1/3
   because all the pieces are the same size, so you are just counting them

COMPARING — SAME TOP number
   SMALLER BOTTOM wins:   4/5 > 4/6
   because a bigger bottom means the whole was cut into MORE pieces,
      so each piece is SMALLER
   like sharing a cake with 5 people rather than 10

FRACTIONS GREATER THAN 1 — the top is bigger than the bottom
   5/2 = 2½    9/4 = 2¼    6/2 = 3
   ask how many pieces make ONE whole, then count the whole sets and
   the leftover
```
