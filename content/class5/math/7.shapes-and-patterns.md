## 7.1 Weaving patterns

**A woven mat is made by threading strips OVER and UNDER the slits in a folded sheet. The pattern is written row by row:**
```
SIMPLEST WEAVE
   Row 1 — 1 under, 1 over, 1 under, 1 over, … (repeat)
   Row 2 — 1 over, 1 under, 1 over, 1 under, … (repeat)

A DIFFERENT MAT
   Row 1 — 2 over, 1 under, 2 over, 1 under, …
   Row 2 — 2 under, 1 over, 2 under, 1 over, …
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY ROW 2 IS ALWAYS THE OPPOSITE OF ROW 1.**
>
> **If two rows went over and under in exactly the same places, the strips would not HOLD each other** — the mat would fall apart.
>
> **By reversing the second row, every strip is trapped by the one above and below it.** The over-and-under alternation is what makes a weave a weave, rather than a stack.

> [!TIP]
> **How to read ANY woven pattern: look at ONE ROW UNTIL IT REPEATS, then write down just that repeating bit.**
>
> **"2 over, 1 under" repeated is the whole of Row 1** — three strips' worth of instruction describes a mat of any size.
>
> **This is what a pattern IS: a short rule that produces something long.**

## 7.2 Tiling and tessellation

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A shape TESSELLATES if copies of it can cover a region completely — WITH NO GAPS AND NO OVERLAPS.**
>
> **A REGULAR shape has all its sides equal.**
> ```
> equilateral triangle — regular 3-sided shape
> square               — regular 4-sided shape
> regular pentagon     — regular 5-sided shape
> regular hexagon      — regular 6-sided shape
> ```

**The test is done AROUND A SINGLE POINT: how many copies fit before you come full circle?**

| Regular shape | Number that fit around a point | Tessellates? |
|---|---|---|
| **Equilateral triangle** | **6** | **YES** |
| **Square** | **4** | **YES** |
| **Regular pentagon** | 3 fit, then a GAP is left | **NO** |
| **Regular hexagon** | **3** | **YES** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY PENTAGONS FAIL — and this is the heart of the chapter.**
>
> **Place three regular pentagons around a point and a gap is left — but a FOURTH pentagon is too big to fit into it.**
>
> **So the corners of a regular pentagon do not divide a full turn evenly.** Three is too few and four is too many, and there is nothing in between.
>
> **Compare with the shapes that succeed: 6 triangles, 4 squares or 3 hexagons close up EXACTLY.** In each case the corners share out a full turn with nothing left over.

> [!TIP]
> **The turns of Chapter 3 explain this completely.**
>
> **Four squares meet at a point, so each square's corner is a QUARTER TURN — a right angle.** Four quarter turns make a full turn ✔
>
> **Six triangles meet, so each corner is a SIXTH of a full turn.** Six sixths make a whole ✔
>
> **A pentagon's corner is bigger than a quarter turn but smaller than a third** — so no whole number of them can add up to a full turn. **That is precisely why it leaves a gap.**

**Tessellations can also use MORE THAN ONE SHAPE together**, which is how many real floor and wall patterns are made — squares with triangles, hexagons with triangles, and so on.

## 7.3 Making shapes from pieces

**Cut a rhombus into triangular pieces and rearrange them, and you can build many different shapes:**
```
from 2 triangles  — a rhombus, a bigger triangle, a parallelogram
from 3 triangles  — a trapezium and other shapes
from 4 triangles  — a larger triangle, and several quadrilaterals
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE FOUR-SIDED SHAPES (QUADRILATERALS) worth knowing:**
> ```
> SQUARE        4 equal sides, 4 right angles
> RECTANGLE     opposite sides equal, 4 right angles
> RHOMBUS       4 equal sides, but the corners are not right angles
> PARALLELOGRAM opposite sides equal and parallel
> TRAPEZIUM     just ONE pair of parallel sides
> KITE          two pairs of equal sides, next to each other
> ```
> **A useful way to see how they relate: a SQUARE is a special rectangle (whose sides happen to be equal) and also a special rhombus (whose corners happen to be right angles).**
>
> **So the categories overlap — a shape can belong to more than one at once.**

> [!TIP]
> **When a puzzle asks "which shape am I?", check the clues ONE AT A TIME and cross out what fails.**
>
> **"Four equal sides" leaves the square and the rhombus. "Four right angles" then leaves only the square.**
>
> **You rarely need every clue** — and noticing which clues were unnecessary is itself worth doing.

## 7.4 Circles, cubes and nets

**A CIRCLE is the shape every point of which is the same distance from the centre.** That is why a compass draws one: the point stays fixed and the pencil keeps its distance.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A NET is a flat shape that FOLDS UP into a solid.**
>
> **A cube's net has SIX squares**, because a cube has six faces — but not every arrangement of six squares folds into a cube. **Some leave two faces landing in the same place.**
>
> **How to check a net without cutting it out: pick one square as the BASE and imagine folding the others up around it.** If any two try to occupy the same position, the net fails.

```
ICOSAHEDRON — "icosa" means twenty  → 20 faces
DODECAHEDRON — "dodeca" means twelve → 12 faces
```
> [!TIP]
> **The names are not decoration — they COUNT THE FACES.** Once you know that, you never have to memorise which is which.
>
> **The same trick works elsewhere: PENTAGON is five, HEXAGON is six, and KILOMETRE is a thousand metres.** Greek and Latin number-words run right through mathematics.

   Row 1: 1 under, 1 over …    Row 2: 1 over, 1 under …
   Row 2 must be the OPPOSITE of Row 1, or the strips would not HOLD
      each other and the mat would fall apart
   a PATTERN is a short rule that produces something long

TESSELLATION — copies of a shape cover a region with NO gaps and NO overlaps
   REGULAR shape = all sides equal

   around a single point:
      6 equilateral triangles fit  → tessellates
      4 squares fit                → tessellates
      3 regular hexagons fit       → tessellates
      3 regular pentagons leave a GAP, and a 4th will not fit
                                   → does NOT tessellate

   WHY: the corners must share out a FULL TURN exactly
      a square's corner is a quarter turn — 4 make a whole ✔
      a triangle's corner is a sixth of a turn — 6 make a whole ✔
      a pentagon's corner divides a full turn into no whole number ✘

QUADRILATERALS
   square · rectangle · rhombus · parallelogram · trapezium · kite
   the categories OVERLAP — a square is a special rectangle AND a special
   rhombus

CIRCLE — every point the same distance from the centre, which is exactly
   what a compass draws

NETS — a flat shape that folds into a solid; a cube's net has SIX squares,
   but not every arrangement of six squares works
