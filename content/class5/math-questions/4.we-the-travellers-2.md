A lorry has 28 litres of fuel and 75 more litres are filled. Find the total, showing the regrouping.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
>      H   T   O
>      1   1        ← carried
>          2   8
>   +      7   5
>   ------------
>      1   0   3
> ```
> **28 + 75 = 103 litres.**
>
> **Step by step: 8 + 5 = 13 ones. That is too many for one column, so REGROUP — 10 Ones = 1 Ten. Write 3, carry 1.**
>
> **Then 1 + 2 + 7 = 10 tens. Regroup again — 10 Tens = 1 Hundred. Write 0, carry 1.**

---

Why can you not simply write 13 in the ones column when 8 + 5 = 13?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because EACH COLUMN HOLDS ONLY ONE DIGIT, from 0 to 9.**
>
> **The moment a column would need two digits, the excess must move LEFT — and moving left is exactly what "carrying" means.**
> ```
> 13 ones = 1 Ten + 3 Ones
>          ↑ this part moves to the tens column
> ```
> **This is the same rule that made 9 + 1 = 10 rather than a single new symbol.**
>
> **Regrouping is not a trick for hard sums — it is what place value IS.**

---

Add these, using an easier way where you can: 46 + 99 · 5 + 89 · 76 + 28.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 46 + 99  →  46 + 100 = 146, then take back 1  →  145
>  5 + 89  →   5 +  90 =  95, then take back 1  →   94
> 76 + 28  →  76 +  30 = 106, then take back 2  →  104
> ```
> **Why this is allowed: you deliberately added a BIT TOO MUCH, so you simply give back the extra.**
>
> **Adding 100 is far easier than adding 99, and the correction is a single subtraction.**
>
> **This is a genuine method, not a shortcut to be ashamed of** — and it is usually faster than writing out columns.

Find 1,855 + 1,862, showing the columns.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
>       Th    H    T    O
>        1    1
>        1    8    5    5
>    +   1    8    6    2
>    -------------------
>        3    7    1    7
> ```
> **1,855 + 1,862 = 3,717 km.**
>
> **Notice that adding large numbers is EXACTLY the same as adding small ones** — only the number of columns has grown.
>
> **The regrouping happens in the same way at every level: 5 + 2 = 7 needs no carry; 5 + 6 = 11 tens does; 8 + 8 + 1 = 17 hundreds does.**

Mahesh spends ₹21,880 on fuel and tolls and ₹38,900 on other expenses. What is his total?

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
>      TTh   Th    H    T    O
>        1    1
>        2    1    8    8    0
>    +   3    8    9    0    0
>    ------------------------
>        6    0    7    8    0
> ```
> **₹21,880 + ₹38,900 = ₹60,780.**
>
> **A quick sense-check before trusting it: ₹22,000 + ₹39,000 would be about ₹61,000** — and our answer, ₹60,780, is close to that. ✔
>
> **Estimating first is worth the few seconds** — it catches a misplaced digit immediately.

Find: 238 + 367 · 878 + 8,789 · 46,120 + 12,890.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 238 + 367 = 605
>
> 878 + 8,789 = 9,667
>
> 46,120 + 12,890 = 59,010
> ```
> **The second one needs the most care: 878 has THREE digits and 8,789 has FOUR.**
> ```
>      8, 7 8 9
>   +     8 7 8       ← the 8 of 878 sits under the 7, not under the 8
>   ------------
>      9, 6 6 7
> ```
> **Getting the alignment right is the whole battle when the numbers are different lengths.** Line up the ONES first and let everything else follow.

A minibus has 18 litres of fuel; after refuelling the meter reads 65 litres. How much was filled? Check your answer.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
>      T    O
>      5   15        ← regrouped: 1 Ten becomes 10 Ones
>      6    5
>   -  1    8
>   ---------
>      4    7
> ```
> **47 litres were filled.**
>
> **The borrowing step: 5 ones is not enough to take 8 from, so one TEN is broken into TEN ONES**, leaving 5 tens and 15 ones. **65 is still 65** — only the way it is written has changed.
>
> **The check: 18 + 47 = 65** ✔ — add your answer back to the number you subtracted, and you should return to where you started.

---

Find: 82 − 37 · 93 − 35 · 70 − 43, and check each.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 82 − 37 = 45      check:  37 + 45 = 82   ✔
> 93 − 35 = 58      check:  35 + 58 = 93   ✔
> 70 − 43 = 27      check:  43 + 27 = 70   ✔
> ```
> **All three needed borrowing, because in each case the top ones digit was smaller than the bottom one.**
>
> **70 − 43 is the trickiest: there are ZERO ones on top**, so a ten must be borrowed to make 10 ones, leaving 6 tens.
>
> **Always check by adding back.** It takes seconds and catches every borrowing slip.

---

If 78 + 164 = 242, write all the other number sentences that follow.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 164 + 78  = 242
> 242 − 78  = 164
> 242 − 164 = 78
> ```
> **Why one fact gives four sentences: the three numbers are simply A PART, ANOTHER PART, and THE WHOLE.**
> ```
> 78 and 164 are the parts;  242 is the whole
> ```
> **Whichever one is missing, the other two can find it.** Add the two parts to get the whole; subtract one part from the whole to get the other part.
>
> **This is why addition and subtraction are not two separate topics** — they are one relationship read in different directions.

---

If 921 − 137 = 784, write the addition sentences that follow.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 784 + 137 = 921
> 137 + 784 = 921
> and also  921 − 784 = 137
> ```
> **Identifying the roles first makes this automatic:**
> ```
> 921 is the WHOLE
> 137 and 784 are the two PARTS
> ```
> **A subtraction sentence always hands you the whole and one part; the answer is the other part.**
>
> **Once you can name which number is the whole, every sentence writes itself.**

---

Fill in: 198 − ___ = 98 and 872 − ___ = 672.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 198 − 100 = 98
> 872 − 200 = 672
> ```
> **How to find a missing number in a subtraction: TURN IT INTO A SUBTRACTION.**
> ```
> if  198 − ? = 98    then  ? = 198 − 98 = 100
> if  872 − ? = 672   then  ? = 872 − 672 = 200
> ```
> **Both of these can also be seen at a glance: 198 and 98 differ only in the hundreds digit**, so the gap is exactly 100. **872 and 672 differ by 2 hundreds**, so the gap is 200.
>
> **Looking at the digits before reaching for a method often saves the work entirely.**

What are consecutive numbers? Give two examples.

> [!ANSWER]
> **CONSECUTIVE NUMBERS follow one another in order WITHOUT SKIPPING any.**
> ```
> 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
> 29, 30, 31, 32
> 2023, 2024, 2025
> ```
> **The test: each number is exactly ONE MORE than the one before it.**
>
> **So 2, 4, 6 is NOT a consecutive run** — those numbers skip. They are consecutive **even** numbers, which is a different thing.

---

Look at the sums of two consecutive numbers: 1+2=3, 2+3=5, 3+4=7, 4+5=9. What is the step, and why?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The step is 2 — each sum is 2 more than the one before.**
>
> **Why: sliding the pair one place to the right adds 1 to EACH of the two numbers.**
> ```
> 1+2 = 3
> 2+3 = 5     the 1 became 2 (+1) and the 2 became 3 (+1)
>             two gains of 1 → total gain of 2
> ```
> **This explains the pattern instead of just describing it — and it immediately tells you what happens for longer runs.**

---

What is the step between successive sums for 5 consecutive numbers? And for 6?

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 5 consecutive numbers → the step is 5
> 6 consecutive numbers → the step is 6
> ```
> **THE STEP ALWAYS EQUALS HOW MANY NUMBERS YOU ARE ADDING.**
>
> **Why: sliding the whole group one place right adds 1 to EVERY number in it.** Five numbers each gaining 1 gives a total gain of 5.
> ```
> 2 numbers → step 2      (3, 5, 7, 9)
> 3 numbers → step 3      (6, 9, 12, 15)
> 4 numbers → step 4      (10, 14, 18, 22)
> ```
> **Once you see WHY, you never have to work out another case** — the answer is the count itself.

---

Find 67 + 68 + 69 without adding them one at a time.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 3 numbers, so:  sum = 3 × MIDDLE number = 3 × 68 = 204
> ```
> **Why the middle number works: the number below it falls short by exactly as much as the number above it overshoots.**
> ```
> 67 is ONE BELOW 68
> 69 is ONE ABOVE 68
> → they cancel, so the total equals 68 + 68 + 68
> ```
> **This works for ANY odd-sized run of consecutive numbers**, and it turns a three-step addition into one multiplication.

---

Find 48 + 49 + 50 + 51 + 52 using the same idea.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 5 numbers, so:  sum = 5 × MIDDLE number = 5 × 50 = 250
> ```
> **The cancelling happens in pairs, moving outwards from the middle:**
> ```
> 49 is 1 below 50 and 51 is 1 above → cancel
> 48 is 2 below 50 and 52 is 2 above → cancel
> → all that is left is 50 five times
> ```
> **And 50 is a very convenient middle number**, which is what makes this particular sum so quick.

---

Find 24 + 25 + 26 + 27. Why does the middle-number method not work here?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because there is an EVEN number of terms, so there is NO single middle number.**
>
> **Instead, PAIR THE ENDS:**
> ```
> 24 + 27 = 51
> 25 + 26 = 51
>
> 2 pairs of 51 = 102
> ```
> **Why every pair gives the same total: as you move IN from the left end you gain 1, and as you move in from the right end you lose 1** — so each pair's total stays put.
>
> **The rule: ODD count → count × middle. EVEN count → pair the ends.**

---

Find 237 + 238 + 239 + 240 + 241 + 242.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 6 numbers — even, so pair the ends:
>
>    237 + 242 = 479
>    238 + 241 = 479
>    239 + 240 = 479
>
> 3 pairs of 479 = 1,437
> ```
> **All six numbers added with two easy steps: one addition to find the pair total, then one multiplication.**
>
> **A check on the size: the numbers are all around 240, and 6 × 240 = 1,440** — very close to our 1,437 ✔

---

Why is the sum of two consecutive numbers always odd?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because ONE of them is EVEN and the other is ODD — always.**
> ```
> consecutive numbers alternate: even, odd, even, odd …
> so any two next to each other are one of each
>
> EVEN + ODD = ODD
> ```
> **Check it: 1+2=3, 2+3=5, 3+4=7, 4+5=9 — every one odd.**
>
> **There is no way to escape it**, because you can never have two evens or two odds sitting side by side.

---

Why is the sum of four consecutive numbers always even?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because among any four consecutive numbers there are TWO EVEN and TWO ODD.**
> ```
> the two ODDS add to an EVEN
> the two EVENS add to an EVEN
> and  EVEN + EVEN = EVEN
> ```
> **Check it: 10, 14, 18, 22 — all even.**
>
> **And for THREE consecutive numbers the answer varies** — sometimes you get two evens and one odd, sometimes two odds and one even, **so the sum can be either.** That is why 6, 9, 12, 15 alternates.

From 5,205 · 6,220 · 7,095 · 8,455 · 4,840, find two numbers whose sum is closest to 10,000.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 5,205 + 4,840 = 10,045      only 45 away  ← CLOSEST
> 5,205 + 6,220 = 11,425      1,425 away
> 6,220 + 4,840 = 11,060      1,060 away
> ```
> **The answer is 5,205 + 4,840 = 10,045.**
>
> **How to search sensibly instead of trying every pair: aim for two numbers each around HALF the target.**
>
> **Half of 10,000 is 5,000, so start with the numbers nearest to 5,000** — which are 5,205 and 4,840. **That is where the answer was.**

From the same list, find two numbers whose sum is closest to 16,000.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 7,095 + 8,455 = 15,550      450 away   ← CLOSEST
> 6,220 + 8,455 = 14,675    1,325 away
> 5,205 + 8,455 = 13,660    2,340 away
> ```
> **The answer is 7,095 + 8,455 = 15,550.**
>
> **A useful observation: 15,550 is the LARGEST sum any two of these numbers can make**, since 8,455 and 7,095 are the two biggest.
>
> **So no pair can reach 16,000 at all** — and the best you can do is get as close from below as possible.
