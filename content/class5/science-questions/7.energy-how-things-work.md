Name the five signs that energy is at work, with an example of each.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> MOVEMENT   a fan, a car, a bird, boiling water
> LIGHT      a bulb, a diya, the Sun
> SOUND      a guitar, a horn, a voice
> HEAT       food cooking, water warming in the sun
> COOLING    a fridge, water in a matka
> ```
> **Why these five are one topic and not five: you can never see energy itself — only what it DOES.**
>
> **Each of these is energy showing itself in a different way.**
>
> **The useful question to ask about anything: what is it doing, and where did the energy come from?** Those two questions carry you through the whole chapter.

---

A blown-up balloon is released and flies across the room. Why does it move forward when the air goes backward?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because the escaping air pushes on the BALLOON, not on the room.**
>
> **Everything that pushes gets pushed back.** The balloon pushes air out one way and is pushed the other way.
>
> **Why this is not "the air pushing against the room": a rocket in empty space has nothing at all to push against, and still moves.** It is pushing on its own exhaust.
>
> **That is exactly why the balloon-on-a-string is called a balloon rocket.** Same principle, smaller scale.

---

How would you make a balloon rocket travel faster along its string?

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> blow the balloon up MORE — more air rushing out means a bigger push
> use a SMOOTHER, TIGHTER string — less rubbing to slow it down
> use a LIGHTER straw and less tape — less weight to move
> keep the string HORIZONTAL — so it is not climbing
> ```
> **Why each works: two separate things decide the speed — how hard it is pushed, and how much resists.**
>
> **Increase the push or reduce the resistance, and it goes faster.**
>
> **This is the shape of nearly every "make it go faster" question in science.** Ask what is pushing and what is holding back.

---

Why does a plucked rubber band make a sound, and a still one does not?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because sound needs VIBRATION.**
>
> **A plucked band moves rapidly to and fro, pushing the air beside it back and forth — and those pushes travelling through the air are what your ear receives as sound.**
>
> **A still band pushes nothing, so there is nothing to hear.**
>
> **No vibration, no sound — every time.** If you can hear something, something somewhere is vibrating.

---

What happens if you use thicker rubber bands on the box guitar? Why?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Thicker bands give a LOWER note.**
>
> **Why: a thicker, heavier band is harder to shake, so it vibrates more SLOWLY.** Slower vibration is heard as a lower note.
>
> **Thinner or tighter bands vibrate faster and give a higher note.**
>
> **This is why a guitar has strings of different thicknesses**, and why the fat strings on any instrument are always the low ones. **You can predict the sound just by looking.**

---

Why do you feel tired when you are hungry?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because food is your fuel, and you are running low.**
>
> **Every movement you make — running, standing, even sitting still — is paid for out of what you ate.** Your brain uses energy even when you are asleep or just thinking.
>
> **So hunger is not simply an unpleasant feeling.** It is a report on your supply, and tiredness is what running low actually feels like.
>
> **Animals are in exactly the same position** — a dog running, a bird flying, a fish swimming, all on food.

---

Two diyas are lit: one with a dry wick, one with a wick sitting in oil. What happens, and what is the fuel?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The dry wick flares and is gone in seconds. The oiled wick burns steadily for a long time.**
>
> **The OIL is the fuel — not the wick.**
>
> **The wick's only job is to carry oil up to the flame, drop by drop, at a steady rate.**
>
> **Why the dry wick fails: with no oil arriving, the flame consumes the wick itself and then has nothing left.** **A lamp keeps burning only as long as it is fed.**

---

Why is burning wood or coal for cooking a health problem, not just an environmental one?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because the smoke is produced inside the kitchen, where somebody is standing all day.**
>
> **Burning wood and coal makes smoke and harmful gases**, and in a closed room those are breathed in for hours, every day, mostly by whoever does the cooking.
>
> **Why this matters more than distant pollution: the concentration is far higher close to the source.**
>
> **Which is why smoke-free stoves and cleaner cooking fuel are not a luxury.** They are a health question first and an environment question second.

---

Why is electricity so useful compared to other forms of energy?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because it is the one form of energy that will turn into ANY other.**
>
> **Oil burns and gives heat and light — not much else.** Food gives movement in a living body only.
>
> **But electricity, sent down the same two wires, will move a fan, warm an iron, light a bulb, sound a bell or cool a fridge** — depending only on what you plug in.
>
> **That flexibility is why we go to the trouble of making it**, even though making it usually means burning something somewhere else.

---

Why do all the electricity safety rules say "stay away and tell an adult" rather than "be careful"?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because electricity gives no warning at all.**
>
> **A live wire looks exactly like a dead one.** There is no smell, no sound, no heat until you touch it.
>
> **So you cannot inspect your way to safety.** Being careful requires information, and here there is none available to you.
>
> **The only safe rule is not to touch:** never wires or the inside of a plugged-in device, never fingers or pens in sockets, never near broken wires, fallen poles or transformers — **and always tell an adult.**

---

Most electricity comes from burning coal. Why is that a problem?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because burning coal produces a great deal of smoke and harmful gases that pollute the air.**
>
> **That polluted air harms people's health and the environment, often far from the power station.**
>
> **And there is a second problem: coal RUNS OUT.** Once burnt it is gone forever, and what took millions of years to form is used in a few decades.
>
> **So coal fails on both counts** — it is dirty while it lasts, and it does not last.

---

Why are the Sun, wind and water called CLEAN energy?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because nothing has to be BURNT.**
>
> **Coal must be burnt to release its energy, and burning always produces something** — smoke, gases, ash. There is no way to burn coal cleanly.
>
> **Sunlight, wind and flowing water are already arriving or already moving.** Capturing some of it leaves nothing behind.
>
> **They are also called RENEWABLE, for a second reason: the supply comes back.** The Sun rises again tomorrow; the river flows again. **Clean and renewable are two different virtues that happen to go together here.**

---

A pinwheel spins in the wind. What full-size machine works the same way?

> [!ANSWER]
> **A WINDMILL — which is a large pinwheel connected to a generator.**
>
> **Why it works: moving air pushes on the angled blades, and because they are angled, the push becomes a TURN rather than a shove.**
>
> **Why the angle matters: flat blades facing the wind would just be pushed backwards.** Tilting them turns a straight push into rotation.
>
> **The generator then turns that rotation into electricity** — so the chain is wind → movement → electricity.

---

A magnifying glass focuses sunlight and the paper chars. Does the lens make the sunlight hotter?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No — it GATHERS it.**
>
> **The lens collects the light falling on its whole surface and delivers it to one tiny spot.**
>
> **So the same amount of energy arrives on perhaps a hundredth of the area — and that spot gets very hot indeed.**
>
> **Concentrating is not the same as creating.** No extra energy has appeared; it has simply been crowded together.
>
> **This is exactly the principle behind solar cookers and solar power towers**, which use mirrors to do the same thing on an enormous scale.

---

A water wheel made of spoons round a paper cup turns when water is poured on it. What full-size use does this have?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Hydroelectricity — and, older and simpler, a watermill.**
>
> **In hilly areas, watermills called *gharaats* use flowing water to grind grain** — no electricity involved at all, just water turning a stone.
>
> **Why falling water can do work: it has been lifted (by the Sun, evaporating it and dropping it as rain on high ground)** and it releases that as it comes down.
>
> **So even hydroelectricity is the Sun's energy, collected by the water cycle** — which is why a drought reduces electricity as well as drinking water.

---

Give three examples of using the Sun, wind or water directly, without electricity.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> drying clothes and papads in the SUN
> a sailing boat, and a windmill pumping water — WIND
> a village watermill (gharaat) grinding grain — flowing WATER
> also: a matka cooling water by evaporation
> ```
> **Why direct use is often the smartest choice: no energy is lost turning it into electricity first.**
>
> **Every conversion wastes some.** Sun → electricity → machine → dry clothes loses far more than Sun → dry clothes.
>
> **The simplest solution is frequently the most efficient one**, which is easy to forget when the modern option looks more impressive.

Complete these energy chains: Sun → ? → dries clothes, and wind → ? → makes electricity.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> SUN  →  HEAT      →  dries clothes
> WIND →  MOVEMENT  →  turns a turbine to make electricity
> FOOD →  MOVEMENT  →  a child walks to school
> PETROL → MOVEMENT →  a scooter runs
> ELECTRICITY → LIGHT → a bulb glows
> ```
> **Why every chain has three parts: something SUPPLIES the energy, it appears in some FORM, and that form does a JOB.**
>
> **Missing out the middle is what makes energy confusing.** "The Sun dries clothes" is true but skips the step that explains it — **the Sun supplies heat, and heat is what evaporates the water.**

---

Trace a scooter's energy back as far as you can.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> the scooter moves ← petrol burns ← petrol came from very ancient
> living things ← those living things grew using sunlight, millions
> of years ago ← THE SUN
> ```
> **Why the chain always leads back further than you think: even food → movement is not the start.** The food was a plant, and the plant grew using sunlight.
>
> **Follow almost any energy chain back far enough and you arrive at the Sun.** That is exactly why it is called the main source of energy for life on Earth.
>
> **The chief exception worth knowing is the tides**, which are driven by the Moon.

Give two examples of stored energy and explain what "stored" means here.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> A BATTERY  stores energy chemically and releases it when connected
> YOUR BODY  stores energy from food — which is why you can still walk
>            after skipping a meal
> also: food itself is stored sunlight; a dam stores water high up
> ```
> **What "stored" means: the energy is there, doing nothing, until it is called for.**
>
> **Why storage is needed at all: supply and demand rarely happen at the same moment.** You eat at eight and run at eleven.
>
> **A torch needs a battery precisely because it must work when no supply is attached.**

---

Why is storing energy the hard part of using solar and wind power?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because the Sun does not shine at night and the wind does not always blow — but people want light every night.**
>
> **Coal has no such problem: it sits in a heap until burnt.** Coal is energy already stored, which is a genuine advantage it has.
>
> **So the difficulty with clean energy is not capturing it — panels and turbines work well.** It is KEEPING it until it is needed.
>
> **Which is why better batteries matter as much as better solar panels**, and why a country with plenty of sun can still be short of electricity at 8 p.m.

---

What does "energy efficiency" mean, and give an example?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Using LESS energy to do the SAME work.**
>
> **An LED bulb gives the same amount of light as an older bulb while using far less electricity.**
>
> **Why this is the best kind of saving: nothing has been given up.** The room is just as bright; only the waste is gone.
>
> **Efficiency is the one way of saving energy that costs you nothing at all** — which is why it is always the first thing to look at.

---

Is switching off a fan in an empty room a sacrifice?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No — it costs you nothing, because the comfort was not being delivered to anybody.**
>
> **Why this matters as a way of thinking: most energy saving is like this.** It is removing WASTE, not accepting less.
>
> **Real sacrifice — being hot, or sitting in the dark — is rarely needed.**
>
> **And it is a shame to argue about the costly savings while the free ones go untaken.** Switch off the empty room first; discuss the rest afterwards.

---

List four things you could do at home this week to save energy.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> switch off lights and fans when leaving a room
> use daylight instead of a bulb where you can
> dry clothes in the sun rather than a machine
> close the fridge door quickly, and do not stand deciding with it open
> walk or cycle for short journeys
> ```
> **Why the fridge one is worth including: every time the door opens, cold air falls out and warm air comes in**, and the fridge must work again to cool it.
>
> **The pattern behind all of them is the same as the dripping tap in Chapter 4** — find the losses that happen without anyone noticing, and stop those first.

---

Traditional Indian houses had small windows and thick walls. Why?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Thick walls take a long time to warm through**, so the inside stays cool through the hottest part of the day and gives that warmth back at night.
>
> **Small windows let in less of the fierce sun** while still lighting the room.
>
> **The result: cool in summer and warm in winter, with no fan and no heater.**
>
> **Why this is worth studying rather than dismissing as old-fashioned: the design does its work for free, for a hundred years, with nothing to switch on.** A modern glass building often needs constant cooling to be liveable at all.

---

In parts of Ladakh and Himachal, families keep cattle on the ground floor and live above. What is the reason?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The animals' body heat rises and warms the rooms above.**
>
> **Why body heat is enough: several large animals in a closed space produce a surprising amount of it**, continuously, day and night.
>
> **What it saves: firewood, which in a cold treeless region is scarce and expensive to gather** — and electricity, which may not be available.
>
> **It also solves a second problem at the same time: the animals are sheltered from the cold.** **One arrangement, two problems solved — which is the mark of a very good design.**

---

What would change in your home if there were no electricity for a whole day?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No lights after dark; no fan; no fridge, so food begins to spoil; no water if a pump is needed; no phone charging, TV, or internet; cooking still possible if the gas is separate.**
>
> **Why the fridge is the most serious of these: it is the only one that ruins something permanently.** Everything else waits; food does not.
>
> **Why the list is longer than people expect: electricity does not do one job.** It is the one form of energy that turns into any other, so its loss shows up in five different ways at once.
>
> **It also explains why hospitals and water plants keep generators.** For them, a day is far too long.
