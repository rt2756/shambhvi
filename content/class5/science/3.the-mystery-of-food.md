## 3.1 Why food spoils — the microbes

**A forgotten uttapam grows coloured patches and smells foul. What happened?**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **MOULD IS NOT A STAIN. IT IS ALIVE.**
>
> **Those coloured patches are a colony of thousands of MICROBES — living things far too small to see without a microscope.**
>
> **They were always there.** Microbes are in the soil, in water, in the air around us, on plants and animals, and inside us.
>
> **They did not appear on the food. They ARRIVED on it and then MULTIPLIED**, feeding on the food, changing its taste, smell and colour as they went.
>
> **This is why food "goes bad" rather than simply getting old.** Something has been eating it before you did.

> [!TIP]
> **You can see the mould only because there are so many of them.** One microbe is invisible; a colony of thousands is a patch you can point at. **By the time you can see spoilage, it is very far advanced.**

**Not all microbes are enemies:**
```
HARMFUL   spoil food · cause upset stomachs and disease
HELPFUL   make curd from milk · make idli batter rise ·
          help digest food inside your body
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY THE SAME KIND OF LIVING THING CAN BE BOTH FRIEND AND ENEMY.**
>
> **"Microbe" is not one creature — it is a whole huge family**, as varied as "animal" is. Saying microbes are bad is like saying animals are bad because tigers exist.
>
> **And even the useful ones are only useful in the right PLACE.** The microbes that turn milk into curd are doing exactly what the ones spoiling the uttapam were doing — breaking food down. **We call it fermentation when we wanted it and spoilage when we did not.**

## 3.2 What microbes need — the key to everything that follows

```
MICROBES NEED    moisture (water)  ·  air  ·  the right temperature
                 and something to feed on

REMOVE ANY ONE OF THESE AND THEY CANNOT GROW
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THIS ONE SENTENCE EXPLAINS EVERY PRESERVATION METHOD EVER INVENTED.**
>
> **You do not have to learn drying, pickling, salting, canning and refrigeration as five separate facts.** Each of them takes away one of the things on that list.
>
> **Once you know what microbes need, you can work out any preservation method for yourself** — and even predict whether a new one will work.
>
> **This is what makes it a scientific idea rather than a kitchen tip.**

**Why bread spoils in two days and pickle lasts two months:**
```
BREAD   moist · exposed to air · at room temperature
        → everything a microbe wants
PICKLE  covered in oil (no air) · full of salt (draws out moisture)
        · spiced
        → almost nothing a microbe can use
```

## 3.3 Preservation methods, and the reason each one works

| Method | What it removes | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Drying / dehydration** | **moisture** | chillies, papad, dried fish, aam papad |
| **Pickling in oil** | **air** | mango pickle, lemon pickle |
| **Salting** | **moisture** (salt draws water out) | fish, vegetables |
| **Sugaring** | **moisture** | jam, murabba |
| **Refrigeration** | **the right temperature** | milk, vegetables, butter |
| **Freezing** | temperature, and freezes the water | peas, ice cream |
| **Canning / airtight packing** | **air**, and keeps new microbes out | canned juice, tinned food |
| **Spices** (pepper, turmeric) | make conditions hostile | almost everything in Indian cooking |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY DRYING CHILLIES IN THE SUN KEEPS THEM FOR A YEAR.**
>
> **Sunlight evaporates the water in the chilli.** Without moisture, microbes cannot grow — they need water as much as any other living thing.
>
> **The chilli itself is unharmed** — its flavour, its colour and its heat stay behind, because those are not water.
>
> **This is the oldest preservation method in the world**, and it needs no equipment at all beyond sun and a mat.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY OIL ON A PICKLE IS A SEAL, NOT AN INGREDIENT.**
>
> **A layer of oil floats on top and keeps AIR from reaching the food underneath.** Most spoilage microbes need air.
>
> **Without oil the same pickle would go mouldy** — the salt and spice slow microbes but do not stop them alone.
>
> **This is why grandmothers insist the oil must cover everything.** A piece of mango sticking out above the oil is the piece that will spoil, and it will spoil the rest with it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY A FRIDGE SLOWS SPOILAGE BUT DOES NOT STOP IT.**
>
> **Cold does not kill microbes — it only slows them down.** They are still alive and still multiplying, just very slowly.
>
> **So refrigerated food lasts days or weeks, not years.** Take it out and warm it, and the microbes speed straight up again.
>
> **A freezer goes further: it freezes the water so the microbes cannot use it at all** — which is why frozen food keeps for months. **But it still does not kill them; thawed food spoils quickly.**

> [!TIP]
> **Why butter needs a fridge and ghee does not — and it is all about water.**
> ```
> 1. heat butter                    3. the water EVAPORATES away
> 2. water separates from the fat   4. what is left is ghee — almost pure fat
> ```
> **Butter still contains water; ghee has had it boiled off.**
>
> **No water means no microbes, so ghee keeps for months in a jar on the shelf** while butter turns rancid in days outside a fridge. **The same reasoning as drying chillies, applied to a fat.**

> [!NOTE]
> **A matka (clay pot) keeps water cool** because water seeps very slowly through the clay and evaporates from the outside — and evaporation takes heat away with it. **No electricity, no moving parts.**
>
> **Black pepper** was once so valuable as a spice — partly because it helped preserve food — that Vasco da Gama crossed oceans to reach India for it.
>
> **Hunting wasps preserved food long before humans did**, storing paralysed prey for their larvae.

## 3.4 Microbes working FOR us

```
CURD      microbes turn milk into curd, and the curd's microbes join
          the good microbes in your stomach to help digestion
IDLI      microbes from the air ferment the batter — the tiny gas
          bubbles they release make the idli fluffy
BREAD     the same idea: it rises because something living is making gas
PAKHALA / KANJI   fermented rice, eaten across India — it cools the
          body and is full of helpful bacteria
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY IDLI BATTER MUST BE LEFT OVERNIGHT, AND WHY IT DOUBLES IN SIZE.**
>
> **Microbes from the air settle into the batter and feed on it.** As they feed they release tiny bubbles of gas.
>
> **The bubbles are trapped inside the thick batter and cannot escape**, so the batter puffs up — sometimes to twice its volume.
>
> **When it is steamed, those trapped bubbles set in place, and that is the soft, holey texture of an idli.** The fluffiness IS the microbes' work, preserved in the cooked food.
>
> **Batter left in a cold room does not rise, for exactly the reason a fridge preserves food** — microbes slow down in the cold.

**When your stomach is upset,** curd or buttermilk often helps: **its microbes reinforce the helpful microbes already in your gut.** For anything severe, a doctor and medicine are needed.

## 3.5 Choosing food well

```
SEASONAL   fruits eaten in their own season taste best, cost least
           and are freshest
LOCAL      food grown nearby has travelled less, so it arrives fresher
           and needs less preserving on the way
FRESH      look for firm skin, good colour, no soft or dark patches,
           no bad smell
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY A LOCAL MANGO BEATS A FAR-TRAVELLED ONE, EVEN IF BOTH LOOK THE SAME.**
>
> **A fruit that must travel for days has to be picked BEFORE it is ripe**, so it survives the journey. It then ripens in a truck rather than on the tree.
>
> **It may also need refrigeration or chemical treatment on the way.**
>
> **A local mango is picked ripe and eaten within a day or two** — no compromise was needed. **The difference you taste is really a difference in distance.**

> [!TIP]
> **Waiting for a seasonal fruit is not a hardship — it is part of the pleasure.** Lychees after a year's wait taste better than lychees available all year. **Scarcity is one of the ingredients.**

## 3.6 What happens to food in your mouth

**Four kinds of teeth, each shaped for a different job:**

| Tooth | Shape | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Incisors** | flat and sharp-edged, at the front | **cut** and bite |
| **Canines** | pointed | **tear** |
| **Premolars** | broad with bumps | **crush** |
| **Molars** | large and flat-topped, at the back | **grind** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY THE CUTTING TEETH ARE AT THE FRONT AND THE GRINDING TEETH AT THE BACK.**
>
> **The front is where food arrives, so that is where it must be bitten off** — and a thin sharp edge does that best.
>
> **The back is close to the jaw hinge, where the bite is strongest** — as with a nutcracker, the closer to the pivot, the greater the force. **Grinding needs force, so the grinders belong there.**
>
> **The tongue's job is to keep passing the food backwards**, from cutting to tearing to crushing to grinding, in that order.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY CHEWING PROPERLY MATTERS MORE THAN IT SEEMS — two reasons, not one.**
>
> **1. It breaks food into small pieces.** Small pieces have far more surface for the digestive juices to work on, so digestion is faster and more complete.
>
> **2. It releases SALIVA, and saliva begins the digestion right there in your mouth.** The longer you chew, the more saliva flows.
>
> **So gulping food down does not just risk choking — it sends the stomach a job that should already have been half done.** That is one common cause of indigestion.

**Oral hygiene:**
```
rinse your mouth after eating   — removes the food bits microbes feed on
brush regularly                 — the same, plus the sticky film they build
eat fewer sweets                — sugar is a microbe's favourite food
see a dentist for pain or a check-up
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHAT A CAVITY ACTUALLY IS, AND WHY SWEETS CAUSE THEM.**
>
> **A cavity is a HOLE in a tooth. It is not made by the sweet — it is made by microbes living on your teeth.**
>
> **They feed on the sugar left behind after eating and produce acid, and the acid slowly dissolves the hard surface of the tooth.**
>
> **So the danger is not how much sugar you eat but how LONG it stays on your teeth.** One sweet eaten quickly and rinsed away is far safer than the same sweet sucked slowly all afternoon.
>
> **And this is why brushing works.** It removes the food before the microbes can turn it into acid.

> [!TIP]
> **Avoiding choking:** take smaller mouthfuls, chew properly before swallowing, eat slowly, sit down to eat, and **do not talk or laugh with food in your mouth.** **The reason for the last one: the same passage carries food and air, and laughing opens the air route at the wrong moment.**
