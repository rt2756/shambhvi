## 4.1 What a "green school" means

**A green school is one that:**
```
manages its WASTE          separates it and sends what can be reused
SAVES WATER                fixes leaks and catches rainwater
uses ELECTRICITY carefully lights and fans off when nobody is there
grows GREEN                plants trees and looks after them
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY EACH ITEM ON THAT LIST IS "SAVE SOMETHING THAT IS EASY TO WASTE".**
>
> **Water, electricity and materials all share a strange property: nobody notices them leaving.** A dripping tap makes no mess; a fan running in an empty room looks exactly like a fan doing its job.
>
> **So they are wasted by DEFAULT, not by decision.** Nobody chooses to waste them.
>
> **That is why a green school starts with LOOKING, not with rules.** You cannot fix what nobody has noticed.

> [!NOTE]
> **Swachh Bharat Swachh Vidyalaya** (Clean India, Clean Schools) is part of the Swachh Bharat Mission. It covers **clean toilets, safe drinking water, proper waste management and hygiene education** — because **every child has the right to study in a clean and safe environment.**

## 4.2 School explorer teams — how to look properly

| Team | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **Water watchers** | leaking taps · water running unused · are tanks clean and covered · any wet patches where mosquitoes could breed |
| **Electricity savers** | lights and fans left on in empty rooms · do classrooms use natural light · which rooms are hottest and coolest |
| **Waste warriors** | where does litter collect and what kind · enough bins · are they used · separate bins for wet and dry |
| **Green guardians** | which trees and plants are there · who cares for them · where could more go · do birds and butterflies visit · is there a compost pit |
| **Traffic trackers** | what happens at the gate at arrival and exit · is there a pedestrian crossing · do vehicles speed past |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY THE TEAMS ARE GIVEN QUESTIONS RATHER THAN TOLD TO "LOOK AROUND".**
>
> **A person told to look around sees what they already expected.** A person given a specific question — "are the water tanks covered?" — goes and finds out.
>
> **This is exactly how scientists work.** You cannot observe everything, so you decide in advance what you are observing FOR.
>
> **The question comes first, the looking second.** Reversing that order is the difference between an investigation and a wander.

> [!TIP]
> **After looking, four questions are worth asking together:** What did you find? Was anything concerning? Did anything surprise you? What made you proud? **The "surprise" question is the most valuable — a surprise is the sign that you had a belief that was wrong.**

## 4.3 Waste, and why separating it matters

```
REDUCE   use only what you need — the waste that is never made
         costs nothing to deal with
REUSE    do not throw away things that still work
RECYCLE  send what can be remade into something new
```
**And what is left is separated:**

| Bin | Colour | What goes in |
|---|---|---|
| **Wet waste** | **green** | food peels, leftovers, garden waste — things that rot |
| **Dry waste** | **blue** | paper, plastic, glass, metal, cloth — things that do not rot |
| **For the recycler** | kept aside | old newspapers, bottles, scrap metal |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY MIXING WET AND DRY WASTE RUINS BOTH.**
>
> **Wet waste can be composted — it rots down into useful soil.** But not if there is plastic and glass mixed into it.
>
> **Dry waste can be recycled — paper into new paper, plastic into new plastic.** But wet food waste soaks paper and dirties everything, and dirty material cannot be recycled.
>
> **So separated, both halves are worth something. Mixed, both halves are rubbish.**
>
> **The separating takes two seconds and has to happen at the moment of throwing.** Nobody can un-mix it afterwards — which is why it is your job and not the sanitation worker's.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY OLD NEWSPAPERS ARE NEVER PUT IN A BIN AT ALL.**
>
> **They are worth money.** Traditional recyclers buy them, and different recyclers collect different things — paper, metal, bottles, plastic.
>
> **This is one of the oldest recycling systems in the world and it works because it is a TRADE, not a favour.** Both sides gain.
>
> **The people who do this work, and the sanitation workers who handle everything else, make the whole system possible.** Separating your waste properly is a way of respecting their work as well as protecting their safety — sharp glass and medical waste in a mixed bin injures the person who opens it.

## 4.4 Keeping a building cool without electricity

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE BLACK TILE AND THE WHITE TILE — a two-minute experiment with a large consequence.**
>
> **Paint one tile black and one white and leave both in the sun. After a while, touch them: the black one is noticeably hotter.**
>
> **Why: a black surface ABSORBS most of the sunlight falling on it and turns it into heat.** A white surface **REFLECTS** most of it straight back — so that energy never becomes heat in the tile at all.
>
> **This is why a white roof keeps a building cooler**, and why light-coloured clothes are more comfortable in summer than dark ones.
>
> **Nothing is being "kept out" by the white paint.** The light simply bounces away instead of being taken in.

**Other things that keep a room cool:**
```
SHADE TREES     stop the sunlight before it reaches the wall at all
WINDOWS placed so a breeze can pass through — moving air carries heat away
THICK WALLS     take a long time to warm through, so the inside stays
                cool through the hottest part of the day
CLAY POTS (matkas) cool water by letting a little seep through and
                evaporate, taking heat with it — no electricity at all
COW DUNG AND MUD FLOORS  used in many villages: they keep insects away
                and keep the house cool
```
> [!TIP]
> **Notice that every one of these is a DESIGN choice, made once, that keeps working for free.** A fan cools a room only while electricity flows into it. **A tree, a white roof and a well-placed window cool a room for thirty years and are never switched on.**

## 4.5 A dripping tap

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE ACTIVITY THAT MAKES AN INVISIBLE LOSS VISIBLE.**
>
> **Put a glass under a dripping tap and time how long it takes to fill.**
>
> ```
> if a glass (about 250 ml) fills in 10 minutes:
>    in one hour  →  6 glasses  = 1.5 litres
>    in one day   →  144 glasses = about 36 litres
>    in one month →  more than 1,000 litres
> ```
> **Why this is so surprising: a drop is obviously nothing.** The mistake is judging the drop instead of the drip — the drop is tiny, but it repeats every second of every day, whether anyone is at school or not.
>
> **A leak works while you sleep. That is what makes it big.**

> [!TIP]
> **Compare this with turning the tap off while brushing your teeth.** That is a good habit, but it acts for two minutes a day. **Fixing one leaking tap can save more than a whole class's careful habits.** **Look for the losses that happen without anyone present** — they are always the large ones.

> [!NOTE]
> **The Jal Shakti Abhiyan** is a national campaign to save and protect water — planting trees, catching rainwater and using water wisely. **In Ladakh, students help build "ice stupas"** — tall towers of ice that freeze in winter and slowly melt in summer to water school gardens, exactly when the water is needed.

## 4.6 Trees and greenery

**Trees do far more than look pleasant:**
```
give SHADE and keep the surroundings cool
keep the AIR clean
provide FOOD and shelter to birds and insects
their roots hold the soil and help rain soak in
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE THERMOMETER EXPERIMENT, AND WHY THE ANSWER IS BIGGER THAN "SHADE IS COOLER".**
>
> **Put two glasses of water out — one under a tree and one in the open sun — and measure both at the start and an hour later.**
>
> **The glass in the sun warms much more.** The tree's canopy stopped the sunlight before it arrived.
>
> **But a tree does more than block light.** Water evaporating from its leaves takes heat away with it, so the air under a tree is cooler than the air under a sheet of tin the same size.
>
> **A tree is a shade AND a cooler.** That is why a row of trees along a street lowers the temperature of the whole street, not just the strip beneath them.

> [!TIP]
> **A butterfly garden works because butterflies are choosy.** Certain plants attract certain butterflies — so planting the right flowers brings them, and the birds that follow them. **You are not asking wildlife to arrive; you are giving it a reason to.**

## 4.7 Safety near school

**Traffic:**
```
THREE RULES worth knowing
   cross only at a pedestrian crossing, and look both ways first
   walk on the footpath; if there is none, walk facing the traffic
   never step out from between parked vehicles — the driver cannot see you
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY "WALK FACING THE TRAFFIC" IS THE RULE WHEN THERE IS NO FOOTPATH.**
>
> **If you walk with your back to the traffic, you cannot see a vehicle coming towards you and must rely entirely on the driver seeing you.**
>
> **Facing it, you can see the danger and step aside.** You have given yourself the ability to react.
>
> **Every good safety rule works like this: it puts you in a position where YOU can act**, rather than one where you can only hope somebody else does.

**A signboard for the school gate** should say one clear thing, in few words or a symbol, so it can be read by a driver in one second. **Other measures: a speed breaker, a marked crossing, a staggered exit time so the crowd is smaller, and adults on duty at the gate.**

**Fire safety — the drill, and the reason behind each step:**

| Step | Why |
|---|---|
| **Move quickly and calmly. Do not run.** | running causes falls and blocked doorways; panic spreads faster than fire |
| **Crawl low under smoke, mouth covered** | **smoke rises**, so the cleanest air is near the floor — and smoke harms people long before flames reach them |
| **Do not hide in closets or bathrooms** | rescuers cannot find you, and there is no way out |
| **Help others only if it is safe** | a second person in danger helps nobody |
| **NEVER go back inside** | conditions change in seconds; more people die returning than in the original escape |
| **Go to the assembly point and stay with your group** | so a head count can be done |
| **Tell staff at once if anyone is missing** | rescuers need to know WHO and WHERE, immediately |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY A DRILL IS PRACTISED WHEN THERE IS NO FIRE.**
>
> **In a real emergency there is no time to read instructions or make a plan.** People do what they have already done before.
>
> **A drill installs the route and the actions into your body**, so that fear does not have to be overcome first.
>
> **That is the entire purpose: not to learn the rules, but to make them automatic.**

## 4.8 How we behave

**A school is not only its buildings. It is also the way people treat each other in it.**

```
waiting for your turn at the tap
using kind words, not teasing
not writing on desks and walls
picking up your own litter
speaking up when someone else is being unkind
being quiet where others need quiet
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY THE WATCHER MATTERS AS MUCH AS THE DOER.**
>
> **When one student scribbles on a bench and another watches and says nothing, the second student has taught the first that it is acceptable.**
>
> **And when a younger child copies someone throwing a wrapper, the litter was not the only thing passed on.**
>
> **Behaviour spreads by example far more than by instruction.** Which is why "I did not do anything" is not quite the same as "I did nothing wrong".
>
> **Speaking up is hard, and there are gentler versions of it** — picking the wrapper up yourself, telling a teacher, or simply not joining in.

> [!TIP]
> **A useful test before acting anywhere — school, home or a public park: would this be all right if EVERYONE did it?**
>
> **One person cutting the queue barely matters; everyone cutting the queue means there is no queue.** **One group being noisy in a park spoils the yoga class beside them.**
>
> **Most rules about behaviour are this one question, already answered.**
