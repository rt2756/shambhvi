## 5.1 Two national days, and the difference between them

```
15 AUGUST 1947   INDEPENDENCE DAY — India became FREE
26 JANUARY 1950  REPUBLIC DAY — India adopted its CONSTITUTION
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY WE CELEBRATE TWO DAYS AND NOT ONE — the question at the heart of this chapter.**
>
> **Becoming free answered one question: who decides? Not a foreign ruler any more — Indians themselves.**
>
> **But freedom is not the same as "everyone can now do whatever they like".** A country with no rules is not free; it is simply unsafe for whoever is weakest.
>
> **So the second question had to be answered too: HOW shall we decide?** That answer is the **Constitution** — the book of rules by which India governs itself.
>
> **Independence Day marks the freedom. Republic Day marks the rules that make the freedom work.** Two and a half years separate them because writing that book took that long.

> [!NOTE]
> **India's Constitution is the longest written constitution of any country in the world**, and India is the world's largest democracy.

| **White** (middle) | peace and truth |

## 5.2 A currency note as a window on India

**Look closely at any Indian note and it tells you a surprising amount about the country.**

```
LANGUAGES     the value is written in many languages — India has more
              than a thousand spoken languages
GANDHIJI      his portrait — and a SECOND, smaller image hidden in the
              paper, visible only when held up to the light
SPECTACLES    the round spectacles are the symbol of the SWACHH BHARAT
              ABHIYAN (Clean India Mission)
EMBLEM        the three lions of the National Emblem, with the
              Ashoka Chakra below
MONUMENT      a different famous monument on each denomination
RAISED MARKS  bumps and shapes that can be FELT
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY THERE IS A SECOND, HIDDEN PICTURE OF GANDHIJI IN THE PAPER.**
>
> **It is a WATERMARK — made by making the paper thinner and thicker in a pattern while it is still being made.** Thin parts let more light through, so the image appears when you hold the note up to the light.
>
> **Why it exists: it cannot be added afterwards by printing.** It has to be built into the paper itself, which is very hard to copy — so it proves the note is genuine.
>
> **A hidden picture is not decoration. It is a lock.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY THE NOTES CARRY RAISED MARKS YOU CAN FEEL.**
>
> **A person who cannot see must still be able to tell a ₹100 note from a ₹500 note.** Colour and printed numbers are no help.
>
> **So each denomination has raised prints and shapes in different positions, which can be identified by TOUCH.** The **MANI app** helps too, by identifying a note aloud.
>
> **This is what good design means: the thing works for everybody who has to use it, not just for most people.** Money that some citizens cannot use safely would not be doing its job.

**The National Emblem** — three lions on a circular platform, with the Ashoka Chakra below — **stands for strength, courage and confidence.** It appears on official documents, including your Aadhaar card.

> [!NOTE]
> **Lions once roamed over vast areas of India.** Today wild Asiatic lions are found only in the **Gir forests of Gujarat**. About 150 years ago their number had fallen to a few dozen — **and careful protection has brought it back to several hundred.** A national symbol that very nearly disappeared.

## 5.3 National and state symbols

```
NATIONAL ANIMAL   the TIGER — orange with black stripes, ruler of
                  the forest
NATIONAL BIRD     the PEACOCK — green and blue feathers, a crown on
                  its head, and it dances in the rain
```
**States have their own symbols too — a state animal, bird, tree and flower.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHAT A SYMBOL IS ACTUALLY FOR.**
>
> **A symbol is a short way of saying something long.** The three lions do not describe strength — they *stand in* for it, so a whole idea can be printed in a corner of a document.
>
> **This only works if everybody agrees on the meaning.** A symbol nobody recognises is just a picture.
>
> **That is why national symbols are taught, displayed and repeated** — the agreeing is the whole mechanism.

> [!TIP]
> **Designing an emblem for your school is a genuine exercise in this.** You must ask: what one idea matters most here? What shape or object stands for it? **Will everyone who sees it understand?** **If it needs a paragraph of explanation, it is not yet a symbol.**

## 5.4 Languages

**More than a thousand languages are spoken in India, and each carries its own history, songs and stories.**

```
mango is  māmpalam  in Tamil
          mamidipandu in Telugu
          amba       in Marathi
          aam        in Bengali and Hindi
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY THE SAME FRUIT HAS SO MANY NAMES — and why that is not a problem to be fixed.**
>
> **Languages did not spread out from one place by a plan.** They grew where people lived, over thousands of years, each borrowing and inventing as it went.
>
> **A language is not just a set of labels for things.** It carries the songs, jokes, proverbs and stories that were made in it — and those cannot simply be swapped into another language.
>
> **So when a language is lost, what is lost is not a list of words.** It is everything that was only ever said in it.

> [!TIP]
> **Translation apps are useful and are not a replacement.** They can tell you what a word means; they cannot tell you what it feels like to a person who grew up with it. **The best reason to learn another Indian language is not to be understood but to understand.**

## 5.5 Diversity as strength — the garden and the forest

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE COMPARISON THIS CHAPTER IS BUILT ON.**
>
> ```
> A GARDEN  has few kinds of plant. It needs constant watering, weeding
>           and care. Left alone for a month, it dies.
>
> A FOREST  has many kinds of plant, tree, animal, bird and insect.
>           Nobody waters it. It looks after itself — and grows
>           stronger while doing so.
> ```
> **Why the forest survives without help: the different living things DO EACH OTHER'S WORK.** Fallen leaves feed the soil; insects pollinate; birds spread seeds; predators keep numbers in balance.
>
> **The variety is not decoration on top of a working system. The variety IS the working system.**
>
> **And if one kind of tree fails in a forest, the others carry on.** In a garden of one plant, one disease ends everything.
>
> **"Diversity makes our country stronger" is this same idea, applied to people** — many skills, many traditions, many ways of solving a problem.

## 5.6 Diversity you can see and hear

**Headgear** tells you where someone is from, what they believe, or what they do:
```
saafa or pagri  Rajasthan        topi  Himachal Pradesh
and many more, worn especially at weddings and formal occasions
```
> [!TIP]
> **Headgear is not only tradition — much of it is practical.** A cloth wrapped round the head **protects from heat in a desert and from cold in the mountains**. **Helmets** for soldiers, miners, construction workers, motorcyclists and cyclists do the same job for injury. **Wearing a helmet on a two-wheeler is compulsory, and it is the same idea a saafa began with.**

**Drums** — one simple instrument, made differently everywhere:
```
pakhawaj · dhama · khanjira · dhol · mridangam · dholak
thavil · idakka · khol · nagara · tabla
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY ONE INSTRUMENT BECAME SO MANY.**
>
> **A drum is a skin stretched over a hollow body — and both parts were made from whatever was locally available.** Different wood, different clay, different hides, different sizes.
>
> **So each region's drum sounds different because each region's materials and music are different**, and the two shaped each other: the music grew to suit the drum, and the drum was changed to suit the music.
>
> **The variety is not random.** It is a record of where each instrument came from.

**Music and dance** are ways of celebrating, praying, telling stories and bringing people together. **Mridangam** belongs to Carnatic music; **sitar** and **shehnai** to Hindustani music. **Every state has dance forms that began there.**

## 5.7 Old and new together

**Modern India launches rockets, runs the world's largest digital payment system (UPI) and built Aadhaar, the world's biggest digital ID.** It also practises **ayurveda, yoga and meditation**, thousands of years old.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THESE MODERN CHANGES ARE NOT REPLACING THE OLD ONES — they are adding to them.**
>
> **A child can learn coding and Bharatanatyam.** A farmer can use a weather app and follow a sowing tradition older than the app's country.
>
> **Why this is worth stating: it is easy to assume that new things must push old ones out.** Often they simply sit alongside.
>
> **The threads metaphor is the chapter's own: many different threads woven into one strong, colourful fabric.** A cloth of one thread would be weaker and far duller.
