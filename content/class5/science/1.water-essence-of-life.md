## 1.1 How much water can we actually use?

**Most of the Earth's surface is covered with water — but almost all of it is SALTY.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE GLASS AND THE TEASPOON — the picture worth remembering.**
>
> **If ALL the water on Earth were a 200 ml glass, the freshwater would be about one 5 ml teaspoonful.**
>
> **Everything humans, animals, birds, crops and forests drink and grow with comes out of that teaspoon.** The rest of the glass is ocean.
>
> **This is why "the Earth has plenty of water" is a dangerous thing to believe.** It has plenty of *water*; it has very little water we can use.

**Why we cannot drink ocean water**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SALTY WATER MAKES YOU THIRSTIER, NOT LESS THIRSTY.**
>
> **Your body has to get rid of the extra salt, and it can only do that by passing it out dissolved in water.**
>
> **So drinking seawater costs your body more water than it delivers.** A person adrift at sea who drinks seawater dries out faster than one who drinks nothing.
>
> **That is why "there is water everywhere" does not help a sailor.**

**But ocean water is far from useless:**
```
SALT       — the salt pans of Gujarat are vast flatlands where seawater is
             dried in the sun and the salt collected; one of India's
             largest salt-producing areas
FISHING    — a huge part of India's food
TRANSPORT  — ships carry most of the world's goods
EVAPORATION — the oceans are where nearly all rain begins
```

**Where the freshwater is:**

| Source | Where it is |
|---|---|
| **Rivers, ponds, lakes** | on the surface — **surface water** |
| **Groundwater** | soaked deep into soil and rock |
| **Glaciers and ice caps** | frozen solid in the mountains and poles |
| **Rain** | falling from the sky, refilling all the others |

> [!NOTE]
> **Wular Lake** in Jammu and Kashmir is one of the largest freshwater lakes in Asia. It also **stores extra river water during heavy rain**, which prevents flooding downstream — a lake doing a job, not just sitting there.

## 1.2 The three forms of water

```
SOLID    ice, snow, hail, glaciers
LIQUID   rain, rivers, the sea, what comes from the tap
GAS      water vapour — invisible, in the air all around you
```

**Water changes from one form into another when it is heated or cooled.**

| Change | What happens | Name |
|---|---|---|
| ice → water | heat it | **melting** |
| water → vapour | heat it | **evaporation** (boiling is fast evaporation) |
| vapour → water | cool it | **condensation** |
| water → ice | cool it | **freezing** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE ACTIVITY THAT SURPRISES EVERYONE: put ice in a steel glass and water droplets appear on the OUTSIDE.**
>
> **Where does that water come from? NOT from inside — steel has no holes, and the outside stays wet even if the glass is sealed.**
>
> **It comes out of the AIR.** There is always invisible water vapour in the air around us. **The cold glass cools the air touching it, and cool air cannot hold as much vapour** — so the extra turns back into liquid on the cold surface.
>
> **This is CONDENSATION, and it is the single most important idea in the water cycle.** It is also what makes dew form on grass at dawn and mist appear on a window in winter.

> [!TIP]
> **Leave a saucer of water in sunlight for three days and it disappears.** **It has not been destroyed — it has EVAPORATED into the air**, one invisible particle at a time. **Water that vanishes has always gone somewhere.**

## 1.3 The water cycle

```
        ☀  the Sun heats water in oceans, rivers, lakes and soil
                              ↓
        EVAPORATION  — water rises as invisible vapour
                              ↓
        CONDENSATION — high up it is cold, so vapour becomes
                       tiny droplets → CLOUDS
                              ↓
        PRECIPITATION — droplets join, grow heavy and fall as
                        rain, snow or hail
                              ↓
        COLLECTION  — water returns to rivers, lakes, oceans
                      and soaks into the ground
                              ↓
                       and it begins again
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY THE WATER CYCLE NEVER RUNS OUT — and why that does NOT mean water is unlimited.**
>
> **Water is not used up. It moves and changes form, over and over.** The water in your glass today has been through rivers, clouds and dinosaurs before.
>
> **But the cycle takes TIME, and it does not deliver water where and when we want it.** Rain may fall on the sea instead of the field; a river may be far from the village.
>
> **So the total amount never falls — but the amount available HERE, NOW, and CLEAN certainly can.** That is what a drought is.

> [!TIP]
> **The bag experiment models the whole cycle in an afternoon.** Coloured water sealed in a transparent bag, left in the sun: **the water heats and evaporates, droplets form on the cool upper inside of the bag, and eventually run back down.**
>
> **Nothing gets in or out of the sealed bag — and yet water keeps moving.** That is the cycle in miniature, and it proves the water is being *recycled*, not created.

## 1.4 Groundwater

**When rain falls, some of it soaks into the soil and sinks slowly through layers of soil and rock. Water stored deep underground is GROUNDWATER.**

```
we reach it with:  wells · borewells · tube wells · handpumps
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY A CITY OF CONCRETE SLOWLY RUNS OUT OF GROUNDWATER.**
>
> **Rain can only sink in where there are GAPS for it to sink through** — soil has spaces between its particles; concrete does not.
>
> **On a paved road or a cemented courtyard, rainwater simply runs off into a drain and away.** It never reaches the ground below.
>
> **So the more a city is paved, the less its groundwater is RECHARGED** — even though exactly the same amount of rain falls on it. **The water arrives and is turned away at the door.**
>
> **Meanwhile the same city keeps pumping groundwater OUT.** Taking out more than goes in has only one ending.

| Surface | Does rain soak in? |
|---|---|
| **Green lawn, open soil, forest floor** | **Yes — well** |
| **Concrete road** | **No** |
| **Paved area** | **No** |

**Ways to help rain get back underground:**
```
soak pits · ponds and human-made lakes · planting more trees
leaving open, uncovered ground around buildings
```
> [!NOTE]
> **Johads** in Rajasthan are traditional small earthen dams built to catch rainwater and let it seep down to recharge groundwater — an old technology doing exactly what a modern soak pit does. **Hiware Bazar** in Maharashtra became a "water-positive" village by rainwater harvesting and careful watershed management.

## 1.5 Surface water and the shape of the land

**A river usually begins high in the mountains and flows down across the land.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY WATER FLOWS WHERE IT DOES: IT ALWAYS GOES DOWNHILL, AND THE LAND DECIDES WHICH DOWNHILL.**
>
> **The mustard-seed activity shows this beautifully.** Pour seeds onto crumpled paper covered by a smooth sheet: **the seeds do not travel in straight lines. They follow the folds, curve round the high bits and collect in the low bits.**
>
> **Water does exactly the same.** It flows, stops, or curves according to the shape of the land in its path — and it gathers in the hollows, which is why lakes and ponds are where they are.
>
> **And the traffic goes both ways: over long times, the flowing water also WEARS AWAY and RESHAPES the land it runs over.** Valleys and gorges are carved by the very rivers that run through them.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY SOME INDIAN RIVERS REACH THE BAY OF BENGAL AND OTHERS THE ARABIAN SEA.**
>
> **Because of which way the land slopes where they start.** A range of mountains acts like the ridge on the crumpled paper: **rain falling on one side runs one way, rain falling on the other side runs the other.**
>
> ```
> to the BAY OF BENGAL   — Ganga, Brahmaputra, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi
>                          (most of India tilts gently eastward)
> to the ARABIAN SEA     — Narmada, Tapi, Mahi, Sabarmati
> ```
> **Two raindrops landing a metre apart on a ridge can end up in seas a thousand kilometres apart.**

> [!NOTE]
> **The Luni is the exception that proves the rule.** Rising in the Aravalli range in Rajasthan, it is the only major Indian river that **reaches no sea at all** — it ends in the marshy Rann of Kutch in Gujarat, because there is no downhill path onwards.

**Landforms water makes and meets:**
```
ice cap · glacier · mountain · waterfall · valley · river · lake · dune · desert
```

## 1.6 Life in and around water

**Water is a completely different place to live from land, so the living things there have different equipment.**

| Living on land | Living in water |
|---|---|
| breathes air with **lungs** | many breathe dissolved oxygen through **gills** |
| **legs** for walking | **fins and a streamlined body** for swimming |
| body is supported by legs | **water holds the body up** |
| must find water to drink | surrounded by it |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY A FISH DIES IN AIR AND A DOG DROWNS IN WATER — the same reason both times.**
>
> **Each animal's breathing organ works in ONE medium.** Gills need to be surrounded by water to take oxygen out of it; in air they collapse and dry out. Lungs need air; filled with water they cannot work.
>
> **There IS oxygen in water — dissolved in it — and there IS water in air — as vapour.** But an animal can only reach the one its body is built for.
>
> **This is what "adapted to its habitat" really means:** not that the animal chose well, but that its body only functions in the place it belongs.

**Some freshwater neighbours:**

| Living thing | Its special feature |
|---|---|
| **Fish** | fins to swim; mouth often facing upward to feed at the surface |
| **Pond heron** | **long thin legs** for standing in shallow water; **long pointed beak** to spear fish |
| **Dragonfly** | flies fast, lays eggs near water, eats mosquitoes |
| **Water scorpion** | lives underwater but **breathes through a tail tube** reaching the surface |
| **Freshwater turtle** | soft-shelled, lives in ponds and rivers |
| **Water snake** | harmless; eats fish and frogs |
| **Reeds** | tall grass-like plants along the edges |
| **Lotus, water lily** | **rooted floating** — roots in the pond bed, flowers and leaves on the surface |
| **Water hyacinth** | **free-floating** — the whole plant drifts, and it spreads very fast |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY A LOTUS LEAF DOES NOT GET WATERLOGGED — and how you can prove it with a crayon.**
>
> **Put a drop of water on plain paper: it soaks in.** **Colour the paper thickly with a wax crayon and try again: the drop sits on top as a bead.**
>
> **Leaves that float have a WAXY COATING that does the same job.** Water rolls off instead of soaking in, so the leaf stays dry on top and the plant is not damaged.
>
> **The wax is not decoration — it is the plant's raincoat**, and without it a floating leaf would rot.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE FOOD CHAIN — and why removing one link matters to all of them.**
>
> ```
> tiny water plants → small fish → big fish → heron / crocodile / otter → humans
> ```
> **Every arrow means "is eaten by".** Take out the small fish and the big fish starve; take out the big fish and the small fish multiply until they eat all the plants.
>
> **A change anywhere travels in BOTH directions along the chain** — upward to those who ate it, downward to those it ate.
>
> **This is why "just one species" is never just one species.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Keibul Lamjao National Park** in Manipur is the **only floating national park in the world**, made of *phumdis* — floating mats of soil and plants — and home to the endangered **Sangai deer**. **The Fishing Cat**, found in India's wetlands, has **partly webbed paws** and dives for fish.
