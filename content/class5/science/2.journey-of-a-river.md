## 2.1 The Godavari, from source to sea

```
SOURCE     Brahmagiri Hills of the Western Ghats, at Trimbakeshwar,
           Maharashtra — it begins as a small STREAM
     ↓
   rainwater and smaller rivers join it, making it wider and stronger
     ↓
TRIBUTARIES  Manjira · Indravati · Sabari
     ↓
   flows through Maharashtra, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Odisha and
   Andhra Pradesh — 1,465 km in all
     ↓
MOUTH      spreads into many small streams — a DELTA — at the
           Coringa Mangrove Forests, and meets the Bay of Bengal
```
**It is the second longest river in India, after the Ganga, and is called *Dakshina Ganga* because it flows through southern India.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY A RIVER GETS BIGGER AS IT TRAVELS, EVEN THOUGH NOTHING PUSHES IT.**
>
> **Every stream that joins adds its own water and never takes any away.** Rain falling anywhere in the whole valley eventually drains into the same channel.
>
> **So the river collects, all along its length, and can only grow.** That is why the Godavari starts as a stream you could step across and ends wide enough to need a delta.
>
> **The smaller rivers joining are its TRIBUTARIES** — and they are joining it, not the other way round, because the land tilts that way.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Source** | where a river begins — usually high ground |
| **Tributary** | a smaller river or stream that joins a bigger one |
| **Mouth** | where a river ends, meeting a sea or lake |
| **Delta** | the fan of many small channels a river splits into at its mouth |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY A RIVER SPLITS INTO MANY CHANNELS AT ITS MOUTH.**
>
> **All the way down, the river has been carrying tiny bits of soil and sand, because moving water can carry things.**
>
> **Where it meets the sea, the water slows almost to a stop — and slow water cannot carry a load.** So the soil is dropped.
>
> **The dropped soil builds up into banks and islands, and the river has to find its way round them** — splitting again and again into the fan we call a delta.
>
> **A delta is a river's cargo, put down where it finally ran out of push.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Mangrove forests** like Coringa grow exactly where fresh river water meets salty sea water. **They are one of the few kinds of plant that can survive in salty, muddy, waterlogged ground** — which is why deltas are ringed with them and almost nothing else.

## 2.2 Perennial and seasonal rivers

```
PERENNIAL — flows all year round        (Godavari, Ganga)
SEASONAL  — flows only when it rains    (many rivers of the Deccan
                                         and of dry regions)
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY SOME RIVERS SURVIVE THE DRY SEASON AND OTHERS DO NOT.**
>
> **A river fed only by rain must stop when the rain stops.** But a river fed by **melting mountain snow, or by groundwater seeping in along its bed**, keeps receiving water even in the driest months.
>
> **So the question "is this river perennial?" is really the question "does it have a source other than this season's rain?"**
>
> **This matters enormously to the people beside it.** A perennial river can supply a city all year; a seasonal one leaves them to find water for six months.

## 2.3 What a river does for the people beside it

```
DRINKING and cooking and washing
IRRIGATION — water carried to fields in canals
INDUSTRY   — factories need large amounts of water
FISHING    — a livelihood for whole communities
TRANSPORT  — boats and ferries
ELECTRICITY — from dams
TOURISM and pilgrimage — Nashik, the Godavari Pushkaram
ECOTOURISM — visiting forests and rivers to watch wildlife
             WITHOUT harming the place
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY EVERY ANCIENT CITY IN THE WORLD WAS BUILT BESIDE A RIVER.**
>
> **Water is heavy and cannot be carried far.** A family needs many litres every day, and before pipes and pumps that meant living within walking distance of it.
>
> **A river also delivers, free of charge: fertile soil on its banks, fish, a route for boats, and water for crops.**
>
> **So a riverbank was not a pleasant choice — it was very nearly the only possible one.** Cities are still there today because cities, once built, do not move.

## 2.4 Dams — what they give and what they cost

**A dam is a giant wall that blocks a river's flow and stores water in a large reservoir. India has more than 900 of them on the Godavari system alone.**

| What a dam gives | What a dam costs |
|---|---|
| water stored for the **dry season** | the reservoir **floods land** upstream |
| **irrigation** for crops all year | **forests and wildlife habitats** are drowned |
| **electricity** from falling water | **people lose their homes** and must move away |
| some **flood control** downstream | less water and less silt reach the land downstream |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY A DAM'S BENEFITS AND ITS COSTS FALL ON DIFFERENT PEOPLE — and why that makes it so hard to decide.**
>
> **The water, the crops and the electricity go to towns and farms DOWNSTREAM, often far away.**
>
> **The flooded land, the lost forest and the moved villages are all UPSTREAM, close by.**
>
> **So the people who gain and the people who lose are not the same people.** This is why building a dam is never simply an engineering question — it is a question about who is being asked to give up what, and what they get in return.

> [!TIP]
> **Some of the costs can be reduced, though never removed:** resettling displaced families properly with land and work; leaving a minimum flow of water in the river below the dam; planting new forest elsewhere; building fish ladders so migrating fish can still get past. **A cost that is planned for is a much smaller cost than one that is ignored.**

## 2.5 Pollution — including the kind you cannot see

**Waste, plastic and dirty water from homes and factories reach the river. Sometimes it looks and smells dirty. Sometimes it does not — and that is more dangerous.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE SUGAR-AND-OIL EXPERIMENT, AND WHY IT IS REALLY ABOUT SAFETY.**
>
> **Stir a spoon of sugar into one glass of water and a spoon of oil into another.**
> ```
> SUGAR — DISSOLVES: it disappears completely and the water looks clear
> OIL   — DOES NOT dissolve: it floats in visible blobs
> ```
> **The alarming part is the sugar glass.** The sugar is entirely there — you could taste it — and yet nothing can be seen.
>
> **Harmful chemicals, fertilisers and soapy waste behave like the sugar, not the oil.** They dissolve, and the water still looks perfectly clean.
>
> **So "it looks clean" is not evidence that water is safe to drink.** You cannot judge water by eye, and that single fact is why water has to be tested.

```
DISSOLVE in water        salt · sugar · many chemicals · fertilisers ·
                         detergents
DO NOT dissolve          oil · sand · plastic · chalk powder
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE "GREEN BLANKET" — how fertiliser from a field kills fish in a river.**
>
> **Fertiliser makes plants grow fast. That is its whole purpose.**
>
> **When rain washes it off the fields into the river, it does exactly the same thing there** — tiny water plants and algae multiply until they cover the surface like a green blanket.
>
> **Then two things go wrong, and both are fatal:**
> - **The blanket blocks sunlight**, so the plants below it die.
> - **All those dead plants rot, and rotting uses up the oxygen dissolved in the water.**
>
> **Fish suffocate in water that is choked with plant life.** A river covered in green looks full of life and is being killed by it.
>
> **Nothing here was poisonous.** The harm came entirely from too much of a good thing arriving in the wrong place.

> [!NOTE]
> **The Namami Gange programme** works to clean the Ganga so it stays healthy for people, fish and birds. **The Ganges river dolphin** — India's national aquatic animal — is **blind, and finds its way by sound**, which makes it especially vulnerable to noisy, polluted water.

## 2.6 When a river floods

**Too much rain, all at once, and the river swells, speeds up, deepens and overflows its banks.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY CUTTING DOWN TREES MAKES FLOODS WORSE — even though trees do not touch the river.**
>
> ```
> WITH TREES     leaves break the fall of the rain → it lands gently on
>                soft soil → roots keep the soil loose → water SOAKS IN
>                → it reaches the river slowly, over days
>
> WITHOUT TREES  rain hits bare hard soil → cannot soak in → runs off
>                the surface fast → ALL of it reaches the river at once
> ```
> **The same amount of rain falls in both cases. What changes is the TIMING.**
>
> **A river can carry a week's rain over a week. It cannot carry a week's rain in an afternoon.**
>
> **So deforestation does not create water — it delivers the same water all at the same moment**, which is exactly what a flood is.

**What a flood damages, and what that affects:**

| Flood problem | What it affects |
|---|---|
| Dirty water | **safe drinking**, and health |
| Broken roads | **travel and transport** |
| Lost crops | **food supply** |
| No electricity | **lights, fans and phones** |
| Spread of diseases | **people's health** |
| Damaged homes | **shelter** |
| Schools shut | **learning** |

> [!TIP]
> **Flood safety, in three stages — and each stage has a different logic.**
> ```
> BEFORE  stay informed through local news · prepare an emergency bag with
>         food, medicine and a torch · know the evacuation routes ·
>         keep sandbags, ladder and rope in flood-prone homes ·
>         LEAVE BEFORE the flooding starts
>
> DURING  disconnect electricity and gas · do NOT walk or drive through
>         flood water · get to higher ground · follow evacuation orders ·
>         free cattle so they can reach safety themselves
>
> AFTER   avoid contact with flood water and never swim in it · do not
>         touch power lines · do not return until it is declared safe ·
>         tell family you are safe · clean and disinfect everything
> ```
> **Why "do not walk through flood water" is repeated so often: moving water only knee-deep can knock an adult over**, and you cannot see holes, open drains or live wires under muddy water.

## 2.7 When a river runs dry

**Too little water is as serious as too much.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY DROUGHT FORCES PEOPLE TO MOVE, WHEN A FLOOD USUALLY DOES NOT — permanently, at least.**
>
> **A flood is a disaster that ends.** The water goes down, and people rebuild in the same place.
>
> **A drought does not end on any known date.** If the wells and the river stay dry for months, there is nothing to wait for — so families leave for places where water is still available, and often do not return.
>
> **Animals suffer in exactly the same way and have even fewer choices.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Both extremes happen in the same country in the same few years.** **Chennai** had to run special water trains from other districts in 2019 when its reservoirs dried up, while **Bengaluru** was flooded in 2022. **Managing water means preparing for too little AND too much.**

**Some responses:**
```
WATER ATMs      — machines that give clean water for a card or coin,
                  in places without reliable piped supply
NATIONAL WATER MISSION — works to manage water and encourage wise use
                  across India
```

> [!TIP]
> **What one household can actually do — and why each item works:**
> ```
> turn off the tap while brushing   — a running tap wastes several litres
>                                     for thirty seconds of use
> fix leaking taps                  — a slow drip wastes far more over a
>                                     month than any single act of care saves
> reuse the water from washing vegetables to water plants
>                                   — it was never dirty enough to throw away
> do not waste drinking water       — treated water costs the most to make
> ```
> **The pattern behind all of them: stop the losses that happen without anybody noticing.** Those are always bigger than the ones you can see.
