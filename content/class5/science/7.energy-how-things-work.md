## 7.1 What energy is

**Look around a kitchen, a street or a forest and you see the same five things happening again and again:**
```
things MOVING          a fan, a car, a bird, boiling water
things giving LIGHT    a bulb, a diya, the Sun
things making SOUND    a guitar, a horn, a voice
things getting HOT     food cooking, water in the sun
things getting COLD    a fridge, water in a matka
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ENERGY IS WHAT MAKES ANY OF THAT HAPPEN.**
>
> **You cannot see energy itself. You only ever see what it DOES** — something moves, shines, sounds, warms or cools.
>
> **So the five things in that list are not five separate topics. They are the five signs that energy is at work.**
>
> **The useful question to ask about anything: what is it doing, and where did the energy come from?** Those two questions will carry you through the whole chapter.

**Three experiments, three signs of energy:**

| Do this | What happens | Energy shows itself as |
|---|---|---|
| Blow up a balloon and let it go | it flies across the room | **movement** |
| Stretch rubber bands over a box and pluck them | they **vibrate** and you hear a note | **sound** |
| Leave one cup of water in sun, one in shade | the sunlit one is warmer | **heat** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY THE BALLOON MOVES FORWARD WHEN AIR RUSHES BACKWARD.**
>
> **The air does not push against the room — it pushes against the BALLOON itself as it leaves.**
>
> **Everything that pushes gets pushed back.** The balloon pushes air out one way and is pushed the other way.
>
> **This is exactly how a rocket works**, which is why the balloon-on-a-string is called a balloon rocket. **A rocket in empty space has nothing to push against and still moves — because it is pushing on its own exhaust.**

> [!TIP]
> **The rubber-band guitar shows why sound needs VIBRATION.** A band at rest is silent; a plucked band moves rapidly to and fro and you hear it. **Thicker or looser bands vibrate more slowly and give a lower note; thinner or tighter bands vibrate faster and give a higher one.** **No vibration, no sound — every time.**

## 7.2 Where the energy comes from

```
FOOD        for people, animals and plants
FUEL        petrol, diesel, cooking gas, wood, coal, oil
ELECTRICITY for machines
THE SUN, THE WIND, FLOWING WATER — nature's own supplies
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY YOU FEEL TIRED WHEN YOU ARE HUNGRY.**
>
> **Food is your fuel.** Every movement you make — running, standing, even sitting still — is being paid for out of what you ate.
>
> **Your brain uses energy even when you are asleep or just thinking.** It never stops.
>
> **So hunger is not just an unpleasant feeling. It is a report on your supply**, and tiredness is what running low actually feels like.
>
> **Animals are in the same position** — a dog running, a bird flying, a fish swimming, all on food.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE TWO-DIYA EXPERIMENT: WHAT IS ACTUALLY BURNING?**
>
> **Light a cotton wick with no oil and a cotton wick sitting in oil.**
>
> **The dry wick flares and is gone in seconds. The oiled wick burns steadily for a long time.**
>
> **Why: the wick is NOT the fuel — the OIL is.** The wick's job is only to carry oil up to the flame, drop by drop, at a steady rate.
>
> **A lamp keeps burning as long as it is fed.** Take away the oil and the flame has nothing left to consume — which is why the dry wick simply burns itself up and stops.

**Cooking fuels:** cooking gas, wood, coal.
> [!WARNING]
> **Burning wood or coal makes SMOKE and pollution.** In a closed kitchen that smoke is breathed in all day, which is why smoke-free stoves matter so much. **Cleaner fuel is not a luxury; it is a health question first.**

## 7.3 Electricity

**Electricity can produce every one of the five signs of energy:**
```
MOVEMENT  fan, mixer, washing machine, electric vehicle
LIGHT     bulb, tube light, torch
SOUND     radio, speaker, doorbell
HEAT      iron, heater, kettle, oven
COOLING   fridge, air conditioner
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY ELECTRICITY IS SO USEFUL: IT IS THE ONE FORM OF ENERGY THAT WILL TURN INTO ANY OTHER.**
>
> **Oil burns and gives heat and light — and not much else.** **Food gives movement in a living body only.**
>
> **But electricity, sent down the same two wires, will move a fan, warm an iron, light a bulb, sound a bell or cool a fridge**, depending only on what you plug in.
>
> **That flexibility is why we go to the trouble of making it**, and why almost every modern machine runs on it.

> [!WARNING]
> **ELECTRICITY IS USEFUL AND IT IS DANGEROUS. THE SAFETY RULES ARE NOT SUGGESTIONS.**
> ```
> do NOT touch wires or the inside of a device that is plugged in
> do NOT put fingers, pens or sticks into sockets
> stay away from broken wires and fallen electric poles — tell an
>    adult at once
> do NOT play near electric boxes or transformers
> if something looks unsafe, do NOT try to fix it — tell an adult
> ```
> **Why every rule is "stay away and tell an adult" rather than "be careful": electricity gives no warning.** A live wire looks exactly like a dead one. **You cannot inspect your way to safety, so the only safe rule is not to touch.**

**Where electricity comes from:** most of it is made by **burning coal** — which makes a great deal of smoke and harmful gases.

> [!NOTE]
> **ENERGY EFFICIENCY means using LESS energy to do the SAME work.** **An LED bulb gives the same light as an older bulb using far less electricity** — so the work is unchanged and the waste is gone. **Efficiency is the one way of saving energy that costs you nothing at all.**

## 7.4 Clean energy — Sun, wind and water

```
SOLAR PANELS   capture sunlight and turn it into electricity
WINDMILLS      spin in the wind and turn it into electricity
HYDRO          fast rivers or falling water turn turbines
```
**Electricity from these sources is called CLEAN ENERGY** — it produces no smoke and no waste.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY SUN, WIND AND WATER ARE "CLEAN" AND COAL IS NOT.**
>
> **Coal has to be BURNT to release its energy, and burning always produces something** — smoke, gases, ash. There is no way to burn coal cleanly.
>
> **Sunlight, wind and flowing water are already moving or already arriving.** Capturing some of it leaves nothing behind.
>
> **A second difference: coal RUNS OUT.** Once burnt it is gone forever. The Sun rises again tomorrow, the wind blows again, the river flows again.
>
> **That is why they are also called RENEWABLE** — the supply renews itself.

**Three experiments, three clean sources:**
```
PINWHEEL         held in the wind, it spins        → wind can turn things
                 → a windmill is a large pinwheel connected to a generator
MAGNIFYING GLASS focuses sunlight to one spot and the paper chars
                 → sunlight carries real heat, enough to burn, if you
                   gather enough of it into one place
WATER WHEEL      spoons round a paper cup; pour water and it turns
                 → falling water can turn things
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY THE MAGNIFYING GLASS BURNS PAPER WHEN SUNLIGHT ALONE DOES NOT.**
>
> **The lens does not make the sunlight hotter.** It gathers the light falling on the whole lens and delivers it to one tiny spot.
>
> **So the same amount of energy arrives on a hundredth of the area — and that spot gets very hot indeed.**
>
> **Concentrating is not the same as creating.** This is exactly the principle behind solar cookers and solar power towers, which use mirrors to do the same thing on a huge scale.

> [!TIP]
> **You do not need a power station to use these.** **Drying clothes and papads in the sun, a sailing boat, a village watermill (*gharaat*) grinding grain, a matka cooling water** — all use the Sun, wind or water directly, with nothing plugged in. **Often the simplest use is the most efficient one, because no energy is lost turning it into electricity first.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Ancient ships crossed oceans on wind alone.** Traders from Gujarat and Tamil Nadu sailed to Africa, Arabia and Southeast Asia with sails. **The Bhadla Solar Park in Rajasthan** is one of the world's largest solar plants, and **Cochin airport in Kerala was the world's first airport running entirely on solar energy.**
>
> **The Sun gives the Earth more energy in one hour than all humans use in a whole year.**

## 7.5 Energy chains

**Energy is never used up in one step. It passes along a chain:**
```
SOURCE          →  TYPE            →  USE
the Sun         →  heat            →  dries clothes
the Sun         →  light           →  helps plants grow
wind            →  movement        →  turns a turbine to make electricity
food            →  movement        →  a child walks to school
petrol          →  movement        →  a scooter runs
electricity     →  light           →  a bulb glows
falling water   →  movement        →  a water wheel grinds grain
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE CHAIN ALWAYS LEADS BACK FURTHER THAN YOU THINK.**
>
> **Food → movement is not the start.** The food was a plant, and the plant grew using sunlight.
>
> **Petrol → movement is not the start either.** Petrol comes from very ancient living things, which grew using sunlight millions of years ago.
>
> **Follow almost any chain back far enough and you arrive at the Sun.** That is why it is called the main source of energy for life on Earth.
>
> **The chief exception worth knowing is the tides**, which are driven by the Moon.

## 7.6 Stored energy

```
YOUR BODY   stores energy from food, which is why you can still walk
            after skipping a meal
A BATTERY   stores energy chemically and releases it when connected
FOOD ITSELF is stored sunlight, captured by a plant
A DAM       stores water high up, and its energy is released when
            the water falls
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY STORING ENERGY MATTERS SO MUCH FOR CLEAN ENERGY.**
>
> **The Sun does not shine at night and the wind does not always blow — but people want light at night and every night.**
>
> **Coal has no such problem: it sits in a heap until burnt.** Coal is energy already stored.
>
> **So the hardest part of using clean energy is not capturing it but KEEPING it** until it is needed. That is why better batteries matter as much as better solar panels.
>
> **It also explains why torches, phones and toys all need a battery.** They must work at moments when no supply is attached.

## 7.7 Using energy wisely

```
switch off lights and fans when you leave a room
use daylight instead of a bulb where you can
choose LED bulbs — same light, far less electricity
dry clothes in the sun rather than in a machine
close the fridge door quickly
walk or cycle for short journeys
```
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY SAVING ENERGY IS NOT THE SAME AS GOING WITHOUT.**
>
> **A fan running in an empty room does nobody any good at all.** Switching it off costs you nothing — the comfort was not being delivered to anyone.
>
> **An LED bulb gives the SAME light for less electricity.** Nothing has been given up.
>
> **The overwhelming majority of energy saving is like this: removing waste, not accepting less.** **Real sacrifice is rarely needed, and it is a shame to skip the free savings while arguing about the costly ones.**

> [!NOTE]
> **VAASTU SHASTRA**, the ancient Indian system of design, places rooms, doors and windows to make the best use of natural light, wind and heat. **Courtyards and well-placed openings keep a home bright and airy without electricity.**
>
> **Traditional Indian houses had small windows and thick walls** — cool in summer, warm in winter. **In parts of Himachal, Uttarakhand and Ladakh, families keep cattle on the ground floor and live above, because the animals' body heat warms the rooms** — no firewood, no electricity.
