# 6. Time signatures & tempo

## Beats come in groups

Say "**ONE** two three four, **ONE** two three four" — the first beat feels **stronger**. Now try
"**ONE** two three, **ONE** two three" — that's a waltz, a swaying, round feel.

Music groups its beats, and each group is called a **bar** (also called a **measure**). A
**bar line** `|` marks where each group ends.

**Why group beats?** The strong first beat is a landmark. Musicians say "start at bar 9" instead
of "start at beat 33", and your ear always knows where "one" is, so you can't get lost.

## Reading a time signature

At the start of the music are two numbers stacked like a fraction — the **time signature**.

| Number | Tells you |
|---|---|
| **Top** | how many **beats** in each bar |
| **Bottom** | which note gets **one beat** — **4** = quarter note, **8** = eighth note |

| Time signature | Means | Feel | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4/4** | 4 quarter-note beats a bar | steady, marching — most pop and rock | *Twinkle Twinkle*, *Ode to Joy* |
| **3/4** | 3 quarter-note beats a bar | swaying waltz | *Happy Birthday* |
| **2/4** | 2 quarter-note beats a bar | quick march, left-right | many folk dances |
| **6/8** | 6 eighth-note beats a bar, felt as **2 groups of 3** | rolling, lilting | many lullabies |

**Why "4" means a quarter note:** it is the fraction again — a quarter is ¼ of a whole note. So
4/4 literally says "four quarters in a bar", and 3/4 says "three quarters in a bar".

> [!TIP]
> 4/4 is so common it is often written as a big **C** — "common time".

## Filling a bar

Every bar must add up to the top number (counting in the bottom note).

**Example:** in 4/4, a half note (2) + quarter (1) + quarter (1) = **4** ✓.
**Example:** in 3/4, a dotted half note = 2 + 1 = **3** ✓ — one note fills the whole bar.

> [!WARNING]
> A whole note (4 beats) **cannot** fit in a 3/4 bar — the bar only holds 3. In 3/4, a note that
> lasts the whole bar is a **dotted half**.

## Tempo — how fast the beat goes

**Tempo** is the speed of the beat, in **BPM** (beats per minute) — the number on the metronome.

| BPM | One beat lasts | Feels like |
|---|---|---|
| 60 | 1 second | a clock ticking — slow |
| 120 | ½ second | brisk walking — most pop songs are near here |
| 30 | 2 seconds | very slow |

**Rule:** seconds per beat = **60 ÷ BPM**.

**Why?** A minute has 60 seconds. If 120 beats share those 60 seconds, each one gets 60 ÷ 120 = ½ second.

Old music writes tempo in Italian words:

| Word | Means | Roughly |
|---|---|---|
| *Largo* | very slow, broad | 40–60 BPM |
| *Andante* | walking pace | 76–108 BPM |
| *Moderato* | moderate | 108–120 BPM |
| *Allegro* | fast, cheerful | 120–156 BPM |
| *Presto* | very fast | 168–200 BPM |

> [!NOTE]
> Tempo and time signature are different things. 4/4 says **how beats are grouped**; 80 BPM
> says **how fast** they go. A song can be slow 4/4 or fast 4/4.

## Indian rhythm — taal

Indian music groups beats too. A repeating cycle of beats is a **taal**, and its first beat,
**sam**, is the strong landmark — just like beat 1 of a bar.

| Taal | Beats | Grouped as | Western cousin |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dadra** | 6 | 3 + 3 | like 6/8 |
| **Keherwa** | 8 | 4 + 4 | like two bars of 4/4 |
| **Teentaal** | 16 | 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 | like four bars of 4/4 |

**Why they line up:** both systems do the same thing — count a steady beat, group it, and mark the
first beat of each group as home. That's why most film songs in Keherwa can be strummed in 4/4.
