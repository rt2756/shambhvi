# 0. Music map — from first note to first song

These lessons are the **ideas behind the guitar**. They are written for an **electric guitar**
and a **complete beginner** — but everything here works on an acoustic too.

Each lesson goes **example → idea → rule**, and every rule comes with **why it works**, so you
can work things out on the fretboard instead of just remembering them.

## The lessons, in order

| # | Lesson | You will be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sound & pitch | say why a shorter, thinner or tighter string sounds higher — and how an electric guitar gets loud |
| 2 | The musical alphabet | name all 12 notes, and say why there is no note between B–C and E–F |
| 3 | The guitar fretboard | name the strings, number your fingers, find any note by counting frets |
| 4 | Reading TAB & chord boxes | read guitar TAB and chord diagrams |
| 5 | Rhythm & beat | count whole, half, quarter and eighth notes — "1 & 2 &" |
| 6 | Time signatures & tempo | read 4/4 and 3/4, bars, and BPM on a metronome |
| 7 | Intervals | measure the distance between two notes in frets |
| 8 | The major scale | build the "Sa Re Ga" / "do re mi" scale from its step pattern |
| 9 | How chords are built | build power chords, major and minor chords — and say why minor sounds sad |
| 10 | Open chords | play E, A, D, G, C, Em, Am, Dm — and see where each comes from |
| 11 | Keys & chord families | say why C, F, G and Am sound good together |
| 12 | Strumming patterns | read and play strumming patterns on the beat grid |
| 13 | Minor & pentatonic scales | play the first lead-guitar shape |
| 14 | Sargam ↔ Western & Indian tunes | turn Sa Re Ga Ma into notes and play Indian melodies |
| 15 | Staff notation — the basics | read simple notes on the five-line staff |

> [!NOTE]
> Lessons 1–12 are ready. The rest are added as the practice weeks reach them.

## How to use a lesson

1. **Read it with the guitar in your lap.** Every lesson has "try it" moments — do them.
2. **Take the four tests** on the 🎯 Tests page: 🟢 simple → 🟡 hard → 🟠 harder → 🔴 hardest.
3. **Practise** — the 🎸 Practice page says which lessons each week uses.

> [!TIP]
> Music theory is like grammar for sound. You can speak before you know grammar, and you can
> play before you know theory — but theory tells you **why** things sound the way they do, so
> you can find them yourself.
