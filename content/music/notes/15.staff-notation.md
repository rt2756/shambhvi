# 15. Staff notation — the basics

TAB tells you **where** to put your fingers. **Staff notation** tells you **which note** and **how long** — in a
way that any musician (piano, violin, flute, singer) can read. You don't need it to play guitar, but knowing
the basics means you can read any song book.

## The staff — five lines, higher means higher

The **staff** is five lines. Notes sit **on a line** or **in a space** between lines.

- Higher on the staff = **higher pitch**.
- Each step up (line → space → line) = **the next letter** of the musical alphabet.

**Why lines AND spaces?** Using both packs 9 notes into 5 lines. If notes only sat on lines, you'd need twice as
many lines, and they'd be too crowded to read.

## The treble clef — which line is which

The curly sign at the start is the **treble clef** (also called the **G clef**). Its curl wraps around the
**second line from the bottom** and marks it as **G**. Guitar music always uses it.

**Why does one sign fix every note?** Once you know one line is G, the rest follow in alphabet order —
up a step is A, down a step is F.

```
 F ─────────────────────   line 5 (top)
 E                          space 4
 D ─────────────────────   line 4
 C                          space 3
 B ─────────────────────   line 3 (middle)
 A                          space 2
 G ─────────────────────   line 2  ← the treble-clef curl
 F                          space 1
 E ─────────────────────   line 1 (bottom)
```

| Remember | Notes | Memory trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Lines** (bottom → top) | E G B D F | **E**very **G**ood **B**oy **D**oes **F**ine |
| **Spaces** (bottom → top) | F A C E | spells **FACE** |

## Going above and below — ledger lines

Notes too high or low for the staff get short extra lines called **ledger lines**.

- One ledger line **below** the staff = **middle C**.
- The space just below the staff = D.

## Where staff notes are on the guitar

Guitar music is written **one octave higher** than it sounds.

**Why?** The guitar's low notes would need lots of ledger lines below the staff. Writing everything an octave up
keeps the notes **on the staff**, where they're easy to read. (Everyone who reads guitar music knows this, so
nobody gets confused.)

So the open strings land neatly on the staff:

| Open string | Written as |
|---|---|
| High **e** | the **top space** (E) |
| **B** | the **middle line** (B) |
| **G** | **line 2** — the clef's curl (G) |
| **D** | the space **just below** the staff (D) |
| **A** | **two ledger lines below** the staff (A) |
| Low **E** | hanging **just under the third ledger line** below the staff (E) |

**Example:** a note on the **top line** (F) → one fret above the open high e → **high e, fret 1**.
**Example:** a note on **line 4** (D) → B string, fret 3 (B → C → C♯ → D).

## Note lengths on the staff

You've met these in lesson 5 — now you can see where they come from:

| Looks like | Name | Beats |
|---|---|---|
| hollow oval, no stem | whole note | 4 |
| hollow oval + stem | half note | 2 |
| filled oval + stem | quarter note | 1 |
| filled + stem + flag (or beam) | eighth note | ½ |

The **position** on the staff gives the **pitch**; the **shape** gives the **length**. One symbol, two jobs.

## Sharps, flats and the key signature

- A **♯** or **♭** written just before a note raises or lowers **that note**.
- A **natural sign ♮** cancels it.
- A **key signature** — sharps or flats right after the clef — applies to **every** note of that letter, all
  the way through.

**Example:** G major has one sharp, F♯ (lesson 8). So its key signature is a single ♯ on the **top line (F)**.
Every F in the song is then played as F♯ — you don't have to mark each one.

**Why use a key signature?** A song in G might have fifty F♯s. Marking the sharp once at the start saves writing
it fifty times — and it tells you the key before you play a note.

## Bars, time signatures and repeats

- **Bar lines** divide the staff into bars (lesson 6).
- The **time signature** (4/4, 3/4) sits after the clef and key signature.
- A **double bar with two dots** `:||` means **repeat** — go back to the matching `||:` (or to the start) and play
  that section again.

## Reading a short tune

*Ode to Joy* (practice Week 2) starts **E E F G**. On the staff:

- **E** = the top space → open high e
- **F** = the top line → high e, fret 1
- **G** = just above the staff → high e, fret 3

You already played it from TAB — now you can see the same notes as dots climbing the staff.

> [!TIP]
> Practise by naming one line of a printed song each day, **without** playing it. Speed comes from recognising
> the shapes, the same way you learned to read words.
