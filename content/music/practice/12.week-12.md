# 🎸 Week 12 — Your own riff and your first concert

**Goal:** write and name **your own riff**, read the open-string notes on the **staff**, and play a short
**concert** of everything you've learned for family or friends.

**Read first:** theory lesson **15** (staff notation).

## 🎚️ Tune (3 min)

All six strings, finishing each one by tuning **up** to the note.

## 🔥 Warm-up (5 min)

1. Finger stretches.
2. Box 1 up and down once, then one minute of improvising over your Am–G backing.

## 🛠️ Technique (10 min)

### A. E minor pentatonic — open position — 3 min

```
e|---------------------0-3-|
B|-----------------0-3-----|
G|-------------0-2---------|
D|---------0-2-------------|
A|-----0-2-----------------|
E|-0-3---------------------|
```

The same pattern as Box 1, but at the very top of the neck with open strings: **E G A B D** (lesson 13).

**Why is this the riff-writer's favourite?** The open low E rings huge, especially with distortion, and you can
play notes on top while it booms. Most of the notes are open strings, so your fretting hand is free to move fast.

### B. Write your own riff — 7 min

A riff is a short, catchy pattern that repeats — usually **1 or 2 bars**.

1. Start on the **open low E**, palm-muted, eighth notes: `0 0 0 0`.
2. Add **2 or 3 notes** from E minor pentatonic on the low E and A strings (frets 0, 2, 3).
3. Add one **power chord** (E5, G5 or A5 — Week 5) as the "hit".
4. Play it **4 times** in a row. If it's still fun the fourth time, it's a riff.
5. **Write it down as TAB** in a notebook, and **give it a name**.

**Why write it down?** You will forget it by next week — every guitarist does. TAB on paper means the riff is yours
for good, and you can build a whole song from it later.

> [!TIP]
> Good riffs usually have **one surprise**: a rest where you expect a note, an accent on an off-beat (lesson 12),
> or one high note among low ones. Try moving one note to the "&".

## 🎵 Notes & tunes (7 min)

### C. Reading the staff — 3 min

Say each open string's place on the staff out loud while you play it (lesson 15):

| Play | Say |
|---|---|
| Open **high e** | "top space — E" |
| Open **B** | "middle line — B" |
| Open **G** | "line 2, the clef's curl — G" |
| High e, **fret 1** | "top line — F" |
| B string, **fret 1** | "space 3 — C" |
| B string, **fret 3** | "line 4 — D" |

**Why start from the open strings?** They're the notes you already know best. Tying them to staff positions gives
you **landmarks**, and every other note is just a step up or down from one of them.

### D. Plan your concert — 4 min

Pick **4 pieces** you can play without stopping. For example:

1. *Happy Birthday* — melody (Week 4)
2. *Amazing Grace* — chords, while someone sings (Week 9)
3. The Bhupali tune (Week 10)
4. **Your own riff** — and announce its name!

Play the whole set through **once a day** this week, in order, without stopping between pieces.

**Why rehearse the whole set, not just each piece?** In a concert you play one piece straight after another, with
nerves. Practising the full run — changing sound, retuning, starting the next one — is a different skill from
playing each piece alone.

## 🎉 Fun — the concert! 🎤

At the end of the week, play your set for family or friends. Bow at the end.
