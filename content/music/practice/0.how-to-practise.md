# 🧭 How to practise

## The 30-minute session

Do this **every day**. Each week's page tells you what goes in each part.

| Time | Part | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 min | 🎚️ **Tune** | tune all six strings with a tuner |
| 3–8 min | 🔥 **Warm-up** | stretch your fingers, then the slow spider exercise |
| 8–18 min | 🛠️ **Technique** | the new skill of the week |
| 18–25 min | 🎵 **Notes & tunes** | the melody, notes or chords of the week |
| 25–30 min | 🎉 **Fun** | play anything you enjoy — an old tune, make up a riff, mess around |

**Why short and daily, not long and weekly?** Your fingers learn a movement by doing it many
times, and your brain stores the practice while you **sleep**. Seven 30-minute sessions give
seven nights of "saving" — one 3½-hour session gives only one. Your fingertips also toughen
up slowly with a little playing each day.

**Why the fun part at the end?** So every practice finishes on something you like. That makes
you want to pick the guitar up again tomorrow — which matters more than anything else here.

## Before you start: setting up the electric guitar

- [ ] I can plug in safely: **amp volume down → plug in → then turn the volume up**.
- [ ] I set the amp to a **clean** sound (gain low, no distortion).
- [ ] Guitar **volume knob all the way up**; I control loudness on the amp.
- [ ] I have a **tuner** (a clip-on tuner, a tuner app, or search "guitar tuner" on Google).
- [ ] I have a **metronome** (an app, or search "metronome" on Google).
- [ ] I have a **medium pick** (about 0.6–0.75 mm thick).

**Why clean, not distorted?** Distortion makes everything sound big — including the mistakes,
and any string you touched by accident. A clean sound lets you **hear exactly what your
fingers are doing**. Distortion comes later, as a reward.

## Sitting and holding

- Sit on a chair **without arms**, feet flat, back straight but relaxed.
- The guitar's waist rests on your **right leg** (for a right-handed player). The neck points
  slightly **up**, not down at the floor.
- **Fretting hand:** thumb behind the neck, roughly behind your middle finger. Fingers curved
  like you are holding a small ball, pressing with the **tips**.
- **Picking hand:** hold the pick between the **side of your index finger** and the **pad of
  your thumb**, with just a little tip showing. Pick from the wrist, not the whole arm.

**Why fingertips and curved fingers?** A flat finger lies across the next string and mutes
it. A curved finger comes down on its tip, so it presses only its own string.

## How to use the metronome

A **metronome** clicks a steady beat. The number is **BPM** — beats (clicks) per minute.
60 BPM = one click every second.

1. Start **slow** — slow enough to play it **perfectly 3 times in a row**.
2. Only then go **up by 5 BPM**.
3. If it falls apart, go back down 5 and stay there for the day.

**Why slow first?** Your fingers learn **whatever you repeat** — mistakes too. Playing slowly
and correctly teaches the right movement; speed comes from repeating it right.

## Taking care of your hands

- Sore fingertips in the first 2–3 weeks are **normal**. They get tougher (calluses) and stop hurting.
- **Sharp pain** in the wrist, hand or arm is **not** normal — stop and rest.
- If your hand is tired, **shake it out** and rest for a minute. Tight muscles play badly.
- Keep nails on the fretting hand **short**, or you can't press with the fingertips.

> [!TIP]
> Squeezing too hard is the most common beginner habit. Try this: press a note, then slowly
> **loosen** until it starts to buzz, then press just a little more. That is all the pressure
> you need.

## The 12-week map

| Weeks | Goal | Theory lessons |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Hold the guitar and pick, name the strings, first notes, first tune | 1, 3, 4 |
| **2** | Up-and-down picking, spider across all strings, two new tunes | 2, 3, 4 |
| **3** | First chords Em and Am; strumming quarter notes; counting a tune | 5 |
| **4** | The E chord; down, down-up strumming; a tune in 3/4 | 6, 7 |
| **5** | Power chords, palm muting, first distortion; the G major scale | 8, 9 |
| **6** | The everyday strum; 3-note power chords; sargam alankar | 12 |
| **7** | A, D, Dm — the key of A; strum along to *Twinkle Twinkle* | 10, 11 |
| **8** | G and C — the key of G; strum along to *Happy Birthday* | 10, 11 |
| 9–10 | First whole song with chords; an Indian melody | 14 |
| 11–12 | The pentatonic scale and a first riff of your own | 13, 15 |

> [!NOTE]
> Weeks 1–8 are ready below. Move to the next week only when you tick its
> **"ready to move on"** list — some weeks will take longer, and that is fine.
