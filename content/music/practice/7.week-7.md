# 🎸 Week 7 — A, D and Dm: your first key

**Goal:** add the **A**, **D** and **Dm** chords, and play the three chords of the **key of A** — **A, D, E** —
to strum along with a tune you know.

**Read first:** theory lessons **10** (open chords) and **11** (keys & chord families).

## 🎚️ Tune (3 min)

All six strings, finishing each one by tuning **up** to the note.

## 🔥 Warm-up (5 min)

1. Finger stretches.
2. The everyday strum (D, DU, UDU) on Em → Am → E → Am, one bar each.

## 🛠️ Technique (10 min)

### A. The new chords — 4 min

```
   A                       D
   E A D G B e             E A D G B e
   x o       o             x x o
   ===========             ===========
1  | | | | | |          1  | | | | | |
   -----------             -----------
2  | | 1 2 3 |          2  | | | 1 | 2
   -----------             -----------
3  | | | | | |          3  | | | | 3 |

   Dm
   E A D G B e
   x x o
   ===========
1  | | | | | 1
   -----------
2  | | | 2 | |
   -----------
3  | | | | 3 |
```

Roll call each one (pick each string alone). **A**: strum from the A string. **D** and **Dm**: strum from the
D string only.

**Why are A and D strummed from different strings?** A chord sounds most solid when its lowest note is its
**root** (lesson 10). A's root is the open A string; D's root is the open D string. Any string below the root
adds a "wrong" bass note.

> [!TIP]
> In **A**, three fingers crowd into fret 2. If the high e string goes dead, your finger 3 is leaning on it —
> arch it more, or try fingers 2-1-3 instead.

### B. D ↔ Dm — hear the 3rd change — 2 min

Strum **D** four times, then **Dm** four times. Only the high e string changes: fret 2 (F♯) → fret 1 (F).

**Why does the mood flip?** F♯ is a major 3rd above D; F is a minor 3rd (lesson 9). One half step on one
string turns happy into sad — the same thing you heard with E ↔ Em in Week 4.

### C. One-minute changes: A ↔ D, D ↔ E — 4 min

Two minutes each. Write the numbers down.

> [!TIP]
> **A → D:** lift all three fingers together and land them as a **triangle** shape for D. Practise the
> "air shape" — make the D shape in the air above the strings, then drop it on.

## 🎵 Notes & tunes (7 min)

### D. The key of A: A – D – E

In the key of A, the I, IV and V chords are **A, D and E** (lesson 11). Strum each progression with the
everyday strum, one bar per chord:

```
| A       | D       | E       | A       |      I – IV – V – I
| A       | E       | D       | A       |      I – V – IV – I
| A       | D       | A       | E       |      ends on V — hear it pull home?
```

**Why does the last line sound unfinished?** It stops on **E** — the V chord. E contains **G♯**, step 7 of
A major, only a half step below home (A). Your ear wants to hear A next. Play A after it and feel the release.

### E. Strum along: *Twinkle Twinkle* in A

Someone sings (or hum it yourself). Each chord lasts **2 beats** — strum ↓ ↓ for each:

```
Twin-kle twin-kle  lit-tle   star,    how  I   won-der   what you   are
| A        A     | D        A      | D        A       | E        A     |

Up a-bove the    world so   high,    like a  dia-mond  in the     sky
| A        D     | A        E      | A        D       | A        E     |
```

Then the first line again. (Changing every 2 beats is quick — go slow, around 50–60 BPM.)

## 🎉 Fun (5 min)

Play **A → Dm → A → E** slowly with lots of ring. Dm is "borrowed" from another key — hear how it adds a
moment of sadness in the middle of a happy progression.
