# 🎸 Week 6 — The everyday strum and sargam on the fretboard

**Goal:** play the **D, DU, UDU** strumming pattern, add **3-string power chords** with roots on the A
string, put **accents** on the backbeat, and play your first **alankar** (sargam exercise) on the guitar.

**Read first:** theory lesson **12** (strumming patterns).

## 🎚️ Tune (3 min)

All six strings, finishing each one by tuning **up** to the note.

## 🔥 Warm-up (5 min)

1. Finger stretches.
2. The palm-muted power-chord riff from Week 5, twice.

## 🛠️ Technique (10 min)

### A. The everyday strum: D, DU, UDU — 5 min

```
1   &   2   &   3   &   4   &
↓   .   ↓   ↑   .   ↑   ↓   ↑
```

Learn it in the four steps from lesson 12:

1. **Say it** while clapping the beat: "down, down-up, — up-down-up".
2. **Mute** the strings with your fretting hand and strum the rhythm on dead strings.
3. Hold **Em** and play it until it's steady.
4. Change chords every bar:

```
| Em      | Am      | Em      | E       |
| Am      | E       | Am      | Em      |
```

**Why does the hand still swing down on beat 3 if it misses?** If the hand stops, the next up-strum comes
in late. The swing **keeps the clock running** — the miss is a choice, not a pause.

> [!TIP]
> Up-strums catch just the top 3–4 strings and should be lighter. The pattern bounces because the
> down-strums are strong and the up-strums are soft.

### B. Power chords with the root on the A string — 3 min

Same shape, one string over. And now add **finger 4** on the D string for a fuller 3-note version:

```
   C5         D5         C5 (3-note)    D5 (3-note)
e|-----|    |-----|    |-----|        |-----|
B|-----|    |-----|    |-----|        |-----|
G|-----|    |-----|    |-5---|        |-7---|
D|-5---|    |-7---|    |-5---|        |-7---|
A|-3---|    |-5---|    |-3---|        |-5---|
E|-----|    |-----|    |-----|        |-----|
```

(3-note version: finger 1 on the A string, finger 3 on the D string, finger 4 on the G string, same fret.)

**What is the third note?** It's the **root again, one octave up** — the octave shape from lesson 7 (two
strings up, 2 frets higher). It adds weight without adding a new note, so the chord is still root + 5th.

| Root fret (A string) | 0 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 7 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Power chord | A5 | B5 | C5 | D5 | E5 | F5 | G5 |

Riff — palm-muted eighths, all downs:

```
e|---------|---------|---------|---------|
B|---------|---------|---------|---------|
G|---------|---------|---------|---------|
D|-5-5-5-5-|-7-7-7-7-|---------|---------|
A|-3-3-3-3-|-5-5-5-5-|-5-5-5-5-|-7-7-7-7-|
E|---------|---------|-3-3-3-3-|-5-5-5-5-|
    C5        D5        G5        A5
```

### C. Accents on 2 and 4 — 2 min

Power chord on E5, eighth notes, all downs — but hit **beats 2 and 4 harder**:

```
     >         >
1 &  2 &  3 &  4 &
↓ ↓  ↓ ↓  ↓ ↓  ↓ ↓
```

**Why does that sound like rock?** The drummer's snare lands on 2 and 4. When your accents line up with
it, the guitar and drums lock together into one groove.

## 🎵 Notes & tunes (7 min)

### D. Alankar 1 — Sa Re Ga, Re Ga Ma … — 4 min

Indian singers and musicians warm up with **alankars**: short patterns that climb the scale. Here's the
first one on the C major scale shape (lesson 8). **Sing the sargam as you play.**

```
  SRG     RGM     GMP     MPD     PDN     DNS'
e|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
B|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
G|-------|-------|-------|-----2-|---2-4-|-2-4-5-|
D|-----2-|---2-3-|-2-3-5-|-3-5---|-5-----|-------|
A|-3-5---|-5-----|-------|-------|-------|-------|
E|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
```

And back down:

```
  S'ND    NDP     DPM     PMG     MGR     GRS
e|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
B|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
G|-5-4-2-|-4-2---|-2-----|-------|-------|-------|
D|-------|-----5-|---5-3-|-5-3-2-|-3-2---|-2-----|
A|-------|-------|-------|-------|-----5-|---5-3-|
E|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
```

Letters are short for the sargam: **S** = Sa, **R** = Re, **G** = Ga, **M** = Ma, **P** = Pa, **D** = Dha,
**N** = Ni, **S'** = the higher Sa. Alternate picking, one note per click at 60 BPM.

**Why patterns instead of just the scale?** Playing the scale straight up only trains one direction.
Alankars make your fingers **turn around** every three notes — the same moves real tunes make. And singing
along trains your ear to **hear** where each note sits.

### E. Mix the two — 3 min

Play Alankar 1 once with the **clean** sound and once with a **little** distortion on the neck pickup.
Listen to how the same notes change character.

## 🎉 Fun (5 min)

Make a riff from C5, D5, G5 and A5 with palm muting and an accent on 2 and 4. Can you make it sound
like the start of a song?
