# 🎸 Week 11 — Lead guitar: the pentatonic box

**Goal:** play **Box 1** of A minor pentatonic up and down, add **bends** and **vibrato**, and **improvise** —
make up your own solo over a chord backing.

**Read first:** theory lesson **13** (minor & pentatonic scales).

## 🎚️ Tune (3 min)

All six strings, finishing each one by tuning **up** to the note. Bends pull strings out of tune a little —
check the tuning again halfway through.

## 🔥 Warm-up (5 min)

1. Finger stretches.
2. Hammer-ons and pull-offs from Week 9, across all six strings.

## 🛠️ Technique (10 min)

### A. Box 1, up and down — 4 min

```
e|---------------------5-8-|-8-5---------------------|
B|-----------------5-8-----|-----8-5-----------------|
G|-------------5-7---------|---------7-5-------------|
D|---------5-7-------------|-------------7-5---------|
A|-----5-7-----------------|-----------------7-5-----|
E|-5-8---------------------|---------------------8-5-|
```

Finger 1 on every fret-5 note, finger 3 on fret 7, finger 4 on fret 8. **Alternate picking**, 60 BPM, one note per
click. Then 70, then 80.

**Why keep finger 1 on fret 5 the whole time?** The whole box fits under one hand position — fingers 1, 3 and 4
are always over frets 5, 7 and 8. You never move the hand, so you never have to look for notes.

### B. Bends — 4 min

Bend the **G string at fret 7** (D) **up** until it sounds like **fret 9** (E) — a whole-step bend.

1. First play **fret 9** to hear the target.
2. Put finger **3** on fret 7, with fingers **1 and 2 behind it on the same string** to help push.
3. Pick, then push the string **up toward the ceiling** until it matches.

```
e|-------------|
B|-------------|
G|-9---7b(9)---|     ← target, then bend to match
D|-------------|
A|-------------|
E|-------------|
```

Then try the **B string at fret 8** (G), bent up to **A** (sounds like fret 10).

**Why use three fingers to bend?** One finger alone isn't strong enough to stretch the string a whole step, and it
wobbles. Three fingers pushing together give strength and control — so the bend lands **in tune**.

> [!WARNING]
> A bend that falls short sounds **out of tune** — like a wrong note. Always check the target first. Electric
> guitar strings are light, so bends are much easier than on an acoustic — another reason electric is great for lead.

### C. Vibrato — 2 min

Hold the B string at fret 8 and gently wiggle it **up and down a tiny amount**, evenly — like a singer holding a
long note.

**Why add vibrato?** A long note held perfectly still sounds flat and dead. A small, regular wobble in pitch makes
it sound alive and expressive — the same reason singers do it.

## 🎵 Notes & tunes (7 min)

### D. Your first solo — improvise!

1. **Record a backing** on a phone: strum **| Am | G | Am | G |** with the everyday strum, about 1 minute, at 70 BPM.
2. Play it back and make up a solo using **only Box 1**.

Rules to start with:

- Play **short phrases** — 3 to 6 notes — then **rest** for a bar. Leave space.
- **End phrases on A** (low E fret 5, D string fret 7, or high e fret 5).
- Use one bend and one hammer-on in each go.

**Why does it all sound good?** Every Box 1 note is from A minor pentatonic — no half-step clashes (lesson 13). And
A minor and G are both in the C / Am chord family (lesson 11), so the backing chords and your notes agree.

> [!TIP]
> Leaving gaps is what makes a solo sound like **speaking** rather than typing. Play a phrase, then "listen" for a
> bar — as if the backing is answering you.

## 🎉 Fun (5 min)

Add distortion, bridge pickup, and play the "down from the top" lick from lesson 13 — then make it your own:
change the order, add a bend at the end.
