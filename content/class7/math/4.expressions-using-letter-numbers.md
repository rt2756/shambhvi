## A letter can stand for a number

**Shabnam is 3 years older than Aftab.** When Aftab is 10, Shabnam is 13. When Aftab is 18, Shabnam is 21. The *rule* never changes: **Shabnam's age = Aftab's age + 3.**

Writing "Aftab's age" every time is long, so we use a short symbol — a **letter**. Let **a** be Aftab's age and **s** be Shabnam's:

> **s = a + 3**

If a = 23, then s = 23 + 3 = **26**. Turned around: Aftab is 3 years younger, so **a = s − 3**. If Shabnam is 20, Aftab is 17.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A letter-number is a letter that stands for a number** (one we don't know yet, or one that can change).
> **An algebraic expression** is an expression with letter-numbers in it, like a + 3 or 2 × n.
> **Why bother?** One short expression describes *every* case at once — Aftab at 4, at 10, at 23, at any age. That's what makes it a **formula**.

**More examples (book):**
- **Matchstick Ls:** each L uses 2 sticks → n Ls need **2 × n** sticks. (5 Ls → 10 sticks, 45 Ls → 90.)
- **Ketaki's laddus:** coconut ₹35, jaggery ₹60 per kg. For c coconuts and j kg jaggery: **c × 35 + j × 60**. 10 coconuts and 5 kg → 350 + 300 = **₹650**. 8 and 9 → 280 + 540 = **₹820**. 7 and 4 → 245 + 240 = **₹485**.
- **Perimeter of a square of side q:** **4 × q**. Side 7 cm → 28 cm.

> [!TIP]
> **How to write a formula:** first say the rule **in words** ("cost = number of coconuts × 35 + …"), try it with a couple of actual numbers, **then** swap the words for letters. If you can't say it in words, you're not ready to write it in letters.

**Book Figure it Out:**
1. Perimeter formulas (side s): equilateral triangle **3s** · regular pentagon **5s** · regular hexagon **6s**. (Regular = all sides and angles equal.)
2. Munirathna's 20 m pipe joined to a pipe of k m: **20 + k** metres.
3. Krithika's notes: 3 × 100 + 5 × 20 + 6 × 5 = **₹430** · 8 × 100 + 4 × 20 + z × 5 = **880 + 5z** · x, y, z notes → **100x + 20y + 5z**.
4. **Flour mill:** 10 seconds to start, then 8 seconds per kg. For y kg: **10 + 8 × y**. (Not (10 + 8) × y — the start-up happens only once.)
5. In letters: 5 more than a number **n + 5** · 4 less **n − 4** · 2 less than 13 times **13n − 2** · 13 less than 2 times **2n − 13**.
6. **Calendar 2 × 3 grid** with bottom-middle w: bottom row w − 1, w, w + 1; top row (a week earlier) w − 8, w − 7, w − 6.

## Dropping the × sign

The multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12, 16, … The 3rd term is 4 × 3, the 29th is 4 × 29, the **nth is 4 × n**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **We write 4 × n as 4n** — number first, then the letter, with no sign between.
> So **7k** means 7 × k. If k = 4, 7k = 28. And **5m + 3** with m = 2 is 5 × 2 + 3 = **13**.
> **Why only with letters:** "4n" can't be misread, but "43" already means forty-three — so between two numbers we must keep the ×.

| j = 5, 2j | 10 | — | **10 ✔** |

| f = 3, g = 1, 2f − 2g | 2 | | 6 − 2 = **4** |

> [!WARNING]
> **When you replace a letter by a negative number, put it in brackets:** 10 − a with a = −4 is 10 − (−4) = 14. Without brackets it's easy to lose the minus.

## Simplifying: like terms

**Rectangle perimeter:** p = l + b + l + b. Terms can be added in any order, so p = l + l + b + b = **2l + 2b**. Check with l = 3, b = 4: both give 14.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Two expressions are equal if they give the same value for EVERY choice of the letters.** "Simplifying" means rewriting an expression in a shorter form that is still equal.

**Pencils and erasers (book):** pencils cost c, erasers d. Pencils sold: 5, 3, 10 on three days → 5c + 3c + 10c. That's c added 5 + 3 + 10 times → **18c**. Erasers 4, 6, 1 → **11d**. Total **18c + 11d** — and it stops there.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Like terms have the same letter-number part** (5c, 3c, 10c; or 12n and −4n). **Add like terms by adding their number parts:** 5c + 3c + 10c = (5 + 3 + 10)c = 18c.
> **Unlike terms** (18c and 11d) **can't be combined.**
> **Why:** this is the distributive property. "5 c's and 3 c's" is 8 c's — like 5 apples and 3 apples. But 18 pencils' worth and 11 erasers' worth don't make 29 of anything.

**Area picture (book):** a rectangle of height v split into widths 4 and 3: 4v + 3v = **7v** (the whole width is 7). Rectangle of height n, width 12, with a width-4 strip removed: 12n − 4n = **8n**.

### Brackets with a minus in front

**Renting furniture (book):** pay ₹40 per chair, ₹75 per table; get back ₹6 per chair, ₹10 per table on return. For x chairs and y tables:
(40x + 75y) − (6x + 10y) = 40x + 75y − 6x − 10y = (40 − 6)x + (75 − 10)y = **34x + 65y**.

> [!NOTE]
> The minus in front of the second bracket flips both signs inside — exactly as with numbers. You could also write it as (40x + 75y) + (−6x − 10y).

**Charu's quiz (book):** rounds 7p − 3q, 8p − 4q, 6p − 2q (p = marks per right answer, q = penalty per wrong one).
- Total: (7 + 8 + 6)p − (3 + 4 + 2)q = **21p − 9q**.
- With p = 4, q = 1: rounds score 25, 28, 22; total 84 − 9 = **75**. No penalty means q = 0.
- Krishita scored 23p − 7q. Difference: 23p − 7q − (21p − 9q) = 23p − 7q − 21p + 9q = **2p + 2q** → Krishita scored more (p and q are positive).

**More (book):** 4(x + y) − y = 4x + 4y − y = **4x + 3y**.

> [!WARNING]
> **5u ≠ 5 + u.** 5u means 5 *times* u; 5 + u means 5 *more than* u. With u = 2: 10 vs 7. With u = 8: 40 vs 13. (They happen to agree only at u = 1.25.)
> **10y − 3 ≠ 10(y − 3).** With y = 2: 17 vs −10. The bracket makes the 10 multiply the 3 as well (10y − 30).

**Adding a picture of numbers (book):** a frame of eight 3s with r, s, r, s inside: row-wise (4 × 3) + (r + s) + (r + s) + (4 × 3), or like terms 8 × 3 + 2r + 2s, or double the top half 2 × (4 × 3 + r + s) — all simplify to **2r + 2s + 24**.

**Book: simplify.**
p + p + p + p = **4p** · p + p + p + q = **3p + q** · p + q + p − q = **2p** · p − q + p − q = **2p − 2q** · p + q − p + q = **2q** · p + q − (p + q) = **0** · p − q − p − q = **−2q** · 2d − d − d − d = **−d** · 2d − d − d − c = **−c** · 2d − d − (d − c) = **c** · 2d − (d − d) − c = **2d − c** · 2d − d − c − c = **d − 2c**

### Mind the mistake — simplifying (book)

| Expression | Wrong answer | Correct simplest form |
|---|---|---|
| 3a + 2b | 5 | **3a + 2b** (unlike terms — can't combine) |
| 3b − 2b − b | 0 | **0 ✔** |
| 6(p + 2) | 6p + 8 | **6p + 12** (6 × 2 = 12) |
| (4x + 3y) − (3x + 4y) | x + y | **x − y** (3y − 4y = −y) |
| 5 − (2 − 6z) | 3 − 6z | **3 + 6z** (−(−6z) = +6z) |
| 2 + (x + 3) | 2x − 6 | **x + 5** |
| 2y + (3y − 6) | −y + 6 | **5y − 6** |
| 7p − p + 5q − 2q | 7p + 3q | **6p + 3q** (7p − p = 6p) |
| 5(2w + 3x + 4w) | 10w + 15x + 20w | **30w + 15x** (combine 10w + 20w) |
| 3j + 6k + 9h + 12 | 3(j + 2k + 3h + 4) | equal ✔ (brackets removed: 3j + 6k + 9h + 12 is the simplest form) |
| 4(2r + 3s + 5) | −20 − 8r − 12s | **8r + 12s + 20** (all signs were flipped wrongly) |

> [!TIP]
> **Pattern the book wants you to spot:** in a fully simplified expression there is **one term per different letter-number, plus at most one plain number**. 30w + 15x has two letters → two terms.

## Patterns and why they always hold

### Formula detective

A number machine takes two inputs a and b and gives one output. With (5, 2) → 8, (8, 1) → 15, (9, 11) → 7, (10, 10) → 10: the rule is **2a − b** (2 × 5 − 2 = 8, 2 × 8 − 1 = 15, 18 − 11 = 7, 20 − 10 = 10). Guess a rule from the first pair, then **test it on every pair**.

### Saree border (book)

Designs A, B, C repeat: A B C A B C …
- C appears at 3, 6, 9, … → **3n**
- B appears at 2, 5, 8, … one before each C → **3n − 1**
- A appears at 1, 4, 7, … → **3n − 2**

**Which design is at position 122?** Divide by 3 and look at the **remainder**: 0 → C, 2 → B, 1 → A.
99 = 3 × 33, remainder 0 → **C**. 122 = 3 × 40 + 2 → **B**. 148 = 3 × 49 + 1 → **A**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **For a repeating pattern of length k, the remainder when you divide the position by k tells you where you are in the repeat.**
> **Why:** every full block of k positions brings you back to the start, so only the leftover (remainder) matters.

### Calendar squares (book)

In any 2 × 2 square of a calendar, the diagonal sums are equal (12 + 20 = 13 + 19 = 32). Is that **always** true? We can't check infinitely many squares — so use a letter.

Call the top-left number **a**. Then: right of it is **a + 1**, below it is **a + 7** (a week later), diagonal is **a + 8**.
- a + (a + 8) = **2a + 8**
- (a + 1) + (a + 7) = **2a + 8**

Same expression → equal for **every** a. That is a **proof**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Checking examples can only suggest a pattern; using a letter for "any number" proves it.** This is the real power of algebra.

**Plus shape:** centre a, with a − 1 and a + 1 beside it, a − 7 above, a + 7 below. Sum = 5a + (−1 + 1 − 7 + 7) = **5a** — always 5 times the centre. (14 + 15 + 16 + 8 + 22 = 75 = 5 × 15 ✔)

### Matchstick triangles (book)

Steps 1, 2, 3, … use 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, … sticks: each new triangle adds 2.
Step y = a starting 3 plus (y − 1) more 2s → **3 + 2(y − 1)** = 3 + 2y − 2 = **2y + 1**.
Step 33 → **67**, Step 84 → **169**, Step 108 → **217**.

Another way to see it: **y** sticks lie horizontally and **y + 1** sit diagonally → y + (y + 1) = 2y + 1 ✔. Two different ways of counting, same simplified expression — that's a great check.

## In a nutshell

- A **letter-number** stands for any number; an **algebraic expression** uses them; a **formula** is a rule written that way.
- **4n means 4 × n**; replace a negative value in brackets.
- **Like terms** (same letter) combine by adding their numbers; **unlike terms** don't.
- A **−** in front of a bracket flips every sign inside.
- Equal expressions give equal values for **every** choice of letters.
- Patterns: describe in words → write with letters → simplify. A letter for "any number" **proves** a pattern.
- Repeating patterns of length k: use the **remainder** on dividing by k.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** 3d with d = 6 is 18, not 36 · 3a + 2b ≠ 5ab ≠ 5 · 5u ≠ 5 + u · 6(p + 2) = 6p + **12** · 5 − (2 − 6z) = 3 **+** 6z · "subtract A from B" = B − A · 3 stations in between means 4 gaps.
