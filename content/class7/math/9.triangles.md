# 9. Triangles

## Parts & types (recap)

- A triangle has **3 sides, 3 angles, 3 vertices** — named by its corners: triangle ABC.
- By sides: **equilateral** (3 equal) · **isosceles** (2 equal) · **scalene** (none equal).
- By angles: **acute** (all angles `< 90°`) · **right** (one angle `= 90°`) · **obtuse** (one angle `> 90°`).

## Median & altitude

- **Median:** segment from a vertex to the **midpoint** of the opposite side — it cuts that side in half. Every triangle has **3 medians**, all meeting inside.
- **Altitude:** **perpendicular** from a vertex to the opposite side — the "height". Every triangle has **3 altitudes**; in an obtuse triangle an altitude can fall **outside** the triangle.

> [!TIP]
> **M**edian → **M**idpoint · **A**ltitude → right **A**ngle (90°).

## Angle sum property

- The 3 angles of **any** triangle add to **180°**.
- Example: two angles are 65° and 45° → third = `180° − (65° + 45°)` = **70°**.
- Equilateral triangle → each angle = `180° ÷ 3` = **60°**.
- Isosceles triangle → the 2 **base angles** (opposite the equal sides) are **equal**.

## Exterior angle property

- Stretch one side outwards: the **exterior angle = sum of the two interior opposite angles**.
- Example: interior opposite angles 60° and 70° → exterior angle = **130°**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Exterior angle = 130° and one interior opposite angle = 60° → the other interior opposite angle = `130° − 60°` = **70°**.

## Triangle inequality

- **The sum of any two sides > the third side** (and the difference of any two sides < the third side).
- Quick check: add the **two smallest** sides — the sum must beat the longest side.
- 4, 5, 6 → ✓ (`4 + 5 = 9 > 6`) · 2, 3, 6 → ✗ (`2 + 3 = 5 < 6` — the triangle can't close).

## Pythagoras property (right triangles)

- **Hypotenuse** = the side opposite the right angle = the **longest** side; the other two sides are the **legs**.
- **hypotenuse² = leg² + leg²**.
- Example: legs 3 and 4 → `3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5²` → hypotenuse = **5**.
- Triplets worth memorising: **3-4-5 · 6-8-10 · 5-12-13 · 8-15-17** (and their multiples).
- Works in **reverse** too: if `a² + b² = c²`, the triangle is right-angled (the right angle faces side c).

> [!WARNING]
> Pythagoras works **only in right triangles** · the hypotenuse never touches the right angle — it sits opposite it.
