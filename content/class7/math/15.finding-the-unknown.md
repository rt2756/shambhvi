## Balancing scales

The chapter starts with a **weighing scale** that balances when both plates hold the same weight.
- Three bread slices of weight 2 balance two equal fried eggs: 2 + 2 + 2 = e + e → **2e = 6** → each egg is **3**.
- A weight of 4 plus two equal objects y balance 16: **4 + 2y = 16** → take 4 off both plates: 2y = 12 → **y = 6**.
- Sacks on both plates? **Take one sack off each plate** — the scale stays balanced, and now the sacks are on one side only.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A balanced scale stays balanced if you add or remove the SAME weight from both plates** (or double both, halve both…). This single idea is how we solve equations.

## Equations

**Jasmine's matchsticks:** position n of a pattern uses **2n + 1** sticks (3, 5, 7, …). Which position uses exactly **99** sticks? We need n with

> **2n + 1 = 99**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **An equation** says two expressions are equal: Left Hand Side (**LHS**) = Right Hand Side (**RHS**). Examples: 3x + 4 = 7, 20 = y − 3, a/5 = 50, 2z + 4 = 5z − 14.
> **Solving** an equation means finding the value of the letter-number that makes LHS = RHS.

### Trial and error

Try values of n in 2n + 1: n = 5 → 11 (far off), 10 → 21, 30 → 61, 40 → 81, 50 → 101 (too high), **49 → 99** ✔. So **n = 49**.
It works, but slowly — try 5x − 4 = 7 this way and you'll never land on a whole number (x = 11/5)!

**Can 200 sticks work?** 2n + 1 is always **odd** (even + 1), and 200 is even → **no**.

## Solving systematically: do the same to both sides

If 15 + 8 = 23, then adding 10 to both sides keeps them equal (33 = 33), and so does subtracting, multiplying or dividing both sides by the same (non-zero) number.

**Warm-up with numbers (book):**
- 14593 − 1459 + 145 − 14 + 88 = 13353 → remove the + 88 by subtracting 88 from both sides: the rest is **13265**.
- 23 × 41 × 11 × 8 × 7 = 5,80,888 → remove the × 7 by dividing both sides by 7: 23 × 41 × 11 × 8 = **82,984**.
- 12345 − 5432 + 135 − 24 − (−67) = 7091 → remove the "− (−67)" (which is +67) by adding −67 to both sides: **7024**.

**Now 5x − 4 = 7:**
1. Remove −4: **add 4** to both sides → 5x = 11.
2. Remove the factor 5: **divide** both sides by 5 → **x = 11/5**.
3. **Check:** 5 × 11/5 − 4 = 11 − 4 = 7 ✔.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **To solve: undo what's been done to the unknown, in reverse order, doing the same thing to both sides.**
> - A term **added** → subtract it from both sides. A term **subtracted** → add it.
> - A **factor** multiplying → divide both sides. A **divisor** → multiply both sides.
> **Always check** by putting your answer back into the original equation.

**11y + (−5) = 61:** subtract (−5) from both sides → 11y = 66 → divide by 11 → **y = 6**.

### Unknowns on both sides

**6y + 7 = 4y + 21:** first gather the y's on one side — **subtract 4y** from both sides: 2y + 7 = 21. Then subtract 7: 2y = 14 → **y = 7**. (Check: 42 + 7 = 49 = 28 + 21 ✔.)

### The shortcut ("moving" terms)

Doing the same to both sides is often written in one step:

| Full step | Short form | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 2y + 7 − 7 = 21 − 7 | 2y = 21 − 7 | a term crosses over with its **inverse** sign |
| 2y ÷ 2 = 14 ÷ 2 | y = 14 ÷ 2 | a **factor** removed → divide the other side |
| u/15 × 15 = 6 × 15 | u = 6 × 15 = 90 | a **divisor** removed → multiply the other side |

> [!WARNING]
> **"Moving a term" is really "doing the same to both sides"** — that's why the sign flips. And **divide the WHOLE other side**, not just one term of it.

**Book Figure it Out:**
- 3x − 10 = 35 → 3x = 45 → **x = 15**
- 5s = 3s → 2s = 0 → **s = 0**
- 3u − 7 = 2u + 3 → **u = 10**
- 4(m + 6) − 8 = 2m − 4 → 4m + 16 = 2m − 4 → 2m = −20 → **m = −10**
- **An equation with no solution:** x + 4 = x + 5 — "4 more than a number" can never equal "5 more than the same number" (subtract x: 4 = 5, false).

## Solving problems with equations

**Ranjana's tiles (book):** step k uses **3k + 1** tiles (seen as three arms of k plus a centre, or k + (2k + 1)). For 100 tiles: 3k + 1 = 100 → 3k = 99 → **k = 33** — yes, step 33.

**Madhubanti's party:** snacks ₹25 a plate + ₹50 delivery, budget ₹500, 5 family members.
- Fatima (no letters): 500 − 50 = 450 for snacks; 450 ÷ 25 = 18 plates; 18 − 5 = **13 friends**.
- Mahesh (p = people): 25p + 50 = 500 → 25p = 450 → p = 18 → 13 friends.
- Srikanth (f = friends): 25(f + 5) = 450 → f + 5 = 18 → **f = 13**.

> [!TIP]
> **Different choices of unknown give different equations — but the same answer.** Choose whatever makes the equation simplest to write.

**Savings (book):** Jahnavi has ₹4000 and saves ₹650 a month; Sunita has ₹5050 and saves ₹500 a month. After m months: 4000 + 650m = 5050 + 500m → 150m = 1050 → **m = 7 months**. (Check: 4000 + 4550 = 8550 = 5050 + 3500 ✔.)

**Brackets (book):** 28(x + 4) + 300 = 1000. Three good routes:
- subtract 300 → 28(x + 4) = 700 → divide by 28 → x + 4 = 25 → **x = 21**;
- divide everything by 4 first → 7(x + 4) + 75 = 250 → …;
- expand → 28x + 412 = 1000 → 28x = 588 → x = 21.

**Riyaz's trick:** think of x, subtract 3, multiply by 4, add 8. That's 4(x − 3) + 8 = **4x − 4**. Akash's answer was 24: 4x − 4 = 24 → x − 1 = 6 → **x = 7**. **Rule for any answer:** divide by 4, then add 1.

**Two unknowns (book):** Ramesh and Suresh have 60 marbles; Ramesh has 30 more. Call Suresh's y, so Ramesh's is y + 30: y + (y + 30) = 60 → 2y = 30 → **Suresh 15, Ramesh 45**.

> [!TIP]
> **With two unknowns, write one in terms of the other** ("Ramesh = Suresh + 30") so only one letter is left.

## Making equations from a solution

Start from y = 5 and keep doing the same thing to both sides — every equation in the chain has the solution 5:
y = 5 → y + 2 = 7 → 3y + 6 = 21 → 3y = 15. Or y + 1 = 6 → −y − 1 = −6 → …
**Equations with solution x = −2:** x + 5 = 3 · 3x = −6 · 2x + 7 = 3 · 10 − x = 12 · x/2 = −1.

**A story for 100x + 75 = 250:** 100 plates of something at ₹x each plus a fixed ₹75 delivery cost ₹250 in total → x = ₹1.75.

**More practice (book):** 2y = 60 → **30** · −8 = 5x − 3 → **x = −1** · 13 − z = 8 → **5** · k + 8 = 12 − k → 2k = 4 → **2** · 7m = m − 3 → 6m = −3 → **−1/2** · 3n = 10 + n → **5**.
- **A 3-digit number:** hundreds = tens − 3, tens = units − 3, digit sum 15. Let hundreds = a: a + (a + 3) + (a + 6) = 15 → a = 2 → **258**.
- **Brick** weighs 1 kg more than half its weight: w = w/2 + 1 → w/2 = 1 → **2 kg**.
- **A quarter of a number + 9 = the number:** n/4 + 9 = n → 9 = 3n/4 → **n = 12**.
- **Given 4k + 1 = 13** (so 4k = 12, k = 3): 8k + 2 = 2(4k + 1) = **26** · 4k = **12** · k = **3** · 4k − 1 = **11** · −k − 2 = **−5**. (Spot the shortcuts — 8k + 2 is just double the LHS!)

## Mind the mistake, mend the mistake (book)

| Equation | What went wrong | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| 4x + 6 = 10 → 4x = 10 + 6 | +6 must cross as **−6** | 4x = 4 → **x = 1** |
| 7 − 8z = 5 → 8z = 7 − 5 → z = 4 | 8z = 2 gives z = 2 ÷ 8, not 8 ÷ 2 | −8z = −2 → **z = 1/4** |
| 2v − 4 = 6 → v − 4 = 6 − 2 | divided only some terms by 2 | 2v = 10 → **v = 5** |
| 5z + 2 = 3z − 4 → 5z + 3z = −4 + 2 | 3z crosses as **−3z**, +2 as **−2** | 2z = −6 → **z = −3** |
| 15w − 4w = 26 → 15w = 26 + 4w → 15w = 30 | can't add 26 and 4w | 11w = 26 → **w = 26/11** |
| 3x + 1 = −12 → x + 1 = −4 | divided 3x by 3 but not the +1 | 3x = −13 → **x = −13/3** |
| 4(4q + 2) = 50 → 4(4q) = 50 − 2 | the 2 inside the bracket is really 4 × 2 = 8 | 16q + 8 = 50 → **q = 21/8** |
| −2(3 − 4x) = 14 → −6 − 8x = 14 | −2 × (−4x) = **+8x** | −6 + 8x = 14 → **x = 5/2** |
| 3(7y + 4) = 9 + 5y → 7y + 4 = 3 + 5y | divided 9 by 3 but forgot 5y | 21y + 12 = 9 + 5y → 16y = −3 → **y = −3/16** |

> [!WARNING]
> **The three most common slips:** forgetting to flip the sign when a term changes side · dividing only part of a side · multiplying a bracket by the outside number for the first term only.

## A pinch of history — bījagaṇita

In India algebra was called **bījagaṇita**: *bīja* = seed. The answer is hidden inside the unknown like a tree in a seed; solving helps it grow.
- **Brahmagupta** (*Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta*, 628 CE, Chapter 18) explained adding, subtracting and multiplying unknowns written as symbols — one of the earliest works of algebra. Unknowns were written **yā** (*yāvat-tāvat*, "as much as"), **kā** (*kālaka*, black), **nī** (*nīlaka*, blue)…; known numbers were marked **rū** (*rūpa*). 2x + 1 was written "yā 2 rū 1"; a dot over a number meant negative.
- **Al-Khwārizmī**, influenced by translated Indian works, wrote *Hisab al-jabr wal-muqabala* (~825 CE, "restoring and balancing") — *al-jabr* became **algebra**.

**Bhāskarāchārya's horse problem (Bījagaṇita, 1150 CE):** one man has ₹300 and 6 horses; another has 10 horses and a debt of ₹100. They are equally rich. Price of a horse x: 300 + 6x = 10x − 100 → 400 = 4x → **x = ₹100**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Brahmagupta's formula:** the solution of **Ax + B = Cx + D** is **x = (D − B) ÷ (A − C)**.
> **Why:** subtract Cx and B from both sides: Ax − Cx = D − B, i.e. (A − C)x = D − B; then divide by (A − C).
> Savings problem: m = (5050 − 4000) ÷ (650 − 500) = 1050 ÷ 150 = 7 ✔. And 2x + 3 = 4x + 5 → x = (5 − 3) ÷ (2 − 4) = **−1**.

## In a nutshell

- An **equation** says LHS = RHS; **solving** finds the value that makes it true.
- **Do the same to both sides** — like a balance scale.
- Undo operations **in reverse order**: remove added/subtracted terms first, then factors.
- Unknowns on both sides → gather them on one side first.
- A term changing sides **flips its sign**; a factor removed **divides the whole other side**.
- **Check** every answer by substituting.
- Word problems: choose the unknown, write each quantity in terms of it, form the equation.
- Brahmagupta: Ax + B = Cx + D → **x = (D − B)/(A − C)**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** 4x + 6 = 10 gives 4x = 10 **−** 6 · 8z = 2 gives z = 2/8, not 4 · divide **every** term · −2(3 − 4x) = −6 **+** 8x · "subtract A from B" means B − A · x + 4 = x + 5 has no solution.
