# 11. Rational numbers

## What counts as rational?

- Any number that can be written as **p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0**: `3/4`, `−5/2`, `7 (= 7/1)`, `0`, `0.3 (= 3/10)`.
- So every natural number, whole number, integer and fraction is rational — the **biggest number family so far**.
- **Positive rational:** numerator & denominator have the **same sign** (`2/3`, `−2/−3`). **Negative rational:** **opposite signs** (`−2/3`).
- **0 is rational but neither positive nor negative**, and the same number has many looks: `−2/3 = 2/−3 = −(2/3)`.

## Standard form

- **Denominator positive**, and numerator & denominator share **no common factor except 1**.
- Example: `−12/18` → divide both by HCF 6 → **`−2/3`**.
- Negative denominator? Multiply top and bottom by −1: `3/−5 = −3/5`.

## Number line & comparing

- Negative rationals sit **left of 0**, positive ones right; `−1/2` is midway between 0 and −1.
- To compare: give them the same **positive** denominator, then compare numerators.
  - `−3/4` vs `−1/4` → −3 < −1 → `−3/4 < −1/4` (further left = smaller).
- **Between any two rational numbers there are infinitely many rationals** — e.g. between `1/4` and `1/2` lies `3/8` (their average), and so on forever.

## Operations

- **Add / subtract:** make the denominators equal first → `−2/3 + 1/6 = −4/6 + 1/6 = −3/6 = −1/2`.
- **Multiply:** tops together, bottoms together (settle the sign first) → `−2/3 × 4/5 = −8/15`.
- **Divide:** multiply by the **reciprocal** → `−2/3 ÷ 4/9 = −2/3 × 9/4 = −18/12 = −3/2`.
- Sign rules are the **integer rules**: same signs → positive, opposite signs → negative.

> [!WARNING]
> On the negative side, bigger digits mean a **smaller** number: `−3/4 < −1/4` · write a **positive denominator** before comparing or adding.
