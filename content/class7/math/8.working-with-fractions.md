## Multiplying a fraction by a whole number

**Aaron walks 3 km in 1 hour.** In 5 hours: 5 × 3 = 15 km — "5 lots of 3".

**His tortoise walks only 1/4 km in an hour.** In 3 hours: **3 × 1/4** = 1/4 + 1/4 + 1/4 = **3/4 km**. The meaning of × hasn't changed: "3 lots of 1/4".

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Whole number × fraction: multiply the numerator by the whole number.** 5 × 2/3 = 10/3.
> **Why:** 5 × 2/3 means 2/3 + 2/3 + 2/3 + 2/3 + 2/3 — five lots of 2 thirds is 10 thirds.

**Examples (book):**
- A farmer gives **2/3 acre** to each of 5 grandchildren → 5 × 2/3 = **10/3 = 3 1/3 acres**.
- Internet costs ₹8 an hour. For **1 1/4 hours** = 5/4 hours → 5/4 × 8 = 5 × 2 = **₹10**.

## A fraction of something: "1/5 of 3"

How far does Aaron walk in **1/5 of an hour**? Distance = **1/5 × 3** km. In 1/5 of an hour he covers the length you get by splitting 3 km into **5 equal parts** = **3/5 km**.

In **2/5 of an hour**: first 1/5 of an hour (3/5 km), then **double** it → **6/5 km**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **"2/5 × 3" means: divide 3 by the denominator (5), then multiply by the numerator (2).** The first fraction (the **multiplier**) tells you how many equal parts to cut the other number (the **multiplicand**) into, and how many of those parts to take.
> **Why "×" here means "of":** walking for 2/5 of an hour covers 2/5 **of** the hourly distance. "Of" and "×" are the same operation.

## Multiplying two fractions — the unit square

**Tortoise at 1/4 km per hour, for half an hour:** 1/2 × 1/4 — half of a quarter.

Draw a **unit square** (area 1). Shade 1/4 of it by cutting it into 4 columns. Now cut it in half the other way (2 rows). The square is now in **2 × 4 = 8** equal pieces, and half of the quarter is **one** of them → **1/2 × 1/4 = 1/8**.

**3/4 × 2/5** (a tortoise at 2/5 km/h, walking 3/4 of an hour):
- 2/5 of the square: cut into 5 rows, shade 2.
- Split that into 4 equal parts by cutting into 4 columns → the square is now 5 × 4 = **20** tiny pieces; one quarter of the shaded strip is **2** pieces → 2/20.
- Take 3 of those quarters → **6/20 = 3/10**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Area model:** a rectangle with sides a/b and c/d has area **(a × c)/(b × d)**. The square is cut into b × d equal tiny rectangles, and the shaded rectangle covers a × c of them.

**Two unit fractions:** 1/12 × 1/18 → the square is cut into 12 × 18 = 216 pieces, one is shaded → **1/216**. In general **1/b × 1/d = 1/(b × d)**.

### Brahmagupta's rule

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **a/b × c/d = (a × c)/(b × d)** — multiply the numerators, multiply the denominators.
> 5/12 × 7/18 = 35/216. Stated in this general form by **Brahmagupta** in his *Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta* (**628 CE**).
> **Why it works:** b × d counts how many equal pieces the whole is cut into; a × c counts how many of them you end up with.

A whole number is a fraction with denominator 1: 3 × 3/4 = 3/1 × 3/4 = **9/4**. And 5/4 × 3/2 = **15/8**.

**Book Figure it Out:** 1/3 × 1/5 = **1/15** · 1/4 × 1/3 = **1/12** · 1/5 × 1/2 = **1/10** · 1/6 × 1/5 = **1/30** · 2/3 × 4/5 = **8/15** · 1/4 × 2/3 = 2/12 = **1/6** · 3/5 × 1/2 = **3/10** · 4/6 × 3/5 = 12/30 = **2/5**.

### Cancel first, then multiply

**12/7 × 5/24:** 12 (on top) and 24 (below) share the factor 12. Divide both by 12 → 1/7 × 5/2 = **5/14**.
**14/15 × 25/42:** 14 and 42 share 14 → 1 and 3; 25 and 15 share 5 → 5 and 3 → 1 × 5 / (3 × 3) = **5/9**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **You may cancel any numerator with any denominator before multiplying.**
> **Why:** the answer is (top × top)/(bottom × bottom) — one big fraction — and dividing its top and bottom by the same number never changes a fraction's value. Cancelling early just keeps the numbers small.

> [!NOTE]
> **Apavartana** — reducing a fraction to lowest terms — was so familiar in India that the Jain scholar **Umāsvāti (c. 150 CE)** used it as a comparison in a philosophy book.

### Order doesn't matter

1/2 × 1/4 = 1/4 × 1/2 = 1/8. A rectangle has the same area if you swap its length and breadth, so **a/b × c/d = c/d × a/b**.

## Is a product always bigger?

With counting numbers bigger than 1, yes: 3 × 5 = 15 is bigger than both. With fractions — not always!

| Numbers multiplied | Example | Product |
|---|---|---|
| both greater than 1 | 4 × 4/3 = 16/3 | **bigger than both** |
| both between 0 and 1 | 3/4 × 2/5 = 3/10 | **smaller than both** |
| one less than 1, one greater | 3/4 × 5 = 15/4 | **in between** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Multiplying by a number between 0 and 1 makes things smaller; multiplying by a number greater than 1 makes things bigger.**
> **Why:** × 3/4 means "take 3/4 **of** it" — less than the whole thing. × 4/3 means "take the whole thing and 1/3 more".

**Book exercise:** 565/465 × 707/676 — both fractions are more than 1, so the product is **more than 565/465, more than 707/676, and more than 1** — no calculation needed.

## Dividing fractions

**Division is multiplication backwards.** 12 ÷ 4 = ? asks "4 × ? = 12".

**1 ÷ 2/3** asks "2/3 × ? = 1". Flip it: 2/3 × 3/2 = 6/6 = 1. So **1 ÷ 2/3 = 3/2**.
**3 ÷ 2/3:** we need 3 times as much → 3 × 3/2 = **9/2**.
**2/3 ÷ 3/5:** "3/5 × ? = 2/3". 3/5 × 5/3 = 1, so 3/5 × (5/3 × 2/3) = 2/3 → answer **5/3 × 2/3 = 10/9**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The reciprocal of a/b is b/a** — flip it. A number times its reciprocal is **1** (2/3 × 3/2 = 1).
> **To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal:** **a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c = (a × d)/(b × c)**.
> **Why it works:** dividing by c/d asks "what times c/d gives a/b?". Multiplying c/d by d/c gives 1, and then multiplying by a/b gives a/b — so the answer is d/c × a/b.
> Brahmagupta (628 CE) stated it; Bhāskara II (*Līlāvatī*, 1150 CE) put it as "multiply the first by the reciprocal of the second".

> [!TIP]
> **Quick check by meaning:** 6 ÷ 1/4 asks "how many quarters fit in 6?" Each whole has 4 quarters → **24**. That matches 6 × 4/1 = 24.

### Is a quotient always smaller?

- 6 ÷ 3 = 2 — **smaller** than 6.
- 6 ÷ 1/4 = **24** — **bigger** than 6!
- 1/8 ÷ 1/4 = **1/2** — bigger than 1/8.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Dividing by a number between 0 and 1 gives a bigger answer; dividing by a number greater than 1 gives a smaller answer.**
> **Why:** dividing by 1/4 means multiplying by 4. Small pieces fit many times.

## Problems from everyday life and old books

**Leena's tea (book):** 1/4 litre of milk for 5 cups. Per cup: 1/4 ÷ 5 = 1/4 × 1/5 = **1/20 litre**.

**Baudhāyana's Śulbasūtra (c. 800 BCE):** cover **7 1/2 square units** with square bricks of side **1/5 unit**.
Area of one brick = 1/5 × 1/5 = 1/25. Number of bricks = 15/2 ÷ 1/25 = 15/2 × 25 = 375/2 = **187 1/2 bricks**.

**Pṛithūdakasvāmī's fountains (c. 860 CE):** four fountains fill a cistern in 1 day, 1/2 day, 1/4 day and 1/5 day. In one day they fill it 1 ÷ 1 = 1, 1 ÷ 1/2 = 2, 1 ÷ 1/4 = 4, 1 ÷ 1/5 = 5 times — together **12 times a day**. So together they fill it once in **1/12 of a day** (2 hours).

> [!TIP]
> **For "working together" problems, count how many jobs each does in one unit of time, add those up, then flip.** Times don't add; rates do.

**Fractional areas (book):** a square split so that a small square is 1/4 of it, a triangle half of that (1/8), and 3/4 of the triangle shaded → 3/4 × 1/8 = **3/32** of the big square. "A fraction of a fraction" is multiplication.

**A dramma-tic donation (Līlāvatī, 1150 CE):** a miser gives a beggar 1/2 of 2/3 of 3/4 of 1/5 of 1/16 of 1/4 of a dramma. Multiply: (1 × 2 × 3 × 1 × 1 × 1)/(2 × 3 × 4 × 5 × 16 × 4) = 6/7680 = **1/1280 dramma**. One dramma = 1280 cowrie shells → he gave **one cowrie** — Bhāskara's joke!

**Old coins:** if 1 gold dinar = 12 drammas and 1 dramma = 4 panas, then 1 pana = 1/12 × 1/4 = **1/48 dinar**. With 30 cowries to a pana, 1 cowrie = 1/30 pana = 1/30 × 1/48 = **1/1440 dinar**.

## Figure it Out — worked answers

**Multiplying with whole numbers:**
- Tenzin drinks 1/2 glass of milk a day → a week: 7 × 1/2 = **7/2 = 3 1/2 glasses**; January (31 days): **31/2 = 15 1/2 glasses**.
- 1 km of canal in 8 days → **1/8 km** a day → 5 days: **5/8 km**.
- 5 litres shared by 3 families → **5/3 litres** each a week; in 4 weeks **20/3 = 6 2/3 litres**.
- The Moon sets 5/6 hour later each day. Monday → Thursday is 3 days → 3 × 5/6 = 15/6 = **2 1/2 hours** later → **12:30 a.m.**
- 7 × 3/5 = 21/5 = **4 1/5** · 4 × 1/3 = **1 1/3** · 9/7 × 6 = 54/7 = **7 5/7** · 13/11 × 6 = 78/11 = **7 1/11**

**Fraction × fraction:**
- A tap fills 7/10 of a tank in an hour. In 1/3 h: **7/30**; 2/3 h: **14/30 = 7/15**; 3/4 h: **21/40**; 7/10 h: **49/100**. To fill it all: 1 ÷ 7/10 = **10/7 hours**.
- **Somu's land:** 1/6 taken for a road → **5/6** left. Krishna gets half of that = **5/12** of the original; Bora gets 1/3 of it = **5/18**; Somu keeps 5/6 − 5/12 − 5/18 = (30 − 15 − 10)/36 = **5/36**.
- Rectangle 3 3/4 ft × 9 3/5 ft = 15/4 × 48/5 = **36 sq ft** (cancel 15 with 5 and 48 with 4 → 3 × 12).
- **Four saplings** 3/4 m apart: 4 saplings make only **3 gaps** → 3 × 3/4 = **9/4 = 2 1/4 m**.
- 12/15 of 500 g = **400 g**; 3/20 of 4 kg = 3/20 × 4000 g = **600 g** → the second is heavier.

**Division:**
- 3 ÷ 7/9 = **27/7** · 14/4 ÷ 2 = **7/4** · 2/3 ÷ 2/3 = **1** · 14/6 ÷ 7/3 = **1** · 4/3 ÷ 3/4 = **16/9** · 7/4 ÷ 1/7 = **49/4** · 8/2 ÷ 4/15 = **15** · 1/5 ÷ 1/9 = **9/5** · 1/6 ÷ 11/12 = **2/11** · 3 2/3 ÷ 1 3/8 = 11/3 × 8/11 = **8/3 = 2 2/3**
- **Maria:** 8 m of lace, 1/4 m per bag → **8 ÷ 1/4 = 32 bags**.
- **Badges:** 1/2 m of ribbon for 8 badges → **1/2 ÷ 8 = 1/16 m** each.
- **Baker:** 1/6 kg per loaf, 5 kg → **5 ÷ 1/6 = 30 loaves**.
- 1/4 kg flour for 12 rotis → 6 rotis (half as many) → **1/8 kg**.
- **Śrīdharāchārya's Pāṭīgaṇita:** 1 ÷ 1/6 + 1 ÷ 1/10 + 1 ÷ 1/13 + 1 ÷ 1/9 + 1 ÷ 1/2 = 6 + 10 + 13 + 9 + 2 = **40**.
- **Mira's novel** (400 pages): read 3/10 (120 pages) and 1/5 (80 pages) → **200 pages left**.
- **Car:** 16 km per litre × 2 3/4 litres = 16 × 11/4 = **44 km**.
- **Amritpal:** train 5 1/6 h, plane 1/2 h → saves 31/6 − 3/6 = 28/6 = **4 2/3 hours**.
- **Mariam's cake:** 4/5 eaten → 1/5 left, shared by 3 → 1/5 ÷ 3 = **1/15** each.
- **Ants** splitting in halves at each fork: the book's answers are **29/32** and **3/32** — follow each path multiplying by 1/2 at every split and add the paths ending at the same place.

**A beautiful pattern (book):**
(1 − 1/2) = 1/2 · (1 − 1/2)(1 − 1/3) = 1/2 × 2/3 = **1/3** · up to (1 − 1/5): 1/2 × 2/3 × 3/4 × 4/5 = **1/5** · up to (1 − 1/10): **1/10**.
**In general, (1 − 1/2)(1 − 1/3) … (1 − 1/n) = 1/n.** **Why:** each bracket is (k − 1)/k, so every numerator cancels the denominator before it: 1/2 × 2/3 × 3/4 × … × (n − 1)/n — only the first 1 and the last n survive.

## In a nutshell

- **"of" means ×.** 2/5 × 3 = 3 cut into 5 parts, take 2.
- **a/b × c/d = (a × c)/(b × d)** — Brahmagupta's rule; the unit-square area model shows why.
- **Cancel** any top with any bottom before multiplying.
- × a number below 1 → **smaller**; × a number above 1 → **bigger**.
- **Reciprocal** of a/b is b/a; their product is 1.
- **a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c.** ÷ a number below 1 → **bigger** answer.
- Combined work: **add rates**, not times.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** 1/2 × 1/4 is 1/8, not 2/8 or 1/6 · multiplying can make things smaller · dividing by 1/4 makes things 4 times bigger · flip the **divisor** (second fraction), not the first · 4 saplings have 3 gaps · change mixed numbers to improper fractions before multiplying or dividing.
