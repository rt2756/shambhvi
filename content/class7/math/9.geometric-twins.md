## Exact copies — congruence

A signboard has a symbol shaped like a bent arm: corner points A, B, C with arms AB = 4 cm and BC = 8 cm. Are the two arm lengths enough to copy it exactly? **No** — you could open the arms wide or close them up, getting many different shapes with the same arm lengths. Add the angle **∠ABC = 80°** and the shape is fixed.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Congruent figures have exactly the same shape and size** — one can be placed on the other and fit exactly (we say **superimposed**). You are allowed to **slide, turn or flip** a figure before placing it.
> The symbol ≅ means "is congruent to".

**What fixes a figure?** (book)
- **Circle:** just the **radius**. Two circles with equal radii are congruent.
- **Rectangle:** **length and breadth**. Two rectangles with equal length and breadth are congruent (all corners are 90°, so nothing else can change).
- **The bent-arm symbol:** **two arm lengths + the angle between them**.

> [!TIP]
> To decide what to measure, ask: **"If I keep these measurements, can I still wiggle the shape?"** If yes, you need more measurements.

## Congruent triangles

Meera and Rabia must copy a big triangular frame with sides 40, 60, 80 cm. Do they also need its angles?

### SSS — three sides

Rabia imagines building a triangle with sides 4, 6, 8 cm: draw AB = 6 cm, then circles of radius 4 cm from A and 8 cm from B. The circles cross at **two** points, E (above) and F (below), giving ∆ABE and ∆ABF. Are these different triangles? **No** — one is just the other **flipped over AB** (the construction is the same above and below the line). So every triangle with these sides is the same.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SSS: if the three sides of one triangle equal the three sides of another, the triangles are congruent.**
> **Why:** three lengths fix the compass construction completely — the only freedom is "above or below the base", which is just a flip.

So Meera was right — the angles are not needed.

### Writing congruence correctly

If ∆ABC fits on ∆XYZ with A on X, B on Y, C on Z, we write **∆ABC ≅ ∆XYZ**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The ORDER of letters matters:** first ↔ first, second ↔ second, third ↔ third.
> - Corresponding vertices: A–X, B–Y, C–Z
> - Corresponding sides: AB–XY, BC–YZ, AC–XZ
> - Corresponding angles: ∠A = ∠X, ∠B = ∠Y, ∠C = ∠Z
>
> So ∆ACB ≅ ∆XZY is also correct (same pairs, reordered together), but **∆ACB ≅ ∆XYZ is wrong** (it pairs C with Y).

**Book: ∆HEN ≅ ∆BIG.** Other correct ways: ∆HNE ≅ ∆BGI, ∆EHN ≅ ∆IBG, ∆ENH ≅ ∆IGB, ∆NHE ≅ ∆GBI, ∆NEH ≅ ∆GIB — any reordering, as long as both names are reordered the **same way**.

**Rectangle ABCD with diagonal BD (book):** AB = CD and AD = CB (opposite sides of a rectangle), BD is shared → **SSS**. But which vertices match? Pairing A–C, B–B, D–D would lay AB on CB — not equal in general! The correct match puts AB on CD: **∆ABD ≅ ∆CDB** (A↔C, B↔D, D↔B).

> [!TIP]
> **Find the correspondence from the equal sides:** the vertex *between* two known sides in one triangle matches the vertex between the equal sides in the other.

**Kite (book):** AB = AD, CB = CD, and AC is shared → **∆ABC ≅ ∆ADC** (SSS). So ∠BAC = ∠DAC and ∠BCA = ∠DCA — **AC cuts both ∠A and ∠C into two equal parts.** (Equal parts of congruent triangles are equal.)

### Angles alone (AAA) are NOT enough

Triangles with angles 30°, 70°, 80° can be drawn small or large — **same shape, different size**. So three equal angles do **not** make triangles congruent.

> [!WARNING]
> **AAA is not a congruence rule.** A photo and its enlargement have equal angles but aren't congruent.

### SAS — two sides and the included angle

AB = XY = 6 cm, AC = XZ = 5 cm, ∠A = ∠X = 30°. Constructing it (Chapter 7) has **no choices left**: draw AB, turn 30° at A, measure 5 cm along. Every student gets the same triangle.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SAS: two sides and the angle BETWEEN them equal → congruent.**

### SSA — two sides and a NON-included angle — fails!

PQ = 6 cm, QR = 4 cm, ∠P = 30° (the angle at P is not between the two given sides). Draw PQ, a 30° line from P, then a 4 cm arc from Q. The arc cuts the line at **two** points R and S — giving two **different** triangles ∆PQR and ∆PQS, one fat and one thin.

> [!WARNING]
> **SSA does NOT guarantee congruence.** The 4 cm side can swing to two positions. (Mnemonic: don't spell it backwards!)

### ASA — two angles and the included side

BC = YZ = 5 cm, ∠B = ∠Y = 50°, ∠C = ∠Z = 30°. Draw BC; the rays at B and C meet in exactly one point.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ASA: two angles and the side between them equal → congruent.**

**Book example:** O is the midpoint of both AD and BC (two segments crossing at O). AO = OD, BO = OC, and ∠AOB = ∠DOC (vertically opposite) → **SAS** → **∆AOB ≅ ∆DOC**, so **AB = DC**.

### AAS — two angles and a non-included side

∠A = ∠X = 35°, ∠C = ∠Z = 75°, BC = YZ = 4 cm. BC is not between ∠A and ∠C. But the **angle sum** gives the third angle: ∠B = 180 − 35 − 75 = **70°** = ∠Y. Now ∠B, BC, ∠C is ASA → congruent.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **AAS: two angles and any matching side equal → congruent.**
> **Why:** knowing two angles means you know the third (they add to 180°), so you can always switch to ASA.

### RHS — the special right-triangle case

Right triangles with ∠B = ∠Y = 90°, BC = YZ = 4 cm and AC = XZ = 5 cm. Construct: QR = 4 cm, a perpendicular at Q, then a 5 cm arc from R. The arc cuts the perpendicular at P above **and** at a point below QR — but the lower triangle is just the **mirror image** of the upper one, so it's congruent.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **RHS (Right angle–Hypotenuse–Side):** two right triangles with equal **hypotenuses** and one other equal side are congruent.
> The **hypotenuse** is the side **opposite the right angle** (the longest side).
> **Why this SSA case works:** the 90° angle makes the perpendicular line symmetric about QR, so the two crossing points give mirror-image (congruent) triangles instead of a fat one and a thin one.

| Rule | What's equal | Congruent? |
|---|---|---|
| SSS | 3 sides | ✔ |
| SAS | 2 sides + angle between them | ✔ |
| ASA | 2 angles + side between them | ✔ |
| AAS | 2 angles + another side | ✔ |
| RHS | right angle + hypotenuse + a side | ✔ |
| AAA | 3 angles | ✘ (size can differ) |
| SSA | 2 sides + angle not between them | ✘ (two possible triangles) |

## Using congruence: angles of isosceles and equilateral triangles

**∆ABC with AB = AC and ∠A = 80°.** Draw the altitude AD to BC. In ∆ADB and ∆ADC:
- AB = AC (given) — these are the hypotenuses,
- ∠ADB = ∠ADC = 90° (altitude),
- AD is common.

**RHS** → ∆ADB ≅ ∆ADC → **∠B = ∠C**. Then ∠B + ∠C = 180 − 80 = 100 → each **50°**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **In a triangle, the angles opposite equal sides are equal.** (In an isosceles triangle, the two "base angles" are equal.)

**Equilateral triangle:** AB = AC → ∠C = ∠B; AB = BC → ∠C = ∠A. So all three angles are equal: 3 × angle = 180° → **each is 60°** — proved, not just measured.

**More from the book:**
- A is the centre of a circle, B and C on it, ∠A = 120°. AB = AC (radii) → ∠B = ∠C = (180 − 120) ÷ 2 = **30°** each.
- **OB = OC and OA = OD** (AD and BC cross at O): ∆AOB ≅ ∆DOC by SAS (vertically opposite angles at O). So ∠OAB = ∠ODC — these are **alternate angles** on transversal AD → **AB ∥ CD**.
- **Square ABCD:** ∆ABC ≅ ∆ADC (AB = AD, BC = DC, AC common). And because *all* sides are equal, also ∆ABC ≅ ∆CDA — one triangle fits on the other in **two** ways. An **equilateral** triangle fits on a copy of itself in **six** ways (3 turns × flip or no flip).
- **CD ∥ AB, AB = CD, with AD and BC crossing at O:** alternate angles ∠OAB = ∠ODC and ∠OBA = ∠OCD, plus AB = DC → **ASA** → ∆AOB ≅ ∆DOC, so AO = DO and BO = CO.

**Book Figure it Out — which pairs are congruent?**
- (a) AB = DE, BC = EF, CA = DF → **SSS**, ∆ABC ≅ ∆DEF.
- (b) AB = EF, ∠A = ∠E, AC = ED → **SAS** (∠A is between AB and AC), ∆ABC ≅ ∆EFD.
- (c) AB = DF, ∠B = ∠D = 90°, AC = FE → **RHS** (AC and FE are hypotenuses), ∆ABC ≅ ∆FDE.
- (d) ∠A = ∠D, ∠B = ∠E, AC = DF → **AAS**, ∆ABC ≅ ∆DEF.
- (e) AB = DF, ∠B = ∠F, AC = DE → this is **SSA** (∠B isn't between AB and AC) → **not necessarily** congruent.
- **∆AIR ≅ ∆FLY:** A–F, I–L, R–Y; sides AI = FL, IR = LY, AR = FY; angles ∠A = ∠F, ∠I = ∠L, ∠R = ∠Y.

> [!TIP]
> **Real life:** congruent triangles appear in the Louvre's glass pyramid, the Pyramid of Giza, domes, rangoli patterns and the steel frame of Howrah Bridge (Rabindra Setu). Congruent triangles in a frame share loads equally.

## In a nutshell

- **Congruent** = same shape and same size; you may slide, turn or flip.
- Triangle tests: **SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS** guarantee congruence; **AAA and SSA don't**.
- In SAS and ASA the A or S must be **between** the other two.
- Write ∆ABC ≅ ∆XYZ with **matching letters in matching places**.
- Corresponding parts of congruent triangles are equal — use them to prove new facts.
- **Angles opposite equal sides are equal**; an equilateral triangle's angles are all **60°**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** equal angles alone don't mean congruent · SSA is not a rule (except RHS for right triangles) · check that the angle in SAS is really the **included** one · ∆ACB ≅ ∆XYZ is wrong if A↔X, B↔Y, C↔Z · the hypotenuse is opposite the **right** angle.
