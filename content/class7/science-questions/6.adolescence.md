<!-- ===== Stages & definitions ===== -->

List the **five stages** of human life, and give the usual age range of adolescence.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Infancy → childhood → adolescence → adulthood → old age.**
>
> **Adolescence: about 10 to 19 years** — though each person reaches and passes through it at their own pace.

---

Why can't humans reproduce straight after birth?

> [!ANSWER]
> The body must first **grow and reach maturity**.
>
> Same as a plant: a just-germinated seedling can't make seeds — it has to grow into a mature plant first. Adolescence is the human body's preparation period for adulthood.

---

What is **puberty**?

> [!ANSWER]
> The stage in which an adolescent's body undergoes **external and internal changes** to develop into an adult **capable of reproduction**.

---

What are **secondary sexual characteristics**? Give two examples.

> [!ANSWER]
> Changes that **distinguish males from females but are not directly involved in reproduction** — they're the visible signal that puberty has begun.
>
> Examples: **change of voice** and **facial/chest hair** in boys; **development of breasts** in girls.

---

<!-- ===== The changes ===== -->

Why does the voice change during adolescence, and what is the **Adam's apple**?

> [!ANSWER]
> The **voice box (larynx)** — the structure in the throat that lets us speak — **grows** during adolescence, and a larger voice box produces a deeper, hoarser voice.
>
> In boys it grows enough to sometimes show as a **bump in the throat, the Adam's apple**. It grows in girls too, but less, so their voice changes only slightly.
>
> Not every boy has a noticeable Adam's apple — that's normal.

---

What causes **acne**, and what is the difference between acne and pimples?

> [!ANSWER]
> During adolescence the skin produces **more oily secretions**. These can **clog skin pores**, leading to infection.
>
> **Acne is the condition; pimples are how it shows** — the small reddish spots, commonly on the face.

---

Two classmates of the same age look very different — one is much taller, one has no facial hair yet. Is something wrong?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No — this is completely normal.**
>
> The **timing, nature and extent** of adolescent changes **vary from person to person**, and so does the duration of the whole stage. Each person develops at their own pace; variation is the rule, not the exception.

---

<!-- ===== Menstrual cycle ===== -->

How often does the menstrual cycle occur, and how long does menstruation usually last?

> [!ANSWER]
> The cycle recurs generally every **28–30 days**, though a healthy range is **21–35 days**.
>
> **Menstruation** — the phase when blood discharge occurs — usually lasts **3 to 7 days**. Some pain or discomfort in the lower abdomen during these days is common.

---

At what age does menstruation usually stop, and what does the cycle indicate about health?

> [!ANSWER]
> It stops naturally at about **45–55 years**, marking the end of reproductive capability.
>
> A regular cycle is **one of the signs of good reproductive health** — it is a normal, natural process, not an illness.

---

Some communities isolate girls during menstruation. What does science say about such practices?

> [!ANSWER]
> **These myths and taboos have no scientific basis.** Menstruation is a **natural, healthy process**.
>
> Such beliefs cause unnecessary **fear, shame and guilt**, and practices like physical isolation are harmful. A scientific outlook helps society support women's reproductive health instead of stigmatising it.

---

<!-- ===== Emotional changes ===== -->

Why do adolescents often experience mood swings and strong emotions, and how can these be channelled positively?

> [!ANSWER]
> Adolescence is marked by **stronger emotions than childhood** — this is a normal part of growing up.
>
> Channelled well they become strengths: **creativity** through music, dance, sport and self-exploration; and **compassion** through social work and helping others.
>
> Understanding that emotions drive behaviour lets you **pause and choose a response** rather than simply reacting.

---

<!-- ===== Nutrition ===== -->

Why is a balanced diet especially important during adolescence?

> [!ANSWER]
> Adolescence is a period of **rapid growth and development** — the body needs both **building material** and **energy** to do it.
>
> That means proteins and carbohydrates, plus enough fats, vitamins and minerals.

---

Which nutrient does each provide, and what is it for: **(a)** milk and paneer · **(b)** spinach and raisins?

> [!ANSWER]
> **(a) Calcium** (with protein and fats) → **optimal bone growth**.
>
> **(b) Iron** → **formation of blood**.

---

Adolescent girls are especially prone to blood-related health problems. Why, and what prevents them?

> [!ANSWER]
> A deficiency of **iron or vitamin B12** can cause blood-related problems (anaemia).
>
> Prevention: iron-rich foods (spinach, kidney beans, raisins, figs) and B12 sources. **Vitamin B12 cannot be made by the body**, so it *must* come from food — which is why diet matters rather than just "being healthy".

---

Who was **Dorothy Hodgkin**?

> [!ANSWER]
> The scientist who determined the **structure of vitamin B12**. In **1964** she became the **third woman** to win the Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

---

<!-- ===== Hygiene ===== -->

Why is personal hygiene especially important during adolescence?

> [!ANSWER]
> Increased secretions and hair growth, particularly in the **armpits and pubic region**, make infections more likely — regular cleaning protects against them.

---

How should used sanitary pads be disposed of, and why does it matter?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Wrap them in newspaper and put them in a dustbin.**
>
> Proper disposal protects **community health and environmental cleanliness**. **Biodegradable pads** are an environment-friendly option.

---

Name two government initiatives supporting menstrual hygiene.

> [!ANSWER]
> Any two of: the **Menstrual Hygiene Scheme (MHS)** (free or low-cost pads plus awareness in rural areas) · **Rashtriya Kishor Swasthya Karyakram (RKSK)** (adolescent health, with peer education) · the **Suvidha** initiative (affordable biodegradable pads via Jan Aushadhi Kendras) · state schemes such as Karnataka's **Shuchi**.

---

<!-- ===== Activity and social life ===== -->

Give three benefits of regular physical activity during adolescence.

> [!ANSWER]
> It keeps **body and mind fit**, builds **stamina**, and **boosts mood** — the benefit is mental as well as physical.

---

What is **cyberbullying**, and what should you do if it happens to you?

> [!ANSWER]
> Using digital devices or online platforms to **harass someone** — sending misleading messages, spreading false rumours, or sharing someone's personal information without consent.
>
> **If it happens: don't feel scared or helpless — tell your parents or teachers and get their help.** Also be cautious about uploading pictures or sharing personal information with strangers.

---

<!-- ===== Substances ===== -->

What is **substance abuse**, and why does addiction make these substances so dangerous?

> [!ANSWER]
> Regular consumption of harmful substances — tobacco, gutka, cigarettes, beedi, alcohol, illegal drugs.
>
> They damage **physical and mental health**, and they are **addictive**: once someone starts, they feel a **strong urge to take them again and again**, so occasional use turns into regular use. That trap is what makes the *first* time the decision that matters.

---

Why are adolescents particularly vulnerable to peer pressure about harmful substances?

> [!ANSWER]
> Adolescence is marked by **curiosity and excitement**, and a pull towards peers and their behaviour — so persuasion or pressure from friends carries extra weight.
>
> Knowing this in advance is itself the defence: recognising the pressure for what it is makes it far easier to **say NO**.
