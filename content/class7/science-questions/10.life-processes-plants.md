<!-- ===== Growth experiment ===== -->

Three identical saplings: **A** in sunlight with water, **B** in sunlight without water, **C** in the dark with water. Predict each result and state the conclusion.

> [!ANSWER]
> **A grows best** — it has everything it needs.
>
> **B may die** — no water, despite plenty of sunlight.
>
> **C grows poorly** — water but no sunlight.
>
> **Conclusion: plants need both sunlight and water to grow.**
>
> **Why three pots:** each differs from A by **one factor only**, so any difference in growth can be blamed on that factor alone.

---

<!-- ===== Iodine test ===== -->

Describe the starch test on a leaf, and say why the leaf is decolourised first.

> [!ANSWER]
> Boil the leaf ~5 minutes to soften it → dip it in **alcohol** (test tube standing in boiling water) until it turns **colourless** → add a few drops of dilute **iodine**.
>
> **Blue-black = starch present.**
>
> **Why decolourise:** the leaf's own green colour would mask the colour change. Removing it makes the blue-black clearly visible.

---

Why must the alcohol be heated in a beaker of hot water rather than directly over a flame?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Alcohol is highly flammable** — placing it near a direct heat source can easily cause fire and burns.
>
> Hot water heats it safely and gently, with no flame near the vapour.

<!-- ===== One experiment per ingredient ===== -->

A leaf with **green and non-green patches** from a plant kept in sunlight is tested with iodine. What is seen, and what does it prove?

> [!ANSWER]
> Only the **green patches turn blue-black**; the non-green patches don't.
>
> Starch is made only where there is **chlorophyll** → **chlorophyll is essential for photosynthesis**.
>
> The non-green patches lack enough chlorophyll to produce detectable starch.

---

The same kind of leaf is taken from a plant kept in the **dark for 36 hours**. What does the iodine test show, and why?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No blue-black anywhere** — not even on the green patches.
>
> Chlorophyll was present, but **sunlight was missing** — so no starch could be made. This proves **sunlight is essential**.

---

Half a destarched leaf is sealed in a bottle containing **caustic soda**, the other half left outside, and the set-up is placed in sunlight. Explain the result.

> [!ANSWER]
> The half **outside** turns blue-black; the half **inside** does not.
>
> Caustic soda **absorbs the carbon dioxide** in the bottle, so the enclosed half had sunlight, water and chlorophyll but **no CO₂** — and made no starch.
>
> → **Carbon dioxide is essential for photosynthesis.**

---

Why is the plant **destarched** (kept in the dark 2–3 days) before that experiment?

> [!ANSWER]
> To remove any **previously stored starch**.
>
> Otherwise old starch would turn the leaf blue-black regardless of what happened during the experiment — a false positive that would ruin the test.

---

A water plant under a funnel in sunlight produces gas that collects in an inverted test tube. A lit matchstick is inserted and flares up. What is the gas, and what does the experiment prove?

> [!ANSWER]
> The intense flame shows the gas is rich in **oxygen**.
>
> → **Oxygen is released during photosynthesis** — and since the identical set-up in the dark produces no bubbles, it also confirms photosynthesis needs **sunlight**.

---

<!-- ===== The equation ===== -->

Write the word equation for **photosynthesis**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **carbon dioxide + water → (in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll) → glucose + oxygen**

---

In what form is food first made, and what happens to it afterwards?

> [!ANSWER]
> First as **glucose**, a simple carbohydrate — an **instant source of energy**.
>
> It is later converted into **starch** for **storage**, which is why the iodine test finds starch in leaves.

---

Why are leaves called the **"food factories"** of a plant? Do other parts photosynthesise?

> [!ANSWER]
> Leaves are the **primary site of photosynthesis** — generally broad and flat to catch the most sunlight, and green with **chlorophyll**, the pigment that captures it.
>
> **Yes** — any other **green** part of the plant has chlorophyll and photosynthesises too.

---

Some leaves are red or violet. Can they still photosynthesise?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Yes.** They contain more of other coloured pigments, which **hide** the green chlorophyll — but the chlorophyll is still there (and some of those pigments help too).
>
> The **iodine test** proves it: those leaves still make starch.

---

<!-- ===== Stomata ===== -->

What are **stomata**, where are they, and what do they do?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Tiny pores on the surface of leaves** (mostly the lower surface), seen by peeling a thin layer and viewing it under a microscope.
>
> They allow the **exchange of gases** — carbon dioxide in and oxygen out during photosynthesis, and the reverse during respiration.

---

<!-- ===== Transport ===== -->

Name the two transport tissues and say what each carries, and in which direction.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Xylem** — carries **water and minerals** **upward from the roots** to the leaves and all parts.
>
> **Phloem** — carries **food made in the leaves** to all parts of the plant, including seeds and roots for storage.
>
> Memory hook: **X**ylem = water up · **Ph**loem = **ph**ood from the leaves.

---

A twig with white flowers is left in **red-inked water** overnight. What is seen, and what does it prove?

> [!ANSWER]
> Red colour appears in the **stem, leaves and flowers**. Cutting the stem above the water line shows red **inside** it.
>
> The ink travelled **up the xylem** — proving that water (and the minerals dissolved in it) is transported from the roots upward through the xylem.
>
> The white flowers are chosen deliberately: the colour change is obvious against white.

---

Where do the water and minerals that a plant transports come from?

> [!ANSWER]
> From the **soil**, taken up by the **roots**. Minerals are important nutrients for growth, and the water is used in **photosynthesis** among other functions.

---

<!-- ===== Respiration ===== -->

Write the word equation for **respiration** in plants.

> [!ANSWER]
> **glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy**
>
> The energy released is used for the plant's **growth and development**.

---

Soaked moong seeds are sealed in a flask in the **dark** for 24 hours; the air from it is passed into lime water. What happens, and why is the dark important?

> [!ANSWER]
> The **lime water turns milky** → the flask air is rich in **carbon dioxide**, produced by the seeds as they **respire**.
>
> **Why the dark:** it prevents **photosynthesis**. If light were allowed, the seeds might use up the CO₂ they produced, hiding the result. In the dark, only respiration is happening — so the test measures exactly one process.

---

Do all parts of a plant respire, or only the green ones?

> [!ANSWER]
> **All parts — green or non-green — respire**, all the time.
>
> (Photosynthesis is the restricted one: it needs chlorophyll, so only green parts, and only in light.)

---

Give three differences between **photosynthesis** and **respiration** in plants.

> [!ANSWER]
> **1. Gases:** photosynthesis **takes in CO₂ and releases oxygen**; respiration **takes in oxygen and releases CO₂**.
>
> **2. Food & energy:** photosynthesis **makes** glucose (storing energy); respiration **breaks down** glucose to **release** energy.
>
> **3. When and where:** photosynthesis needs **sunlight and chlorophyll**, so only in **green parts, in light**; respiration happens in **every part, all the time**.
