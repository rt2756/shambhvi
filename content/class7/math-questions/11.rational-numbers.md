<!-- ===== What counts as rational? ===== -->

Is **7** a rational number? Is **0**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Both are.** Rational = expressible as `p/q` with integers p, q and q ≠ 0.
>
> `7 = 7/1` ✓ and `0 = 0/1` ✓ (q = 1 is fine — the rule only forbids the **denominator** being 0).
>
> That's why every natural number, whole number, integer and fraction is rational — the biggest number family so far.

---

Positive or negative: **−2/3**, **2/−3**, **−2/−3**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Read the signs of numerator and denominator:
>
> `−2/3` → opposite signs → **negative** · `2/−3` → opposite signs → **negative** (it equals −2/3) · `−2/−3` → same signs → **positive** (it equals 2/3).
>
> Same-sign = positive, opposite-sign = negative — the integer division rule, because a fraction *is* a division.

---

Is **0** a positive or a negative rational number?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Neither.** 0 sits exactly on the boundary between positives and negatives on the number line — it belongs to neither side. It is rational, though: `0/1`.

---

Why is **5/0** not a rational number?

> [!ANSWER]
> The definition demands **q ≠ 0**, because dividing by 0 has no answer: `5 ÷ 0` asks "what times 0 gives 5?" — but *anything* times 0 is 0, never 5. No answer exists, so the expression names no number.

---

<!-- ===== Standard form ===== -->

Write **−12/18** in standard form.

> [!ANSWER]
> Standard form = positive denominator + no common factor except 1.
>
> HCF of 12 and 18 is 6 → divide both: `−12 ÷ 6 = −2`, `18 ÷ 6 = 3` → **−2/3**.

---

Write **3/−5** in standard form.

> [!ANSWER]
> The denominator must be **positive** — multiply top and bottom by −1:
>
> `3/−5 = −3/5` → **−3/5**.
>
> **Why allowed:** ×(−1)/(−1) is multiplying by 1 — the value doesn't change, only the look.

---

Write **−44/72** in standard form.

> [!ANSWER]
> HCF of 44 and 72 is 4: `−44 ÷ 4 = −11`, `72 ÷ 4 = 18` → **−11/18**.
>
> Check nothing more cancels: `11 = 11`, `18 = 2 × 3²` — no shared prime ✓.

---

Are **−4/6** and **2/−3** the same number?

> [!ANSWER]
> Put both in standard form: `−4/6 = −2/3` (÷2) and `2/−3 = −2/3` (×−1 top and bottom).
>
> **Yes — both are −2/3.** Same number, many looks; standard form is the agreed "official look" that makes matching instant.

---

<!-- ===== Number line & comparing ===== -->

Where does **−1/2** sit on the number line?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Exactly midway between 0 and −1**, on the left (negative) side of 0.
>
> The negative sign mirrors 1/2 to the left of zero — same distance from 0, opposite direction.

---

Compare **−3/4 and −1/4**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Same positive denominator already → compare numerators: `−3 < −1` → **−3/4 < −1/4**.
>
> ⚠️ On the negative side, the bigger-looking digits are **smaller** — −3/4 sits further left (more below zero) than −1/4.

---

Compare **2/3 and 3/5**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Same denominator first — LCM of 3 and 5 is 15:
>
> `2/3 = 10/15` · `3/5 = 9/15` → `10 > 9` → **2/3 > 3/5**.
>
> Only same-sized pieces compare fairly — that's the whole point of the common denominator.

---

Compare **−2/3 and −3/5**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Over 15: `−2/3 = −10/15` · `−3/5 = −9/15`.
>
> `−10 < −9` → **−2/3 < −3/5** — the one further below zero is smaller, even though "2/3" looks smaller than "3/5" without the signs.

---

Arrange in **ascending order**: **−1/2, 1/4, −3/4, 0**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Over denominator 4: `−2/4, 1/4, −3/4, 0/4` → numerators: −2, 1, −3, 0.
>
> Ascending: `−3 < −2 < 0 < 1` → **−3/4 < −1/2 < 0 < 1/4** — exactly their left-to-right order on the number line.

---

Find a rational number **between 1/4 and 1/2**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Take their **average** — it always lands midway between them:
>
> `(1/4 + 1/2) ÷ 2 = (1/4 + 2/4) ÷ 2 = (3/4) ÷ 2 = ` **3/8**.
>
> Check: `1/4 = 2/8 < 3/8 < 4/8 = 1/2` ✓
>
> And the trick repeats forever (between 1/4 and 3/8 lies 5/16, …) — that's why there are **infinitely many** rationals between any two.

---

Write **−4/6** as an equal rational number with denominator **15**... is that possible? If not, use **−2/3** with denominator 15.

> [!ANSWER]
> `−4/6` in standard form is `−2/3`. To reach denominator 15: `3 × 5 = 15`, so multiply top and bottom by 5:
>
> `−2/3 = −10/15`.
>
> (Directly from −4/6 it's impossible — 6 doesn't divide 15 evenly; simplifying first opened the way.)

---

<!-- ===== Operations ===== -->

Add: **−2/3 + 1/6**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Same denominator first — LCM of 3 and 6 is 6:
>
> `−2/3 = −4/6` → `−4/6 + 1/6 = −3/6 = ` **−1/2** (simplified).
>
> The numerator sum `−4 + 1` follows the integer mix rule: `1 − 4 = −3`.

---

Add: **−3/8 + 5/12**.

> [!ANSWER]
> LCM of 8 and 12: `8 = 2³`, `12 = 2² × 3` → larger powers `2³ × 3 = 24`.
>
> `−3/8 = −9/24` · `5/12 = 10/24` → `−9 + 10 = 1` → **1/24**.

---

Subtract: **1/4 − 2/3**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Over 12: `1/4 = 3/12` · `2/3 = 8/12`.
>
> `3/12 − 8/12 = (3 − 8)/12 = ` **−5/12** — smaller minus larger goes negative, exactly as with integers.

---

Multiply: **−2/3 × 4/5**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Sign first:** one minus (odd) → negative.
>
> Tops together, bottoms together: `2 × 4 = 8`, `3 × 5 = 15` → **−8/15**.

---

Multiply: **−3/7 × (−14/9)**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Sign:** two minuses (even) → **positive**.
>
> Cancel before multiplying: `3` into `9` → 3; `7` into `14` → 2:
>
> `(3 × 14)/(7 × 9) = (1 × 2)/(1 × 3) = ` **2/3**.
>
> Cancelling first keeps numbers small — same answer as `42/63` simplified, with less work.

---

What is the **reciprocal** of **−5/8**, and why?

> [!ANSWER]
> **−8/5** — flip numerator and denominator, keep the sign.
>
> **Why keep the minus:** a reciprocal must multiply the original to give **1**: `−5/8 × −8/5 = +40/40 = 1` ✓. Flipping the sign too would give −1 instead.

---

Divide: **−2/3 ÷ 4/9**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Dividing = multiplying by the **reciprocal**:
>
> `−2/3 × 9/4` → sign: one minus → negative. Cancel: 3 into 9 → 3, 2 into 4 → 2:
>
> `= −(2 × 9)/(3 × 4) = −3/2` → **−3/2**.
>
> **Why flip:** "÷ 4/9" asks how many 4/9-sized pieces fit — and ×9/4 counts exactly that.

---

Divide: **5/6 ÷ (−10/3)**.

> [!ANSWER]
> `5/6 × 3/(−10)` → sign: one minus → negative.
>
> Cancel: 5 into 10 → 2, 3 into 6 → 2: `= −(5 × 3)/(6 × 10) = −(1 × 1)/(2 × 2) = ` **−1/4**.

---

Simplify: **(−1/2) + (3/4) − (−1/4)**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Minus of a minus becomes plus: `… − (−1/4) = … + 1/4`.
>
> Over 4: `−2/4 + 3/4 + 1/4 = (−2 + 3 + 1)/4 = 2/4 = ` **1/2**.
