<!-- ===================== FRACTIONS ===================== -->

<!-- ===== Types & converting (improper ↔ mixed) ===== -->

Is **9/5** a proper or an improper fraction? Convert it to a **mixed number**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Numerator (9) > denominator (5), so it's **improper** — more than one whole.
>
> **Improper → mixed:** `9 ÷ 5 = 1 remainder 4` → write `Q R/D` → **1 4/5**.

---

Convert the mixed number **3 2/7** to an **improper fraction**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Mixed → improper:** `(whole × denominator + numerator) / denominator`.
>
> `(3 × 7 + 2) / 7 = (21 + 2)/7 = ` **23/7**.

---

Write **two fractions equivalent to 2/3**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Multiply top and bottom by the **same** number — the value doesn't change.
>
> `×2 → 4/6` · `×3 → 6/9`. So **4/6** and **6/9** (also 8/12, …).

---

Reduce **18/24** to its **simplest form**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Top and bottom share the factor **6** (`18 = 6 × 3`, `24 = 6 × 4`).
>
> Divide both by 6 → **3/4**. Nothing else divides both, so that's simplest.

---

<!-- ===== Comparing & ordering ===== -->

Which is greater, **3/5** or **4/7**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Unlike** fractions — make the denominators match first. LCM of 5 and 7 is **35**.
>
> `3/5 = 21/35` · `4/7 = 20/35`. Same slice size now, so compare numerators: `21 > 20`.
>
> So **3/5 > 4/7**.

---

Arrange in **ascending order**: **1/2, 2/3, 5/6**.

> [!ANSWER]
> LCM of 2, 3, 6 is **6**. Rewrite each over 6:
>
> `1/2 = 3/6` · `2/3 = 4/6` · `5/6 = 5/6`.
>
> Compare numerators `3 < 4 < 5` → **1/2 < 2/3 < 5/6**.

---

<!-- ===== Adding & subtracting fractions ===== -->

Add **3/8 + 2/8**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Like** fractions (same denominator) — add the numerators, keep the denominator.
>
> `(3 + 2)/8 = ` **5/8**.

---

Add **1/2 + 1/3**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Unlike** — match denominators first. LCM of 2 and 3 is **6**.
>
> `1/2 = 3/6` · `1/3 = 2/6` → `3/6 + 2/6 = ` **5/6**.

---

Subtract **5/6 − 1/4**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Unlike** — LCM of 6 and 4 is **12**.
>
> `5/6 = 10/12` · `1/4 = 3/12` → `10/12 − 3/12 = ` **7/12**.

---

Add the mixed numbers **2 1/4 + 1 1/2**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Make them improper: `2 1/4 = 9/4`, `1 1/2 = 3/2`.
>
> Match denominators (LCM 4): `3/2 = 6/4` → `9/4 + 6/4 = 15/4`.
>
> Back to mixed: `15 ÷ 4 = 3 r3` → **3 3/4**.

---

Subtract **4 1/3 − 1 5/6**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Improper first: `4 1/3 = 13/3`, `1 5/6 = 11/6`.
>
> LCM of 3 and 6 is 6: `13/3 = 26/6` → `26/6 − 11/6 = 15/6`.
>
> Simplify and convert: `15/6 = 5/2 = ` **2 1/2**.

---

A jug holds **3/4 litre** of juice. **1/3 litre** is poured out. How much is **left**?

> [!ANSWER]
> "Poured out" is subtraction: `3/4 − 1/3`. LCM of 4 and 3 is **12**.
>
> `3/4 = 9/12` · `1/3 = 4/12` → `9/12 − 4/12 = ` **5/12 litre** left.

---

<!-- ===== Multiplying fractions (incl. "of" & mixed) ===== -->

Multiply **2/3 × 4/5**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Top × top, bottom × bottom:** `(2 × 4)/(3 × 5) = ` **8/15** (already simplest).

---

Find **3/4 of 20**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **"of" means ×:** `3/4 × 20 = (3 × 20)/4 = 60/4 = ` **15**.

---

Multiply **5 × 2/7**.

> [!ANSWER]
> A whole number is `5/1`: `5/1 × 2/7 = 10/7`.
>
> Convert: `10 ÷ 7 = 1 r3` → **1 3/7**.

---

Multiply the mixed numbers **2 1/2 × 1 1/5**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Make mixed numbers improper first:** `2 1/2 = 5/2`, `1 1/5 = 6/5`.
>
> `5/2 × 6/5 = 30/10 = ` **3**.
>
> (Shortcut: cancel the 5s first → `1/2 × 6/1 = 3`.)

---

A rectangle is **3/4 m** long and **2/3 m** wide. What is its **area**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Area = length × width = `3/4 × 2/3 = 6/12`.
>
> Simplify → **1/2 m²**.

---

<!-- ===== Reciprocals & division of fractions ===== -->

What is the **reciprocal** of **3/7**? And of **5**?

> [!ANSWER]
> The reciprocal is the fraction **flipped** — it multiplies with the original to give 1.
>
> Reciprocal of `3/7` = **7/3**. Since `5 = 5/1`, its reciprocal = **1/5**.

---

Divide **2/3 ÷ 4/5**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Keep-Change-Flip:** keep `2/3`, change ÷ to ×, flip the **second** fraction.
>
> `2/3 × 5/4 = 10/12 = ` **5/6**.

---

Divide **3/4 ÷ 2**.

> [!ANSWER]
> `2 = 2/1`. Keep-Change-Flip: `3/4 × 1/2 = ` **3/8**.
>
> (Dividing by 2 halves it — and 3/8 is half of 3/4. ✓)

---

Divide **6 ÷ 3/4**.

> [!ANSWER]
> `6 = 6/1`. Keep-Change-Flip: `6/1 × 4/3 = 24/3 = ` **8**.
>
> **Why bigger than 6?** Dividing by a fraction **less than 1** asks "how many 3/4s fit in 6?" — small pieces fit many times.

---

Divide the mixed numbers **2 1/4 ÷ 1 1/2**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Improper first: `2 1/4 = 9/4`, `1 1/2 = 3/2`.
>
> Keep-Change-Flip: `9/4 × 2/3 = 18/12 = 3/2 = ` **1 1/2**.

---

A **6 m** ribbon is cut into pieces each **3/4 m** long. How many pieces?

> [!ANSWER]
> "How many 3/4 m pieces fit in 6 m?" is division: `6 ÷ 3/4`.
>
> Keep-Change-Flip: `6/1 × 4/3 = 24/3 = ` **8 pieces**.

---

<!-- ===== More comparing & word problems (fractions) ===== -->

Compare **3/8** and **5/12** using **cross-multiplication**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Cross-multiply** — multiply each numerator by the *other* denominator:
>
> `3 × 12 = 36` (for 3/8) · `5 × 8 = 40` (for 5/12). Since `36 < 40`, **3/8 < 5/12**.
>
> **Why it works:** it's the same as putting both over the common denominator 96 (`3/8 = 36/96`, `5/12 = 40/96`) — the cross-products *are* those new numerators.

---

Arrange in **descending order**: **2/3, 3/4, 5/6, 7/12**.

> [!ANSWER]
> LCM of 3, 4, 6, 12 is **12**: `2/3 = 8/12`, `3/4 = 9/12`, `5/6 = 10/12`, `7/12 = 7/12`.
>
> Largest first: `10/12 > 9/12 > 8/12 > 7/12` → **5/6 > 3/4 > 2/3 > 7/12**.

---

By **how much is 5/6 greater than 3/8**?

> [!ANSWER]
> "How much greater" is subtraction: `5/6 − 3/8`. LCM of 6 and 8 is **24**.
>
> `5/6 = 20/24` · `3/8 = 9/24` → `20/24 − 9/24 = ` **11/24**.

---

<!-- ===== Fraction of a quantity ===== -->

**2/5 of 60 students** play football. How many students is that?

> [!ANSWER]
> **"of" means ×:** `2/5 × 60 = (2 × 60)/5 = 120/5 = ` **24 students**.

---

How many **minutes** are in **3/4 of an hour**?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1 hour = 60 minutes. `3/4 of 60 = 3/4 × 60 = 180/4 = ` **45 minutes**.

Subtract **1 − 3/8**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Write 1 as `8/8` (same denominator): `8/8 − 3/8 = ` **5/8**.

---

Subtract **3 − 1 1/4**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Make them improper: `3 = 12/4`, `1 1/4 = 5/4`.
>
> `12/4 − 5/4 = 7/4 = ` **1 3/4**.

---

Add the three fractions **1/2 + 1/3 + 1/4**.

> [!ANSWER]
> LCM of 2, 3, 4 is **12**: `1/2 = 6/12`, `1/3 = 4/12`, `1/4 = 3/12`.
>
> `6/12 + 4/12 + 3/12 = 13/12 = ` **1 1/12**.

---

Multiply **2/3 × 6/7 × 7/8**, cancelling as you go.

> [!ANSWER]
> The **7s** cancel (top and bottom), leaving `(2 × 6)/(3 × 8) = 12/24`.
>

<!-- ===== Multi-step fraction word problems ===== -->

Shyam spends **1/3** of his pocket money on books and **1/4** on food. What **fraction is left**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Spent = `1/3 + 1/4`. LCM 12: `4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12`.
>
> Left = `1 − 7/12 = 12/12 − 7/12 = ` **5/12**.

---

A water tank is **3/4 full**. **Half of the water** in it is used. What **fraction of the tank** is still filled?

> [!ANSWER]
> Water used = `1/2 of 3/4 = 1/2 × 3/4 = 3/8` of the tank.
>
> Still filled = `3/4 − 3/8 = 6/8 − 3/8 = ` **3/8** of the tank.

---

<!-- ===== Word problems: multiplying mixed numbers ===== -->

Lipika reads a book for **1 3/4 hours** every day, and finishes the whole book in **6 days**. How many hours in all did she need?

> [!ANSWER]
> The same 1 3/4 hours, **6 times over** — repeated addition, so **multiply**.
>
> Mixed → improper: `1 3/4 = (1×4 + 3)/4 = 7/4`.
>
> `7/4 × 6 = 42/4 = 21/2 = ` **10 1/2 hours**.

---

Find the **area of a rectangular park** which is **41 2/3 m** long and **18 3/5 m** broad.

> [!ANSWER]
> Area of a rectangle = **length × breadth**.
>
> `41 2/3 = 125/3` · `18 3/5 = 93/5` → `125/3 × 93/5`.
>
> Cancel before multiplying: `125` and `5` share 5 → `25`; `93` and `3` share 3 → `31`.
>
> `25 × 31 = ` **775 m²**.
>
> **Why cancel first:** smaller numbers, same answer — cancelling only removes a factor that was going to be divided out at the end anyway.

---

Milk is available at **₹17 3/4 per litre**. Find the **cost of 7 2/5 litres**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Cost = rate **per** litre × number of litres.
>
> `17 3/4 = 71/4` · `7 2/5 = 37/5` → `71/4 × 37/5 = 2627/20`.
>
> `2627 ÷ 20 = 131` remainder `7` → **₹131 7/20 = ₹131.35**.

---

Sharada can walk **8 1/3 km in one hour**. How much distance will she cover in **2 2/5 hours**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Distance = speed × time.
>
> `8 1/3 = 25/3` · `2 2/5 = 12/5` → `25/3 × 12/5`.
>
> Cancel: `25` with `5` → `5`; `12` with `3` → `4`. So `5 × 4 = ` **20 km**.

---

A sugar bag contains **30 kg** of sugar. After **2/3 of it** is used, how much sugar is **left** in the bag?

> [!ANSWER]
> "of" means **×**, so used = `2/3 × 30 = 60/3 = 20 kg`. Left = `30 − 20 = ` **10 kg**.
>
> **Faster way:** if 2/3 is gone, the part left is `1 − 2/3 = 1/3` of the bag → `1/3 × 30 = ` **10 kg**. Same answer, one step.

---

Each side of a square is **6 2/3 m** long. Find its **area**.

> [!ANSWER]
> A square's area = **side × side** (both sides are equal).
>
> `6 2/3 = 20/3` → `20/3 × 20/3 = 400/9`.
>
> `400 ÷ 9 = 44` remainder `4` → **44 4/9 m²**.

---

There are **45 students** in a class and **3/5 of them are boys**. How many **girls** are there in the class?

> [!ANSWER]
> Boys = `3/5 of 45 = 3/5 × 45 = 135/5 = 27`. Girls = `45 − 27 = ` **18**.
>
> **Faster way:** the whole class is `5/5`. If boys are `3/5`, the girls must be the rest — `5/5 − 3/5 = 2/5`.
>
> `2/5 × 45 = 90/5 = ` **18 girls**.
>
> ⚠️ The question asks for **girls**, not boys. Finding 27 and stopping is the trap — always re-read what was asked before writing the answer.

<!-- ===== Word problems: dividing mixed numbers ===== -->

A wire of length **12 1/2 m** is cut into **10 pieces of equal length**. Find the length of **each piece**.

> [!ANSWER]
> One length shared into equal parts → **divide**.
>
> `12 1/2 = 25/2` → `25/2 ÷ 10 = 25/2 × 1/10` (Keep-Change-Flip).
>
> `= 25/20 = 5/4 = ` **1 1/4 m**.

---

The **area** of a rectangular plot is **65 1/3 m²** and its **length** is **12 1/4 m**. What is its **width**?

> [!ANSWER]
> `area = length × width`, so to undo the multiplication, **width = area ÷ length**.
>
> `65 1/3 = 196/3` · `12 1/4 = 49/4` → `196/3 ÷ 49/4 = 196/3 × 4/49`.
>
> `196 ÷ 49 = 4`, so `= (4 × 4)/3 = 16/3 = ` **5 1/3 m**.

---

**By what number should 6 2/9 be multiplied to get 4 4/9?**

> [!ANSWER]
> A **missing factor** is found by dividing: `answer ÷ the factor you know`.
>
> `6 2/9 = 56/9` · `4 4/9 = 40/9` → `40/9 ÷ 56/9 = 40/9 × 9/56`.
>
> The 9s cancel → `40/56 = ` **5/7**.
>
> Check: `56/9 × 5/7 = 280/63 = 40/9` ✓

---

The **product of two numbers is 25 5/6**. If one of the numbers is **6 2/3**, find the **other**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Same idea — the other number = `product ÷ known number`.
>
> `25 5/6 = 155/6` · `6 2/3 = 20/3` → `155/6 ÷ 20/3 = 155/6 × 3/20`.
>
> Cancel `3` with `6` → `155/2 × 1/20 = 155/40 = 31/8 = ` **3 7/8**.

---

The cost of **6 1/4 kg of apples** is **₹400**. At what **rate per kg** are the apples being sold?

> [!ANSWER]
> "Per kg" means the cost of **one** kg → divide the total by the number of kg.
>
> `6 1/4 = 25/4` → `400 ÷ 25/4 = 400 × 4/25`.
>
> `400 ÷ 25 = 16`, so `16 × 4 = ` **₹64 per kg**.

---

By selling oranges at **₹5 1/4 per orange**, a fruit-seller gets **₹630**. How many **dozens** of oranges does he sell?

> [!ANSWER]
> First find **how many oranges**: `total money ÷ price of one`.
>
> `5 1/4 = 21/4` → `630 ÷ 21/4 = 630 × 4/21`. `630 ÷ 21 = 30`, so `30 × 4 = 120` oranges.
>
> The question asks for **dozens**, and 1 dozen = 12 → `120 ÷ 12 = ` **10 dozens**.

---

In the mid-day meal scheme, **3/10 litre** of milk is given to each student. If **30 litres** are distributed every day, **how many students** are there in the school?

> [!ANSWER]
> "How many 3/10-litre shares fit into 30 litres?" → **divide**.
>
> `30 ÷ 3/10 = 30 × 10/3 = 300/3 = ` **100 students**.
>
> **Why the answer got bigger:** dividing by a number **less than 1** always gives more than you started with — each share is smaller than a litre, so 30 litres stretches to many shares.

---

In a charity show **₹6496** was collected by selling tickets. If each ticket was **₹50 3/4**, how many **tickets** were sold?

> [!ANSWER]
> `number of tickets = total collected ÷ price of one ticket`.
>
> `50 3/4 = (50×4 + 3)/4 = 203/4` → `6496 ÷ 203/4 = 6496 × 4/203`.
>
> Divide first, it's easier: `6496 ÷ 203 = 32` → `32 × 4 = ` **128 tickets**.
>
> Check: `203/4 × 128 = 203 × 32 = 6496` ✓

<!-- ===== Place value & expanded form ===== -->

In the number **4.73**, what is the **place value of the 7**?

> [!ANSWER]
> The first place to the **right** of the decimal point is **tenths**.
>
> So the 7 means **7 tenths = 7/10**.
>
> **Why:** every step right of the point divides by 10 — ones → tenths → hundredths → …

---

Write **6.259** in **expanded form**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Read the places right of the point: 2 = tenths, 5 = hundredths, 9 = thousandths.
>
> `6.259 = 6 + 2/10 + 5/100 + 9/1000`

---

Write **4 + 7/10 + 3/100** as a single decimal.

> [!ANSWER]
> 4 ones, 7 tenths, 3 hundredths → fill each place in order:
>
> **4.73**

---

What is the **place value of the 8** in **12.048**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Count the places right of the point: 0 = tenths, 4 = hundredths, **8 = thousandths**.
>
> So the 8 means **8 thousandths = 8/1000**.

---

<!-- ===== Comparing decimals ===== -->

Which is greater, **0.5** or **0.45**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Don't count digits.** Line up the points and add a helper zero: write `0.5` as `0.50`.
>
> Now compare **50 hundredths** with **45 hundredths** → `0.50 > 0.45`, so **0.5 is greater**.
>
> **Why the zero is safe:** a zero at the *end* of a decimal doesn't change its value (`0.5 = 0.50`).

---

Which is greater, **0.7** or **0.68**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Helper zero: write `0.7` as `0.70`.
>
> `70` hundredths vs `68` hundredths → `0.70 > 0.68`, so **0.7 is greater**.

---

Put the correct sign (`<` or `>`): **2.5 ___ 2.45**

> [!ANSWER]
> Whole parts are equal (both 2), so compare the decimal parts. Write `2.5` as `2.50`.
>
> `50 > 45` → **2.5 > 2.45**

---

Arrange in **ascending order**: **0.3, 0.03, 0.33, 0.303**

> [!ANSWER]
> Give them all the **same number of decimal places** with helper zeros (thousandths):
>
> `0.300, 0.030, 0.330, 0.303`
>
> Compare as whole numbers of thousandths: `030 < 300 < 303 < 330`.
>
> Ascending → **0.03, 0.3, 0.303, 0.33**

**6 friends** share **₹27.60** equally. How much does each get?

<!-- ===== ×/÷ by a number less than 1 ===== -->

Without full working, is **8 × 0.5** **bigger or smaller** than 8? Then find it.

> [!ANSWER]
> `0.5` is **less than 1**, so multiplying by it makes the answer **smaller**.
>
> `8 × 0.5 = ` **4** — and 4 is indeed smaller than 8. ✓
>
> **Why:** `× 0.5` means "take half of," which shrinks the number.

---

Is **8 ÷ 0.5** **bigger or smaller** than 8? Then find it.

> [!ANSWER]
> Dividing by a number **less than 1** makes the answer **bigger**.
>
> `8 ÷ 0.5 = 80 ÷ 5 = ` **16** — bigger than 8. ✓
>
> **Why:** "how many halves fit in 8?" — twice as many as wholes, so the answer grows.

<!-- ===== Applied decimal word problems ===== -->

A rectangular tile is **2.5 m** long and **1.4 m** wide. Find its **area**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Area = length × width = `2.5 × 1.4`.
>
> Ignore the points: `25 × 14 = 350`. Decimal digits: `1 + 1 = 2` → **3.50 m²**.

---

**What must be added to 3.65 to make 5?**

> [!ANSWER]
> "What to add to reach 5" is subtraction: `5 − 3.65`. Helper zeros → `5.00 − 3.65`.
>
> ```text
>   5.00
> - 3.65
> ------
>   1.35
> ```
>
> Add **1.35**. (Check: `3.65 + 1.35 = 5.00` ✓)

---

A car travels **15.5 km on 1 litre** of petrol. How far on **4 litres**?

> [!ANSWER]
> `15.5 × 4`. Ignore the point: `155 × 4 = 620`. One decimal digit → **62.0 km**.

---

**2.5 kg** of sugar costs **₹90**. What is the cost of **1 kg**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Cost per kg = `90 ÷ 2.5`. Dividing by a **decimal** — slide both points right by 1:
>
> `90 ÷ 2.5 = 900 ÷ 25 = ` **₹36**.

Arrange in **descending order**: **3.03, 3.3, 3.303, 3.033**.

<!-- ===== More multiplying & dividing decimals ===== -->

Find **0.25 × 0.4**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Ignore the points: `25 × 4 = 100`. Decimal digits: `2 + 1 = 3` → `0.100`.
>
> Trailing zeros at the end can be dropped → **0.1**.

---

Find **12.6 ÷ 0.6**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Dividing by a decimal — slide **both** points right by 1: `12.6 ÷ 0.6 = 126 ÷ 6 = ` **21**.

---

Find **5 ÷ 0.25**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Slide both points right by 2 (so 0.25 becomes 25): `5 ÷ 0.25 = 500 ÷ 25 = ` **20**.
>
> Bigger than 5 — dividing by a number less than 1 grows it. ✓

<!-- ===== Multi-step decimal word problems ===== -->

Rice costs **₹40.50 per kg**. What do **2.5 kg** cost?

> [!ANSWER]
> `2.5 × 40.50`. Ignore points: `25 × 4050 = 101250`. Decimal digits `1 + 2 = 3` → **₹101.25**.

Ravi has **₹100**. He spends **₹37.50** and **₹28.25**. How much is **left**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Spent = `37.50 + 28.25 = 65.75`. Left = `100.00 − 65.75`:
>
> ```text
>   100.00
> −  65.75
> --------
>    34.25
> ```
>
> **₹34.25** left.

<!-- ===== Word problems: multiplying decimals ===== -->

Find the **area of a rectangle** whose length is **5.5 m** and breadth is **3.4 m**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Area = `5.5 × 3.4`. Ignore the points: `55 × 34 = 1870`.
>
> Decimal digits: `1 + 1 = 2` → count 2 places from the right → **18.70 m²** (= 18.7 m²).

---

If the cost of a book is **₹25.57**, find the cost of **24** such books.

> [!ANSWER]
> `25.57 × 24`. Ignore the point: `2557 × 24 = 61368`
> (`2557 × 20 = 51140`, `2557 × 4 = 10228`, add → `61368`).
>
> Decimal digits: `2 + 0 = 2` → **₹613.68**.

---

A car covers **14.75 km** on one litre of petrol. How much distance will it cover on **15.5 litres**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Distance = km per litre × litres = `14.75 × 15.5`.
>
> Ignore the points: `1475 × 155 = 228625`
> (`1475 × 100 = 147500`, `1475 × 55 = 81125`, add → `228625`).
>
> Decimal digits: `2 + 1 = 3` → **228.625 km**.

---

One kg of rice costs **₹42.65**. What will be the cost of **18.25 kg** of rice?

> [!ANSWER]
> `42.65 × 18.25`. Ignore the points: `4265 × 1825 = 7783625`
> (`4265 × 1800 = 7677000`, `4265 × 25 = 106625`, add → `7783625`).
>
> Decimal digits: `2 + 2 = 4` → **₹778.3625**, which is **₹778.36** to the nearest paisa.
>
> **Sanity check:** 18.25 kg is a bit under 20 kg, and `42.65 × 20 = 853` — so an answer near ₹780 is the right size.

One metre of cloth costs **₹152.50**. What is the cost of **10.75 metres** of cloth?

> [!ANSWER]
> `152.50 × 10.75`. Ignore the points: `15250 × 1075 = 16393750`
> (`15250 × 1000 = 15250000`, `15250 × 75 = 1143750`, add → `16393750`).
>
> Decimal digits: `2 + 2 = 4` → **₹1639.3750 = ₹1639.375**, i.e. **₹1639.38** to the nearest paisa.
>
> **Sanity check:** a bit more than 10 m at about ₹152 per m ≈ ₹1600. ✓
