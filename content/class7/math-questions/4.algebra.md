<!-- ===== Expressions — the vocabulary ===== -->

Write down the **terms** of the expression **5x² − 3x + 8**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Terms are the parts being **added**, and **the sign travels with its term**:
>
> **5x², −3x, 8** — three terms.
>
> Writing `−3x` (not `3x`) matters: the expression is really `5x² + (−3x) + 8`.

---

What is the **coefficient** of y in **−7y**? And of x² in **−x²**?

> [!ANSWER]
> The coefficient is the **number multiplying the variable**:
>
> In `−7y` it is **−7**. In `−x²` it is **−1** — because `−x²` means `−1 × x²`; the 1 is invisible but there.

---

Which of these are **like terms**: `3ab`, `−5ab`, `2a`, `4ab²`?

> [!ANSWER]
> Like terms need **exactly the same variable part**.
>
> **`3ab` and `−5ab`** are like (both have variable part `ab`).
>
> `2a` has just `a`, and `4ab²` has `ab²` — different variable parts → unlike. The powers must match too, not just the letters.

---

Classify by number of terms: **7x** · **x + 4** · **x² + 2x + 1**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Count the terms: `7x` → 1 term → **monomial** · `x + 4` → 2 terms → **binomial** · `x² + 2x + 1` → 3 terms → **trinomial**.
>
> (mono = 1, bi = 2, tri = 3 — same prefixes as bicycle and tricycle.)

---

In the expression **3n + 12**, which part is the **variable** and which is the **constant**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **n** is the variable — a letter standing for an unknown number that can change.
>
> **12** is the constant — fixed, always 12. (And 3 is the **coefficient** of n.)

---

<!-- ===== Working with expressions ===== -->

Simplify **5x + 3x**. Why can they be combined?

> [!ANSWER]
> **8x.** Add the coefficients (`5 + 3 = 8`), keep the variable part.
>
> **Why it works:** 5 x's plus 3 more x's is simply 8 x's — like 5 apples + 3 apples = 8 apples. That's also why unlike terms *can't* combine: apples + bananas is not 8 of anything.

---

Simplify **7xy − 2xy**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Like terms (same variable part `xy`) → subtract coefficients: `7 − 2 = 5` → **5xy**.

---

Simplify **2x + 3y**, if possible.

> [!ANSWER]
> **It stays `2x + 3y`.** The variable parts (`x` and `y`) are different, so these are **unlike terms** — 2 x's and 3 y's can't be counted as one kind of thing. Leaving it as is *is* the final answer.

---

Simplify **4x + 7 − 2x + 3**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Group the like terms: x-terms `4x − 2x = 2x`, plain numbers `7 + 3 = 10`.
>
> = **2x + 10**

---

Add: **(3a + 2b) + (4a − 5b)**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Adding a bracket changes nothing — drop the brackets and collect like terms:
>
> a-terms: `3a + 4a = 7a` · b-terms: `2b − 5b = −3b`.
>
> = **7a − 3b**

---

Subtract: **(5x + 4) − (2x − 3)**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Subtracting a bracket **flips every sign inside** — you are taking away the *whole* package:
>
> `5x + 4 − 2x + 3` (note `−(−3)` became `+3`).
>
> Collect: `5x − 2x = 3x`, `4 + 3 = 7` → **3x + 7**.
>
> **⚠️ The classic mistake** is flipping only the first sign (`5x + 4 − 2x − 3` ✗). Removing a parcel means removing everything in it — every term's sign flips.

---

Find the value of **x² + 2x + 1** when **x = −2**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Replace every x with (−2), keeping the brackets:
>
> `(−2)² + 2 × (−2) + 1 = 4 + (−4) + 1 = ` **1**
>
> Brackets matter: `(−2)² = +4` because minus × minus is plus.

---

Find the value of **3y² − 4y + 7** when **y = 2**.

> [!ANSWER]
> `3 × 2² − 4 × 2 + 7`
>
> Powers first (BODMAS): `2² = 4` → `3 × 4 − 8 + 7 = 12 − 8 + 7 = ` **11**

---

The sequence **2, 4, 6, 8, …** has nth term **2n**. What is its **50th term**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Put n = 50 into the formula: `2 × 50 = ` **100**.
>
> **Why the formula works:** each step adds 2, so after n steps you have n twos — `2n`. The formula lets you jump straight to term 50 without listing 49 terms first.

---

The odd numbers **3, 5, 7, 9, …** have nth term **2n + 1**. Which term equals **41**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Set the formula equal to 41 and solve: `2n + 1 = 41` → `2n = 40` → `n = 20`.
>
> **41 is the 20th term.** Check: `2 × 20 + 1 = 41` ✓

---

Write the formula for the **perimeter of a square** of side s, and use it for s = 12 cm.

> [!ANSWER]
> A square has 4 equal sides, so perimeter `P = 4s` — the rule written once, working for **every** square.
>
> At `s = 12`: `P = 4 × 12 = ` **48 cm**.

<!-- ===== Simple equations ===== -->

Solve **2x + 3 = 11** and check your answer.

> [!ANSWER]
> Undo in reverse order — clear the `+ 3` first, then the `× 2`:
>
> `2x = 11 − 3 = 8` (the +3 moved across and became −3)
>
> `x = 8 ÷ 2 = ` **4** (the ×2 moved across and became ÷2)
>
> **Check:** `2 × 4 + 3 = 11` ✓
>
> **Why transposing works:** it's the balance rule in disguise — subtracting 3 from *both* sides removes it from the left and plants `−3` on the right.

---

Solve **x − 7 = 10**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Move the `−7` across; it becomes `+7`:
>
> `x = 10 + 7 = ` **17**. Check: `17 − 7 = 10` ✓

---

Solve **x/4 = 5**.

> [!ANSWER]
> x is being **divided** by 4, so undo it by **multiplying** both sides by 4:
>
> `x = 5 × 4 = ` **20**. Check: `20 ÷ 4 = 5` ✓

---

Solve **3x − 5 = 16**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Clear the `− 5` first: `3x = 16 + 5 = 21`.
>
> Then the `× 3`: `x = 21 ÷ 3 = ` **7**. Check: `3 × 7 − 5 = 16` ✓

---

Solve **5(x − 2) = 15**.

> [!ANSWER]
> The whole bracket is multiplied by 5, so divide both sides by 5 first:
>
> `x − 2 = 3` → `x = 3 + 2 = ` **5**. Check: `5 × (5 − 2) = 5 × 3 = 15` ✓
>
> (Expanding first — `5x − 10 = 15` → `5x = 25` → `x = 5` — gives the same answer; dividing first just keeps the numbers smaller.)

---

Is **x = 4** a solution of **5x − 3 = 18**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Test it: `5 × 4 − 3 = 20 − 3 = 17`, but the RHS is 18.
>
> `17 ≠ 18` → **no**. A solution must make LHS **exactly equal** to RHS. (The real solution: `5x = 21` → `x = 21/5`.)

---

Write as an equation and solve: **"5 added to 3 times a number gives 26."**

> [!ANSWER]
> Let the number be x. "3 times a number" → `3x`; "5 added to" → `3x + 5`; "gives" → `=`:
>
> `3x + 5 = 26` → `3x = 21` → `x = ` **7**. Check: `3 × 7 + 5 = 26` ✓

---

Write as an equation and solve: **"When 9 is subtracted from twice a number, the result is 3."**

> [!ANSWER]
> Let the number be x: `2x − 9 = 3`.
>
> `2x = 3 + 9 = 12` → `x = ` **6**. Check: `2 × 6 − 9 = 3` ✓

---

A father's age is **4 years more than 3 times** his daughter's age. The father is **40**. How old is the daughter?

> [!ANSWER]
> Let the daughter's age be x. "3 times, then 4 more" → `3x + 4 = 40`.
>
> `3x = 36` → `x = ` **12 years**. Check: `3 × 12 + 4 = 40` ✓

<!-- ===== Ratio & proportion ===== -->

Simplify the ratio **15 : 20**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Divide both parts by their HCF, 5: `15 ÷ 5 = 3`, `20 ÷ 5 = 4` → **3 : 4**.
>
> **Why allowed:** a ratio is a division in disguise (`15/20`), and equivalent ratios work exactly like equivalent fractions — same value, smaller numbers.

---

Write the ratio of **500 g to 2 kg**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Same units first** — a ratio compares pure numbers: `2 kg = 2000 g`.
>
> `500 : 2000` → divide both by 500 → **1 : 4**.
>
> Skipping the conversion would give the nonsense 500 : 2 — comparing grams with kilograms.

---

Are **2 : 3** and **8 : 12** in proportion?

> [!ANSWER]
> Test: **product of extremes = product of means** — in `2 : 3 = 8 : 12` the extremes are 2 and 12, the means are 3 and 8.
>
> `2 × 12 = 24` and `3 × 8 = 24` — equal → **yes, in proportion**.
>
> **Why the test works:** `2/3 = 8/12` cross-multiplied gives exactly `2 × 12 = 3 × 8`; equal products = equal fractions.

---

Find x if **3 : 5 = x : 20**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Extremes × means: `3 × 20 = 5 × x` → `60 = 5x` → `x = ` **12**.
>
> Check: `12 : 20` divided by 4 is `3 : 5` ✓

---

**12 notebooks cost ₹144.** What do **7 notebooks** cost?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Unitary method — find the value of 1 first:**
>
> 1 notebook = `144 ÷ 12 = ₹12` → 7 notebooks = `7 × 12 = ` **₹84**.
>
> **Why via 1:** the price of one notebook is the fixed building block; any quantity is then just that block × how many.

---

Share **₹120** between two sisters in the ratio **2 : 3**.

> [!ANSWER]
> The ratio splits the money into `2 + 3 = 5` equal parts.
>
> One part = `120 ÷ 5 = ₹24` → shares are `2 × 24 = ` **₹48** and `3 × 24 = ` **₹72**.
>
> Check: `48 + 72 = 120` ✓ — the parts must rebuild the whole.

---

A recipe uses flour and sugar in the ratio **5 : 2**. If you use **250 g of sugar**, how much flour do you need?

> [!ANSWER]
> `5 : 2 = flour : 250` → extremes × means: `5 × 250 = 2 × flour` → flour = `1250 ÷ 2` = **625 g**.
>
> Sense-check: flour's share (5) is bigger than sugar's (2), and 625 g > 250 g ✓ — the bigger ratio number must get the bigger amount.
