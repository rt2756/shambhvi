<!-- ===== Complementary & supplementary ===== -->

Find the **complement** of **35°**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Complementary angles add to **90°**, so complement = `90° − 35° = ` **55°**.
>
> Memory hook from the notes: **C**omplementary → **C**orner (90°).

---

Find the **supplement** of **110°**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Supplementary angles add to **180°**, so supplement = `180° − 110° = ` **70°**.
>
> **S**upplementary → **S**traight line (180°).

---

Find the complement of **62°** and the supplement of **47°**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Complement of 62° = `90° − 62° = ` **28°**.
>
> Supplement of 47° = `180° − 47° = ` **133°**.

---

Can **two acute angles** ever be **supplementary**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No.** Each acute angle is less than 90°, so together they make **less than 180°** — they can never reach the straight-line total.
>
> (For the same reason, two obtuse angles can't be supplementary either — they'd *overshoot* 180°. A supplementary pair that isn't 90° + 90° is always one acute + one obtuse.)

---

Does **95°** have a complement?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No.** A complement would be `90° − 95° = −5°` — and a negative angle isn't an angle.
>
> Only angles **less than 90°** have complements; 95° does have a *supplement* (`180° − 95° = 85°`).

---

Which angle **equals its own complement**? Which equals its own **supplement**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Equal complements: the two equal angles add to 90° → each = `90° ÷ 2 = ` **45°**.
>
> Equal supplements: `180° ÷ 2 = ` **90°**.
>
> The "why" is just sharing the total equally between two equal partners.

---

Two complementary angles are in the ratio **2 : 3**. Find them.

> [!ANSWER]
> The 90° splits into `2 + 3 = 5` equal parts → 1 part = `90° ÷ 5 = 18°`.
>
> Angles = `2 × 18° = ` **36°** and `3 × 18° = ` **54°**. Check: `36 + 54 = 90` ✓

---

<!-- ===== Adjacent angles & linear pair ===== -->

∠AOB and ∠BOC share the vertex **O** and the arm **OB**, with their other arms on opposite sides of OB. What are such angles called?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Adjacent angles** — same vertex, one common arm, no overlap.
>
> They sit side by side like neighbouring slices of pizza: sharing one cut, never covering each other.

---

Two adjacent angles measure **x** and **x + 40°**, and their outer arms form a **straight line**. Find both angles.

> [!ANSWER]
> Outer arms in a straight line → **linear pair** → they add to 180°:
>
> `x + (x + 40°) = 180°` → `2x = 140°` → `x = 70°`.
>
> Angles = **70° and 110°**. Check: `70 + 110 = 180` ✓
>
> **Why 180°:** together the two angles fill everything on one side of a straight line — half a full turn.

---

Can two angles of **80° and 110°** form a linear pair?

> [!ANSWER]
> `80 + 110 = 190 ≠ 180` → **no**.
>
> A linear pair must total exactly 180°, because the straight line fixes the total — no flexibility.

---

<!-- ===== Vertically opposite angles ===== -->

Two lines cross, and one of the four angles is **50°**. Find the other three.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Vertically opposite** the 50° → **50°** (opposite angles are equal).
>
> Each neighbour forms a **linear pair** with 50° → `180° − 50° = ` **130°**, and the fourth angle is opposite that → **130°**.
>
> So the four angles are **50°, 130°, 50°, 130°**. Check: `50 + 130 + 50 + 130 = 360°` ✓ — a full turn around the crossing point.
>
> **Why opposite angles are equal:** both 50° angles are `180° − (the same 130° neighbour)` — two things equal to the same thing are equal to each other.

---

Two lines intersect. One angle is **90°**. What are all four angles?

> [!ANSWER]
> Opposite angle = 90° (vertically opposite). Each neighbour = `180° − 90° = 90°` (linear pair).
>
> **All four are 90°** — this is exactly what *perpendicular* lines are.

---

Two lines cross and one pair of vertically opposite angles are **(3x + 10)°** and **(4x − 15)°**. Find x.

> [!ANSWER]
> Vertically opposite angles are **equal**:
>
> `3x + 10 = 4x − 15` → `10 + 15 = 4x − 3x` → `x = ` **25**.
>
> Check: both angles = `3 × 25 + 10 = 85°` and `4 × 25 − 15 = 85°` ✓

---

<!-- ===== Transversal across parallel lines ===== -->

A **transversal** cuts two lines. How many angles are formed in total?

> [!ANSWER]
> **8** — the transversal crosses each line once, and every crossing makes 4 angles: `2 × 4 = 8`.

---

Lines l and m are **parallel**; a transversal makes an angle of **65°** with line l. What is the **corresponding** angle at line m?

> [!ANSWER]
> **65°.** Corresponding angles (the **F shape** — same corner position at each crossing) are **equal when the lines are parallel**.
>
> Intuition: parallel lines point in exactly the same direction, so the transversal crosses each at the same slant — the crossings are carbon copies of each other.

---

Parallel lines, and one **alternate interior angle** is **72°**. What is its partner?

> [!ANSWER]
> **72°.** Alternate interior angles (the **Z shape** — between the lines, on opposite sides of the transversal) are **equal** when the lines are parallel.

---

Parallel lines: one **co-interior** angle is **65°**. Find the other.

> [!ANSWER]
> Co-interior angles (the **C shape** — between the lines, same side of the transversal) are **supplementary**:
>
> `180° − 65° = ` **115°**.
>
> **Why 180° and not equal:** the 65° angle's *corresponding* angle at the other line is also 65°, and that copy forms a **linear pair** with the co-interior partner → `65° + partner = 180°`. Equal-copy + straight-line = the supplement rule.

---

Parallel lines cut by a transversal; one angle is **120°**. Find **all eight** angles.

> [!ANSWER]
> At the first crossing: 120° with neighbours `180° − 120° = 60°` (linear pairs) and its vertical opposite 120° → angles are **120°, 60°, 120°, 60°**.
>
> The second crossing **copies the first** (corresponding angles equal, since the lines are parallel) → also **120°, 60°, 120°, 60°**.
>
> So all eight angles are either **120° or 60°** — four of each. With parallel lines, one known angle unlocks all eight.

---

A transversal cuts two lines, and a pair of **alternate interior angles** both measure **45°**. What can you conclude?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The lines are parallel.** The notes' test works in reverse: if any one pair (corresponding equal, alternate equal, or co-interior supplementary) behaves as the parallel rules say, the lines must be parallel.
>
> Equal alternate angles mean both lines cross the transversal at the same slant — same slant, same direction, never meeting.

---

Two lines are cut by a transversal, and the **co-interior angles** measure **95° and 75°**. Are the lines parallel?

> [!ANSWER]
> Co-interior angles of parallel lines must add to **180°**. Here `95 + 75 = 170°` → **not parallel**.
>
> Falling 10° short of 180° means the lines tilt towards each other on that side — follow them far enough and they'll meet.

---

Lines l ∥ m. A transversal makes a **(2x + 30)°** angle with l and its **corresponding** angle at m is **(3x − 10)°**. Find x and the angles.

> [!ANSWER]
> Corresponding angles of parallel lines are equal:
>
> `2x + 30 = 3x − 10` → `x = ` **40**.
>
> Both angles = `2 × 40 + 30 = ` **110°** (check: `3 × 40 − 10 = 110°` ✓).
