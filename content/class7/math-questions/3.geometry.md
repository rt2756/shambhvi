<!-- ===== Angles — types ===== -->

Classify each angle: **35°, 90°, 120°, 180°, 250°**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Compare each with the milestone values 90°, 180°, 360°:
>
> **35°** → less than 90° → **acute** · **90°** → exactly → **right** · **120°** → between 90° and 180° → **obtuse** · **180°** → exactly → **straight** · **250°** → between 180° and 360° → **reflex**.

---

An angle measures **89°**. Is it a right angle?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No — it's acute.** A right angle is **exactly 90°**, not "about 90°". Even 1° short means the two arms aren't perpendicular.

---

Two lines meet at exactly **90°**. What do we call these lines?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Perpendicular lines.** Meeting at a right angle is exactly what "perpendicular" means.

---

<!-- ===== Angles — clock ===== -->

How many degrees does the **hour hand** turn in **3 hours**?

> [!ANSWER]
> In 12 hours the hour hand makes a full turn = 360°, so 1 hour = `360° ÷ 12 = 30°`.
>
> 3 hours → `3 × 30° = ` **90°**.

---

How many degrees does the **minute hand** turn in **20 minutes**?

> [!ANSWER]
> In 60 minutes the minute hand makes a full turn = 360°, so 1 minute = `360° ÷ 60 = 6°`.
>
> 20 minutes → `20 × 6° = ` **120°**.

---

What is the angle between the hands of a clock at **3 o'clock**?

> [!ANSWER]
> At 3:00 the minute hand points at 12 and the hour hand at 3 — they are **3 hour-marks apart**.
>
> Each hour-mark = 30° (since `360° ÷ 12 = 30°`), so the angle = `3 × 30° = ` **90°** — a right angle.

---

What is the angle between the hands at **6 o'clock**, and what type of angle is it?

> [!ANSWER]
> Hands at 12 and 6 are **6 hour-marks apart** → `6 × 30° = ` **180°** — a **straight angle** (the hands make one straight line).

---

How many degrees does the **second hand** sweep in **30 seconds**?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1 second = `360° ÷ 60 = 6°`, so 30 seconds → `30 × 6° = ` **180°** — half a full turn, which is why the second hand points exactly opposite to where it started.

---

<!-- ===== Angles — naming ===== -->

Rays **OP** and **OQ** meet at the point **O**. Write **two correct names** for the angle they make.

> [!ANSWER]
> The meeting point O is the **vertex**, and the vertex letter must sit **in the middle**:
>
> **∠POQ** or **∠QOP** — both correct, because both keep O in the middle.
>
> **Why the middle?** The name traces a path: start on one arm (P), pass through the corner (O), end on the other arm (Q). Writing ∠OPQ would put the corner at the start — a different angle entirely.

---

An angle of **60°** is drawn. What is the **reflex angle** at the same vertex (the "long way round")?

> [!ANSWER]
> Going all the way around a point is **360°**. The reflex angle is everything except the 60°:
>
> `360° − 60° = ` **300°** — and 300° is between 180° and 360°, which is exactly the reflex range. ✓

---

<!-- ===== Shapes — triangles ===== -->

A triangle has sides **5 cm, 5 cm and 8 cm**. What type of triangle is it by sides?

> [!ANSWER]
> Exactly **2 sides are equal** (5 and 5) → **isosceles**.
>
> Bonus fact from the notes: its 2 base angles (opposite the equal sides) are equal too.

---

A triangle has all three sides **6 cm**. What type is it, and what is each angle?

> [!ANSWER]
> All sides equal → **equilateral** — and all its angles are equal too.
>
> The three angles of any triangle add to 180°, so each = `180° ÷ 3 = ` **60°**.

---

Can a **scalene** triangle also be a **right-angled** triangle?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Yes.** Scalene talks about **sides** (none equal); right-angled talks about **angles** (one is 90°). They are separate labels, so one triangle can have both — e.g. sides 3, 4, 5: no equal sides, and one 90° corner.

---

<!-- ===== Shapes — the quadrilateral family ===== -->

What is the difference between a **square** and a **rhombus**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Both have **4 equal sides** with opposite sides parallel.
>
> The difference is the **angles**: a square's 4 angles are all right angles; a rhombus is the "leaning" version — usually no right angles.
>
> That's why a square is just a **special rhombus** — a rhombus that happened to stand up straight.

---

Why is **every square a rectangle**, but **not every rectangle a square**?

> [!ANSWER]
> A rectangle needs: 4 right angles, opposite sides equal and parallel. A square has **all of that** — so it passes the rectangle test → every square **is** a rectangle.
>
> But a rectangle only needs *opposite* sides equal; a square demands **all four** equal. A 5 × 3 rectangle fails that extra demand → not a square.
>
> The family rule: the shape with **more conditions** (square) always fits inside the club with **fewer conditions** (rectangle) — never the other way round.

---

A quadrilateral has **2 pairs of equal adjacent sides**, and its diagonals cross at **right angles**. Name it.

> [!ANSWER]
> **A kite.** "Adjacent" is the giveaway — the equal sides are *neighbours* (side 1 = side 2, side 3 = side 4), unlike a parallelogram where the equal sides sit *opposite* each other.

---

What is the difference between a **rectangle** and a **parallelogram**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Both have opposite sides **equal and parallel**.
>
> A rectangle additionally has **4 right angles**; a parallelogram is the leaning version, usually with no right angles. So a rectangle is a **special parallelogram** — the one that stands up straight.

---

<!-- ===== Shapes — polygons & circle ===== -->

How many sides does a **heptagon** have? A **nonagon**? A **decagon**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Heptagon (septagon) = 7** · **nonagon = 9** · **decagon = 10** — and each has the same number of corners as sides, because every side ends at a corner where the next side begins.

---

A **regular pentagon** is drawn. What is its central (turn) angle?

> [!ANSWER]
> A regular shape shares the full 360° at the centre **equally among its 5 sides**:
>
> `360° ÷ 5 = ` **72°**.
>
> Same idea for a regular hexagon: `360° ÷ 6 = 60°`.

---

A circle has a radius of **7 cm**. What is its diameter?

> [!ANSWER]
> `diameter = 2 × radius = 2 × 7 = ` **14 cm**.
>
> **Why ×2:** the diameter runs edge → centre → edge, so it is exactly **two radii** laid end to end.

---

What is a **regular shape**? Is a rectangle a regular shape?

> [!ANSWER]
> A regular shape needs **all sides equal AND all angles equal**.
>
> A rectangle passes the angle test (all 90°) but **fails the sides test** (length ≠ breadth in general) → **not regular**. The regular quadrilateral is the **square**.

---

<!-- ===== 3D shapes, nets & views ===== -->

How many **faces, edges and vertices** does a **cube** have?

> [!ANSWER]
> **6 faces** (all squares), **12 edges**, **8 vertices**.
>
> A quick way to see it: a cube has a top and bottom (2 faces) plus 4 walls → 6. Each of the 2 square ends has 4 corners → `2 × 4 = 8` vertices. Edges: 4 around the top + 4 around the bottom + 4 standing up = 12.

---

A **cuboid** (like a brick) — how do its faces, edges and vertices compare with a cube's?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Same counts: 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 corners** — a cuboid is just a stretched cube.
>
> The difference: a cube's faces are all **squares**, a cuboid's are **rectangles**.

---

Describe the faces of a **cylinder**, a **cone** and a **sphere**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Cylinder:** 2 flat circular faces (top and bottom) + 1 curved face wrapped around → 3 faces.
>
> **Cone:** 1 flat circular face (the base) + 1 curved face + **1 corner** (the tip).
>
> **Sphere:** just 1 curved surface — **no edges, no corners** (nothing flat anywhere).

---

What is the difference between a **prism** and a **pyramid**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Prism:** the **same shape on two opposite faces** (e.g. two triangles), joined by rectangles — like a tent or a Toblerone box.
>
> **Pyramid:** **one base** only, with triangular faces rising to meet at **one top corner**.
>
> Memory hook: a prism has the special shape **twice**, a pyramid has it **once** with a point on top.

---

A flat pattern of **6 equal squares** is folded up. What solid does it make, and what is such a pattern called?

> [!ANSWER]
> The pattern is a **net**, and 6 squares fold into a **cube** (6 faces, all squares).
>
> Check when folding: **opposite faces must not touch** in the net — faces that share an edge in the net become **adjacent** (touching) faces of the cube.

---

You look at a **cylinder** standing on a table. What shape do you see from the **top**? From the **front**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Top view: a circle** (you look straight down onto the circular face).
>
> **Front view: a rectangle** (the curved wall seen side-on has straight left and right edges).
>
> This is the notes' point: the **same object looks different** from top, front and side — one view alone can't tell you the whole solid.

<!-- ===== Perimeter, area & volume (basics) ===== -->

Find the **perimeter** of a square of side **9 cm**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Perimeter = distance **around** = all 4 equal sides added: `4 × 9 = ` **36 cm**.
>
> Answer in **cm** (a length around, not a surface) — no squared units.

---

Find the **area** of a rectangle **8 cm long and 5 cm wide**, and explain the unit.

> [!ANSWER]
> Area = `l × b = 8 × 5 = ` **40 cm²**.
>
> **Why cm²:** area counts how many **1 cm × 1 cm squares** cover the surface — 8 columns of 5 squares = 40 unit squares. Counting squares → squared units.

---

A box (cuboid) is **4 cm long, 3 cm wide, 2 cm high**. What is its volume?

> [!ANSWER]
> Volume = `l × b × h = 4 × 3 × 2 = ` **24 cm³**.
>
> **Why cm³:** volume counts **1 cm cubes** that fill the box — the floor holds `4 × 3 = 12` cubes, and there are 2 layers → 24 cubes.

---

<!-- ===== Symmetry ===== -->

How many **lines of symmetry** does each have: **square, rectangle, equilateral triangle, circle**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Square → 4** (2 through the middles of opposite sides + 2 diagonals) · **rectangle → 2** (only through the middles of opposite sides) · **equilateral triangle → 3** · **circle → infinite** (every diameter is one).
>
> **⚠️ The trap:** a rectangle's diagonals are **not** lines of symmetry — folding along a diagonal makes the two halves overhang; they don't match.

---

What is the **order of rotational symmetry** of a square, and its **angle of rotation**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Turn a square about its centre: it looks the same after every quarter turn — **4 matches** in a full turn → **order 4**.
>
> Angle of rotation = `360° ÷ order = 360° ÷ 4 = ` **90°**.

---

A **regular hexagon**: how many lines of symmetry, what order of rotational symmetry, and what angle of rotation?

> [!ANSWER]
> The notes' rule for a **regular polygon with n sides**: **n lines** of symmetry and rotational symmetry of **order n**.
>
> n = 6 → **6 lines, order 6**, angle = `360° ÷ 6 = ` **60°**.
>
> **Why:** a regular shape's sides and angles are all identical, so a turn by one side's worth (60°) lands every side exactly onto the next — a perfect match, 6 times per full turn.

---

A shape looks the same after **half a turn (180°)** but not after a quarter turn. What is its order of rotational symmetry? Give an example.

> [!ANSWER]
> It matches at 180° and at 360° — **2 matches** per full turn → **order 2**.
>
> Example from the notes: a **rectangle** — a half turn swaps its ends and it looks unchanged, but a quarter turn stands it on its short side, visibly different.

---

Which shape has **infinite** lines of symmetry **and** infinite order of rotational symmetry?

> [!ANSWER]
> The **circle** — every diameter is a mirror line, and a turn by *any* angle about the centre leaves it looking identical, because every point of the edge is the same distance from the centre.
