<!-- ===== Length ===== -->

Convert **3 m** to centimetres.

> [!ANSWER]
> `1 m = 100 cm`, so `3 m = 3 × 100 = ` **300 cm**.
>
> **Why multiply:** a centimetre is a *smaller* unit, so the same length needs **more** of them — smaller steps, more steps.

---

Convert **4.5 km** to metres.

> [!ANSWER]
> `1 km = 1000 m` → `4.5 × 1000 = ` **4500 m**.
>
> (×1000 slides the decimal point 3 places right: 4.5 → 4500.)

---

Convert **250 cm** to metres.

> [!ANSWER]
> Going to a **bigger** unit → divide: `250 ÷ 100 = ` **2.5 m**.
>
> Fewer of a bigger unit covers the same length — that's why it's ÷ this time, not ×.

---

Convert **7 cm** to millimetres, and **2500 m** to kilometres.

> [!ANSWER]
> `1 cm = 10 mm` → `7 × 10 = ` **70 mm** (smaller unit → multiply).
>
> `1 km = 1000 m` → `2500 ÷ 1000 = ` **2.5 km** (bigger unit → divide).

---

Which is longer: **350 cm** or **3.2 m**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Compare in **one unit** first: `3.2 m = 320 cm`.
>
> `350 > 320` → **350 cm is longer**.
>
> Comparing 350 with 3.2 directly is meaningless — the numbers only compare fairly once the units match.

---

Add **2 m 40 cm** and **1 m 80 cm**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Work in cm: `240 + 180 = 420 cm`.
>
> Back to metres: `420 ÷ 100 = ` **4.2 m** (= 4 m 20 cm).
>
> Note the carry: `40 + 80 = 120 cm` is more than a metre, so one metre "carries over" — same idea as carrying in column addition.

---

The full length ladder: how many **mm** are in **1 m**? Show the steps.

> [!ANSWER]
> Each step of the ladder is ×10: `1 m = 10 dm = 100 cm = 1000 mm`.
>
> So **1 m = 1000 mm** — three steps of ×10 → `10 × 10 × 10 = 1000`.

---

<!-- ===== Weight ===== -->

Convert **3 kg** to grams, and **4500 g** to kilograms.

> [!ANSWER]
> `1 kg = 1000 g` → `3 kg = 3 × 1000 = ` **3000 g**.
>
> `4500 g = 4500 ÷ 1000 = ` **4.5 kg** — dividing because kg is the bigger unit.

---

A tablet contains **500 mg** of medicine. How many grams is that?

> [!ANSWER]
> `1 g = 1000 mg` → `500 ÷ 1000 = ` **0.5 g** — half a gram, since 500 is half of 1000.

---

A shopkeeper packs **250 g** packets of tea from a **2 kg** box. How many packets?

> [!ANSWER]
> Same units first: `2 kg = 2000 g`.
>
> `2000 ÷ 250 = ` **8 packets**. Check: `8 × 250 = 2000 g` ✓

---

<!-- ===== Capacity ===== -->

Convert **2.5 L** to millilitres, and **750 ml** to litres.

> [!ANSWER]
> `1 L = 1000 ml` → `2.5 × 1000 = ` **2500 ml**.
>
> `750 ml = 750 ÷ 1000 = ` **0.75 L** — three-quarters of a litre.

---

A **2-litre** bottle of juice fills glasses of **250 ml** each. How many glasses?

> [!ANSWER]
> `2 L = 2000 ml` → `2000 ÷ 250 = ` **8 glasses**.
>
> The division asks: "how many 250s fit inside 2000?" — that's exactly what ÷ means.

---

<!-- ===== Time ===== -->

Convert **3 minutes** to seconds, and **2 hours** to minutes.

> [!ANSWER]
> `1 min = 60 sec` → `3 × 60 = ` **180 seconds**.
>
> `1 hour = 60 min` → `2 × 60 = ` **120 minutes**.
>
> ⚠️ Time steps are **60**, not 10 or 100 — the decimal habits don't apply.

---

How many **seconds** are there in **1 hour**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Two steps: `1 hour = 60 min`, each minute = 60 sec:
>
> `60 × 60 = ` **3600 seconds**.

---

Convert **90 minutes** to hours.

> [!ANSWER]
> `90 ÷ 60 = ` **1.5 hours** (1 hour 30 minutes).
>
> ⚠️ Not 0.90 hours — 90 minutes is *more* than an hour, and dividing by 60 (not 100) shows it.

---

A movie starts at **5:40 pm** and runs for **2 hours 35 minutes**. When does it end?

> [!ANSWER]
> Add the hours first: `5:40 + 2 h = 7:40`. Then the minutes: `7:40 + 35 min`.
>
> `40 + 35 = 75 min = 1 h 15 min` → carry the hour → **8:15 pm**.
>
> Minutes roll over at **60** (not 100) — that's the whole trick with time sums.

---

<!-- ===== Quantity — powers of ten ===== -->

Write **1 million** in figures. How many **thousands** make a million?

> [!ANSWER]
> Million = 10⁶ = **1,000,000** (six zeros).
>
> Thousand = 10³. `10⁶ ÷ 10³ = 10³` → **1000 thousands** make a million — the powers subtract because dividing cancels three of the six tens.

---

How many **hundreds** are there in **ten thousand**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Ten thousand = 10⁴ = 10,000; hundred = 10² = 100.
>
> `10⁴ ÷ 10² = 10² = ` **100 hundreds**. Check: `100 × 100 = 10,000` ✓

---

Arrange from smallest to largest: **billion, thousand, million, trillion** — and give each as a power of 10.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Thousand (10³) < million (10⁶) < billion (10⁹) < trillion (10¹²)**.
>
> Each jump is ×1000 — three more zeros every step: 1,000 → 1,000,000 → 1,000,000,000 → 1,000,000,000,000.

---

India's population is about **1.4 billion**. Write that in figures.

> [!ANSWER]
> 1 billion = 1,000,000,000 (10⁹) → 1.4 billion = `1.4 × 1,000,000,000 = ` **1,400,000,000**.
>
> (×10⁹ slides the decimal point 9 places right.)
