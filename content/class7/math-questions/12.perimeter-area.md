<!-- ===== Squares & rectangles ===== -->

Find the **perimeter and area** of a square of side **12 cm**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Perimeter = `4 × 12 = ` **48 cm** (four equal sides added).
>
> Area = `12 × 12 = ` **144 cm²** (12 rows of 12 unit squares).
>
> Note the units: perimeter is a length (**cm**), area counts squares (**cm²**).

---

A rectangle is **15 cm long and 8 cm wide**. Find its perimeter and area.

> [!ANSWER]
> Perimeter = `2 × (l + b) = 2 × (15 + 8) = 2 × 23 = ` **46 cm** — the ×2 is because both the length and the breadth appear twice (top & bottom, left & right).
>
> Area = `l × b = 15 × 8 = ` **120 cm²**.

---

A rectangle has area **96 cm²** and length **12 cm**. Find its breadth.

> [!ANSWER]
> Run the formula backwards: `A = l × b` → `b = A ÷ l = 96 ÷ 12 = ` **8 cm**.

---

A square has perimeter **64 m**. Find its area.

> [!ANSWER]
> First recover the side: `4s = 64` → `s = 16 m`.
>
> Then area = `16 × 16 = ` **256 m²**.
>
> Two-step problems chain the formulas — perimeter unlocks the side, the side unlocks the area.

---

A square and a rectangle **both have perimeter 24 cm**. The rectangle is 8 × 4. Which has more area?

> [!ANSWER]
> Square: side = `24 ÷ 4 = 6` → area = **36 cm²**. Rectangle: `8 × 4 = ` **32 cm²**.
>
> **Same perimeter, different areas** — the square wins. The more stretched a rectangle gets, the more area it loses for the same fence; equal sides pack the most area in.

---

<!-- ===== Parallelogram & triangle ===== -->

A parallelogram has base **10 cm** and height **6 cm**. Find its area.

> [!ANSWER]
> Area = `b × h = 10 × 6 = ` **60 cm²**.
>
> ⚠️ The height is the **perpendicular** distance to the base — never the slanted side. Why the formula works: slice the leaning triangle off one end and it fits the other end exactly, turning the parallelogram into a 10 × 6 rectangle.

---

A parallelogram has area **72 cm²** and base **9 cm**. Find its height.

> [!ANSWER]
> `h = A ÷ b = 72 ÷ 9 = ` **8 cm**.

---

Find the area of a triangle with base **10 cm** and height **7 cm**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Area = `1/2 × b × h = 1/2 × 10 × 7 = ` **35 cm²**.
>
> **Where the 1/2 comes from:** two copies of this triangle fit together into a parallelogram of base 10 and height 7 (area 70) — so one triangle is exactly **half**.

---

A triangle has area **54 cm²** and base **12 cm**. Find its height.

> [!ANSWER]
> `54 = 1/2 × 12 × h` → `54 = 6h` → `h = ` **9 cm**.

---

Find the area of a **right triangle** whose legs are **6 cm and 8 cm**.

> [!ANSWER]
> The legs meet at 90°, so one leg is the base and the other **is** the height:
>
> `1/2 × 6 × 8 = ` **24 cm²** — no extra height needed; the right angle supplies it.

---

<!-- ===== Circle ===== -->

Find the **circumference and area** of a circle with radius **7 cm** (use π = 22/7).

> [!ANSWER]
> Circumference = `2πr = 2 × 22/7 × 7 = ` **44 cm** (the 7s cancel — that's why 22/7 is the handy choice here).
>
> Area = `πr² = 22/7 × 7 × 7 = 22 × 7 = ` **154 cm²**.

---

Find the circumference of a circle with **diameter 10 cm** (use π = 3.14).

> [!ANSWER]
> Circumference = `π × d = 3.14 × 10 = ` **31.4 cm**.
>
> (Works because `d = 2r`, so `2πr` and `πd` are the same formula in two costumes.)

---

A circular wheel has circumference **88 cm**. Find its radius (π = 22/7).

> [!ANSWER]
> Backwards: `2 × 22/7 × r = 88` → `44/7 × r = 88` → `r = 88 × 7/44 = ` **14 cm**.
>
> Check: `2 × 22/7 × 14 = 88` ✓

---

Which grows more when the radius doubles from 7 to 14 — the circumference or the area?

> [!ANSWER]
> Circumference: `44 → 88` — **doubles** (r appears once in 2πr).
>
> Area: `154 → 616` — **quadruples** (r appears **squared** in πr², and 2² = 4).
>
> Doubling lengths always ×4-s areas — the same reason a 2×-photo enlargement uses 4× the paper.

<!-- ===== Reading the question: cost, fencing, tiling ===== -->

A park is **60 m × 40 m**. Fencing costs **₹15 per metre**. Find the total cost.

> [!ANSWER]
> Fencing runs **around** → perimeter, not area:
>
> `P = 2 × (60 + 40) = 200 m` → cost = `200 × 15 = ` **₹3000**.
>
> Rate **per metre** is the giveaway — metres means length means perimeter.

---

A room's floor is **5 m × 4 m**. Carpet costs **₹120 per square metre**. Find the cost of carpeting.

> [!ANSWER]
> Carpet **covers** → area: `5 × 4 = 20 m²` → cost = `20 × 120 = ` **₹2400**.
>
> Rate **per m²** signals area, just as per-metre signalled perimeter.

---

How many **50 cm × 50 cm** tiles cover a **4 m × 3 m** floor?

> [!ANSWER]
> One unit first: tile = `0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 m²`; floor = `4 × 3 = 12 m²`.
>
> `12 ÷ 0.25 = ` **48 tiles**.
>
> (Or count directly: `8` tiles fit along 4 m, `6` along 3 m → `8 × 6 = 48` ✓ — two roads, same answer.)

---

<!-- ===== Path problems: outer − inner ===== -->

A garden is **50 m × 30 m**. A **2 m wide path** runs around it **on the outside**. Find the area of the path.

> [!ANSWER]
> The path adds 2 m on **every** side, so the outer rectangle is `(50 + 4) × (30 + 4) = 54 × 34` — +4 per direction because the path sits on both ends.
>
> Outer area = `54 × 34 = 1836 m²` · inner (garden) = `50 × 30 = 1500 m²`.
>
> **Path = outer − inner** = `1836 − 1500 = ` **336 m²** — the ring is what's left when the garden is scooped out of the bigger rectangle.

---

A field is **40 m × 25 m**, and a **2.5 m wide path** runs around it **inside** the boundary. Find the area of the path.

> [!ANSWER]
> Inside → the field shrinks: inner rectangle = `(40 − 5) × (25 − 5) = 35 × 20 = 700 m²` (−5 per direction: 2.5 m off each end).
>
> Outer = `40 × 25 = 1000 m²` → path = `1000 − 700 = ` **300 m²**.

---

<!-- ===== Unit conversions ===== -->

Why is **1 m² = 10,000 cm²** (not 100)? Convert **2.5 m²** to cm².

> [!ANSWER]
> A square metre is a 1 m × 1 m square = `100 cm × 100 cm` = `100 × 100 = 10,000 cm²` — the conversion factor gets **squared** because area has two directions, each swapping m → cm.
>
> `2.5 m² = 2.5 × 10,000 = ` **25,000 cm²**.

---

A field measures **200 m × 150 m**. Express its area in **hectares**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Area = `200 × 150 = 30,000 m²`.
>
> 1 hectare = 10,000 m² (a 100 m × 100 m square) → `30,000 ÷ 10,000 = ` **3 hectares**.

---

A wire is bent into a **square of side 22 cm**. It is then re-bent into a **circle**. Find the circle's radius (π = 22/7).

> [!ANSWER]
> The wire's **length doesn't change** — that's the key insight:
>
> Square perimeter = `4 × 22 = 88 cm` = circle's circumference.
>
> `2 × 22/7 × r = 88` → `r = ` **14 cm** (same computation as the wheel above — recognising "same length, new shape" is the whole problem).
