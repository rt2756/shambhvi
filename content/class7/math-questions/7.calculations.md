<!-- ===== Tables — rebuild tricky facts from easy ones ===== -->

Find **17 × 6** without the table.

> [!ANSWER]
> Split 17 into easy parts: `17 = 10 + 7`.
>
> `17 × 6 = 10 × 6 + 7 × 6 = 60 + 42 = ` **102**
>
> **Why it works (distributive):** 6 groups of 17 is the same as 6 groups of 10 plus 6 groups of 7.

---

Find **19 × 12** the fast way.

> [!ANSWER]
> 19 is "20 minus 1", and ×20 is easy:
>
> `19 × 12 = 20 × 12 − 1 × 12 = 240 − 12 = ` **228**
>
> Going *near and correct back* beats multiplying by an awkward 19 directly.

---

Find **14 × 13**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Split the 13: `14 × 13 = 14 × 10 + 14 × 3 = 140 + 42 = ` **182**

---

Find **16 × 15**.

> [!ANSWER]
> ×15 = ×10 then add half of that again (because 15 = 10 + 5, and ×5 is half of ×10):
>
> `16 × 10 = 160` → half = 80 → `160 + 80 = ` **240**

---

Find **18 × 7**.

> [!ANSWER]
> `18 = 20 − 2` → `20 × 7 − 2 × 7 = 140 − 14 = ` **126**

---

A hall has **18 rows of 15 chairs**. How many chairs in all?

> [!ANSWER]
> `18 × 15` → ×10 then add half: `18 × 10 = 180`, half = 90 → `180 + 90 = ` **270 chairs**

---

<!-- ===== Squares ===== -->

What is **15²**? Show how to rebuild it if forgotten.

> [!ANSWER]
> `15² = 15 × 15 = 15 × 10 + 15 × 5 = 150 + 75 = ` **225**
>
> Any square can be rebuilt the same way — the table is a time-saver, not a thing you're stuck without.

---

Rebuild **21²** from **20²**.

> [!ANSWER]
> `21 × 21 = 21 × 20 + 21 × 1 = 420 + 21 = ` **441**
>
> Knowing the round-number square next door (20² = 400) plus one distributive step gets you any neighbour.

---

Find **19 × 19** using **19 × 20**.

> [!ANSWER]
> `19 × 20 = 380` is one 19 too many, so take it back off:
>
> `380 − 19 = ` **361** — and that's 19². ✓ (matches the table)

---

Which number squared gives **324**?

> [!ANSWER]
> From the squares list: `18² = 324` → **18**.
>
> Sense-check without the list: `17² = 289` (too small), `18² = 324` ✓ — squares climb fast, so the answer is pinned between neighbours.

---

Is **400** a perfect square? Is **500**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **400 yes:** `20² = 400`.
>
> **500 no:** `22² = 484` and `23² = 529` — 500 falls **between two consecutive squares**, so no whole number squares to it. That between-two-squares check is the standard test.

---

By how much is **13²** bigger than **12²**? Spot the pattern.

> [!ANSWER]
> `169 − 144 = ` **25** — and `12 + 13 = 25` too!
>
> **Why:** picture a 12 × 12 square of dots. To grow it to 13 × 13, add one column of 12 and one row of 13 → `12 + 13` new dots. Consecutive squares always differ by the two numbers added together.

---

Use that pattern to get **26²** from **25² = 625**.

> [!ANSWER]
> `26² = 25² + (25 + 26) = 625 + 51 = ` **676**
>
> The pattern extends the memorised list (1–25) as far as you like, one neighbour at a time.

---

A square courtyard is covered by **12 rows of 12 tiles**. How many tiles — and why is this "12 squared"?

> [!ANSWER]
> `12 × 12 = ` **144 tiles**.
>
> "Squared" literally means "arranged as a square" — 12 rows of 12 *is* a square of tiles. That geometric picture is where the name comes from.

<!-- ===== Cubes ===== -->

What is **7³**? Rebuild it, don't just recall it.

> [!ANSWER]
> `7³ = 7 × 7 × 7` — step it: `7² = 49`, then `49 × 7` → **343**
>
> (Shortcut for `49 × 7`: `50 × 7 = 350`, one 7 too many → `350 − 7 = 343`.)

---

Which number cubed gives **512**?

> [!ANSWER]
> From the cubes list: `8³ = 512` → **8**.
>
> Pin it between neighbours: `7³ = 343` (too small), `9³ = 729` (too big) → only 8 can work.

---

A cube-shaped box has edges of **5 cm**. How many 1-cm cubes fill it — and why is this "5 cubed"?

> [!ANSWER]
> Floor layer: `5 × 5 = 25` small cubes; there are 5 layers → `25 × 5 = ` **125 cubes** = 5³.
>
> "Cubed" literally means "stacked into a cube" — length × breadth × height with all three equal. That's why volume uses cubic units.

---

Which is bigger: **2³ or 3²**? And **2⁴ or 4²**?

> [!ANSWER]
> `2³ = 8` vs `3² = 9` → **3² wins** (just).
>
> `2⁴ = 16` vs `4² = 16` → **a tie!**
>
> Moral: swapping base and exponent usually changes the value — check by computing, never assume.

---

Between which two numbers on the cubes list does **600** fall? Is it a perfect cube?

> [!ANSWER]
> `8³ = 512` and `9³ = 729` → 600 sits **between 8³ and 9³**.
>
> No whole number between 8 and 9 exists, so **600 is not a perfect cube**.
