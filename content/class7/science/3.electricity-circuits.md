# 3. Electricity: circuits & components

<!-- NCERT Class 7 "Curiosity" Chapter 3 — Electricity: Circuits and their Components. -->

> [!WARNING]
> **Never experiment with mains electricity** (wall sockets, poles) — it is dangerous. Use only **cells/batteries** like those in torches, clocks and remotes.

## Electric cell & battery

- An **electric cell** is a portable source of electrical energy with **two terminals**: the metal **cap = positive (+)**, the flat metal **disc = negative (−)**.
- A **battery** = two or more cells joined so that the **+ of one touches the − of the next**.
- **Why that order:** the cells then push the current the *same* way, like people pushing a cart from the same side. One cell reversed pushes *against* the others — the torch lamp won't glow.
- More cells → energy for a **longer time and/or more energy**. (Everyday language calls even a single cell a "battery" — like a phone battery.)

## Two kinds of lamp

- **Incandescent lamp:** a thin wire — the **filament** — glows when current heats it. Its two terminals are the metal **case** and the metal **tip** at the base.
  - Works **either way round** — current through the filament glows regardless of direction.
  - A **fused** lamp = **broken filament** → the path is broken → no current → no glow.
- **LED (Light Emitting Diode):** no filament. **Longer wire = + terminal**, shorter = −.
  - Lets current through in **one direction only**: + of LED must meet + of battery, else it stays dark.

> [!TIP]
> Lamp doesn't glow? Check in order: cells the right way round → connections tight → (for LED) long wire to + → filament not broken.

## The circuit — a complete loop

- An **electrical circuit** = a **complete, unbroken path** from one terminal of the cell, through the lamp, back to the other terminal.
- **Why a loop:** current only flows when it can go all the way round — any gap anywhere stops it **everywhere**, just as one broken bridge stops the whole ring road.
- The **direction of current** is taken as **+ terminal → − terminal** outside the cell.
- **Switch:** a device that **closes** the gap (ON — circuit **closed**, current flows) or **opens** it (OFF — circuit **open**, no current). It works from *anywhere* in the loop.

## Circuit diagrams

- Components are drawn with standard **symbols**; the drawing is a **circuit diagram**.
- Cell symbol: **long line = +**, short line = − (battery = several cells in a row).
- LED symbol: the **triangle points the way the current can flow**; the two little arrows mean "light comes out".
- **Why symbols:** everyone worldwide draws the same shapes, so any circuit can be read by anyone — no need to draw realistic torches.

## Conductors & insulators

- **Conductors** let current pass: all **metals** (spoons, keys, coins, aluminium foil) and **pencil lead (graphite)**.
- **Insulators** block it: wood, plastic, glass, paper, wax, rubber, cork.
- Test with a **conduction tester** (cell + lamp + two free wire ends): touch the object between the free ends — **lamp glows → conductor**.
- **Why wires are metal wrapped in plastic:** the metal core *carries* the current; the plastic skin *stops* it leaking into your hand — a conductor for the job, an insulator for safety.
