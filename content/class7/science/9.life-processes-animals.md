# 9. Life processes in animals

<!-- NCERT Class 7 "Curiosity" Chapter 9 — Life Processes in Animals. -->

**Life processes** = the processes essential for survival: nutrition, respiration, circulation, excretion, reproduction.

## The alimentary canal — the food's journey

**Mouth → oesophagus (food pipe) → stomach → small intestine → large intestine → rectum → anus**, with the **liver** and **pancreas** as associated organs.

**Digestion** = breaking complex food components (carbohydrate, protein, fat) into **simpler forms** the body can use.

### Mouth

- Teeth crush and chew — the initial breakdown into fine pieces is **mechanical digestion**.
- **Saliva** contains a digestive juice that breaks **starch → sugar**, and moistens food so it's easy to swallow. The tongue mixes and pushes it back.
- **Why chapati or rice tastes sweet if you chew it long enough:** the starch is being turned into sugar in your mouth.

**The iodine test:** iodine turns **blue-black with starch**.
- Test tube A (plain boiled rice) → **blue-black** → starch present.
- Test tube B (rice chewed 30–60 s) → **no colour or only faint** → the starch has been broken into sugars by saliva.

### Oesophagus (food pipe)

- Its walls **contract and relax in a wave-like motion**, pushing food down to the stomach.
- This wave-like movement happens **throughout the whole alimentary canal** — which is why swallowing works even lying down.

### Stomach

- The walls **churn** the food, mixing it with secretions from the inner lining: **digestive juice, acid and mucus**.

| Secretion | Job |
|---|---|
| **Digestive juice** | breaks down **proteins** |
| **Acid** | helps break down proteins **and kills harmful bacteria** |
| **Mucus** | **protects the stomach lining from the acid** |

- The food leaves as a partially digested **semi-liquid** mass.

> [!NOTE]
> How we learned this: in 1822 **Alexis St. Martin** survived a gunshot to the stomach, but the wound left a permanent small hole. Dr **William Beaumont** used it to watch digestion happening — and even studied how emotions affect it.

### Small intestine — where absorption happens

- About **6 metres** long — the **longest** part of the alimentary canal.
- Receives digestive juices from **three** sources: its own lining, the **liver**, and the **pancreas**.
  - **Bile** (from the liver) is **mildly basic**: it **neutralises the acid** coming from the stomach and breaks fats into **tiny droplets** so they're easier to digest.
  - **Pancreatic juice** is also basic; it neutralises acid **and** breaks down carbohydrates, proteins and fats.
  - The intestine's own juice completes the breakdown of fats, proteins and remaining carbohydrates.
- **Absorption:** digested nutrients pass into the **blood** in the vessels of its walls.
- **Why it can absorb so much:** the inner lining has **thousands of finger-like projections**, which hugely **increase the surface area** — more surface touching the food means more nutrients absorbed.

> [!NOTE]
> **Celiac disease:** the body reacts to **gluten** (in wheat, barley, rye), damaging the small intestine's lining so it can't absorb properly. Managed by avoiding gluten — **millets (jowar, bajra, ragi) are naturally gluten-free**.

### Large intestine

- About **1.5 m** — **shorter** than the small intestine, but **wider**, which is why it's called "large".
- **Absorbs water and some salts** from the undigested food, making the waste **semi-solid** — this is **stool**.
- Stored in the **rectum**, then expelled through the anus — **egestion**.
- **Fibre-rich foods** (fruits, vegetables, whole grains) make stool easier to pass.

> [!TIP]
> The large intestine hosts helpful **bacteria** that break down fibre and produce essential nutrients. **Fermented foods** — curd, buttermilk, kanji, pickles, gundruk, poita bhat — support a healthy digestive system.

## Other animals digest differently

- **Ruminants** (cows, buffaloes): partially chew grass, swallow it, and later bring the partially digested food **back to the mouth** for thorough chewing — **rumination**. A cow spends about **8 hours a day** chewing.
- **Birds** have **no teeth**: food is ground in a chamber called the **gizzard**, whose walls contract and relax, often helped by **grit (small stones)** the bird swallows.

**The pattern:** animals' alimentary canals vary in structure and function as **adaptations** to the kind of food they eat.

## Breathing & respiration — not the same thing

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Breathing** = inhaling and exhaling air — a **physical** process.
> **Respiration** = using oxygen to break down glucose and release energy — a **chemical** process, inside the cells.
> Breathing supplies the oxygen; respiration spends it.

**glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy**

### The respiratory system

**Nostrils → nasal passages → windpipe → two branches → finer branches → alveoli**, inside the lungs, protected by the **rib cage**.

- **Tiny hair and mucus in the nostrils trap dust and dirt** — which is exactly why we should breathe **through the nose, not the mouth**.
- **Alveoli** are small balloon-like sacs with **thin walls surrounded by fine blood vessels** — this is where **gas exchange** happens: carbon dioxide leaves the blood into the alveoli, oxygen passes from the alveoli into the blood.

### The mechanism

| | Ribs | Diaphragm | Space in chest | Air |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Inhalation** | move **up and outwards** | moves **down** | increases | drawn **in** |
| **Exhalation** | move **down and inwards** | moves **up** | decreases | forced **out** |

- The **diaphragm** is the dome-shaped muscle below the lungs.
- **In the bottle model:** the **balloons = lungs**, the **rubber sheet = diaphragm**. Pull the sheet down → balloons inflate; release it → they deflate.

### What we breathe out

**Lime water test:** exhaled air (through a straw) turns lime water **milky**; ordinary air pushed in with a syringe does **not**. → **Exhaled air contains much more carbon dioxide.**

| | Oxygen | Carbon dioxide |
|---|---|---|
| **Inhaled air** | ~21% | ~0.04% |
| **Exhaled air** | ~16–17% | ~4–5% |

- Note that **not all the oxygen is used up** — which is why mouth-to-mouth resuscitation can work.

### Breathing in other animals

- **Lungs:** birds, elephants, lions, cows, goats, lizards, snakes — all have lungs, though of quite different structure.
- **Gills:** most aquatic animals such as fish — richly supplied with blood vessels, exchanging gases with the **gases dissolved in water**.
- **Frogs (amphibians):** **tadpoles use gills**; **adults use lungs** on land and **skin** for gas exchange in water.
- **Earthworms:** through their **moist skin**.

## Circulatory system

- **Heart + blood + blood vessels.**
- The heart **pumps blood** through the vessels, delivering **oxygen and nutrients** everywhere and **carrying waste away**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Smoking** damages the lungs and raises the risk of lung cancer and other respiratory illness. It also harms others through **passive smoking** — especially dangerous for children, pregnant women and the elderly.
