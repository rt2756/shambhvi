# 9. Life processes in animals

<!-- NCERT Class 7 "Curiosity" Chapter 9 — Life Processes in Animals.

> *"If your food is fully digested before you eat again, you won't need medicine for pain."* — **Thirukkural 942**

## What life processes are

**Life processes** are the processes every living thing needs to survive — **nutrition, respiration, circulation, excretion, reproduction** (Class 6). This chapter goes deep into two of them in animals: **nutrition (digestion)** and **respiration**, with a look at **circulation**.

Animals eat in very different ways: **bees and sunbirds** suck **nectar**; young humans and many animals drink their **mother's milk**; **pythons swallow** their prey whole; some water animals **filter** tiny floating food particles. But all of them get **energy** from food.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Food contains COMPLEX components — carbohydrates, proteins, fats.** The body can't use them as they are. They must first be **broken down into SIMPLER forms**.
>
> **DIGESTION** = breaking complex food components into simpler forms the body can use.

## The alimentary canal — the food's journey

Digestion happens in a **long tube, the alimentary canal**, which **starts at the mouth and ends at the anus**. Along the way, **digestive juices** break food down, and the simple products are **absorbed** and carried round the body.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Mouth → oesophagus (food pipe) → stomach → small intestine → large intestine → rectum → anus**
> **Associated organs:** the **liver** and the **pancreas** (food doesn't pass through them, but they pour juices in).

### 1. The mouth — digestion begins

- **Teeth crush and chew** food into fine pieces. This first breakdown is **mechanical digestion**.
- Your mouth waters when you think of your favourite food — that's **saliva** being released.
- **Saliva contains a digestive juice that breaks down STARCH into SUGAR.**
- Saliva also **moistens** food so it's soft and easy to swallow, and the **tongue mixes** it with saliva and pushes it back.

> [!TIP]
> **Chew a bit of chapati or boiled rice for 30–60 seconds — it starts tasting sweet.** Chapati and rice are full of **starch**; saliva turns some of it into **sugar** right in your mouth.

**Activity 9.1 — proving it with iodine.** (Iodine turns **blue-black with starch** — Class 6.)

| Test tube | Contents | After adding iodine | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | plain boiled rice + water | **blue-black** | lots of **starch** |
| **B** | rice **chewed 30–60 s** + water | **no change** or only **faint** blue-black | starch **broken into sugar** by saliva |

> [!NOTE]
> **Why test tube A matters:** it's the **control** — the same rice without saliva. Only by comparing A with B can we say the *saliva* made the difference. If B still turns quite dark, **chew for longer** and repeat.

> [!NOTE]
> **Keep your mouth healthy:** **brush your teeth and clean your tongue twice a day**, and **rinse your mouth after each meal** to prevent tooth decay and bad breath.

### 2. The oesophagus (food pipe)

A long **flexible tube** from the mouth to the stomach.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Its walls contract and relax in a WAVE-LIKE motion, pushing food down to the stomach.** This wave happens **all along the alimentary canal**, moving food forward.
>
> **Why this matters:** food isn't just falling down by gravity — the muscles push it. That's why you can **swallow even lying down** (or upside down!).

### 3. The stomach

The stomach's **walls contract and relax to churn** the food, mixing it with a **secretion from its inner lining** that contains three things:

| Secretion | Job |
|---|---|
| **Digestive juice** | breaks down **proteins** into simpler parts |
| **Acid** | helps break down proteins **and kills many harmful bacteria** |
| **Mucus** | **protects the stomach lining from the acid** |

The food leaves as a **partially digested, semi-liquid mass**.

> [!TIP]
> **Why the stomach doesn't digest itself:** the acid is strong enough to damage the stomach wall — the **mucus coating** is what keeps it safe.

> [!NOTE]
> **How we learned this.** In **1822, Alexis St. Martin** was accidentally **shot in the stomach**. Dr **William Beaumont** treated him, but the wound left a **small permanent hole**. Through it, Beaumont could **watch digestion happening** — he studied how different foods broke down, and even **how emotions affect digestion**.

### 4. The small intestine — where digestion finishes

- Although called "small", it's **about 6 metres long** — nearly **twice the height of your classroom** — the **longest part** of the alimentary canal. ("Small" refers to its **narrow** width.)
- It receives digestive juices from **three sources**:

| Source | Juice | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Liver** | **bile** — **mildly basic** | **neutralises the acid** coming from the stomach; breaks **fats into tiny droplets** so they're easier to digest |
| **Pancreas** | **pancreatic juice** — also **basic** | neutralises acid **and** breaks down **carbohydrates, proteins and fats** |
| **Wall of the small intestine** | intestinal juice | finishes breaking down fats, proteins and remaining carbohydrates |

> [!TIP]
> **Bile and pancreatic juice neutralising stomach acid is the neutralisation of Chapter 2 — happening inside you.** Acid from the stomach + bases from the liver and pancreas.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ABSORPTION** = the digested nutrients passing from the small intestine **into the blood** in vessels in its walls.
>
> **Why it absorbs so well:** its inner lining is **thin** and covered with **thousands of finger-like projections** (each about half a millimetre). They **increase the surface area** hugely — more surface touching the food means more nutrients pass into the blood.

The blood carries these nutrients round the body to **give energy, support growth and repair, and keep the body working**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Celiac disease:** the body reacts to **gluten**, a protein in **wheat, barley and rye**. This **damages the lining of the small intestine**, so nutrients can't be absorbed properly. It's managed only by **avoiding gluten** — and **millets (jowar, bajra, ragi)** are naturally **gluten-free**.

### 5. The large intestine

- About **1.5 m** long — **shorter** than the small intestine. It's called "large" because it's **wider**.
- It **absorbs water and some salts** from the **undigested** food, making the waste **semi-solid** — this is **stool**.
- Stool is stored in the **rectum** and then expelled through the **anus**. Getting rid of undigested waste is **egestion**.
- **Fibre-rich foods** (fruits, vegetables, whole grains) help stool **pass more easily**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Helpful bacteria** live in the large intestine. They **break down fibre** and **produce essential nutrients**. **Fermented foods** — **curd, buttermilk, shrikhand, kanji, pickles, gundruk, poita bhat** — support a healthy gut.
>
> The ancient Ayurvedic text ***Charaka Samhita*** stressed **easily digestible food** and spices like **ginger, black pepper and cumin** to aid digestion. Modern nutrition adds: **regular meal times, mindful eating, and not overeating**.

## Do all animals digest food the same way?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **No — the alimentary canal varies in structure and function as an ADAPTATION to the kind of food an animal eats.**

- **Ruminants — cows, buffaloes (and goats):** they **partly chew grass and swallow it** into a stomach chamber (the **rumen**), where it's **partly digested**. Later they **bring it back to the mouth** and chew it thoroughly — **rumination** ("chewing the cud"). Then it goes down again for further digestion. **A cow spends about 8 hours a day just chewing!**
  - **Why:** grass is tough and hard to digest, so it gets processed twice.
- **Birds have no teeth.** Food is ground in a muscular chamber, the **gizzard**, whose walls **contract and relax** — often helped by **grit (small stones)** the bird swallows.
  - **Why:** the grit does the job teeth would do.

## From food to energy — respiration

Some nutrients **build and repair** the body; others, like **sugar (glucose)**, are **broken down to release energy**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **RESPIRATION** = the process by which **nutrients are converted into usable energy**, using **oxygen**:
>
> **Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy**

### Breathing ≠ respiration

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **BREATHING** = **inhaling and exhaling air** — a **PHYSICAL** process. It **brings in oxygen and removes carbon dioxide**.
> - **RESPIRATION** = using that **oxygen to break down glucose and release energy** — a **CHEMICAL** process that happens **inside the body's cells**.
>
> Breathing **supplies** the oxygen; respiration **spends** it. The energy lets us **walk, run, play — and even think**.

> [!TIP]
> **Why breathing is so urgent:** we can survive about a week without food and a day or two without water — but only **a few minutes without breathing**.

## The human respiratory system

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Nostrils → nasal passages → windpipe → two branches (one to each lung) → finer and finer branches → ALVEOLI**
>
> The lungs are protected by the **rib cage**; below them is the **diaphragm**.

- **Tiny hairs and mucus** in the nostrils **trap dust and dirt**. **That's why we should breathe through the NOSE, not the mouth.**
- **Sneezing** when we breathe in dusty air is the body **forcing out** the dust that irritated the nose.
- Some tiny infectious particles still get through — e.g. the **SARS-CoV-2 virus** (COVID-19) attacked the respiratory system and caused serious lung problems.

### How we breathe in and out

| | **Ribs** | **Diaphragm** | **Space in chest** | **Air** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Inhalation** | move **up and outwards** | moves **down** | **increases** | **drawn in** |
| **Exhalation** | move **down and inwards** | moves **up** | **decreases** | **forced out** |

The **diaphragm** is a **dome-shaped muscle below the lungs**. Its job is to **help inhalation and exhalation**.

**Activity 9.2 — the bottle model:** a bottle with its bottom cut off, a **Y-tube** through the sealed lid with **two balloons** on its forked end, and a **rubber sheet** stretched over the open base.

- **Pull the sheet down → balloons inflate** (inhalation).
- **Let it go back up → balloons deflate** (exhalation).
- **Balloons = lungs · rubber sheet = diaphragm.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Breathing practices:** **pranayama** (better respiratory health, relaxation, concentration); **Tummo breathing** in **Ladakh** (helps keep the body warm in the cold); deep breathing with chanting; **box breathing**.

### What do we breathe out? (Activity 9.3)

- Air pushed into lime water with a **syringe/pichkari** (ordinary air) → **no change**.
- **Breathed-out** air blown in with a straw → **milky**.
- **So exhaled air contains much more carbon dioxide than inhaled air.**

| | Oxygen | Carbon dioxide |
|---|---|---|
| **Inhaled air** | nearly **21%** | nearly **0.04%** |
| **Exhaled air** | nearly **16–17%** | nearly **4–5%** |

> [!NOTE]
> **Not all the oxygen is used up** — exhaled air still has about 16–17%. (Some other animals use a larger share of it.)

> [!TIP]
> **"We inhale oxygen" is not quite right.** We inhale **air**, which is **rich in oxygen** (about 21%) — mostly it's other gases.

### Gas exchange in the alveoli

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ALVEOLI** are tiny **balloon-like sacs** with **thin walls**, surrounded by **fine blood vessels**.
> - **Carbon dioxide** carried by the blood from the body **passes into the alveoli** and is breathed out.
> - **Oxygen** from the alveoli **passes into the blood** and is carried to every part of the body.
>
> **Why thin walls and lots of blood vessels:** gases cross thin walls quickly, and many vessels mean lots of exchange at once.

> [!TIP]
> **Why one friend breathes faster than another after the same run:** they may be **less fit**, may have **run harder**, or simply have a body that needs **more oxygen** for the same effort — more energy needed means more respiration, which needs more oxygen.

## Circulation — the delivery system

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The CIRCULATORY SYSTEM = heart + blood + blood vessels.**
> The **heart pumps blood** through the vessels, **delivering nutrients and oxygen** to all parts of the body and **carrying waste away**.

Digestion, respiration and circulation **work together** — food gives glucose, lungs give oxygen, blood brings both to the cells, and respiration releases the energy. Other body systems also work in coordination (higher classes).

> [!WARNING]
> **Smoking** damages the lungs and raises the risk of **lung cancer** and other respiratory illness, with persistent coughing and infections. It also harms **others** — breathing someone else's smoke is **passive smoking**, especially dangerous for **children, pregnant women and the elderly**.

## Breathing in other animals

| Animal | Breathes through |
|---|---|
| Birds, elephants, lions, cows, goats, lizards, snakes | **lungs** (of quite different structures) |
| **Fish** and most water animals | **gills** — rich in blood vessels; exchange gases with the **oxygen dissolved in water** |
| **Frogs** (amphibians) | **tadpoles: gills**; **adults: lungs on land, skin in water** |
| **Earthworms** | **moist skin** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Different animals have different breathing mechanisms, each suited to their habitat.** Birds even fly at high altitudes where oxygen is low — their respiratory systems are specially adapted for it.

## Think-it-through questions

1. **Chapati in tube A, chewed chapati in tube B, boiled mashed potato in tube C, iodine added to all.** → **A and C turn blue-black** (starch present); **B shows little or no colour** (saliva broke the starch down).
2. **Yadu mixes rice flour in water in two tubes, adds saliva to B, waits 35–45 min, then adds iodine.** → He's testing whether **saliva breaks down starch**. A turns blue-black; B doesn't.
3. **Rakshita passes inhaled air into lime water in tube A and blows exhaled air into tube B.** → She's testing whether **exhaled air has more carbon dioxide**. B turns milky; repeating the test confirms it.
4. **Anil says breathing and respiration are the same.** → Ask him: *"Where does respiration happen? Is any new substance made in breathing?"* Breathing just moves air (physical); respiration happens in cells and makes CO₂, water and energy (chemical).
5. **Match: nostrils, nasal passages, windpipe, alveoli, rib cage.** → fresh air enters · hairs and mucus trap dust · carries air to the lungs · gas exchange · protects the lungs.

## In a nutshell

- **Life processes:** nutrition, respiration, circulation, excretion, reproduction.
- **Alimentary canal:** mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine → anus, with **liver** and **pancreas**.
- **Saliva: starch → sugar.** **Stomach:** juice + acid + mucus. **Small intestine** (~6 m): bile, pancreatic juice, intestinal juice; **absorption** via finger-like projections. **Large intestine** (~1.5 m): absorbs water → stool → **egestion**.
- **Ruminants** chew the cud; **birds** grind food in a **gizzard**.
- **Breathing** (physical) vs **respiration** (chemical): glucose + oxygen → CO₂ + water + energy.
- **Inhalation:** ribs up and out, diaphragm down. **Exhalation:** the reverse.
- Gas exchange in the **alveoli**; exhaled air turns lime water milky.
- **Circulatory system:** heart, blood, blood vessels.
- **Lungs, gills, skin** — breathing suits the habitat.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** "small" intestine is the longest · bile is basic, not acidic · we inhale air, not pure oxygen · not all inhaled oxygen is used · breathing is physical, respiration chemical · diaphragm moves DOWN when we breathe in.
