## Fireflies and moonlight

**Keshav**, a city boy, spends his holidays in **Jatin's grandparents' village in the Western Ghats of Maharashtra**. The most magical sight: **hundreds of fireflies** flashing at night. Fireflies are **seasonal insects** that **use light to communicate** — but their numbers are **falling** because of **light pollution, shrinking forests and too much tourism**.

On the bus home, Keshav watches the **moonlit** hills and the **beams of headlights**, and wonders:

- **Does the Moon make its own light?** (Class 6 said everything else in the solar system shines by reflecting sunlight.)
- And why do the headlight beams look like **straight lines**?

## Sources of light — luminous and non-luminous

- **The Sun** gives out its own light — the **main source of natural light on Earth**. **Stars, lightning, natural fire and some animals** (fireflies) do too.
- Long ago, humans learnt to make **fire** — the **earliest artificial light** — using fuels like **animal fat, oil, wax and gas**. Now most lighting is **electric**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **LUMINOUS** objects **emit their own light** — the Sun, stars, fire, a lit bulb, a firefly.
> - **NON-LUMINOUS** objects **don't** — we see them only by the light they **reflect**.
>
> **The Moon is non-luminous** — moonlight is **sunlight reflected** off it. **Planets (Mars, Venus) are non-luminous too.** A **star** (the Pole Star, the Sun) is luminous. A **mirror** is non-luminous — it only reflects.

> [!NOTE]
> **LED lamps** use **much less power**, are **brighter**, and **last longer** than older lamps — cutting electricity bills and helping the environment. The government has promoted them across India. At the end of their life they must be **disposed of or recycled properly**, not thrown in the garbage.

> [!NOTE]
> **Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes 20 seconds to reach Earth.** If the Sun suddenly stopped shining, we wouldn't know for 8 minutes 20 seconds!

## Light travels in a straight line

**Activity 11.1 — three matchboxes.** Make a hole in the **same spot** in three matchbox trays. Line them up so the holes are at the **same height in a straight line**, shine a torch through, and you get a **bright spot** on a cardboard screen.

- Move **any one** box slightly — sideways or up/down — and **the spot disappears**.

**Activity 11.2 — a candle and a pipe.** You can see a candle flame through a **straight** flexible pipe — but **not** when the pipe is **bent**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Light travels in a straight line.**
>
> **Why these experiments prove it:** if light could curve, it would still wriggle through the misaligned holes or round the bend in the pipe. It doesn't — so it must travel straight.

> [!NOTE]
> **A laser beam** shone through water with **a drop of milk** in it (the milk makes the beam visible) also goes **straight**. (In higher classes you'll learn that light can bend around corners in special situations.)
>
> ⚠️ **Never point a laser at anyone's eyes** — it can cause serious eye damage. Use only a low-power pointer, under supervision.

## Transparent, translucent, opaque

**Activity 11.3:** shine a torch on a screen and put different materials in the way — **predict first, then observe**.

| Material type | Examples | Light passes… | You can see through it… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Transparent** | clear glass, clear plastic, water | **almost completely** | clearly |
| **Translucent** | tracing paper, butter paper, thin cloth, frosted glass | **partially** | dimly |
| **Opaque** | cardboard, wood, metal, thick cloth | **not at all** | not at all |

## Shadows

When an **opaque** object is placed in the path of light, a **dark patch** appears on the screen.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A SHADOW is the dark patch where light does not reach**, because an object **blocked** it. It forms **because light travels in straight lines** — it can't bend round the object to fill in the space behind.

| Object | Shadow |
|---|---|
| **Opaque** | **darkest** |
| **Translucent** | **lighter** |
| **Transparent** | **faint** (some) |

**Activity 11.4 — what controls a shadow?**

| Change | What happens to the shadow |
|---|---|
| **remove the screen** | **no shadow seen** (nothing to fall on) |
| **remove the object** | **no shadow** (nothing blocks the light) |
| **switch off the torch** | **no shadow** (no light to block) |
| object moved **closer to the SCREEN** | shadow gets **smaller** (and sharper) |
| object moved **closer to the TORCH** | shadow gets **bigger** |
| **tilt** the object | shadow's **shape changes** |
| change the object's **colour** | **no change** — the shadow stays dark |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **To see a shadow you need THREE things: a source of light, an opaque object, and a screen.** (Walls, floors and the ground act as screens in everyday life.)
>
> - The **shape, size and sharpness** of a shadow depend on the **positions** of the object, light source and screen.
> - A shadow **may or may not** tell you what the object is.
> - **Changing the colour of an opaque object does NOT change the colour of its shadow** — a shadow is the **absence of light**, not a picture of the object.

> [!TIP]
> **Why a bird high in the sky casts no shadow you can see on the ground, but one swooping low does:** a high bird is tiny compared to the distance, so its shadow is spread out and blurred into almost nothing; near the ground it blocks a clear patch of light.
>
> **Two torches** on one side of a ball → **two shadows**.

> [!TIP]
> **Your shadow is shortest at noon (12 PM)** — the Sun is highest overhead. At **9 AM and 4 PM** the Sun is lower, so shadows are **longer**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Shadow puppetry** — flat cut-out puppets between a lamp and a screen — is centuries old in India, used for entertainment and to share messages: **Charma Bahuli Natya** (Maharashtra), **Keelu Bomme and Tholu Bommalata** (Andhra Pradesh), **Togalu Gombeyaata** (Karnataka), **Ravana Chhaya** (Odisha), **Tholpavakoothu** (Kerala), **Bommalattam** (Tamil Nadu).

## Reflection of light

A student notices that a **shiny steel plate** makes a shadow — **and also** throws a **bright spot** onto the opposite wall. Why?

**Activity 11.5:** outdoors, let sunlight fall on a **plane mirror** (a flat mirror) or a shiny plate and turn it — you can **send a spot of light onto a wall the Sun isn't reaching**. Tilt the mirror and the spot **moves**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **REFLECTION OF LIGHT** = the **change in direction of light** when it falls on a **mirror or shiny surface**.
>
> Light still travels in **straight lines** — the mirror just **sends it off in a new direction**.

**Activity 11.6 — seeing a light beam reflect:** cover all but **one gap of a comb** with black paper to make a **thin slit**. Hold the comb upright on white paper and shine a torch through — a **thin beam** shows on the paper. Put a mirror in its path: the beam **changes direction** at the mirror.

## Images in a plane mirror

When you look in a mirror, what you see is an **image** — light from you reflected back to your eyes. The thing in front of the mirror is the **object**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The image formed by a plane mirror is:**
> 1. **the same SIZE** as the object (Activity 11.7 — move a pen around; its image never changes size)
> 2. **ERECT** (upright — the pen's tip stays on top)
> 3. **NOT obtainable on a screen** (put a screen behind or in front of the mirror — no image appears on it)
> 4. **LATERALLY INVERTED** — left and right appear swapped
>
> And the image appears **as far behind the mirror as the object is in front** (Activity 11.8 — step closer, your image comes closer).

- **Lateral inversion:** **raise your left arm** — your image raises its **right**. Touch your **right ear** — the image touches its **left**.
- **Why "AMBULANCE" is written mirror-reversed** on the front of ambulances: the driver ahead sees it in the **rear-view mirror**, which reverses it back so it reads **AMBULANCE**.
- **Write your name and hold it up to a mirror** → the letters appear reversed left-to-right.
- Letters like **A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y** look **the same** in a mirror (they're symmetrical left-to-right). So "images of T and O look identical to themselves" is **true**, and so is "the image is laterally inverted".

> [!TIP]
> **Can a small mirror show a whole tree?** Yes — if you stand back far enough. The mirror doesn't have to be as big as the object; light from the whole tree can reach your eye through a small piece of mirror.

> [!NOTE]
> **Two mirrors at an angle, or facing each other,** make **many images** — reflections of reflections.

> [!NOTE]
> Nobody knows exactly when mirrors were invented. Early mirrors were **polished stone or metal**. When glass mirrors spread, the craft of metal mirrors was mostly lost — but it survives in **Kerala** as the **Aranmula Kannadi**, a metal mirror made there for centuries.

## The pinhole camera

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A PINHOLE CAMERA** = a device in which light from an object passes through a **tiny hole** and forms an **image on a screen**.

**Activity 11.9:** in a dim room, a **lit candle**, a **cardboard with a small hole**, and a **screen** beyond it → an image of the flame appears on the screen — **upside down (inverted)**!

**Activity 11.10 — build one:** two **cardboard boxes**, one sliding inside the other. Make a **pinhole** in the middle of one face of the bigger box. On the smaller box, cut a **5–6 cm square** and cover it with **tracing paper** (the screen); slide it in with the tracing paper inside. Point the pinhole at a **tree or building in bright sunlight**, cover your head and the camera with a **dark cloth**, and slide the inner box until the image is sharp.

- The image is **inverted** and shows the object's **real colours**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why the image is upside down:** light travels in **straight lines**. A ray from the **top** of the tree passes through the pinhole and keeps going **downward** to the **bottom** of the screen; a ray from the **bottom** goes **upward** to the **top**. The rays **cross at the pinhole**, so the picture is flipped.

> [!TIP]
> **Mirror vs pinhole camera:**
> - **Plane mirror:** image is **erect** but **laterally inverted** (left ↔ right).
> - **Pinhole camera:** image is **upside down** (inverted).
> Different causes, different results.

## Things to build

### Periscope

**Two plane mirrors fixed in a Z-shaped box**, each tilted to face the other. Light enters the top opening, **reflects off the first mirror down the tube**, then **off the second mirror into your eye**.

- **Why it works:** two reflections carry light round the "corners" — so you see things **not directly visible**.
- **Uses:** **submarines**, **tanks**, soldiers looking out of **bunkers** — or you, looking over **taller friends** in a crowd.

### Kaleidoscope

**Three equal mirror strips** (or shiny reflective paper) joined in a **triangle**, fixed inside a **tube**. One end: a transparent sheet with **broken coloured bangle pieces or beads**, covered with tracing paper. Look through the other end — a beautiful pattern.

- **Why so many images:** three mirrors make **reflections of reflections**, multiplying the pieces into a symmetrical pattern.
- **Turn it and the pattern changes** every time. Designers and artists use kaleidoscopes for **pattern ideas**. Even with both ends open, pointed at a tree, it makes patterns.

## Think-it-through questions

1. **Which are luminous: Mars, Moon, Pole Star, Sun, Venus, mirror?** → Only the **Pole Star and the Sun** (both stars).
2. **Match:** pinhole camera → **forms an inverted image**; opaque object → **blocks light completely**; transparent object → **light passes almost completely**; shadow → **the dark region behind the object**.
3. **Ball and torch:** ball close to the **torch** → **larger** shadow; ball close to the **wall** → **smaller** shadow; ball removed → **a bright spot** on the screen; two torches → **two shadows**.
4. **Who sees the candle through the pipe?** → Only the person looking through a **straight** pipe lined up with the flame.

## In a nutshell

- **Luminous** objects emit their own light; **non-luminous** ones (like the Moon) reflect it.
- **Light travels in a straight line.**
- **Transparent** — light passes almost completely; **translucent** — partly; **opaque** — not at all.
- A **shadow** forms where light is blocked: needs **light source + opaque object + screen**; darkest for opaque, lighter for translucent, faint for some transparent objects; **colour of object doesn't change shadow colour**.
- **Reflection** = change of direction of light at a mirror.
- **Plane mirror image:** same size, erect, not on a screen, laterally inverted, as far behind as the object is in front.
- **Pinhole camera:** inverted image.
- **Periscope:** two mirrors, Z-shaped box. **Kaleidoscope:** three mirrors in a triangle.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** the Moon and planets are non-luminous · shadow colour never changes · closer to the light = bigger shadow · mirror image is erect but swapped left-right · pinhole image is upside down · never aim a laser at eyes.
