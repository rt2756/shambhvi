# 11. Light: shadows & reflections

<!-- NCERT Class 7 "Curiosity" Chapter 11 — Light: Shadows and Reflections. -->

## Luminous & non-luminous

- **Luminous:** objects that **emit their own light** — the Sun, other stars, lightning, fire, fireflies, electric lamps.
- **Non-luminous:** objects that **don't** — they're seen only by the light they **reflect**.
- **The Moon is non-luminous.** "Moonlight" is just **reflected sunlight** — the same is true of all planets.

> [!NOTE]
> **LED lamps** use much less power, are brighter, and last longer than older lamps. At end of life they must be **properly disposed of or recycled**, not thrown in the garbage.

## Light travels in a straight line

Two experiments, same conclusion:

- **Three matchboxes** with holes at the same height: line them up and a light spot appears on the screen. Move **any one** box sideways or up and down → **the spot disappears**.
- **A candle through a pipe:** visible through a **straight** pipe, **invisible** through a **bent** one.

**Why this proves it:** if light could curve, it would still find its way through the misaligned holes or round the bend. Only straight-line travel explains why breaking the straight path breaks the beam.

> [!WARNING]
> **Never point a laser at anyone's eyes** — it can cause serious eye damage. Use only a low-power pointer, under supervision.

## Transparent, translucent & opaque

| Material | Light passes | Shadow |
|---|---|---|
| **Transparent** (glass, clear plastic) | almost **completely** | faint, if any |
| **Translucent** (tracing paper, thin cloth) | **partially** | lighter |
| **Opaque** (cardboard, wood, metal) | **not at all** | **darkest** |

## Shadows

**A shadow is the dark patch where light does not reach**, because an object blocked it.

**Three things are needed:** a **source of light**, an **object** (blocking the light), and a **screen** (wall, floor, ground) to catch it. Remove any one and there's no shadow to see.

- The **shape, size and sharpness** depend on the **positions** of object, light source and screen. Moving the object closer to the light makes the shadow **bigger**; closer to the screen makes it **smaller and sharper**.
- A shadow may or may not reveal what the object is — tilt the object and the shape can change completely.
- **Changing the colour of the object does NOT change the colour of its shadow.** A shadow is *absence of light*, not a picture of the object — so it has no colour of its own.

> [!NOTE]
> **Shadow puppetry** is a centuries-old Indian art: flat cut-out puppets between a lamp and a screen. Regional styles include **Charma Bahuli Natya** (Maharashtra), **Tholu Bommalata** (Andhra Pradesh), **Togalu Gombeyaata** (Karnataka), **Ravana Chhaya** (Odisha), **Tholpavakoothu** (Kerala) and **Bommalattam** (Tamil Nadu).

## Reflection

**Reflection** = the **change in direction of light** when it falls on a shiny surface or mirror.

- A mirror in sunlight can throw a bright spot onto a wall the Sun isn't reaching — and tilting the mirror moves the spot. Light still travels straight; the mirror simply **sends it a new way**.
- Shown neatly with a **comb experiment**: block all but one gap of a comb to get a thin beam on paper, then place a mirror in its path — the beam's path visibly bends at the mirror.

## Image in a plane mirror — four properties

1. **Same size** as the object.
2. **Erect** (upright).
3. **Cannot be obtained on a screen** — it only *appears* to be behind the mirror.
4. **Laterally inverted** — left and right are swapped.

- The image appears **as far behind** the mirror as the object is in front: stand closer, the image comes closer.
- **Lateral inversion:** raise your left arm and the image raises its right. This is why **"AMBULANCE" is painted reversed** on the front of an ambulance — in the rear-view mirror of the car ahead, it reads the right way round.

> [!NOTE]
> Before glass mirrors, mirrors were made by polishing stone or metal. That craft survives in Kerala as the **Aranmula Kannadi**, a metal-surface mirror made for centuries.

## Pinhole camera

Light from an object passes through a **tiny hole** and forms an image on a screen.

- **The image is inverted (upside down)** — and it shows the object's **colours**.
- **Why upside down:** light travels in **straight lines**, so a ray from the **top** of the object goes through the pinhole and continues **downward** to the bottom of the screen, while a ray from the bottom crosses **upward**. The rays cross at the hole, flipping the picture.

> [!TIP]
> **Mirror vs pinhole camera:** a mirror image is **laterally inverted but erect**; a pinhole-camera image is **upside down**. Different causes, different results.

## Two things to build

- **Periscope** — two plane mirrors in a **Z-shaped box**. Reflection from two mirrors lets you see objects **not directly visible** — used in submarines, tanks, and for looking over a crowd.
- **Kaleidoscope** — three mirror strips joined in a **triangle** inside a tube, with coloured bangle pieces at one end. **Reflections of reflections** make many images, so every turn gives a **new pattern**. Designers use them for pattern ideas.
