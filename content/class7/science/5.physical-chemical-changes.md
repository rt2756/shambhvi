# 5. Physical & chemical changes

<!-- NCERT Class 7 "Curiosity" Chapter 5 — Changes Around Us: Physical and Chemical. -->

## The one question that separates them

**Is a new substance formed?** That's the whole test.

- **Physical change:** only **physical properties** change — shape, size, state. **No new substance.**
  - Folding paper, inflating a balloon, crushing chalk to powder, melting ice, boiling water, chopping vegetables.
- **Chemical change:** **one or more new substances** are formed, through a **chemical reaction** (written as a chemical equation).
  - Rusting, burning, curdling milk, cooking.

> [!WARNING]
> **Don't use "can I undo it?" as the test.** Crushing chalk can't be undone but is *physical*; ice melting can be undone and is also physical. Reversibility is a **separate** question from new-substance-or-not.

## Lime water — the carbon dioxide detector

- Blow through a straw into **tap water** → only bubbles, no change → **physical**.
- Blow into **lime water** → turns **milky**, a white solid settles → **new substance** → **chemical**.

**calcium hydroxide (lime water) + carbon dioxide → calcium carbonate + water**

- The white insoluble **calcium carbonate** is what makes it look milky.
- So **"lime water turns milky" is the standard test for carbon dioxide.**
- **Vinegar + baking soda** → fizzing; pass the gas into lime water → milky → the gas is **carbon dioxide**, so this is a chemical change too.

## Combustion & the fire triangle

**Combustion** = a substance reacts with **oxygen**, producing **heat and/or light**. Substances that do this are **combustible** (fuel): wood, paper, cotton, kerosene.

**magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide + heat + light**

**Three things are needed — remove any one and the fire dies:**

1. **Fuel** (a combustible substance)
2. **Oxygen**
3. **Heat** — enough to reach the **ignition temperature**

- **Ignition temperature** = the **lowest temperature at which a substance catches fire**.
- **Why paper sits safely in air but burns under a magnifying glass:** fuel and oxygen were always there — the missing leg was *heat*. Focused sunrays raise the paper past its ignition temperature and it burns, with no flame ever touching it. A lit matchstick works instantly because it's already hotter than that temperature.
- **Candle under a glass tumbler goes out:** the oxygen leg is cut off. (Lime water in the dish turns milky, proving carbon dioxide formed from the wax's carbon + air's oxygen.)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **If clothes catch fire:** wrap the person in a **blanket or thick cloth** — it cuts off the **air** (oxygen leg of the triangle).
> ⚠️ **Never a synthetic blanket** — it can melt and stick to the skin.

## A burning candle is both at once

- Wax **melts**, flows, **solidifies**, and **evaporates** → **physical** changes.
- The wax **vapour burns** → **chemical** change.

So one everyday process can carry both kinds of change together.

> [!NOTE]
> **Michael Faraday** gave a famous lecture series, *The Chemical History of a Candle* — he thought the candle was the perfect object for teaching science, exactly because melting, vaporisation and combustion all happen in one small flame.

## Three ways to sort changes

- **New substance?** → physical vs chemical.
- **Can it be reversed?** → ice melting **yes**; popcorn, chopped vegetables **no**.
- **Is it wanted?** → **desirable** (milk → curd, fruit ripening, cooking) vs **undesirable** (rusting, food decay).
  - The same change can flip: food decaying in storage is undesirable, but decomposing food waste into **compost** is desirable. It depends on the situation, not the chemistry.

## Slow natural changes

- **Weathering** — the breaking down of rocks in place, both kinds together:
  - **Physical:** temperature changes, growing tree roots, and water **freezing in cracks** split rocks into **sediments**.
  - **Chemical:** water and chemicals react with the rock — black **basalt** contains iron, which becomes **iron oxide**, leaving a **red** layer.
  - Weathering eventually forms **soil**.
- **Erosion** — the **moving away** of broken rock, soil and sediment by **wind and flowing water**; a **physical** change. It's why river pebbles are smooth.
  - Where the water or wind slows (a lake, the ocean), the material **settles** and hardens over thousands of years into new rock — not reversible.

> [!TIP]
> **Weathering breaks it, erosion carries it.** That one line keeps the two apart.

> [!NOTE]
> **Fireflies** make light by a chemical change **without heat** — called **bioluminescence**. A reminder that "chemical change" doesn't always mean burning.
