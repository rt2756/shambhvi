## Plants don't eat — so how do they grow?

Animals grow by eating food. As a plant grows, **new leaves and branches appear, it gets taller, and its stem thickens** — but nobody has ever seen a plant eat. Students suggest guesses: *water helps* · *sunlight plays a role* · *plants take food from the soil through their roots*. Science tests guesses with experiments.

Animals get food from plants — **directly** (eating plants) or **indirectly** (eating animals that ate plants). Plants are different: **they MAKE their own food.**

## Experiment 1: do plants need sunlight and water?

**Activity 10.1** — three pots of the **same soil**, each with a **similar-sized sapling** of a fast-growing plant (chilli or tomato). Count the leaves, then watch for **two weeks**:

| Pot | Sunlight | Water | After two weeks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | ✅ direct sunlight | ✅ kept moist | **grows best** — taller, more green leaves |
| **B** | ✅ direct sunlight | ✗ no water | may **die** |
| **C** | ✗ kept in the dark | ✅ kept moist | **poor growth** (leaves may turn yellow) |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Plants need BOTH sunlight AND water to grow.**
>
> **Why the experiment can be trusted:** the three pots are **identical in every way except one condition**. B lacks water only; C lacks light only. So any difference must be caused by that missing condition. (Changing two things at once would make the result impossible to read.)

> [!NOTE]
> ***Vrikshayurveda*** — an ancient Indian text on plants — says *"Trees do not produce fruits and flowers merely because they are planted."* It recorded practical observations on **plant growth, soil and farming**, including ways to make **organic manure** (mixing water, barley and seeds such as green, black and horse gram).

## Leaves: the food factories

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Plants store food as STARCH** (a carbohydrate). The starch is made in the **leaves**, which are generally **broad and flat** and **green** because of the pigment **CHLOROPHYLL**, which **captures sunlight**.
>
> **Why broad and flat:** a wide, thin leaf **catches as much sunlight as possible** — the more light captured, the more food made.

### The iodine test for starch in a leaf (Activity 10.2 — teacher demonstration)

1. **Boil** the leaf in water for **5 minutes** to soften it.
2. Put it in a test tube of **alcohol**, standing in a beaker of **boiling water**, until the leaf turns **colourless**.
3. Rinse it, lay it on a plate, and add **dilute iodine**.
4. **Blue-black = starch is present.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why remove the green colour first?** The leaf's own green would **hide** the colour change. A pale leaf makes the blue-black easy to see.

> [!WARNING]
> **Alcohol catches fire very easily** — never put it directly on a flame. That's why it's heated **inside a beaker of hot water**.

## What does a leaf need to make food?

### Sunlight and chlorophyll (Activity 10.3)

**Bhaskar**, a curious gardener, takes leaves with **green and non-green (white/yellow) patches** from two similar potted plants — one kept **in sunlight**, one kept **in the dark for 36 hours**. He traces the patches first, then does the iodine test.

| Plant | Green patches | Non-green patches |
|---|---|---|
| **In sunlight** | **blue-black** — starch | no colour change |
| **In the dark** | **no colour change** | no colour change |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - The **dark** plant made **no starch anywhere** → **sunlight is needed**.
> - In the sunlit plant, **only the green patches** made starch → **chlorophyll is needed**.
>
> **Chlorophyll helps make starch in the presence of sunlight** — so leaves are called the **"food factories"** of plants.

> [!NOTE]
> **Do the non-green patches have *no* chlorophyll?** Not necessarily — they may have **too little** to make enough starch for the iodine test to show.
>
> **Red, violet or brown leaves** have so much of those coloured pigments that they **hide the green chlorophyll**. Many still make food (some of those pigments help too) — the iodine test proves it.

### Carbon dioxide (Activity 10.4 — teacher demonstration)

1. Keep a potted plant in the **dark for 2–3 days** so it **destarches** (uses up its stored starch).
2. Put **caustic soda (sodium hydroxide)** in a wide-mouthed bottle — it **absorbs carbon dioxide** from the air.
3. Push **half of one leaf** into the bottle through a **split cork**; the other half stays **outside**.
4. Leave in **sunlight** for a few hours, then test the leaf with iodine.

| Part of leaf | Water | Sunlight | Chlorophyll | Carbon dioxide | Starch? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **inside** the bottle | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✗ (absorbed) | **no** |
| **outside** the bottle | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | **yes — blue-black** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Carbon dioxide from the air is essential for making food.** The two halves of the **same leaf** had everything else in common — only CO₂ was missing inside.
>
> **Why destarch first:** otherwise any **old stored starch** would turn blue-black and we couldn't tell whether **new** starch had been made.

> [!WARNING]
> **Caustic soda** is a strong chemical that **burns skin** — only teachers should handle it.

### Oxygen is given out (Activity 10.5)

**Barkha didi's set-up:** a **water plant** under an upside-down **funnel** in a beaker of water, with a **test tube full of water** inverted over the funnel's neck.

- In **sunlight (A)**: **gas bubbles** rise and collect in the test tube.
- In the **dark (B)**: no bubbles.
- She lifts the tube off with her **thumb over the mouth** and pushes in a **glowing/lit matchstick** → it burns with an **intense flame** → the gas is **rich in OXYGEN**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Oxygen is released during food-making — and only in sunlight.**

## Photosynthesis — putting it together

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **PHOTOSYNTHESIS** = the process by which plants **prepare food** using **carbon dioxide and water**, in the presence of **sunlight and chlorophyll**, and **release oxygen**:
>
> **Carbon dioxide + Water —(sunlight, chlorophyll)→ Glucose + Oxygen**
>
> - The food is first made as **GLUCOSE** — a simple carbohydrate that is an **instant energy source**. Extra glucose is later turned into **STARCH** for storage.
> - The **leaf** is the main site, but **every green part** with chlorophyll (green stems, for example) can photosynthesise.

> [!TIP]
> **Where does the starch in a potato come from?** It was made as food in the **leaves** by photosynthesis, carried down through the **phloem**, and **stored** in the potato (an underground stem).

> [!NOTE]
> **Rustom Hormusji Dastur (1896–1961)** — head of the **Botany Department** at the **Royal Institute of Science, Bombay** (now the Institute of Science, Mumbai) from **1921–1935** — studied how **water, temperature and the colour of light** affect photosynthesis.

## Stomata — how leaves swap gases

**Activity 10.6 (teacher demo):** peel a **thin layer from the lower surface** of a leaf (rhoeo, money plant, onion, hibiscus, coleus or grass), put it on a slide in water with a **drop of ink**, cover with a coverslip and look under a **microscope**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> You see **tiny pores — STOMATA** — on the leaf surface (mostly the lower one).
> They let gases **in and out**: **carbon dioxide in and oxygen out** during photosynthesis, and the reverse during respiration.

## Transport in plants — two separate pipe systems

Plants need water for photosynthesis. **Roots take in water along with minerals** from the soil (minerals are important nutrients for growth).

**Activity 10.7 — the red-ink experiment:** two tender twigs with **white flowers** (white sadabahar or balsam). Cut their stems **at a slant, under water**, and put one in plain water (A) and one in water with **red ink** (B). Next day:

- Twig **B** shows **red in the stem, leaves and flowers**; twig A doesn't.
- Cut B's stem **above** the water line and look with a magnifying glass — the **red is inside**, in thin tubes.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> | Tube | Carries | Direction |
> |---|---|---|
> | **XYLEM** | **water and minerals** | from the **roots up** to the stem, branches, leaves and flowers |
> | **PHLOEM** | **food** made in the leaves | from the **leaves to all parts** — including **seeds and roots**, where it may be stored |
>
> The red ink travels up the xylem exactly as **dissolved minerals** do.

> [!TIP]
> **X**ylem carries **water** up · **Ph**loem carries **food** (**ph** for *phood*) everywhere.
>
> **Why cut the stems under water and at a slant?** Cutting under water stops **air bubbles** blocking the xylem; a slanted cut gives a **bigger opening** for water to enter.

> [!NOTE]
> **To test whether water moves faster when warm or cold:** set up two identical red-ink twigs, keep one tumbler **warm** and the other **cold**, and compare how far the red has climbed after the same time — changing only the temperature.

## Do plants respire?

**Activity 10.8 (teacher demo):** soak **moong seeds** overnight and put them on **wet cotton** in a flask sealed with a cork. Leave it **in the dark for 24 hours**, then lead the air from the flask into **lime water**. Compare with a second tube of lime water not connected to the flask.

- The lime water connected to the flask **turns milky**; the other doesn't.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why:** the germinating seeds **respired** and **released carbon dioxide**, adding to the tiny amount normally in air.
>
> **Plant respiration: Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy**
>
> - The energy is used for the plant's **growth and development**.
> - **ALL parts of a plant — green or non-green — respire, all the time.**
> - **Why keep the flask in the DARK?** In light, green parts might photosynthesise and use up the CO₂, hiding the result. (The seeds have no chlorophyll anyway, but darkness keeps the test clean.)

> [!NOTE]
> **Kamala Sohonie (1911–1998)** earned a **PhD from Cambridge** for her work on **respiration in plants**. In India she worked at **Lady Hardinge Medical College (New Delhi)**, the **Nutrition Research Laboratory (Coonoor)**, and became **Director** of the Royal Institute of Science, Bombay. Her work improved the **nutritive value of plant foods**, including **Neera**, a nutritious drink from coconut-palm sap.

## Photosynthesis vs respiration

| | **Photosynthesis** | **Respiration** |
|---|---|---|
| **Raw materials** | carbon dioxide + water | glucose + oxygen |
| **Products** | glucose + oxygen | carbon dioxide + water + **energy** |
| **Needs** | **sunlight + chlorophyll** | — |
| **Where** | **green parts only** | **every living part** |
| **When** | only in **light** | **all the time**, day and night |
| **Importance** | **makes food**; adds oxygen to air | **releases energy** from food |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **They are opposites that balance each other.** Photosynthesis uses CO₂ and gives O₂; respiration uses O₂ and gives CO₂. Together they keep the gases in the air in balance.
>
> **A sealed bottle garden** (spider plant or jade plant) keeps growing because the plant **reuses its own gases** — CO₂ from respiration feeds photosynthesis, and O₂ from photosynthesis feeds respiration.

> [!WARNING]
> **If every photosynthesising organism disappeared**, there'd be **no new food** for any living thing (all food chains start with plants) and **no oxygen added** to the air. Life would collapse.

## Think-it-through questions

1. **X + Y → carbon dioxide + Z + energy. What are X, Y, Z?** → **X = glucose, Y = oxygen, Z = water** (respiration).
2. **Krishna keeps one pot in sunlight and one in a dark room.** → She's testing whether **sunlight** is needed. The dark plant looks **pale/yellow and weak**; only the **sunlit** plant's leaves turn blue-black with iodine.
3. **Vani's four plants: sun + CO₂, sun without CO₂, dark + CO₂, dark without CO₂.** → **Starch and oxygen only in "sunlight with CO₂"**; none in the other three.
4. **Ananya: test tubes with a snail, a water plant, both, and only water, plus a CO₂ indicator.** → She's finding out **who adds and who uses carbon dioxide**. The **snail** tube shows more CO₂; the **plant** tube (in light) shows less; **snail + plant** stays more balanced; **water alone** shows no change (the control).

## In a nutshell

- Plants need **sunlight and water** to grow.
- **Leaves** are the **food factories**; **chlorophyll** captures sunlight; food is stored as **starch**.
- **Photosynthesis:** CO₂ + water → (sunlight, chlorophyll) → **glucose + oxygen**.
- **Stomata** let gases in and out.
- **Xylem:** water and minerals **up from roots**. **Phloem:** food **from leaves** to all parts.
- **Respiration** goes on in **all parts, all the time**: glucose + oxygen → CO₂ + water + energy.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** destarch before the CO₂ test · decolourise before iodine · alcohol never on a direct flame · non-green patches may have a little chlorophyll · plants respire at night too · xylem = water, phloem = food.
