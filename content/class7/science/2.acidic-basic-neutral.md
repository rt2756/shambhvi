# 2. Acidic, basic & neutral substances

## The three families

- Every substance around us is **acidic**, **basic**, or **neutral**.
- **Acidic** (contain acids, taste **sour**): lemon juice, amla juice, tamarind water, vinegar.
- **Basic** (taste **bitter**, feel **soapy/slippery**): soap solution, baking soda solution, lime water, washing powder solution.
- **Neutral** (neither): tap water, sugar solution, salt solution.

> [!WARNING]
> Never taste or touch unknown substances to test them — that's what **indicators** are for. And bitter ≠ basic: bitter gourd (karela) is bitter but **not** basic.

## Litmus — the main indicator

- **Litmus** comes from **lichens** (a fungus + alga living together); used as paper strips or solution, in two colours.
- **Acid turns BLUE litmus RED** · **base turns RED litmus BLUE** · **neutral changes neither**.
- A substance that shows different colours in acids and bases is called an **acid–base indicator** — the colour *tells* you the nature without tasting or touching.

> [!TIP]
> Memory hook: **B**lue→**R**ed = **A**cid ("**BRA**very") — and the other pair is then automatic: red→blue = base.

## Other natural indicators

- **Red rose extract:** turns a shade of **red in acids**, **green in bases**, no change in neutral. (Crush petals in hot water, filter — the filtrate is the indicator.)
- **Turmeric (haldi):** yellow paper that turns **red with bases** — but stays yellow with acids *and* neutrals, so it **cannot separate acidic from neutral**.
  - This is why a curry (haldi) stain on a white shirt turns **red with soap** — soap is a base!
- Beetroot, purple cabbage, jamun and red hibiscus also work as indicators.
- **Olfactory indicators** change **smell** instead of colour: cloth kept with onion loses its onion smell in a **base** (baking soda solution) but keeps it in an acid.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Invisible-ink trick (the science-fair welcome!):** write with **soap solution** on paper (invisible), spray with **turmeric solution** → the writing appears **red**, because the base turns turmeric red.

> [!NOTE]
> **Hydrangea flowers** read the *soil* like an indicator: **acidic soil → blue** flowers, **basic soil → pink/red**.

## Acids in everyday foods

- Lemon → **citric acid** · amla → **ascorbic acid (Vitamin C)** · tamarind → **tartaric acid** · vinegar → **acetic acid** · curd → **lactic acid**.
- All taste sour — sourness is the tongue's acid detector.

## Neutralisation — acid + base cancel out

- Mixing an acid with enough base gives a solution that is **neither acidic nor basic**:
- **Acid + Base → Salt + Water + Heat** (heat is *released* — the mixture warms slightly).
- **Why "cancel":** the base destroys the acid's effect (and vice versa) — seen in the litmus test when red slowly turns back to blue as lime water is added to lemon juice.

## Neutralisation in daily life

- **Ant bite / bee sting:** the ant injects **formic acid** → rub **moist baking soda** (a base) to neutralise the sting.
- **Acidic soil** (from too much chemical fertiliser) → plants grow badly → farmers add **lime** (a base).
- **Basic soil** → add **organic matter / manure** — it releases acids that neutralise the base.
- **Acidic factory waste** → neutralise with **basic substances** before releasing into lakes, or the fish die.
- The pattern is always the same: **too much of one nature → add the opposite nature**.
