## Why living things need a growing-up stage

A plant's life starts with a **seed germinating**. It becomes a **sapling**, then a young plant, growing taller, adding leaves, and **only later** making **flowers, fruits and new seeds**. A seedling **cannot** make seeds right after it sprouts — it must first **grow and mature**.

Animals are the same. Some lay **eggs** that hatch; others, like humans, give **birth** to young ones. Either way, the young must **grow and mature before they can reproduce**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The stages of human life:** **infancy → childhood → adolescence → adulthood → old age.**
>
> - Until about **10–12 years**, most changes are just **height and weight**.
> - After that, other changes begin — the start of **ADOLESCENCE**, roughly **ages 10 to 19**: a period of **rapid growth and development** when the body **prepares for adulthood**.
> - Each person passes through these stages **at their own pace**, and how long each lasts **varies**.

**Why adolescence exists:** like the seedling, a human body must grow and mature before it is able to reproduce. Adolescence is that preparation — with **physical, emotional and behavioural** changes, some easy to see and some happening **inside** the body.

## The changes you can see

**Venkatesh** visits his grandparents. His 12-year-old cousin **Devyani** says, *"You have a thin moustache now! Your voice has changed!"* He replies, *"You've become so tall!"* Both are noticing adolescence.

**Activity 6.1** — every student writes, **without names**, the changes they've seen between Grades 5 and 8. The most common:

### 1. Height

- Growth in height happens **from birth**, but it becomes **most rapid during adolescence** — the "growth spurt".

### 2. Body shape, weight and strength

- **Boys:** gain weight, get **broader shoulders** and a **wider chest**.
- **Girls:** change in height and weight, and **breasts develop**.

### 3. Voice

- The **voice box (larynx)** is the structure in the throat that helps us speak. In adolescence it **grows**.
- In **boys** it grows **larger**, so the voice sounds **hoarse / deeper**, and the growth may show as a **bump in the throat — the Adam's apple**. (It's **not noticeable in everyone**.)
- In **girls** the voice box grows too, but **less**, so the voice changes only **slightly**.

### 4. Hair

- **Both** boys and girls grow hair in the **armpits** and **pubic region**.
- **Boys** often grow **facial hair** (later a moustache and beard); some grow **chest and back hair** — and **some have very little hair growth at all**.

### 5. Skin — acne

- Glands in the skin make **more oil** in adolescence. This oil can **clog skin pores**, which can then get **infected** — showing as small **reddish pimples**, usually on the face.
- **Acne** is the **condition**; **pimples** are **how it shows**.

> [!TIP]
> **To get relief from pimples:** wash the face gently and regularly to remove the extra oil, avoid squeezing them (that spreads infection), eat well and drink water. If they're severe, see a doctor.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The key point of the whole chapter:** the **timing, nature and extent** of these changes **vary from person to person — and every variation is completely normal.** Each person goes through adolescence **at their own pace**.

## Secondary sexual characteristics and puberty

Some of these changes — **voice change, facial and chest hair in boys, breast development in girls** — are **not themselves part of reproduction**, but they **help tell males from females**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SECONDARY SEXUAL CHARACTERISTICS** = features that **distinguish males from females** but are **not directly involved in reproduction**. They are **natural signs that the body is preparing for adulthood** and mark the **start of puberty**.
>
> **PUBERTY** = the stage in which an adolescent's body goes through **external and internal changes** to become an adult **capable of reproduction**.

## Changes inside — reproductive maturity

Not every change can be seen. Inside the body, the **parts involved in reproduction mature** gradually, in both boys and girls.

### The menstrual cycle

- An important internal change in adolescent girls is the start of the **menstrual cycle**, commonly called **"the period"**.
- It usually repeats every **28–30 days**. In many healthy girls it is **shorter or longer — anywhere from 21 to 35 days** — and that's normal.
- **Menstruation** is the phase of the cycle when **blood is discharged** from the body. It usually lasts **3 to 7 days**. Some girls feel **pain or discomfort in the lower abdomen** during these days.
- It is a **sign of good reproductive health**.
- It stops naturally at about **45–55 years**, marking the end of a woman's reproductive years.

> [!WARNING]
> **Breaking myths about menstruation.** Many wrong beliefs cause **unnecessary fear, shame or guilt** — for example **keeping menstruating girls physically isolated**. **Menstruation is a natural process, and such myths have no scientific basis.** A scientific outlook helps society support **women's reproductive health** and healthier lives.

## Emotional and behavioural changes

Adolescence often brings **stronger emotions than childhood** — exciting, confusing, or both.

| Emotional change | Possible effect | A positive direction |
|---|---|---|
| **Mood swings** | energetic and happy one day, low the next | music, dance, **sports** |
| **Strong emotions** | intense reactions; deeper interest in new things | **self-exploration** → creativity and innovation |
| **Increased sensitivity** | feeling others' problems strongly | **compassion**, **social work**, helping the disadvantaged |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why it helps to understand this:** once you know that emotions **drive** behaviour, you can **pause and choose your response** thoughtfully, instead of just reacting.

> [!WARNING]
> **"Adolescents don't need to worry about behavioural changes" is not right.** Changes in behaviour are normal — but noticing them is what lets you handle them well. **"Adolescence is only physical changes" is also wrong** — it is **physical, emotional and behavioural**.

## Making adolescence healthy and joyful

### 1. Nutrition

Growth needs fuel and building material — so a **balanced, nutritious diet** matters most now. Adolescents need **proteins and carbohydrates**, plus enough **fats, vitamins and minerals**.

| Food sources | Nutrients | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Milk, **millets**, curd, cheese, paneer | **calcium**, proteins, fats | **bone growth** |
| Pulses, eggs, nuts, beans | **proteins** | **growth, strength, energy** |
| **Spinach, kidney beans, raisins, figs** | **iron** | **formation of blood** |

- A shortage of **iron or vitamin B12** can cause **blood-related problems (anaemia)** — especially common in **adolescent girls**. Iron deficiency is managed by **iron-rich foods** and, when a doctor advises, **iron tablets**.
- **Vitamin B12 cannot be made by the human body** — like most vitamins, it must come **from food** (mainly milk, curd, eggs and other animal foods).

> [!NOTE]
> **Dorothy Hodgkin** worked out the **structure of vitamin B12**. In **1964** she became the **third woman** to win the **Nobel Prize in Chemistry**.

> [!TIP]
> **Mary (11) and Manoj (12) both get a bump on the front of the neck.** Mary's doctor prescribes an **iodine-rich diet** — her swelling is **goitre**, from too little iodine (Class 6). Manoj's doctor says it's **part of growing up** — it's his **Adam's apple**. Same place, different causes.

### 2. Personal hygiene

- Keep the body clean, **especially the armpits and pubic region**, to prevent infections (more sweat and oil now).
- **Menstrual hygiene:** use **sanitary pads** or **reusable cloth pads** made for the purpose.
- **Dispose of used pads properly — wrapped in newspaper, inside a dustbin** — for **community health** and a clean environment. **Biodegradable pads** are environment-friendly.
- Schools and public places should have proper facilities, and we should all help **remove the stigma** around sanitary pads.

> [!NOTE]
> **Government initiatives for menstrual hygiene:**
> - **Menstrual Hygiene Scheme (MHS)** — free or low-cost sanitary pads for adolescent girls in **rural areas**, plus awareness programmes.
> - **Rashtriya Kishor Swasthya Karyakram (RKSK)** — overall adolescent health, including menstrual health; uses **peer education** (older students teach younger ones).
> - **Suvidha** — **biodegradable** sanitary pads at low prices through **Jan Aushadhi Kendras**.
> - **State schemes** — e.g. **Shuchi** in **Karnataka**; free pads in government schools in **Tamil Nadu and Odisha**.

### 3. Physical activity

Regular **exercise, games and sports** keep **body and mind** fit, build **stamina** and **boost mood**. (**21 June is International Yoga Day.**)

### 4. A balanced social life — in person and online

- Be **polite and respectful** — it makes a safe, friendly environment.
- Adolescents often **feel drawn to peers and copy their behaviour** — so think about whom you copy.
- Online platforms are great for information and connection, but use them **responsibly**:

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| be respectful and kind | share personal photos with strangers / "virtual friends" |
| **think before posting** | share your personal information online |
| **protect your privacy** | forward rumours |
| ask parents or teachers for guidance | — |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **CYBERBULLYING** = using phones, computers or online platforms to **harass** someone — **misleading messages, false rumours, or sharing personal information without consent**.
>
> **If it happens to you: don't feel scared or helpless.** Handle it wisely and **get help from parents and teachers**.

### 5. Saying NO to harmful substances

Some people — even friends — may **tempt, persuade, force or pressure** you to try **tobacco, gutka, cigarettes, beedi, alcohol or illegal drugs**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> These substances harm **body and mind** — and they are **ADDICTIVE**: after starting, a person feels a **strong urge to take them again and again**, until they use them regularly. This is **SUBSTANCE ABUSE**.
>
> **Say "NO" the first time — and every time.** *Someone who is addicted today might have started with "just one time".*

- Damage can include **breathing difficulty, memory loss and lung damage** (smoke-blackened lungs).
- **Why adolescents are especially at risk:** this stage is full of **curiosity and excitement**, and the wish to fit in — exactly what makes pressure powerful. Knowing that in advance makes it easier to stay firm.
- **"I can try it once and stop any time" is false** — that's how addiction begins.
- **Getting out of addiction:** seek help from **family, friends, parents or teachers**; **counselling and medical advice** help.

> [!NOTE]
> **Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan** — launched by the **Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment** to spread awareness against substance abuse, with special focus on **young children**. **National De-addiction Helpline: 14446.**

## The "why" question — hormones

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why do these changes happen now?** Mainly because of **HORMONES** — **chemicals produced in our body**.
>
> - They're made in **different parts of the body** and released **at the right time** in response to **signals from the brain**.
> - They control **growth and development** — including **menstruation and the other signs of puberty**.
> - **Some hormones also affect mood and behaviour** — part of the reason for mood swings.

## In a nutshell

- **Adolescence** — about **10 to 19 years** — is the change from childhood to adulthood: **physical, biological, emotional** changes.
- **Secondary sexual characteristics** distinguish males and females but aren't directly part of reproduction; they mark the onset of **puberty**.
- Girls begin the **menstrual cycle** (~28–30 days, 21–35 normal; bleeding 3–7 days; stops at 45–55).
- **Balanced diet, hygiene, exercise, responsible social life** keep adolescents healthy.
- **Say NO** to addictive substances.
- The changes are controlled by **hormones**.

> **Top traps:** variations are normal · Adam's apple isn't seen in everyone · acne is the condition, pimples the sign · menstruation myths have no scientific basis · "just once" is how addiction starts.
