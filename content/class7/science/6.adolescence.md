# 6. Adolescence: growth & change

<!-- NCERT Class 7 "Curiosity" Chapter 6 — Adolescence: A Stage of Growth and Change. -->

## The stages of life

- **Infancy → childhood → adolescence → adulthood → old age.**
- Until about **10–12 years**, most changes are just **height and weight**. After that, other changes begin — the start of **adolescence**, roughly **ages 10 to 19**.
- **Why this stage exists:** no living thing can reproduce right after birth — a seed must grow into a mature plant before it can make seeds of its own. Adolescence is the body's preparation period for adulthood.

## Puberty & secondary sexual characteristics

- **Puberty** = the stage when the body undergoes external and internal changes to develop into an adult **capable of reproduction**.
- **Secondary sexual characteristics** = changes that **distinguish males from females but are not themselves part of reproduction**: voice change, facial/chest hair in boys, breast development in girls.
  - They are the visible **signal** that puberty has begun.

**Common changes:**

- **Height** — growth is continuous from birth, but becomes most rapid during adolescence.
- **Body structure** — boys: weight gain, broader shoulders and chest. Girls: changes in height and weight, development of breasts.
- **Voice** — the **voice box (larynx)** grows. In boys it grows larger, giving a hoarse/deeper voice and sometimes a visible bump called the **Adam's apple**. It grows in girls too, but less, so the change is slight.
- **Body hair** — in both, hair grows in the armpits and pubic region; boys often develop facial hair.
- **Acne** — increased **oily secretions** clog skin pores and lead to infection, showing as pimples. (**Acne** is the condition, **pimples** are how it shows.)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The timing, nature and extent of all these changes vary from person to person — and every variation is completely normal.** Some boys have little hair growth; some people never show an Adam's apple. Each person moves through adolescence at their own pace.

## The menstrual cycle

- An internal change in adolescent girls: the cycle recurs about every **28–30 days**, though anywhere from **21–35 days** is common and healthy.
- **Menstruation** = the phase when blood discharge occurs, usually lasting **3 to 7 days**. Some discomfort or pain in the lower abdomen during these days is common.
- It is a **sign of good reproductive health**, and stops naturally at about **45–55 years**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myths and taboos around menstruation have no scientific basis.** Practices like isolating menstruating girls cause needless fear, shame or guilt. Menstruation is a normal, healthy, natural process — a scientific outlook is what supports women's health.

## Emotional & behavioural changes

- Adolescence brings **stronger emotions** than childhood — mood swings and heightened sensitivity are normal.
- Channelled well, these become strengths: **creativity** (music, dance, sport, self-exploration) and **compassion** (social work, helping the disadvantaged).
- **Why it helps to understand this:** knowing that emotions drive behaviour lets you pause and **choose your response** instead of just reacting.

## Staying well in adolescence

### Nutrition

Growth needs fuel and building material, so a **balanced diet** matters most at this stage.

| Food | Nutrient | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Milk, curd, cheese, paneer, millets | calcium, protein, fats | **bone growth** |
| Pulses, eggs, nuts | **protein** | growth, strength, energy |
| Spinach, kidney beans, raisins, figs | **iron** | **formation of blood** |

- **Iron or vitamin B12 deficiency** can cause blood-related problems (anaemia), especially in adolescent girls.
- **Vitamin B12 cannot be made by the body** — it must come from food.

> [!NOTE]
> **Dorothy Hodgkin** worked out the structure of **vitamin B12**, and in **1964** became the **third woman** to win the Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

### Personal hygiene

- Keep the body clean, especially armpits and pubic region, to prevent infections.
- **Menstrual hygiene:** use sanitary pads or reusable cloth pads. Dispose of used pads by **wrapping in newspaper and putting in a dustbin** — for community health and cleanliness. **Biodegradable pads** are environment-friendly.
- Government schemes support this: **Menstrual Hygiene Scheme (MHS)**, **Rashtriya Kishor Swasthya Karyakram (RKSK)**, the **Suvidha** biodegradable-napkin initiative through Jan Aushadhi Kendras, and state schemes such as Karnataka's **Shuchi**.

### Physical activity

Regular exercise and games keep body and mind fit, build **stamina**, and **boost mood**.

### A balanced social life

- Be polite and respectful — it creates a safe environment for everyone.
- **Online too:** think before posting, protect privacy, never share personal photos or information with strangers.
- **Cyberbullying** = using phones, computers or online platforms to harass — misleading messages, false rumours, sharing someone's personal information without consent.
  - **If it happens to you: don't feel scared or helpless — tell parents or teachers and handle it with their help.**

### Saying NO to harmful substances

- Tobacco, gutka, cigarettes, beedi, alcohol and illegal drugs damage **physical and mental health**.
- They are **addictive** — after starting, a person feels a strong urge to take them again and again, leading to regular use. This is **substance abuse**.
- **Why adolescents are especially at risk:** this is a stage of curiosity, excitement and wanting to fit in with peers — which is exactly what makes peer pressure powerful. Knowing that in advance is what makes it easier to say no.
