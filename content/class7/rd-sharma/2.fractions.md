# 2. Fractions

## Kinds of fractions

**Example:** a pizza cut into 8 slices, you eat 3 → you ate **3/8**. The bottom (**denominator**) says how many equal parts; the top (**numerator**) says how many you take.

| Kind | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Proper | top < bottom (less than 1) | 3/8 |
| Improper | top ≥ bottom (1 or more) | 11/4 |
| Mixed | whole + proper fraction | 2 ¾ |
| Like | same denominator | 2/7, 5/7 |
| Unlike | different denominators | 1/3, 1/4 |
| Equivalent | same value | 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 |

- **Mixed → improper:** 2 ¾ = (2 × 4 + 3)/4 = **11/4**. **Why:** each whole is 4 quarters, so 2 wholes = 8 quarters, plus 3 = 11 quarters.
- **Improper → mixed:** 17/5 → 17 ÷ 5 = 3 remainder 2 → **3 ⅖**.
- **Equivalent fractions:** multiply or divide top and bottom by the **same** number. **Why:** that's the same as multiplying by 2/2 or 3/3, which is 1 — so the value doesn't change, you just cut the slices finer.
- **Lowest terms:** divide top and bottom by their HCF: 18/24 → ÷6 → **3/4**.

## Comparing fractions

- **Same denominator:** bigger top wins. 5/9 > 4/9.
- **Same numerator:** smaller bottom wins. 3/5 > 3/8. **Why:** fewer slices means each slice is bigger.
- **Otherwise:** make the denominators equal using the LCM, or cross-multiply.
  - 2/3 vs 3/5 → 2 × 5 = 10 and 3 × 3 = 9 → 10 > 9, so **2/3 > 3/5**.

**Why cross-multiplying works:** it is a shortcut for writing both over 15: 2/3 = 10/15 and 3/5 = 9/15. You just compare the new tops.

## Adding and subtracting

**Example:** 1/4 + 2/3. Quarters and thirds are different-sized slices, so first cut both into twelfths: 3/12 + 8/12 = **11/12**.

- **Rule:** make the denominators equal (use the LCM), then add or subtract the numerators. **Keep the denominator.**
- **Mixed numbers:** turn into improper first, or add wholes and parts separately: 2 ½ + 1 ⅓ = 3 + (3/6 + 2/6) = **3 ⅚**.

> [!WARNING]
> 1/4 + 2/3 is **not** 3/7. You can only add slices of the same size, and you never add the denominators.

## Multiplying

**Example:** 2/3 × 3/4. "Of" means ×, so this is 2/3 **of** 3/4. Answer = (2 × 3)/(3 × 4) = 6/12 = **1/2**.

- **Rule:** top × top, bottom × bottom. Cancel common factors first to keep numbers small.
- **Whole × fraction:** 5 × 2/7 = 10/7 = 1 3/7.

**Why it works:** take 3/4 of a bar. Now take 2/3 of that. You've split the bar into 3 × 4 = 12 small pieces and kept 2 × 3 = 6 of them.

- Multiplying by a **proper** fraction makes a number **smaller** (½ of 10 = 5). That surprises people — "times" doesn't always mean "more".

## Reciprocal and dividing

- **Reciprocal** = flip the fraction: reciprocal of 3/5 is 5/3; of 4 is 1/4. A number × its reciprocal = **1**. 0 has no reciprocal.
- **Rule:** to divide, **multiply by the reciprocal**: 3/4 ÷ 2/5 = 3/4 × 5/2 = **15/8** = 1 7/8.

**Why it works:** 6 ÷ 1/2 asks "how many halves fit in 6?" Each whole holds 2 halves, so 6 × 2 = 12. Dividing by 1/2 = multiplying by 2 — the flip.

## Word problems

**Example:** A ribbon of 7 ½ m is cut into pieces of 1 ¼ m. How many pieces? 15/2 ÷ 5/4 = 15/2 × 4/5 = 60/10 = **6 pieces**.

> [!TIP]
> "of" → multiply · "how many … in …" or "shared equally" → divide · "more/total" → add · "left/difference" → subtract.

## Quick practice

1. Write 4 ⅔ as an improper fraction.
2. Reduce 36/48 to lowest terms.
3. Which is bigger, 5/7 or 7/10?
4. 3/8 + 5/12 = ?
5. 2 ½ − 1 ¾ = ?
6. 4/9 of 27 = ?
7. 5/6 ÷ 10/9 = ?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. (4 × 3 + 2)/3 = **14/3**
2. HCF 12 → **3/4**
3. 5 × 10 = 50 vs 7 × 7 = 49 → **5/7** is bigger
4. 9/24 + 10/24 = **19/24**
5. 5/2 − 7/4 = 10/4 − 7/4 = **3/4**
6. 27 ÷ 9 × 4 = **12**
7. 5/6 × 9/10 = 45/60 = **3/4**
