# 17. Constructions

Tools: ruler, compasses, protractor. Each construction below is followed by **why** it works — most come from congruence (Chapter 16).

## A line parallel to a given line through a point

**Steps** (line l, point P not on it):
1. Draw any line through P crossing l at Q.
2. With centre Q, draw an arc cutting l and PQ.
3. With the **same radius** and centre P, draw an arc cutting PQ.
4. Copy the opening of the first arc onto the second (measure the gap with compasses).
5. Join P to the new point and extend. That line is parallel to l.

**Why it works:** you copied the angle at Q to P, in the **alternate** (or corresponding) position. Equal alternate angles mean the lines are parallel (Chapter 14).

## Triangle from three sides (SSS)

**Example:** AB = 5 cm, BC = 6 cm, AC = 4 cm.
1. Draw BC = 6 cm.
2. Centre B, radius 5 cm → arc. Centre C, radius 4 cm → arc.
3. The arcs cross at A. Join AB and AC.

**Why it works:** every point on the first arc is 5 cm from B, every point on the second is 4 cm from C. Where they cross is the one point that is both.

> [!TIP]
> Check the triangle inequality first: 5 + 4 > 6 ✓. If it fails, the arcs won't meet.

## Triangle from two sides and the angle between (SAS)

**Example:** PQ = 5 cm, PR = 4 cm, ∠P = 60°.
1. Draw PQ = 5 cm.
2. At P, draw a 60° angle with the protractor (or compasses).
3. Mark R on that arm with PR = 4 cm. Join QR.

**Why it works:** the two arms and the angle between them are fixed, so the gap QR is fixed too (SAS).

## Triangle from two angles and the side between (ASA)

**Example:** XY = 6 cm, ∠X = 45°, ∠Y = 75°.
1. Draw XY = 6 cm.
2. Draw 45° at X and 75° at Y on the same side.
3. The arms meet at Z.

**Why it works:** each angle fixes a direction; two lines meet at just one point.

> [!WARNING]
> If the two angles add to 180° or more, the arms never meet — no triangle.

## Right triangle from hypotenuse and one side (RHS)

**Example:** hypotenuse 5 cm, one side 3 cm.
1. Draw the side BC = 3 cm. At C, draw a 90° angle.
2. Centre B, radius 5 cm → arc cutting the 90° arm at A. Join AB.

**Why it works:** the right angle and two lengths fix the triangle (RHS) — and Pythagoras says CA must be 4 cm.

## Quick practice

1. Which congruence test is behind the parallel-line construction?
2. Can you construct a triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm, 8 cm? Why?
3. Can you construct a triangle with ∠A = 100°, ∠B = 90°? Why?
4. Which construction needs only ruler and compasses (no protractor)?
5. For ASA with ∠X = 50°, ∠Y = 60°, what will ∠Z measure?
6. In the RHS example (hyp 5, side 3), how long is the third side?
7. In SAS, why must the angle be **between** the two sides?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **SSS** — the equal arcs make two triangles with all three sides equal, so the copied angle is equal. Equal alternate angles then make the lines parallel.
2. **No** — 3 + 4 = 7 < 8
3. **No** — 100 + 90 = 190 > 180
4. **SSS**
5. **70°**
6. **4 cm**
7. Otherwise (SSA) two different triangles can sometimes be drawn.
