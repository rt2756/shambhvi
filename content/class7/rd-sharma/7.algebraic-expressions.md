# 7. Algebraic Expressions

## Variables, constants, expressions

**Example:** a box holds x pencils. 3 boxes and 5 loose pencils = **3x + 5** pencils.

- A **variable** is a letter for a number that can change (x, y, n). A **constant** has a fixed value (5, −7, ½).
- An **expression** joins variables and constants with + − × ÷: 3x + 5, 2a² − ab, 7.

**Why letters:** one expression covers every case at once. 3x + 5 tells you the count for 2 pencils per box, 10 per box — any x.

## Terms (the sign belongs to the term)

- **Terms** are the parts joined by + or −. In 4x² − 3xy + 7, the terms are **4x², −3xy, 7**.

**Why the sign goes with it:** 4x² − 3xy + 7 = 4x² + (−3xy) + 7. Subtracting is adding a negative, so the − is part of the term.

> [!WARNING]
> In 5 − 2y, the term is **−2y**, not 2y.

## Factors and coefficients

- A term is a **product of factors**: −3xy = −3 × x × y.
- The **numerical coefficient** is the number part: in −3xy it's **−3**. In x² it's **1**; in −ab it's **−1**.
- The coefficient of x in 5xy is **5y** (everything else multiplying x).

## Like and unlike terms

| Terms | Like? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 3x, −7x | ✓ | same letter, same power |
| 2ab, 5ba | ✓ | ab = ba |
| 4x, 4x² | ✗ | powers differ |
| 3xy, 3x | ✗ | letters differ |

- **Like terms** have exactly the same letters with the same powers. Only the numbers in front may differ.

## Monomial, binomial, trinomial, polynomial

| Name | Number of terms | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Monomial | 1 | −5xy |
| Binomial | 2 | a + 3 |
| Trinomial | 3 | x² + 2x + 1 |
| Polynomial | 1 or more | x³ − x² + x − 4 |

## Adding and subtracting

**Example:** (3x² − 2x + 5) + (x² + 4x − 8) → group like terms: (3 + 1)x² + (−2 + 4)x + (5 − 8) = **4x² + 2x − 3**.

- **Rule:** only like terms combine. Add their coefficients, keep the letters.
- **Subtracting a bracket:** change **every** sign inside it, then add.
  - (5a − 3b) − (2a − 4b) = 5a − 3b − 2a + 4b = **3a + b**.

**Why only like terms:** 3 apples + 2 apples = 5 apples, but 3 apples + 2 bananas stays as it is. 3x + 2x = 5x, but 3x + 2y can't be shortened.

**Why every sign flips:** −(2a − 4b) means −1 × (2a − 4b). The distributive law multiplies **each** term by −1.

> [!WARNING]
> x + x = 2x, but x × x = x². And 3x + 2y is **not** 5xy.

## Value of an expression

**Example:** find 2x² − 3x + 1 at x = −2: 2(4) − 3(−2) + 1 = 8 + 6 + 1 = **15**.

- **Rule:** put the number in **brackets** in place of the letter, then follow BODMAS.

**Why brackets:** without them, −2² looks like −4, but (−2)² = 4. The bracket keeps the minus attached.

## Patterns as expressions

- 3, 5, 7, 9, … goes up by 2 → nth term is **2n + 1**. Check n = 1: 3 ✓.
- Matchsticks for n squares in a row: **3n + 1**.

**Why 2n + 1:** "goes up by 2 each time" gives the 2n; the +1 fixes the starting value.

## Quick practice

1. List the terms of 7a − 4ab + b² − 9.
2. Coefficient of y² in −6xy²?
3. Pick the like terms: 3pq, −5p, 4qp, p², −pq.
4. Add 4x − 3y + 2 and −x + 5y − 7.
5. Subtract 2m² − 3m + 4 from 5m² + m − 1.
6. Find a² + 2ab + b² at a = 3, b = −1.
7. Write the nth term of 4, 7, 10, 13, …

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **7a, −4ab, b², −9**
2. **−6x**
3. **3pq, 4qp, −pq**
4. **3x + 2y − 5**
5. 5m² + m − 1 − 2m² + 3m − 4 = **3m² + 4m − 5**
6. 9 − 6 + 1 = **4**
7. **3n + 1**
