# 19. Visualising Solid Shapes

## Flat vs solid

- **2-D (plane) shapes** have length and breadth: square, circle, triangle.
- **3-D (solid) shapes** also have height: cube, cuboid, cylinder, cone, sphere, pyramid.

## Faces, edges, vertices

- **Face:** a flat surface · **Edge:** where two faces meet · **Vertex:** a corner where edges meet.

| Solid | Faces (F) | Vertices (V) | Edges (E) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cube | 6 | 8 | 12 |
| Cuboid | 6 | 8 | 12 |
| Triangular prism | 5 | 6 | 9 |
| Square pyramid | 5 | 5 | 8 |
| Triangular pyramid (tetrahedron) | 4 | 4 | 6 |
| Cylinder | 2 flat + 1 curved | 0 | 2 (curved) |
| Cone | 1 flat + 1 curved | 1 | 1 (curved) |
| Sphere | 1 curved | 0 | 0 |

- **Euler's formula** (for solids with flat faces): **F + V − E = 2**. Cube: 6 + 8 − 12 = 2 ✓.

**Why it's useful:** it's a check. If you count a solid and get something other than 2, you've miscounted. (The full proof comes in higher classes.)

> [!TIP]
> Count edges of a prism as: (sides of base) × 3. Triangle base → 9 edges. Pyramid: (sides of base) × 2. Square base → 8.

## Nets

- A **net** is a flat pattern that folds up into a solid.
- A cube has 11 different nets — all made of 6 squares. A "cross" shape is the easiest one.
- Cylinder net: 2 circles + 1 rectangle. **Why a rectangle:** unroll the curved surface; its length is the circle's circumference.
- Cone net: 1 circle + a sector (a fan shape).

> [!WARNING]
> Six squares in a row (or any layout with 4 squares meeting at one corner) will **not** fold into a cube — faces overlap.

## Drawing solids

- **Oblique sketch:** draw the front face at its true shape, then slanting lines for depth. Quick, but not to scale.
- **Isometric sketch:** drawn on dotted isometric paper; lengths are kept true.

## Views and cross-sections

- A solid looks different from the **front, side and top**. A cylinder: front view = rectangle, top view = circle.
- **Cross-section:** the flat face you get when you cut a solid. A horizontal cut of a cone → circle; a vertical cut through its tip → triangle.
- **Shadow play:** a cone's shadow can be a circle (light from above) or a triangle (light from the side).

## Quick practice

1. Faces, vertices and edges of a cuboid?
2. Check Euler's formula for a triangular prism.
3. A solid has 7 faces and 10 vertices. How many edges?
4. Which solid has 1 vertex and 1 curved surface?
5. What two shapes make the net of a cylinder?
6. Top view of a cone standing on its base?
7. Edges of a hexagonal prism?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **6, 8, 12**
2. 5 + 6 − 9 = **2** ✓
3. 7 + 10 − E = 2 → **E = 15**
4. **Cone**
5. **2 circles and a rectangle**
6. **A circle** (with a dot at the centre for the tip)
7. 6 × 3 = **18**
