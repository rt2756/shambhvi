# 14. Lines and Angles

## The basic words

- **Line:** goes on forever both ways. **Ray:** starts at a point, goes on one way. **Line segment:** has two end points.
- An **angle** is made by two rays from one point (the **vertex**). It measures the **turn** from one ray to the other.
- A full turn = 360°, a straight line = 180°, a right angle = 90°.

| Angle | Size |
|---|---|
| Acute | less than 90° |
| Right | exactly 90° |
| Obtuse | between 90° and 180° |
| Straight | exactly 180° |
| Reflex | between 180° and 360° |

## Pairs of angles

| Pair | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Complementary** | add to **90°** | 35° and 55° |
| **Supplementary** | add to **180°** | 110° and 70° |
| **Adjacent** | share a vertex and an arm, don't overlap | ∠AOB and ∠BOC |
| **Linear pair** | adjacent **and** together make a straight line → add to **180°** | 120° and 60° on a line |
| **Vertically opposite** | opposite each other where two lines cross → **equal** | |

- Complement of x = 90° − x · supplement of x = 180° − x.

**Why a linear pair adds to 180°:** the two angles together fill a straight line, and a straight line is half a turn = 180°.

**Why vertically opposite angles are equal:** two lines cross making angles a, b, c (a and c opposite). a + b = 180° (linear pair) and b + c = 180° (linear pair). Both equal 180°, so a = c.

> [!WARNING]
> **Complementary → Corner** (90°), **Supplementary → Straight** (180°). The C and S help you not mix them up.

## Parallel lines and a transversal

A **transversal** is a line crossing two other lines. When the two lines are **parallel**:

| Angle pair | Where | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Corresponding** | same position at each crossing (both "top-left") | **equal** |
| **Alternate interior** | between the lines, opposite sides of the transversal (a Z shape) | **equal** |
| **Alternate exterior** | outside the lines, opposite sides | **equal** |
| **Co-interior** (same side interior) | between the lines, same side (a C/U shape) | **add to 180°** |

**Why corresponding angles are equal:** parallel lines point in the same direction. The transversal crosses both at exactly the same slant, so the picture at the top crossing is a copy of the one at the bottom.

**Why alternate angles are equal:** an alternate angle is vertically opposite to a corresponding angle — equal to equal.

**Why co-interior angles add to 180°:** one of them makes a linear pair with an angle that is equal (corresponding) to the other.

- **Checking for parallel:** if corresponding (or alternate) angles are equal, or co-interior angles add to 180°, the lines **are** parallel.

## Worked example

**Example:** two angles of a linear pair are (2x + 10)° and (3x − 20)°. Then 2x + 10 + 3x − 20 = 180 → 5x = 190 → x = 38. Angles: **86° and 94°**.

## Quick practice

1. Complement of 37°?
2. Supplement of 108°?
3. Two complementary angles are in the ratio 2 : 3. Find them.
4. An angle is equal to its supplement. Find it.
5. Two lines cross; one angle is 65°. Find the other three.
6. Parallel lines cut by a transversal; one co-interior angle is 72°. The other?
7. Angles of a linear pair are (x + 30)° and (2x)°. Find x.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **53°**
2. **72°**
3. 90 ÷ 5 = 18 → **36° and 54°**
4. x = 180 − x → **90°**
5. **115°, 65°, 115°**
6. **108°**
7. 3x + 30 = 180 → **x = 50**

</details>
