# 12. Profit and Loss

## The words

**Example:** a shopkeeper buys a bag for ₹500 and sells it for ₹600. Profit = **₹100**.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cost price (CP)** | what the seller paid |
| **Selling price (SP)** | what the seller gets |
| **Profit (gain)** | SP − CP, when SP > CP |
| **Loss** | CP − SP, when CP > SP |
| **Overheads** | extra costs (transport, repairs) — **add them to CP** |

**Why overheads go into CP:** they are money the seller spent to have the item ready to sell. If you buy a cycle for ₹2000 and repair it for ₹300, it really cost you ₹2300.

## Profit % and loss %

- **Profit % = profit ÷ CP × 100** · **Loss % = loss ÷ CP × 100**.
- Bag example: 100/500 × 100 = **20% profit**.

**Why divide by CP (not SP):** profit is measured against the money you **put in**. That's the starting amount — same as percentage change in Chapter 11.

> [!WARNING]
> Always divide by **CP**, never by SP — unless a question clearly says "on the selling price".

## Finding SP from CP

- **SP = CP × (100 + profit%)/100** · **SP = CP × (100 − loss%)/100**.
- CP ₹1200, profit 15% → SP = 1200 × 115/100 = **₹1380**.
- CP ₹1200, loss 10% → SP = 1200 × 90/100 = **₹1080**.

**Why 115/100:** CP is 100%. A 15% profit adds 15% on top, so SP is 115% of CP.

## Finding CP from SP (working backwards)

- **CP = SP × 100/(100 + profit%)** · **CP = SP × 100/(100 − loss%)**.
- Sold for ₹690 at 15% profit → CP = 690 × 100/115 = **₹600**.

**Why it works:** ₹690 is 115% of CP. So 1% is 690 ÷ 115 = 6, and 100% is **600** (unitary method).

> [!WARNING]
> Don't take 15% of ₹690 and subtract. 15% of 690 is 103.50, giving 586.50 — wrong, because the 15% was calculated on CP, not on SP.

## Many items

**Example:** buy 12 pens for ₹60, sell 10 pens for ₹60. CP of 1 pen = ₹5, SP of 1 pen = ₹6. Profit % = 1/5 × 100 = **20%**.

> [!TIP]
> When buying and selling different counts, bring both to the price of **one** item first.

## Quick practice

1. CP ₹450, SP ₹540. Profit %?
2. CP ₹800, SP ₹720. Loss %?
3. A TV bought for ₹15 000, ₹1000 spent on transport, sold for ₹18 400. Profit %?
4. CP ₹2500, profit 12%. Find SP.
5. SP ₹1140 at a loss of 5%. Find CP.
6. Oranges bought at 6 for ₹30, sold at 5 for ₹30. Profit %?
7. By selling for ₹840 a man gains 20%. What would he gain or lose if he sold it for ₹630?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. 90/450 × 100 = **20%**
2. 80/800 × 100 = **10%**
3. CP = 16 000, profit 2400 → **15%**
4. 2500 × 112/100 = **₹2800**
5. 1140 × 100/95 = **₹1200**
6. CP each ₹5, SP each ₹6 → **20%**
7. CP = 840 × 100/120 = 700 → 630 is a loss of 70 → **10% loss**

</details>
