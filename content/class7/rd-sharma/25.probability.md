# 25. Data Handling IV — Probability

## Chance

- Some things are **certain** (the sun will rise), some **impossible** (a die shows 7), most are **in between** (it may rain).
- **Probability** puts a number on chance, from **0** (impossible) to **1** (certain).

## The words

| Word | Meaning | Coin example |
|---|---|---|
| **Experiment** | an action with uncertain results | tossing a coin |
| **Outcome** | one possible result | Head |
| **Event** | the outcome(s) you care about | getting a Head |
| **Equally likely** | each outcome has the same chance | H and T |

## The formula

**P(event) = number of favourable outcomes ÷ total number of outcomes** (when all outcomes are equally likely).

**Example:** a die is rolled. P(even number) = {2, 4, 6} ÷ 6 = 3/6 = **1/2**.

**Why it works:** if all 6 faces are equally likely, each gets an equal 1/6 share of the chance. Three faces are even, so 3 shares = 3/6.

- **Coin:** P(Head) = 1/2.
- **Die:** P(any one number) = 1/6. P(a number > 4) = {5, 6} = 2/6 = **1/3**.
- **Cards (52):** P(a king) = 4/52 = **1/13**. P(a red card) = 26/52 = **1/2**.
- **Bag of balls:** 3 red, 5 blue. P(red) = 3/8.

## Useful facts

- **0 ≤ P ≤ 1** always.
- **P(event) + P(not the event) = 1.** P(not 6 on a die) = 1 − 1/6 = **5/6**.

**Why they add to 1:** every outcome either is the event or isn't. Together they cover all the chances — which is 1 whole.

> [!WARNING]
> P can never be more than 1 or less than 0. If you get 7/6, you've miscounted the total.

## Experimental probability

- From actually trying: **P = number of times it happened ÷ number of trials**.
- A coin tossed 50 times shows 28 heads → experimental P(H) = 28/50 = 0.56.
- The more trials, the closer it gets to the theoretical 1/2.
