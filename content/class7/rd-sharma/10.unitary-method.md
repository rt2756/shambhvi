# 10. Unitary Method

## The idea: find the value of ONE

**Example:** 6 pens cost ₹90. Cost of 10 pens? 1 pen = 90 ÷ 6 = ₹15. So 10 pens = 15 × 10 = **₹150**.

- **Unitary method:** first find the value for **one unit**, then multiply up to the number you want.

**Why it works:** "one" is a stepping stone that connects any two amounts. Once you know one pen's price, any number of pens is just a multiplication.

> [!TIP]
> Write it in two lines: "6 pens → ₹90", "1 pen → ₹90 ÷ 6", "10 pens → ₹90 ÷ 6 × 10". Do the arithmetic at the end, and cancel first: 90 × 10 ÷ 6 = 150.

## Direct variation (more → more)

- Two amounts vary **directly** when one doubles, the other doubles too. **Ratio stays the same.**
- Examples: number of items and cost · distance and fuel (at the same speed) · hours worked and wages.
- **Rule:** to go to one, **divide**; then **multiply**.

**Example:** a car goes 108 km on 6 litres. How far on 15 litres? 1 litre → 108 ÷ 6 = 18 km → 15 litres → 18 × 15 = **270 km**.

## Inverse variation (more → less)

- Two amounts vary **inversely** when one doubles, the other **halves**. **Product stays the same.**
- Examples: number of workers and days to finish · speed and time for a fixed distance · number of people and how long food lasts.
- **Rule:** to go to one, **multiply**; then **divide**.

**Example:** 12 workers build a wall in 10 days. How long for 8 workers?
- 1 worker would need 12 × 10 = 120 days (one person does all the work alone — takes longer).
- 8 workers → 120 ÷ 8 = **15 days**.

**Why the steps flip:** total work = workers × days = 120 "worker-days" and doesn't change. Fewer workers must mean more days.

| | Direct | Inverse |
|---|---|---|
| One goes up, the other… | goes up | goes down |
| Stays constant | the ratio | the product |
| To find the value for 1 | divide | multiply |

> [!WARNING]
> First ask: **"more of this means more or less of that?"** Using the direct method on a workers-days question is the most common mistake.

## Time and work

- If A can finish a job in 6 days, A does **1/6 of the job per day**.
- Working together, **add the daily work**: A in 6 days, B in 3 days → 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/6 + 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2 per day → together **2 days**.

**Why:** a whole job = 1. If each day half is done, two days finish it.

## Time, speed and distance

- **Distance = speed × time** · speed = distance ÷ time · time = distance ÷ speed.
- **Example:** a train covers 240 km in 4 hours. Speed = 60 km/h. Time for 330 km at the same speed = 330 ÷ 60 = **5 ½ hours**.
- **km/h → m/s:** × 1000/3600 = × 5/18. 72 km/h = 72 × 5/18 = **20 m/s**.

**Why × 5/18:** 1 km = 1000 m and 1 hour = 3600 s, so 1 km/h = 1000/3600 m/s = 5/18 m/s.

## Quick practice

1. 8 notebooks cost ₹120. Find the cost of 15.
2. A machine fills 840 bottles in 6 hours. How many in 11 hours?
3. 15 men dig a pit in 8 days. How many days for 20 men?
4. Food for 40 students lasts 15 days. How long for 50 students?
5. A does a job in 10 days, B in 15 days. How long together?
6. Convert 54 km/h to m/s.
7. A cyclist covers 36 km in 2 ¼ hours. Find the speed.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. 120 ÷ 8 = 15 → 15 × 15 = **₹225**
2. 840 ÷ 6 = 140 → 140 × 11 = **1540 bottles**
3. Inverse: 15 × 8 = 120 → 120 ÷ 20 = **6 days**
4. Inverse: 40 × 15 = 600 → 600 ÷ 50 = **12 days**
5. 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = 1/6 → **6 days**
6. 54 × 5/18 = **15 m/s**
7. 36 ÷ 9/4 = 36 × 4/9 = **16 km/h**

</details>
