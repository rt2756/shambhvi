# 11. Percentage

## What "per cent" means

**Example:** you score 18 out of 25. As "out of 100" that is 18 × 4 = **72 out of 100 = 72%**.

- **Per cent** = "per hundred". x% = x/100.
- Percentages let you compare things with different totals: 18/25 (72%) beats 35/50 (70%).

**Why out of 100:** a common denominator. Turning every score into hundredths is the same trick as making denominators equal before comparing fractions (Chapter 2).

## Converting

| From → to | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Fraction → % | × 100 | 3/8 × 100 = 37.5% |
| % → fraction | ÷ 100, simplify | 45% = 45/100 = 9/20 |
| Decimal → % | × 100 (point 2 places right) | 0.07 = 7% |
| % → decimal | ÷ 100 (point 2 places left) | 125% = 1.25 |
| Ratio → % | write as fraction, × 100 | 2 : 3 → 2/5 of total = 40% (first part) |

**Why × 100 works:** 3/8 × 100% — and 100% = 1 whole, so you are multiplying by 1. The value doesn't change, only the way it's written.

> [!TIP]
> Know these by heart: ½ = 50% · ¼ = 25% · ¾ = 75% · ⅕ = 20% · ⅒ = 10% · ⅛ = 12.5% · ⅓ = 33⅓%.

## Finding a percentage of a number

**Example:** 15% of 240 = 15/100 × 240 = **36**.

- **Rule:** x% of N = x/100 × N.
- **Mental shortcut:** 10% = move the point one place left. 10% of 240 = 24, 5% = 12, so 15% = 24 + 12 = 36.

## Finding the whole, or the percentage

- **Whole from a part:** 30% of a number is 45. Then 1% is 45 ÷ 30 = 1.5, and 100% is **150**. (Unitary method, Chapter 10.)
- **What percent:** 12 is what % of 80? 12/80 × 100 = **15%**.

**Why it works:** "part ÷ whole" is a fraction; × 100 turns it into "out of 100".

## Percentage increase and decrease

**Example:** price goes from ₹400 to ₹460. Increase = 60. % increase = 60/400 × 100 = **15%**.

- **Rule:** % change = change ÷ **original** × 100.
- **Increase N by x%:** N × (100 + x)/100. 500 up by 20% → 500 × 120/100 = 600.
- **Decrease N by x%:** N × (100 − x)/100. 500 down by 20% → 500 × 80/100 = 400.

**Why divide by the original:** the change is measured against where you **started**. 60 more on 400 is a smaller jump than 60 more on 100.

> [!WARNING]
> Up 10% then down 10% does **not** get you back. 100 → 110 → 99. The 10% off is taken from the bigger number (110).

## Quick practice

1. Write 7/20 as a percentage.
2. Write 62.5% as a fraction in lowest terms.
3. Find 35% of 260.
4. 18% of a number is 54. Find the number.
5. 45 is what percent of 180?
6. A town's population rose from 25 000 to 28 000. Find the % increase.
7. A shirt of ₹800 is sold at 15% less. New price?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. 7/20 × 100 = **35%**
2. 62.5/100 = 625/1000 = **5/8**
3. 0.35 × 260 = **91**
4. 54 ÷ 18 × 100 = **300**
5. 45/180 × 100 = **25%**
6. 3000/25 000 × 100 = **12%**
7. 800 × 85/100 = **₹680**
