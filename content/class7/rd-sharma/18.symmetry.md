# 18. Symmetry

## Line symmetry

**Example:** fold the letter A down the middle — the two halves land exactly on each other.

- A shape has **line symmetry** if a line (the **axis of symmetry**) splits it into two **mirror images**.
- The fold test: fold along the line; if the halves match exactly, it's an axis.

| Shape | Lines of symmetry |
|---|---|
| Equilateral triangle | 3 |
| Isosceles triangle | 1 |
| Scalene triangle | 0 |
| Square | 4 |
| Rectangle | 2 (not the diagonals) |
| Rhombus | 2 (the diagonals) |
| Parallelogram | 0 |
| Regular pentagon / hexagon | 5 / 6 |
| Circle | infinitely many |

- **A regular polygon with n sides has n lines of symmetry.** **Why:** every side and every corner is identical, so a line through each corner (or each side's midpoint) works the same way — n of them.

> [!WARNING]
> A rectangle's **diagonals are not** lines of symmetry. Fold along one and the corners stick out.

- Letters: A, M, T, U, V, W, Y have a vertical line · B, C, D, E, K have a horizontal line · H, I, O, X have both.

## Rotational symmetry

**Example:** turn a square by 90° about its centre — it looks exactly the same.

- A shape has **rotational symmetry** if it looks the same after a turn of **less than 360°**.
- **Centre of rotation:** the fixed point it turns about.
- **Angle of rotation:** the smallest turn that brings it back to looking the same.
- **Order:** how many times it looks the same in one full turn. **Order = 360° ÷ angle**.

| Shape | Angle | Order |
|---|---|---|
| Square | 90° | 4 |
| Equilateral triangle | 120° | 3 |
| Rectangle | 180° | 2 |
| Regular hexagon | 60° | 6 |
| Letter S, Z, N | 180° | 2 |
| Circle | any angle | infinite |

**Why order = 360 ÷ angle:** if it matches every 90°, then in a full 360° turn it matches 360 ÷ 90 = 4 times.

- Every shape matches itself after 360°, so order 1 means **no** rotational symmetry.

## Both kinds

- Square, equilateral triangle, circle, regular polygons → both line and rotational symmetry.
- Parallelogram, letter S, Z → rotational only. Isosceles triangle, letter A → line only.

## Quick practice

1. How many lines of symmetry does a regular octagon have?
2. Lines of symmetry of a rhombus?
3. Order of rotational symmetry of a regular pentagon? Angle?
4. Name a quadrilateral with rotational symmetry of order 2 but no line symmetry.
5. Which letters have both a horizontal and a vertical line: E, H, M, O, X?
6. A shape looks the same after 72°. What is its order?
7. Does the letter N have line symmetry? Rotational symmetry?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **8**
2. **2** (its diagonals)
3. **5**, angle **72°**
4. **Parallelogram**
5. **H, O, X**
6. 360 ÷ 72 = **5**
7. No line symmetry; **rotational, order 2**
