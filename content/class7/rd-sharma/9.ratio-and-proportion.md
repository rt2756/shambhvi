# 9. Ratio and Proportion

## Ratio

**Example:** a class has 12 girls and 18 boys. Girls : boys = 12 : 18 = **2 : 3**. For every 2 girls there are 3 boys.

- A **ratio** a : b compares two amounts **of the same kind** by division: a : b = a/b.
- a is the **antecedent** (first term), b the **consequent** (second term).
- A ratio has **no units** — the units cancel.

**Why same units:** 50 paise : ₹2 is not 50 : 2. Convert first: 50 paise : 200 paise = **1 : 4**. Otherwise you'd be comparing different-sized things.

> [!WARNING]
> Order matters: girls : boys = 2 : 3, but boys : girls = 3 : 2.

## Simplest form and equivalent ratios

- Divide both terms by their HCF: 24 : 36 → ÷12 → **2 : 3**.
- **Equivalent ratios:** multiply or divide both terms by the same number: 2 : 3 = 4 : 6 = 10 : 15.
- With fractions or decimals, clear them first: ½ : ⅓ → × 6 → **3 : 2**; 0.4 : 1.2 → × 10 → 4 : 12 = **1 : 3**.

**Why it works:** a ratio is a fraction, so it follows the equivalent-fraction rule (×k/k = ×1).

## Comparing ratios

- Write both as fractions and compare (Chapter 2): 3 : 4 vs 5 : 7 → 3 × 7 = 21 vs 4 × 5 = 20 → **3 : 4 is greater**.

## Dividing in a given ratio

**Example:** share ₹600 between A and B in the ratio 2 : 3. Total parts = 2 + 3 = 5. One part = 600 ÷ 5 = 120. A gets 2 × 120 = **₹240**, B gets 3 × 120 = **₹360**.

**Why add the parts:** the ratio says "out of every 5 rupees, A gets 2 and B gets 3". So A's share is 2/5 of the total.

## Proportion

- Four numbers are **in proportion** when the two ratios are equal: a : b = c : d, written **a : b :: c : d**.
- a and d are the **extremes** (outside), b and c the **means** (inside).
- **Test:** product of extremes = product of means → **a × d = b × c**.
  - 3 : 5 :: 12 : 20 → 3 × 20 = 60 and 5 × 12 = 60 ✓.

**Why the test works:** a/b = c/d. Multiply both sides by b × d: a × d = c × b. That's cross-multiplication.

**Example:** find x if 4 : x :: 6 : 15 → 4 × 15 = 6x → **x = 10**.

## Continued proportion and mean proportional

- a, b, c are in **continued proportion** if a : b = b : c, so **b² = ac**.
- b is the **mean proportional** of a and c: b = √(ac).
  - Mean proportional of 4 and 9 = √36 = **6** (4 : 6 = 6 : 9 ✓).
- **Third proportional** to a and b is c where a : b = b : c → c = b²/a. Third proportional to 2 and 6 → 36/2 = **18**.

## Quick practice

3. Which is greater: 5 : 8 or 7 : 11?
4. Divide 84 cm in the ratio 3 : 4.
5. Are 7, 21, 9, 27 in proportion?
6. Find x: 5 : 8 :: x : 56.
7. Find the mean proportional of 3 and 27.
