# 22. Data Handling I — Collection and Organisation of Data

## Data

- **Data** = facts or numbers collected to answer a question (marks, heights, votes).
- **Primary data:** you collect it yourself (a class survey). **Secondary data:** someone else collected it (a census, a newspaper).
- **Raw data:** the numbers as collected, unsorted.

## Arranging data

- Put raw data in **ascending** (small → big) or **descending** order. Then you can see the smallest, largest and spread at a glance.
- **Range = highest − lowest**. Marks 23, 41, 37, 15, 48 → range = 48 − 15 = **33**.

**Why range:** it tells you how spread out the data is.

## Frequency tables and tally marks

**Example:** shoe sizes of 15 students: 5, 6, 6, 7, 5, 6, 8, 7, 6, 5, 6, 7, 8, 6, 7.

| Size | Tally | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | \|\|\| | 3 |
| 6 | \|\|\|\| \| | 6 |
| 7 | \|\|\|\| | 4 |
| 8 | \|\| | 2 |
| **Total** | | **15** |

- **Frequency** = how many times a value occurs. Tally in **bundles of 5** (four lines and a cross-stroke).

**Why bundles of 5:** counting groups of five is quick and hard to get wrong.

> [!TIP]
> Check: the frequencies must add up to the total number of observations (15 here).

## Grouped frequency tables

When there are many different values, group them into **class intervals**.

| Marks | Frequency |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | 2 |
| 10–20 | 5 |
| 20–30 | 8 |
| 30–40 | 4 |

- In 10–20, **10 is the lower limit**, **20 the upper limit**, the **class size** is 20 − 10 = 10.
- A value on the boundary (exactly 20) goes in the **next** class (20–30).

**Why:** otherwise 20 would be counted twice.
