# 8. Linear Equations in One Variable

## What an equation is

**Example:** "I think of a number, double it and add 3, and get 11." → **2x + 3 = 11**.

- An **equation** says two expressions are equal: Left side (LHS) = Right side (RHS).
- **Linear** = the variable has power 1 only (no x², no xy). **One variable** = only one letter.
- The value that makes it true is the **solution** (or root). Here x = 4, because 2(4) + 3 = 11.

| Is it a linear equation in one variable? | |
|---|---|
| 3x − 5 = 10 | ✓ |
| x² + 1 = 5 | ✗ (power 2) |
| x + y = 7 | ✗ (two letters) |
| 2x + 3 | ✗ (no = sign, it's an expression) |

## Solving by balancing

**Rule:** do the **same thing to both sides** — add, subtract, multiply or divide (not by 0).

**Example:** 3x − 5 = 10 → add 5 to both sides: 3x = 15 → divide both by 3: **x = 5**.

**Why it works:** think of a balance scale. If both pans weigh the same and you add 5 kg to each, they still balance.

## Transposing (the shortcut)

- Move a term across = and **change its operation**: + ↔ −, × ↔ ÷.
  - 3x − 5 = 10 → 3x = 10 + 5 → x = 15 ÷ 3 = **5**.

**Why it works:** it's balancing with the middle step skipped. "Move −5 across as +5" is "add 5 to both sides" written faster.

> [!TIP]
> Undo in reverse order: the last thing done to x is undone first. In 3x − 5, x was ×3 then −5, so undo −5 first, then ×3.

## Letters on both sides, and brackets

**Example:** 5x − 4 = 2x + 11 → 5x − 2x = 11 + 4 → 3x = 15 → **x = 5**.

**Example:** 3(x − 2) = 2(x + 1) → 3x − 6 = 2x + 2 → x = **8**.

**Example (fractions):** x/3 + 2 = x/2 → multiply every term by LCM 6: 2x + 12 = 3x → **x = 12**.

**Why multiply by the LCM:** it's a legal balancing move (× both sides by 6), and it clears every fraction at once.

> [!WARNING]
> Multiply **every** term by the LCM, including the plain numbers. And open brackets fully: 3(x − 2) is 3x − **6**, not 3x − 2.

## Always check

Put the answer back into the **original** equation: x = 12 → 12/3 + 2 = 6 and 12/2 = 6 ✓.

## Word problems

Steps: **(1)** let the unknown be x, **(2)** turn the words into an equation, **(3)** solve, **(4)** check it makes sense.

| Words | Algebra |
|---|---|
| 5 more than a number | x + 5 |
| a number decreased by 3 | x − 3 |
| twice / thrice a number | 2x / 3x |
| two consecutive numbers | x, x + 1 |
| two consecutive even (or odd) numbers | x, x + 2 |
| A is 4 years older than B | B = x, A = x + 4 |

**Example:** the sum of two consecutive numbers is 45. x + (x + 1) = 45 → 2x = 44 → x = 22. Numbers: **22 and 23**.

**Example:** a father is 3 times as old as his son. In 12 years he'll be twice as old. Son = x, father = 3x. 3x + 12 = 2(x + 12) → 3x + 12 = 2x + 24 → x = 12. **Son 12, father 36.**

## Quick practice

1. Is 2x + 3y = 5 a linear equation in one variable?
2. Solve 4x + 7 = 31.
3. Solve 7y − 3 = 4y + 12.
4. Solve 2(3x − 1) = 4x + 8.
5. Solve x/4 − 1 = 2.
6. The sum of three consecutive odd numbers is 57. Find them.
7. A number is 12 more than another; their sum is 50. Find them.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **No** — it has two variables.
2. 4x = 24 → **x = 6**
3. 3y = 15 → **y = 5**
4. 6x − 2 = 4x + 8 → 2x = 10 → **x = 5**
5. x/4 = 3 → **x = 12**
6. x + x + 2 + x + 4 = 57 → 3x = 51 → x = 17 → **17, 19, 21**
7. x + x + 12 = 50 → x = 19 → **19 and 31**

</details>
