# 24. Data Handling III — Bar Graphs

## What a bar graph is

- A **bar graph** shows data as bars of **equal width**, with **equal gaps** between them. The **height** (or length) of each bar shows the value.
- One axis has the **categories** (days, subjects); the other has the **values**, on a scale.

**Why equal widths:** only the height should carry information. If widths varied, a wider bar would look bigger even with the same value.

## Choosing a scale

**Example:** sales 120, 250, 180, 300. A scale of **1 unit = 50** gives heights 2.4, 5, 3.6, 6 units — they fit on the page.

- Pick a scale so the biggest value fits and the numbers are easy (1 cm = 10, 50, 100…).
- **Write the scale** on the graph.

> [!WARNING]
> The value axis should start at **0**. Starting at, say, 100 makes small differences look huge.

## Drawing a bar graph — steps

1. Draw two perpendicular axes; label them.
2. Mark categories on the horizontal axis, equally spaced.
3. Choose and write a scale on the vertical axis.
4. Draw each bar to its height (value ÷ scale).
5. Give the graph a title.

## Double bar graphs

- Two bars side by side for each category — e.g. boys and girls in each class, or marks in Term 1 and Term 2.
- Use **two colours** and a **key** (legend).

**Why:** it lets you compare two sets of data in the same picture, category by category.

## Reading bar graphs

**Example:** a graph of books read — Mon 4, Tue 7, Wed 3, Thu 7, Fri 9.
- Most books: **Friday**. Least: **Wednesday**.
- Total = 30. Mean = 30 ÷ 5 = **6 books a day**.
- Friday vs Wednesday: 9 is **3 times** 3.

## Quick practice

1. Why must bars have equal widths?
2. Values are 45, 80, 65, 120. Suggest a scale.
3. On a scale of 1 cm = 25 students, how tall is the bar for 175 students?
4. A bar is 4.5 cm on a scale of 1 cm = 20 km. What value?
5. In the books example, on which two days were the same number read?
6. When would you use a double bar graph?
7. What does a key (legend) tell you?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. So only the **height** shows the value.
2. e.g. **1 cm = 20** (heights 2.25, 4, 3.25, 6 cm)
3. 175 ÷ 25 = **7 cm**
4. 4.5 × 20 = **90 km**
5. **Tuesday and Thursday** (7 each)
6. To **compare two sets** of data side by side.
7. Which colour is which data set.
