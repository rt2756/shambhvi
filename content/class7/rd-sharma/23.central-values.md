# 23. Data Handling II — Central Values

A **central value** is one number that represents the whole data set. There are three.

## Mean (arithmetic mean, average)

**Example:** marks 6, 8, 5, 9, 7. Mean = (6 + 8 + 5 + 9 + 7) ÷ 5 = 35 ÷ 5 = **7**.

- **Mean = sum of observations ÷ number of observations.**

**Why it works:** the mean is the "fair share". If all 35 marks were shared equally among 5 tests, each would get 7.

- **Sum = mean × count.** Use it for "missing number" questions:
  - Mean of 5 numbers is 20; four are 18, 22, 15, 25. Sum = 100, so the fifth = 100 − 80 = **20**.

> [!TIP]
> Add the same number to every value and the mean goes up by that number. Multiply every value by k and the mean is multiplied by k.

## Median (the middle value)

- **Arrange in order first.**
- **Odd count n:** median = the ((n + 1)/2)th value. 3, 5, **7**, 9, 12 → median **7**.
- **Even count n:** median = mean of the two middle values. 2, 4, **6, 8**, 10, 12 → (6 + 8) ÷ 2 = **7**.

**Why use the median:** one extreme value can drag the mean far away. Salaries ₹10k, 12k, 11k, 13k, 200k → mean ₹49.2k (nobody earns that), median ₹12k (a fair picture).

> [!WARNING]
> Forgetting to sort first is the most common median mistake.

## Mode (the most frequent value)

- The **mode** is the value that occurs **most often**. 2, 3, 3, 5, 3, 7, 5 → mode **3**.
- There can be **two modes** (bimodal) or none.

**Why use the mode:** it works even for non-numbers — the most popular ice-cream flavour, the most common shoe size a shop should stock.

## Which one to use?

| | Best when |
|---|---|
| Mean | values are spread evenly (test averages) |
| Median | there are extreme values (incomes, house prices) |
| Mode | you want the most popular choice (sizes, colours) |

## Quick practice

1. Mean of 12, 15, 18, 21, 24?
2. Mean of 6 numbers is 15. Their sum?
3. Mean of 4 numbers is 25; three are 20, 30, 22. Find the fourth.
4. Median of 9, 3, 7, 1, 5?
5. Median of 10, 4, 8, 2, 6, 12?
6. Mode of 4, 6, 4, 8, 6, 4, 9?
7. Runs: 20, 25, 22, 0, 23. Mean or median — which better describes the batter?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. 90 ÷ 5 = **18**
2. **90**
3. 100 − 72 = **28**
4. 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 → **5**
5. 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 → (6 + 8) ÷ 2 = **7**
6. **4**
7. Mean = 18, median = 22 → **median** (the 0 drags the mean down)
