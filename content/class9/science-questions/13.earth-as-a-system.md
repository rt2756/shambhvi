Name the five spheres of the Earth system with an Indian example of each.

> [!ANSWER]
> | Sphere | What it is | Indian example |
> |---|---|---|
> | **GEOSPHERE** | solid rocks, soil, landforms and the Earth's interior | **Deccan plateau, Thar desert** |
> | **HYDROSPHERE** | **LIQUID** water — oceans, rivers, lakes, groundwater | **Ganga–Brahmaputra river system** |
> | **CRYOSPHERE** | **SOLID** water — ice and snow | **Himalayan glaciers, snow in Ladakh, polar ice caps** |
> | **ATMOSPHERE** | the air surrounding the Earth | cleaner air in mountains and forests |
> | **BIOSPHERE** | all living organisms and their habitats | mangroves, forests, farms, **ocean plankton, coral reefs** |
>
> **Note the hydrosphere/cryosphere split is by STATE, not by substance** — the same water belongs to one or the other depending on whether it is liquid or solid.

---

Trace what happens to a mountain lake and the sheep grazing beside it if there is less snowfall for several winters.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> less snowfall (CRYOSPHERE)
>    → less meltwater in summer → lower lake level (HYDROSPHERE)
>    → less water to support grass growth (BIOSPHERE)
>    → less food for the sheep
> ```
> **This is the central idea of the whole chapter in miniature: A DISTURBANCE IN ONE SPHERE LEADS TO CHANGES IN OTHERS.**
>
> **Nothing stays local.** That interconnection is exactly what makes it a *system* rather than a collection of separate parts.

---

Explain how warmer Arabian Sea water can eventually threaten Indian coastal cities. Name the spheres involved at each step.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> warmer Arabian Sea water (HYDROSPHERE)
>    → MORE EVAPORATION (ATMOSPHERE)
>    → fluctuations in the southwest monsoon
>    → floods in some regions of India, DROUGHT in others (hydrosphere disrupted)
>
> rising atmospheric temperature
>    → accelerated MELTING of glaciers and polar ice (CRYOSPHERE)
>    → flooding of low-lying regions, and in the long run RISING SEA LEVELS
>    → threat to COASTAL CITIES
>    → HABITAT LOSS disturbing ecosystems (BIOSPHERE)
> ```
> **One temperature change propagates through four spheres** — which is why the Earth has to be studied as a single system.

---

Why can sunlight cross space to reach us while the Sun's sound never could?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because solar radiation travels as ELECTROMAGNETIC (EM) waves, which need NO MEDIUM**, while **SOUND is a MECHANICAL wave that requires one** (Chapter 10).
>
> **EM waves travel through a vacuum at the SPEED OF LIGHT, 3 × 10⁸ m/s.**
>
> Space between the Sun and the Earth is a near vacuum — **fatal to sound, no obstacle at all to light.**

---

List the electromagnetic spectrum in order, and say which parts matter for Earth's climate.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> gamma rays → X-rays → UV → VISIBLE → infrared → microwaves → radio waves
>  high frequency / short wavelength        low frequency / long wavelength
>  high energy                                              low energy
> ```
> **About 99% of the Sun's energy reaching Earth is in the UV, VISIBLE and INFRARED range** — and **those three shape the Earth's climate and support life.**
>
> - **Gamma rays and X-rays** have very high energy and would be **harmful to life**, but are **mostly filtered out by the upper atmosphere**.
> - **Microwaves and radio waves** carry **too little energy** to warm the Earth significantly.

---

What does each of UV, visible and infrared radiation do at the Earth?

> [!ANSWER]
> - **UV** — short-wavelength UV is **mostly ABSORBED by the OZONE layer** in the upper atmosphere, **protecting life** and contributing some atmospheric heating.
> - **VISIBLE** — reaches the surface and **provides the energy for PHOTOSYNTHESIS**, the primary source of food for most organisms. It also **partly warms land and water**.
> - **INFRARED** — **warms the Earth's surface**, which then **RE-RADIATES that heat** back into the atmosphere, where **greenhouse gases trap a portion of it**.

---

Why are UV rays both dangerous and useful?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Dangerous:** UV lies between **100 nm and 400 nm** in wavelength and has **much higher energy than visible light**. **Prolonged exposure can damage the eyes and skin and increase the risk of cancer** — which is why UV-protective glasses and sunscreen matter.
>
> **Useful:** the same high energy that damages living cells **kills germs**, so UV is used in **water purifiers**, and it also **powers fluorescent lights**.
>
> **The property that makes it harmful is exactly the property that makes it useful** — it depends entirely on what you point it at.

---

Distinguish the solar constant from insolation, and explain why the two values differ.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **SOLAR CONSTANT = the average solar energy received per unit time per unit area PERPENDICULAR to the Sun's rays, AT THE TOP OF THE ATMOSPHERE** — about **1.4 kW/m²**, or **1400 J s⁻¹ m⁻²**. It is the energy available **before** any interference.
> - **INSOLATION = the amount that actually REACHES THE EARTH'S SURFACE** — a maximum of about **1 kW/m²** under clear skies.
>
> **Why they differ: some energy is ABSORBED and SCATTERED by gases, clouds and dust particles in the atmosphere on the way down.**
>
> **The solar constant matters because it lets scientists work out the Earth's energy balance**, climate and weather patterns — you cannot balance a budget without knowing the income.

---

How much solar energy falls on 1 m² in one hour at an insolation of 1 kW/m²? What everyday quantity does that equal?

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> E = intensity × area × time
>   = 1000 J s⁻¹ m⁻² × 1 m² × 3600 s
>   = 3,600,000 J = 3.6 × 10⁶ J
> ```
> **That equals ONE UNIT OF ELECTRICITY** as shown on a household bill — and is roughly **the energy needed to melt 5 kg of ice and then heat the water to 100 °C.**
>
> **That is one square metre for one hour.** Scaled up, **even a fraction of the Thar desert covered with solar panels could supply India's electricity needs.**

---

Why is India particularly well placed to use solar energy?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because of its geographical location in the TROPICAL and SUB-TROPICAL regions, India receives abundant sunlight throughout the year.**
>
> **Two consequences:**
> - that insolation **drives the southwest monsoon**, which shapes India's **climate and agriculture**
> - it offers **immense potential for solar energy** as a renewable, sustainable source
>
> **Anna Mani**, India's pioneering atmospheric scientist, **mapped solar insolation across India in the 1950s** and, with **S. Rangarajan**, published ***Solar Radiation Over India* in 1982** — the country's **first insolation atlas**, showing exactly this potential.

---

Define albedo and give the approximate albedo of snow, ice and ocean water.

> [!ANSWER]
> **ALBEDO is the FRACTION of solar radiation REFLECTED by a surface** — from the Latin word for *whiteness*.
>
> | Material | Albedo |
> |---|---|
> | **Snow** | **0.80 – 0.90** |
> | **Ice** | **0.50 – 0.70** |
> | Crushed rock | 0.25 – 0.30 |
> | **Black soil, ocean water** | **low** |
>
> **HIGH albedo → reflects more → stays COOL.** **LOW albedo → absorbs more → heats up QUICKLY.**

---

How does albedo help explain why polar regions are so cold, and why is that a dangerous feedback?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Snow and ice have very HIGH albedo (0.5–0.9), so they REFLECT most of the incoming solar radiation instead of absorbing it** — a major reason the polar regions are so cold.
>
> **The dangerous feedback:**
> ```
> warming → ice MELTS
>        → high-albedo ice is replaced by LOW-albedo ocean water
>        → more radiation ABSORBED
>        → MORE warming
>        → more melting …
> ```
> **A change that AMPLIFIES ITSELF is far more dangerous than a one-off change**, because it does not settle down — it accelerates.

---

Why does a concrete house feel hot at night in summer while a thick mud-walled house stays cool?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because ALL OBJECTS RADIATE HEAT.**
>
> **Concrete absorbs a great deal of solar radiation by day and RE-RADIATES it at night**, so the house feels hot long after sunset.
>
> **Traditional houses with thick mud and wooden walls RE-RADIATE far less**, so they offer cool conditions even in hot summer.
>
> **It is not that the mud house gets less sun — it is that it holds and returns less heat.**

---

Explain the urban heat island effect and one of its consequences.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Cities are WARMER than surrounding rural areas, especially in summer and at night.**
>
> **Why:** cities have far more built-up area — **buildings of steel, concrete and brick, and roads of concrete and asphalt** — all of which **absorb solar radiation and retain heat**, then **re-radiate it**, warming the city.
>
> **Rural areas and forests stay cool through SHADE and plant TRANSPIRATION.**
>
> **The consequence: increased energy demand for air conditioning**, which further stresses urban ecosystems — and, since that energy often comes from fossil fuels, adds to the warming.
>
> **It shows how human LAND USE can alter LOCAL climate**, quite apart from global effects.

---

Why do equatorial regions stay warm all year while polar regions are cold?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because the Earth is SPHERICAL, so the Sun's rays strike different latitudes at DIFFERENT ANGLES.**
>
> **At the equator the radiation is concentrated over a SMALLER area; near the poles the same amount of radiation is spread over a MUCH LARGER area.**
>
> **The same energy spread thinner means less heating per square metre** — exactly the reason a torch beam is bright when shone straight at a wall and dim when shone at a slant.
>
> **And this uneven heating creates the equator-to-pole temperature difference that DRIVES global winds and ocean currents.**

---

What is the composition of the atmosphere, and what holds it in place?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Nitrogen 78%, oxygen 21%**, plus small amounts of **argon, carbon dioxide, water vapour** and other gases.
>
> **It is held in place by the force of the EARTH'S GRAVITY.**
>
> **Without sufficient gravity a planet loses its atmosphere to space**, which is why small bodies like the Moon have essentially none.

---

Compare the troposphere and stratosphere, and explain why all weather happens in the lower one.

> [!ANSWER]
> | Layer | Altitude | Temperature with height | Feature |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **TROPOSPHERE** | **0 – 12 km** | **DECREASES** (about 6.5 °C/km) | **all weather formation** |
> | **STRATOSPHERE** | **12 – 50 km** | **INCREASES** | contains the **OZONE layer**, which absorbs UV |
>
> **Why weather is confined to the troposphere:** it is **heated FROM THE EARTH'S SURFACE**, so the air is **warmest at the bottom**. **Warm air rises**, and that **vertical mixing** drives winds and storms.
>
> **In the stratosphere the temperature RISES with height**, so the warm air is already on top and **there is no vertical mixing at all**. That **calms the layer and keeps weather below it.**
>
> **The troposphere is highest above the equator and lowest above the poles**, because stronger heating at the equator pushes it further up.

---

State the two ways the atmosphere protects life, and explain why the Earth would be uninhabitable without either.

> [!ANSWER]
> **1. It ABSORBS incoming solar radiation.** The **ozone layer blocks harmful UV**, and clouds and other gases absorb some sunlight before it reaches the surface.
>
> **2. It TRAPS outgoing heat.** The surface absorbs sunlight and **re-radiates it as INFRARED**; **greenhouse gases — CO₂, CH₄ and water vapour — absorb that re-radiated heat**, preventing it escaping into space.
>
> **Without the atmosphere the Earth would be too COLD for life to survive.**
>
> **But excess CO₂ from human activities ENHANCES the greenhouse effect, causing global warming** — which, unchecked, could make the Earth uninhabitable in the other direction.
>
> **The atmosphere is not "good" or "bad" — the BALANCE is what matters.**

---

Which correctly describes the warming of the Earth: CO₂ absorbs sunlight directly, dust particles absorb it, or the surface absorbs and re-radiates it?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The Earth's SURFACE absorbs solar radiation, which is then RE-RADIATED and TRAPPED by greenhouse gases.**
>
> **Why the order matters:** greenhouse gases are largely **transparent to incoming visible sunlight** but **absorb outgoing INFRARED**. So the energy must first be **absorbed by the ground and re-emitted at a longer wavelength** before the greenhouse effect can act on it at all.
>
> **The other options fail:** CO₂ does not simply absorb incoming sunlight and release it as heat; dust and particles play only a minor role; and radiation reflected by clouds is **lost to space**, not used to heat us.

---

Venus is farther from the Sun than Mercury, yet hotter. Why?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because Venus has a thick ATMOSPHERE with an UNCONTROLLED GREENHOUSE EFFECT, while Mercury has essentially none.**
>
> Mercury receives far more solar radiation, but with no atmosphere the heat **escapes straight back to space**. Venus traps its heat so effectively that its surface is hotter than Mercury's despite receiving less energy.
>
> **It is the clearest demonstration available that ATMOSPHERE MATTERS MORE THAN DISTANCE** — and a warning about what an unchecked greenhouse effect can do to a planet.

---

What is the ozone hole, what caused it, and what does its recovery show?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The ozone layer acts as a protective shield, absorbing harmful UV radiation.** When ozone molecules are **destroyed faster than they are naturally formed**, the layer thins.
>
> **In the late 20th century, human-made CHLOROFLUOROCARBONS (CFCs)** — used in **refrigerators and aerosols** — caused **severe ozone loss over Antarctica**, known as the **OZONE HOLE**. Increased UV reaching the Earth **harms living organisms and ecosystems**.
>
> **The MONTREAL PROTOCOL, a global agreement, reduced CFC use, and the ozone layer is now SLOWLY RECOVERING.**
>
> **What it shows: international scientific cooperation can actually work.** The damage was identified, the cause was traced, an agreement was reached, and the trend reversed.

---

Explain how valley and mountain breezes form.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Both arise because slopes and the valley floor do not heat and cool at the same rate.**
>
> | | **VALLEY BREEZE** (day) | **MOUNTAIN BREEZE** (night) |
> |---|---|---|
> | What happens first | **sunlit slopes heat MORE RAPIDLY** than the valley floor | **slopes LOSE heat faster**; the valley floor stays warmer |
> | Air over the slopes | becomes **warm and RISES**, creating **low pressure** | becomes **cooler and DENSER** |
> | The wind | **cooler valley air moves UP the slopes** | **cold dense air flows DOWN into the valley** |
>
> **These daily reversals are familiar in Shimla, Dehradun and other Himalayan valleys**, and they help **regulate temperature and moisture, supporting soil and crop health.**

---

Would the mountain breeze from a grass-covered mountain differ from one off barren rock? Explain.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Yes — the breeze from the BARREN ROCK would generally be WARMER.**
>
> **Why:** bare rock has a **lower albedo** and **absorbs more solar radiation** by day, then **RE-RADIATES that heat at night** — exactly like the concrete house. So the air above it stays warmer for longer.
>
> **A grass-covered slope** reflects more, **loses heat to TRANSPIRATION** during the day, and re-radiates less at night — so the descending air is **cooler**.
>
> **This is the urban heat island effect at a small scale:** the surface covering, not just the latitude, decides the local temperature.

---

Describe the global pressure belts and the circulation between them.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> POLAR HIGH (90°)          very low temperature → cold dense air SINKS
>     ↓ air flows towards the sub-polar belt along the surface
> SUB-POLAR LOW (60°)       air RISES where poleward surface air meets cold polar air
>     ↑
> SUB-TROPICAL HIGH (30°)   air that rose at the equator has cooled, become denser
>                           and SINKS here
>     ↓ part returns to the equator along the surface; part moves poleward
> EQUATORIAL LOW (0°)       intense heating → warm air RISES and moves poleward aloft
> ```
> **The whole pattern is driven by ONE cause: uneven heating between the equator and the poles.** Warm air rises and leaves low pressure behind; cool air sinks and creates high pressure; air flows from high to low.

---

Why do planetary winds follow curved paths rather than going straight from high to low pressure?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because the EARTH'S ROTATION deflects them.**
>
> **Winds are deflected towards the RIGHT in the Northern Hemisphere and towards the LEFT in the Southern Hemisphere.**
>
> **So instead of a straight line from high pressure to low pressure, the wind curves** — and the same deflection turns ocean currents into the great circular **gyres**.
>
> **It is a consequence of measuring motion on a spinning surface** — the air moves in a straight line, but the ground beneath it turns.

---

What drives ocean currents? Name four factors.

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. **Planetary WINDS**, which **drag the surface water by FRICTION**, setting surface currents in motion
> 2. **TEMPERATURE differences** — **warm equatorial water travels along the surface towards the poles**, while **colder, denser water flows back towards the equator at DEEPER levels**
> 3. **SALINITY differences** — **less salty water is less dense and stays near the surface; saltier, denser water sinks** and moves at depth
> 4. **The Earth's ROTATION**, which deflects the water into **GYRES — clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere, anticlockwise in the Southern**
> 5. **The distribution of LAND MASSES**, which blocks and redirects currents

---

What happens to warm surface water travelling from the equator to the poles, and why does it matter?

> [!ANSWER]
> **It gradually LOSES heat to the atmosphere, becomes COLDER AND DENSER, and SINKS — then flows back towards the equator through DEEPER ocean levels.**
>
> **Why it matters enormously: by TRANSPORTING HEAT from the equator towards the poles, ocean currents REDUCE the temperature differences across the planet.**
>
> **The clearest example:** the **NORTH ATLANTIC DRIFT**, an extension of the **Gulf Stream**, carries warm water across the Atlantic to **north-western Europe and keeps many ports ICE-FREE in winter, even at high latitudes.** Without it those coasts would be far colder than their latitude suggests.
>
> **Currents also support massive ecosystems by transporting NUTRIENTS.**

---

What is a biogeochemical cycle, and why must matter cycle when energy does not?

> [!ANSWER]
> **A BIOGEOCHEMICAL CYCLE is the cyclic movement of MATTER and ENERGY between the ABIOTIC (non-living) and BIOTIC (living) components of the Earth.**
>
> **Why matter must cycle: the Earth receives a CONSTANT FLOW OF ENERGY from the Sun, but it receives NO SUPPLY OF MATTER.**
>
> **The atoms available are all the atoms there will ever be.** Carbon, nitrogen and oxygen must therefore be **recycled again and again** to remain available to support life.
>
> **Energy flows THROUGH the system; matter goes ROUND it.** That single distinction explains why there is no "sunlight cycle" but there is a carbon cycle.

---

Describe the water cycle and name the five spheres it links.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> EVAPORATION from oceans, rivers and lakes  +  TRANSPIRATION from plants
>    → CONDENSATION into clouds
>    → PRECIPITATION as rain, hail or snow
>    → RUN OFF back to the ocean, and INFILTRATION into soil and rock as GROUNDWATER
> ```
> **Water dissolves minerals from soil and rocks, supports all terrestrial organisms, and transports nutrients to the oceans to support marine life.**
>
> **The spheres it links: CRYOSPHERE (glaciers), HYDROSPHERE (rivers and oceans), ATMOSPHERE (moisture), GEOSPHERE (soil and infiltration) and BIOSPHERE (crops and fisheries)** — all five, in one cycle.

---

How is climate change affecting the water cycle? Give three examples.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **A warmer atmosphere HOLDS MORE MOISTURE** → **heavier rains in some areas** (intensified monsoons) and **droughts elsewhere**. More water in the air does not mean more rain everywhere — it means rain is redistributed and comes in bigger bursts.
> - **Melting glaciers add more water to rivers**, raising sea levels in the long run and **threatening coastal cities such as Mumbai and Chennai**.
> - **Sudden bursts of intense rainfall** cause **more RUN OFF, which erodes soil**, and **less INFILTRATION, which reduces groundwater recharge** — making agriculture difficult in dry months.
>
> **Note the last one carefully: MORE rain can mean LESS usable water**, because water that runs off is water that never soaked in.

---

Distinguish the fast and slow carbon cycles, and explain why the difference matters.

> [!ANSWER]
> **FAST CYCLE — days to years:**
> ```
> plants convert atmospheric CO₂ into glucose by PHOTOSYNTHESIS
> RESPIRATION releases CO₂ back
> animals eat plants and other animals
> when organisms die, DECOMPOSITION returns CO₂ to the air
> ```
> **SLOW CYCLE — millions of years:**
> ```
> dead plants and animals are BURIED and converted into FOSSIL FUELS — coal, oil, gas
> ```
> **Why the difference matters: fossil fuels took MILLIONS OF YEARS to form, and we BURN them on a VERY SHORT time scale.**
>
> **Carbon that took an age to bury is being released in a century.** The cycle is not broken — it has been made violently **lopsided**, releasing far faster than it stores.

---

What is the ocean's role in the carbon cycle, and how much carbon does it hold?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The atmosphere and ocean water CONTINUOUSLY EXCHANGE CO₂.**
>
> **Ocean water absorbs atmospheric CO₂ to form CARBONATE and BICARBONATE ions.** **Phytoplankton use them for photosynthesis**, and **some marine organisms use them to build SHELLS**. When those organisms die they **sink to the ocean floor**, where their organic matter is **stored as carbon for a long period**.
>
> **Of the total global carbon, 71% is in the OCEANS** — while **the atmosphere holds only about 1%**.
>
> **That is why the oceanic reservoir REGULATES atmospheric CO₂**: a small change in how much the ocean holds makes a large difference to the tiny atmospheric share.
>
> (Carbon also makes up about **49% of the dry weight of living organisms**.)

---

Atmospheric CO₂ has risen from about 315 ppm in 1960 to about 420 ppm. What does the Keeling curve show, and why is its sawtooth pattern there?

> [!ANSWER]
> **It shows a rise of about 35% since 1960 — an UNPRECEDENTED rise in the history of human civilisation** — caused by **burning fossil fuels and deforestation**.
>
> **The SAWTOOTH pattern comes from SEASONAL PLANT GROWTH in the NORTHERN HEMISPHERE.** Each growing season, the vast land vegetation of the north **absorbs CO₂**, pulling the curve down; each winter, respiration and decomposition release it again, pushing the curve up.
>
> **Why the northern hemisphere specifically: that is where most of the world's land is**, and so most of the world's land plants.
>
> **The dips are the Earth breathing; the upward slope is us.**

---

Some CO₂ is necessary to keep the Earth warm. Why is excess CO₂ still undesirable?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because the BALANCE is what is critical, not the presence.**
>
> **Without any greenhouse gases the Earth would be too cold for life.** But **excess CO₂ intensifies the greenhouse effect**, leading to:
> - **global warming**
> - **melting of glaciers and Arctic sea ice**
> - **rising sea levels**
> - **more extreme weather**
> - in India, **more intense monsoons** (warmer air holds more moisture) and **threats to agriculture from changing rainfall patterns**
>
> **It is exactly like a blanket:** one keeps you comfortable, ten do not keep you ten times more comfortable.

---

Why can't plants use atmospheric nitrogen directly, and what must happen first?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because nitrogen gas (N₂) is RATHER NON-REACTIVE and cannot be absorbed and used by plants or animals as it is.**
>
> **Recall from Chapter 9 why: the two nitrogen atoms are held by a TRIPLE BOND**, which takes a great deal of energy to break. That is also why nitrogen makes up most of the air and takes part in almost nothing.
>
> **It must first be converted into SOLUBLE COMPOUNDS that living beings can absorb** — the process called **NITROGEN FIXATION**.

---

Name and describe the five steps of the nitrogen cycle with the organisms responsible.

> [!ANSWER]
> | Step | What happens | Organisms |
> |---|---|---|
> | **NITROGEN FIXATION** | atmospheric **N₂ → ammonia (NH₃)** | **Rhizobium** in the root nodules of legumes; **Azotobacter** in soil |
> | **NITRIFICATION** | **ammonia → nitrite (NO₂⁻)**, then **nitrite → nitrate (NO₃⁻)** | **Nitrosomonas**, then **Nitrobacter** |
> | **ASSIMILATION** | plants **absorb** nitrogen compounds from the soil; animals get nitrogen by **eating** plants or other animals | — |
> | **AMMONIFICATION** | dead organisms and waste are broken down, **returning ammonia to the soil** | decomposer **bacteria and fungi** |
> | **DENITRIFICATION** | **nitrates → nitrogen gas**, completing the cycle | **Pseudomonas** |
>
> **LIGHTNING also fixes a small amount of nitrogen**, producing nitrogen oxides.

---

What is the Haber–Bosch process, why is it called "Bread from Air", and what are its costs?

> [!ANSWER]
> **A process from the early 1900s that makes AMMONIA from ATMOSPHERIC NITROGEN artificially**, producing **most of the fertilisers used today**.
>
> **Why "Bread from Air": it takes nitrogen from the air and turns it into food.** It **revolutionised agriculture, enabled India's Green Revolution and feeds billions** — **more than half the nitrogen atoms in the human body come from it.**
>
> **The costs:**
> - it is **energy-intensive, using about 1–2% of global energy**
> - **overuse of fertilisers has degraded soil and water**
>
> **A single technology that both feeds the world and damages it** — which is why it is worth understanding rather than simply praising or condemning.

---

Describe the oxygen cycle, and explain its relationship to the carbon cycle.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> CONSUMED by:  RESPIRATION (plants and animals)  +  COMBUSTION of fuels
>                            ↓ releasing CO₂
> PRODUCED by:  PHOTOSYNTHESIS — plants use sunlight, water and CO₂
>               to form glucose and release O₂
> ```
> **About 21% of the atmosphere is free O₂**, and oxygen is an essential component of **carbohydrates, proteins, nucleic acids and fats**. It also exists **combined** — as **metal oxides and minerals in the crust** and as **carbon dioxide in the air**.
>
> **The relationship: the carbon and oxygen cycles are the SAME TWO REACTIONS read in opposite directions.** Photosynthesis takes CO₂ and gives O₂; respiration and combustion take O₂ and give CO₂.
>
> **So you cannot disturb one without disturbing the other** — cutting forests reduces oxygen production *and* raises CO₂, in one stroke.

---

What would happen if the biogeochemical cycles stopped?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Life would end, because the supply of essential matter would run out.**
>
> - **Carbon cycle stops** → no CO₂ returned to the air → **photosynthesis ceases** → no food is made, and every food chain collapses
> - **Nitrogen cycle stops** → no fixed nitrogen in soil → **plants cannot make PROTEINS or NUCLEIC ACIDS** → growth and reproduction fail
> - **Oxygen cycle stops** → oxygen is consumed by respiration and combustion and **never replaced** → aerobic life suffocates
> - **Water cycle stops** → no precipitation → **land dries out**, and nutrients are never transported
>
> **The underlying reason is always the same:** the Earth gets **energy** from outside but **no new matter**. Stop recycling and the atoms simply stay locked where they are.

---

What is eutrophication, and what causes it?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Excess nitrogen — as NITRATES from the OVERUSE OF FERTILISERS in agriculture — runs off into rivers and lakes**, causing **widespread growth of algae (ALGAL BLOOMS)**.
>
> **The blooms DEPLETE OXYGEN in the water and KILL FISH.** That process is **EUTROPHICATION**, and it **threatens water bodies and coastal fisheries.**
>
> **The irony worth noticing: fertiliser is added to make plants grow — and that is exactly what it does in the water too.** The problem is not that it fails, but that it works in the wrong place.

List five effects of deforestation across different spheres.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **Less PHOTOSYNTHESIS** → less CO₂ removed from the air and less oxygen released (**atmosphere**)
> - **Less TRANSPIRATION** → **decline in LOCAL RAINFALL** (**atmosphere and hydrosphere**)
> - **Altered surface ALBEDO** → changed local heating
> - **More SOIL EROSION**, with no tree roots to hold the soil together (**geosphere**)
> - **Habitat destruction → decline in BIODIVERSITY** as species lose their homes (**biosphere**)
> - **Saturated carbon sinks** — forests that would have absorbed CO₂ no longer do
>
> **One action, effects in every sphere** — which is exactly why the chapter insists on treating the Earth as a system.

Why is ozone protective in the stratosphere but harmful at ground level?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Because of WHERE it is, not what it is.**
>
> - **In the STRATOSPHERE** (12–50 km up) ozone **absorbs harmful UV radiation far above us**, acting as a protective shield for all life.
> - **At GROUND LEVEL** ozone is formed when **vehicular emissions react with sunlight**, along with **smog** — and here **we BREATHE it**, which is **harmful to health**.
>
> **The same molecule, opposite verdicts** — an excellent reminder that a substance is not simply "good" or "bad" independent of context.

Name two feedbacks that make global warming worse, and explain why feedbacks are especially dangerous.

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. **Warmer ocean water REDUCES the ocean's capacity to absorb CO₂** — so warming weakens the very carbon sink that was slowing it.
> 2. **Melting ice replaces HIGH-albedo surfaces with LOW-albedo ones** — so warming increases the absorption that causes warming.
>
> **Why feedbacks are especially dangerous: a change that AMPLIFIES ITSELF does not settle down — it accelerates.**
>
> A one-off change reaches a new balance and stops. A self-amplifying one keeps going **even if the original cause stops**, which makes it far harder to reverse.

The Montreal Protocol succeeded where the Kyoto Protocol and Paris Agreement have been less successful. Why might that be?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The Montreal Protocol reduced CFC use and the ozone layer is now slowly recovering.** The **Kyoto Protocol and Paris Agreement**, under which countries were to **reduce CO₂ emissions, have been less successful.**
>
> **A plausible reason for the difference:**
> - **CFCs came from a HANDFUL of specific products** — refrigerators and aerosols — **and substitutes were available.** Agreement was cheap and affected few industries.
> - **CO₂ comes from ALMOST EVERYTHING WE DO** — electricity, transport, industry, agriculture, construction. There is no single substitute, and every country's economy is involved.
>
> **The lesson is not that cooperation fails, but that it succeeds most easily where the change asked for is narrow.** Montreal proves the mechanism works; CO₂ is simply a far harder problem.

Which best describes the role of biogeochemical cycles — to provide food directly, to recycle nutrients, to create new elements, or to remove toxins?

> [!ANSWER]
> **To RECYCLE essential nutrients between BIOTIC and ABIOTIC components.**
>
> **Why the others are wrong:**
> - **"Provide food directly"** — only photosynthesis makes food; the cycles move the raw materials.
> - **"Create new elements"** — **no chemical or biological process creates elements.** Cycles move existing atoms; they never make new ones. That is precisely why recycling is necessary.
> - **"Remove pollutants"** — some organisms do break down pollutants, but that is not what the cycles are for.

If the Earth were a flat disc facing the Sun instead of a sphere, how would heating and temperature differ?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Solar radiation would strike the whole surface at the SAME ANGLE**, so **every part would be heated equally** — no equator-to-pole difference at all.
>
> **The consequences would be dramatic:**
> - **No temperature difference between "equator" and "poles"** → **no pressure belts** → **no planetary winds**
> - **No wind dragging the ocean surface** → **greatly reduced ocean currents**
> - **No latitudinal climate zones** — no tropics, no polar regions, no monsoon
>
> **Almost everything in this chapter follows from ONE fact: the Earth is a sphere, so the same sunlight is spread differently at different latitudes.** Remove that and the atmosphere and oceans would largely stop circulating.

Trace the path carbon takes from the atmosphere and back again.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> CO₂ in the atmosphere
>    ↓ PHOTOSYNTHESIS
> glucose and other compounds in PLANTS
>    ↓ eaten
> carbon compounds in ANIMALS
>    │
>    ├─ RESPIRATION (plants and animals) ──────────→ CO₂ back to the atmosphere
>    │
>    ├─ DEATH → DECOMPOSITION by microbes ─────────→ CO₂ back to the atmosphere
>    │
>    └─ DEATH → BURIAL over millions of years → FOSSIL FUELS (coal, oil, gas)
>                    ↓ COMBUSTION
>                    └────────────────────────────→ CO₂ back to the atmosphere
>
> also:  atmosphere ⇄ OCEAN (dissolved CO₂, carbonate and bicarbonate ions,
>        phytoplankton, shells, sinking to the ocean floor)
> ```
> **Three routes return carbon to the air — respiration, decomposition and combustion — but they run on utterly different timescales**, and that mismatch is the heart of the climate problem.

How is heat lost from the Earth's surface, and why does it matter?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The surface absorbs solar radiation and RE-RADIATES it as INFRARED radiation.**
>
> **Its significance:**
> - **A PORTION is trapped by GREENHOUSE GASES** — CO₂, CH₄ and water vapour — **keeping the Earth warm enough to support life.** Without this, the Earth would be far too cold.
> - **The rest escapes to space**, and that outgoing loss is what **balances** the incoming solar energy.
>
> **The balance is the whole point:** if outgoing heat exactly matched incoming energy, temperatures would be steady. **Adding greenhouse gases reduces the outgoing side**, so energy accumulates and the planet warms.

Describe the interrelationship between the Earth's spheres with one worked example.

> [!ANSWER]
> **The spheres are connected by natural processes — heating by solar radiation, movement of air and water, and nutrient cycling — in a DELICATE BALANCE.**
>
> **A worked example — clearing a large forest:**
> ```
> forest cleared (BIOSPHERE)
>    → less transpiration → less moisture in the air (ATMOSPHERE)
>    → decline in local rainfall (HYDROSPHERE)
>    → no roots to hold the soil → erosion, less infiltration (GEOSPHERE)
>    → less groundwater recharge → rivers run lower in dry months (hydrosphere)
>    → less photosynthesis → more CO₂ in the air (atmosphere)
>    → warming → faster glacier melt (CRYOSPHERE)
>    → habitat loss → species decline (biosphere again)
> ```
> **The chain returns to where it started** — which is the definition of a system rather than a sequence. **You cannot change one thing only.**
