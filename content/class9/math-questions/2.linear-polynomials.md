<!-- ===== Vocabulary ===== -->

In the expression `4x + 5y + 3`, name the terms, variables, coefficients and constant.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **Terms:** `4x`, `5y`, `3`
> - **Variables:** `x` and `y`
> - **Coefficients:** **4** (of x) and **5** (of y)
> - **Constant:** **3**

---

Raju buys x red boxes of 4 pens and y blue boxes of 5 pencils, plus 3 free pens. Write the expression for the total.

> [!ANSWER]
> x red boxes give `4x` pens; y blue boxes give `5y` pencils; plus 3 free pens.
>
> **Total = `4x + 5y + 3`**

---

A garden l m by w m is fenced with wire along the length at ₹100/m, wood along the width at ₹80/m, and seeded at ₹50 per m². Write the total cost.

> [!ANSWER]
> - Wire along **both** lengths: `2l × 100 = 200l`
> - Wood along **both** widths: `2w × 80 = 160w`
> - Seeds over the **area**: `50 × l × w = 50lw`
>
> **Total = ₹(200l + 160w + 50lw)**
>
> **Note `50lw` is not linear** — it involves two variables multiplied together.

<!-- ===== Degree ===== -->

Define the degree of a polynomial, and name polynomials of degrees 0, 1, 2 and 3.

> [!ANSWER]
> **The degree is the HIGHEST power of the variable in the polynomial.**
>
> | Degree | Name |
> |---|---|
> | 0 | **constant** |
> | 1 | **linear** |
> | 2 | **quadratic** |
> | 3 | **cubic** |

---

Find the degrees of `2x² − 5x + 3`, `y³ + 2y − 1`, `−9`, `4z − 3`.

> [!ANSWER]
> - `2x² − 5x + 3` → **2** (quadratic)
> - `y³ + 2y − 1` → **3** (cubic)
> - `−9` → **0** (constant)
> - `4z − 3` → **1** (linear)

---

Why does the constant 8 count as a polynomial of degree 0?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because it can be written as **`8x⁰`**, and `x⁰ = 1`.
>
> So the variable **is** there — raised to the power **zero**.

---

What are the coefficients of x² and x³ in `x⁴ − 3x³ + 6x² − 2x + 7`?

> [!ANSWER]
> - Coefficient of `x³` is **−3** — **take the minus sign with it**
> - Coefficient of `x²` is **6**

---

What is the coefficient of z in `4z³ + 5z² − 11`?

> [!ANSWER]
> **0.**
>
> There is no z term at all — and that counts as a coefficient of **zero**, not as "nothing".

---

What is the constant term of `9x³ + 5x² − 8x − 10`?

> [!ANSWER]
> **−10** — the term with no variable in it (again, keep the sign).

---

Write a polynomial of degree 1, one of degree 2 and one of degree 3.

> [!ANSWER]
> - Degree 1: **`3x + 7`**
> - Degree 2: **`x² − 4x + 1`**
> - Degree 3: **`2y³ + y − 5`**

<!-- ===== Linear polynomials ===== -->

What is the characteristic feature of a linear polynomial?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The difference between successive values (at consecutive integers) is CONSTANT.**
>
> Such patterns are called **linear patterns**.

---

A chess club charges ₹200 to join plus ₹50 per match. Write the cost for m matches, and check the pattern.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Cost = ₹(200 + 50m)** — a linear polynomial in m.
>
> Matches 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 give ₹250, 300, 350, 400, 450 — **each ₹50 more than the last**, a constant difference.

---

A player at that club paid ₹750. How many matches?

> [!ANSWER]
> `200 + 50m = 750` → `50m = 550` → **m = 11 matches**

---

What happens to the perimeter of a square when its side increases by 0.5 cm each time?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The perimeter increases by 2 cm every time.**
>
> Perimeter `= 4x`, so a 0.5 cm rise in x gives `4 × 0.5 =` **2 cm**.
>
> A constant increase in the input giving a constant increase in the output is exactly what makes it **linear**.

---

What is a linear equation, and how does it differ from a linear polynomial?

> [!ANSWER]
> **A linear equation is what you get when you equate a linear polynomial to a constant.**
>
> `2x + 10` is a **polynomial** — an expression. `2x + 10 = 64` is an **equation** — a statement to be solved.

---

Two numbers add to 64, one being 10 more than the other. Find them.

> [!ANSWER]
> Smaller = x, larger = `x + 10`.
>
> `x + (x + 10) = 64` → `2x + 10 = 64` → `2x = 54` → `x = 27`
>
> **The numbers are 27 and 37.**

---

Salil's mother is three times his age. In 5 years their ages add to 70. Find both.

> [!ANSWER]
> Salil = x, mother = `3x`.
>
> `(x + 5) + (3x + 5) = 70` → `4x + 10 = 70` → `4x = 60` → **x = 15**
>
> **Salil is 15, his mother 45.**

---

Two positive integers differ by 63 and are in the ratio 2 : 5. Find them.

> [!ANSWER]
> Let them be `2k` and `5k`. Then `5k − 2k = 63` → `3k = 63` → `k = 21`.
>
> **The integers are 42 and 105.**

---

Ruby has 3 times as many two-rupee coins as five-rupee coins, totalling ₹88. How many of each?

> [!ANSWER]
> Five-rupee coins = x, two-rupee coins = `3x`.
>
> `5x + 2(3x) = 88` → `11x = 88` → **x = 8**
>
> **8 five-rupee coins and 24 two-rupee coins.**

---

A 300 ft fence is cut into two pieces, the longer four times the shorter. Find both.

> [!ANSWER]
> Shorter = x, longer = `4x`. `x + 4x = 300` → `5x = 300` → **x = 60**
>
> **60 ft and 240 ft.**

---

A rectangle's length is 3 more than twice its width, and its perimeter is 24 cm. Find its dimensions.

> [!ANSWER]
> Width = w, length = `2w + 3`.
>
> `2(w + 2w + 3) = 24` → `2(3w + 3) = 24` → `6w + 6 = 24` → `w = 3`
>
> **Width 3 cm, length 9 cm.** Check: `2(3 + 9) = 24` ✓

---

<!-- ===== Input-output ===== -->

Explain how a polynomial acts as an input–output machine, using `2x + 3`.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Feed in a value of x, get out a value of the expression.**
>
> - `x = 4` → `2(4) + 3 =` **11**
> - `x = −6` → `2(−6) + 3 =` **−9**
>
> This is what makes it a **function** — each input gives exactly one output.

---

Find the value of `5x − 3` at x = 0, x = −1 and x = 2.

> [!ANSWER]
> - `x = 0` → **−3**
> - `x = −1` → `−5 − 3 =` **−8**
> - `x = 2` → `10 − 3 =` **7**

---

Find the value of `7s² − 4s + 6` at s = 0, s = −3 and s = 4.

> [!ANSWER]
> - `s = 0` → **6**
> - `s = −3` → `7(9) + 12 + 6 = 63 + 12 + 6 =` **81** (note `−4 × −3 = +12`)
> - `s = 4` → `112 − 16 + 6 =` **102**

---

A 20 cm wire is bent into a rectangle of length x. Write the area, and find it when x = 6.

> [!ANSWER]
> Half the perimeter is 10, so the width is `10 − x`.
>
> **Area = `x(10 − x) = 10x − x²`** — a **quadratic** function.
>
> At `x = 6`: `60 − 36 =` **24 cm²**

<!-- ===== Linear patterns ===== -->

A tile pattern gives 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13. Find the rule for stage n and explain it.

> [!ANSWER]
> **The number of squares at stage n is one less than twice n: `2n − 1`.**
>
> Check: stage 2 → `2(2) − 1 = 3` ✓, stage 5 → `2(5) − 1 = 9` ✓
>
> It is a **linear polynomial** (degree 1), and the difference between consecutive terms is the constant **2**.

---

Using `2n − 1`, how many tiles at stage 15 and 26? Which stages have 21 and 47 tiles?

> [!ANSWER]
> - Stage 15 → `2(15) − 1 =` **29 tiles**
> - Stage 26 → `2(26) − 1 =` **51 tiles**
> - 21 tiles → `2n − 1 = 21` → **stage 11**
> - 47 tiles → `2n − 1 = 47` → **stage 24**

---

Bela has ₹100 and spends ₹5 a day. Write the expression for what's left on day n, and find when she has ₹40 and when she has nothing.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Left on day n = ₹(100 − 5n)**
>
> - ₹40 left: `100 − 5n = 40` → **day 12**
> - Nothing left: `100 − 5n = 0` → **day 20**
>
> On day 15: `100 − 75 =` **₹25**

---

An auto charges ₹25 for the first 2 km, then ₹15 per km. Write the fare for n km (n ≥ 2), and find the fare for 10 km.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Fare = `25 + 15(n − 2) = 15n − 5`**
>
> For 10 km: `25 + 15 × 8 =` **₹145** (or `15(10) − 5 = 145` ✓)

---

For how many km would that auto fare be ₹130?

> [!ANSWER]
> `15n − 5 = 130` → `15n = 135` → **n = 9 km**

---

A student has ₹500 saved and gets ₹150 pocket money each month. Write the amount in the nth month.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Amount = ₹(500 + 150n)** after n months.
>
> Month 1 → ₹650, month 2 → ₹800, month 3 → ₹950 — **a constant ₹150 more each month**.

---

A rally starts with 120 members and 9 drop out each hour. Write the number after n hours, and say when it empties.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Members = `120 − 9n`**
>
> After 1, 2, 3 hours: 111, 102, 93.
>
> Empty when `120 − 9n = 0` → `n = 13⅓`, so **during the 14th hour** the last members leave.

---

Sarita reads 20 pages a day of a 500-page book. How many are left after 15 days? Express as a linear pattern.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Pages left after n days = `500 − 20n`**
>
> After 15 days: `500 − 300 =` **200 pages**

---

The length of a rectangle is fixed at 13 cm. Write the area as a function of the breadth b, and evaluate for b = 12, 10, 8.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Area = `13b`** — a **linear** pattern, because the length is a constant.
>
> - `b = 12` → **156 cm²**
> - `b = 10` → **130 cm²**
> - `b = 8` → **104 cm²**
>
> Each 2 cm drop in breadth loses a constant 26 cm².

---

A box is 7 cm by 11 cm with height h. Write the volume, and evaluate for h = 5, 9, 13.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Volume = `7 × 11 × h = 77h`** — again **linear**, since two dimensions are fixed.
>
> - `h = 5` → **385 cm³**
> - `h = 9` → **693 cm³**
> - `h = 13` → **1001 cm³**

<!-- ===== Growth and decay ===== -->

Define linear growth and linear decay.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **Linear growth** — the quantity **increases by a CONSTANT amount** over equal intervals.
> - **Linear decay** — the quantity **decreases by a CONSTANT amount** over equal intervals.

---

Why is "increases every year" not enough to call something linear growth?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because it must increase by the **same amount** each interval — **not the same percentage**.
>
> A quantity growing by a fixed **percentage** each year (like compound interest) grows **exponentially**, and its graph curves upward rather than staying straight.

---

A journey costs `C(d) = 100 + 60d`. Find the cost of 15 km, and the distance for ₹700.

> [!ANSWER]
> - 15 km → `100 + 60(15) = 100 + 900 =` **₹1000**
> - ₹700 → `100 + 60d = 700` → `60d = 600` → **d = 10 km**
>
> The ₹100 is the **fixed** part and ₹60 the **rate per km** — so this is **linear growth**.

---

A tank's height is `h(t) = 3 − 0.5t` metres after t months. Find the height after 5 months and when it empties.

> [!ANSWER]
> - 5 months → `3 − 2.5 =` **0.5 m**
> - Empty → `3 − 0.5t = 0` → **t = 6 months**
>
> Height falls by a constant 0.5 m each month — **linear decay**.

---

A plant is 1.75 ft tall and grows 0.5 ft a month. Find its height after 7 months and write an expression.

> [!ANSWER]
> **`h(t) = 1.75 + 0.5t`**
>
> After 7 months: `1.75 + 3.5 =` **5.25 ft**
>
> **Linear growth**, because the increase is a constant 0.5 ft every month.

---

A ₹10,000 phone loses ₹800 of value a year. Find its value after 3 years and write an expression.

> [!ANSWER]
> **`v(t) = 10000 − 800t`**
>
> After 3 years: `10000 − 2400 =` **₹7,600**
>
> **Linear decay** — a constant ₹800 lost each year. (Real depreciation is usually a fixed *percentage*, which is not linear.)

---

A telecom recharge of ₹600 loses ₹15 a day. Write `b(x)` and find when the balance runs out.

> [!ANSWER]
> **`b(x) = 600 − 15x`** — **linear decay**, a constant ₹15 lost per day.
>
> Runs out when `600 − 15x = 0` → **x = 40 days**

<!-- ===== Finding a relationship ===== -->

A telecom bill is ₹350 for 10 GB and ₹550 for 20 GB. Find `y = ax + b`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 350 = 10a + b        …(1)
> 550 = 20a + b        …(2)
>
> From (1): b = 350 − 10a
> Substituting: 550 = 20a + 350 − 10a = 10a + 350
>               10a = 200  →  a = 20
>                 b = 350 − 200 = 150
> ```
>
> **`y = 20x + 150`**

---

What do the numbers 20 and 150 in that equation mean?

> [!ANSWER]
> - **a = 20** is the **cost per GB**
> - **b = 150** is the **fixed monthly fee** — what you'd pay for 0 GB
>
> **In `y = ax + b`, b is always the starting value and a is always the rate of change.**

---

A learning platform bills ₹400 for 10 modules and ₹500 for 14. Find a and b.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 400 = 10a + b
> 500 = 14a + b
> Subtracting: 100 = 4a  →  a = 25
>              b = 400 − 250 = 150
> ```
>
> **`y = 25x + 150`** — ₹25 per module plus a ₹150 fixed fee.

---

A gym bills ₹800 for 10 hours of court use and ₹1100 for 15 hours. Find a and b.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 800 = 10a + b
> 1100 = 15a + b
> Subtracting: 300 = 5a  →  a = 60
>              b = 800 − 600 = 200
> ```
>
> **`y = 60x + 200`** — ₹60 an hour plus a ₹200 monthly fee.

---

Given that ice melts at 0 °C = 32 °F and water boils at 100 °C = 212 °F, find the linear relation `C = a·F + b`.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> 0 = 32a + b        →  b = −32a
> 100 = 212a + b = 212a − 32a = 180a
>                    →  a = 100/180 = 5/9
>                    →  b = −32 × 5/9 = −160/9
> ```
>
> **`C = (5/9)F − 160/9 = (5/9)(F − 32)`** — the familiar conversion formula, recovered from just two data points.

<!-- ===== Graphing ===== -->

How do you plot a linear equation, and how do you check a point lies on it?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Find any TWO points, plot them, join and extend.**
>
> For `y = 2x + 1`: `x = 0 → y = 1` gives (0, 1); `x = 3 → y = 7` gives (3, 7).
>
> **To check a point, substitute it.** Is (7, 15) on the line? `2(7) + 1 = 15` ✓ — **yes**.

Complete this table for `y = 2x + 1`: x = 1, 2, 5, 7, 9, 12, 20.

> [!ANSWER]
> | x | 1 | 2 | 5 | 7 | 9 | 12 | 20 |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | y | 3 | 5 | 11 | 15 | 19 | 25 | 41 |

---

Points (−1, −3), (0, 0), (1, 3), (3, 9), (4, 12) lie on a line. What is its equation?

> [!ANSWER]
> **`y = 3x`** — in every pair the **y-coordinate is three times the x-coordinate**.

---

Points (−3, 6), (−2, 4), (0, 0), (1, −2), (2, −4), (3, −6) lie on a line. What is its equation?

> [!ANSWER]
> **`y = −2x`** — each y is **−2 times** its x.
>
> The negative multiplier is why the line **falls** from left to right.

<!-- ===== Slope and intercept ===== -->

In `y = ax + b`, what do a and b represent?

> [!ANSWER]
> - **a is the SLOPE** — how steep the line is
> - **b is the y-INTERCEPT** — the line cuts the y-axis at **(0, b)**

---

Why does every line `y = ax` pass through the origin?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because putting `x = 0` gives `y = a × 0 =` **0**.
>
> So **(0, 0) satisfies the equation** for every value of a.

---

Compare the graphs of `y = ½x`, `y = x` and `y = 2x`.

> [!ANSWER]
> All three pass through the origin, but with different steepness:
>
> - **`y = 2x` (a > 1)** — **steeper** than `y = x`
> - **`y = x` (a = 1)** — equally inclined to both axes, at 45°
> - **`y = ½x` (0 < a < 1)** — **less steep** than `y = x`
>
> **The bigger the value of a, the steeper the line.**

---

What happens when a is negative, as in `y = −x` and `y = −3x`?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The line goes DOWNWARDS from left to right.**
>
> As x increases, y decreases — which is exactly what a negative rate of change means.

---

How do slope and growth/decay connect?

> [!ANSWER]
> - **Positive slope → LINEAR GROWTH**
> - **Negative slope → LINEAR DECAY**
>
> **And the slope IS the constant difference between consecutive terms.** The tile sequence 1, 3, 5, 7… has constant difference 2, and its line `y = 2x − 1` has slope **2**.

---

Where do `y = 2x + 5`, `y = x + 3` and `y = 3x − 2` cut the y-axis?

> [!ANSWER]
> - `y = 2x + 5` → **(0, 5)**
> - `y = x + 3` → **(0, 3)**
> - `y = 3x − 2` → **(0, −2)** — below the origin, because b is negative
>
> **In every case the line cuts the y-axis at (0, b).**

---

What happens to the graph if you change b but keep a fixed?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The line shifts up or down but stays PARALLEL** to the original.
>
> `y = 2x − 1`, `y = 2x + 1` and `y = 2x + 5` are three parallel lines — same steepness, different starting heights.
>
> **Lines with equal slopes but different y-intercepts are parallel.**

---

What happens if you change a but keep b fixed?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The slope changes** — the lines become steeper or shallower — but **they all still cross the y-axis at the same point (0, b)**.
>
> They fan out from that single shared point.

---

Compare the graphs of `y = 3x + 1` and `y = −3x + 1`.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Both cut the y-axis at (0, 1)** — same b.
>
> But `y = 3x + 1` **rises** steeply while `y = −3x + 1` **falls** equally steeply. They are **mirror images** in the vertical line through (0, 1).

---

Summarise everything `y = ax + b` tells you at a glance.

> [!ANSWER]
> ```
> a > 0   → line rises   → linear GROWTH
> a < 0   → line falls    → linear DECAY
> |a| big → steep line
> |a| small → shallow line
> b       → where the line crosses the y-axis, at (0, b)
> b = 0   → the line passes through the origin
> same a  → parallel lines
> ```
