## The Earth as one system

**Life on Earth is powered by a constant flow of ENERGY and MATTER.** The **Sun is the main source of energy**; the **Earth's hot interior** and **chemical reactions in air, water and rocks** also drive these flows.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Earlier chapters treated these as separate topics.** This chapter puts them together as **ONE EARTH SYSTEM** made of interacting **SPHERES**:
>
> | Sphere | What it is | Indian examples |
> |---|---|---|
> | **GEOSPHERE** | solid rocks, soil, landforms, and the Earth's interior | Deccan plateau, Thar desert |
> | **HYDROSPHERE** | **liquid** water — oceans, rivers, lakes, groundwater | Ganga–Brahmaputra river system |
> | **CRYOSPHERE** | **solid** water — ice and snow | Himalayan glaciers, snow in Ladakh, polar ice caps |
> | **ATMOSPHERE** | the air surrounding the Earth | cleaner air in mountains and forests |
> | **BIOSPHERE** | all living organisms and their habitats | mangroves, forests, farms, ocean plankton, coral reefs |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The central idea of the chapter: A DISTURBANCE IN ONE SPHERE LEADS TO CHANGES IN OTHERS.**
>
> **A small example:** less snowfall in winter (**cryosphere**) → less water in the lake in summer (**hydrosphere**) → less water to support the grass (**biosphere**) → less food for sheep.
>
> **A large example:** warmer Arabian Sea water → **more evaporation** → **fluctuations in the southwest monsoon** → **floods in some regions of India, drought in others** → rising atmospheric temperature → **melting glaciers and polar ice** → **rising sea levels threatening coastal cities** → **habitat loss disturbing ecosystems**.
>
> **Nothing stays local.** That is what makes it a system rather than a collection of parts.

## 13.1 Uneven heating of the Earth

**Solar radiation reaches the Earth as ELECTROMAGNETIC (EM) WAVES**, which travel through a **vacuum** at the **speed of light, 3 × 10⁸ m/s**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Contrast with Chapter 10: SOUND waves are MECHANICAL and need a medium; EM waves do NOT.**
>
> That is exactly why sunlight crosses empty space to reach us while the Sun's sound never could.

**The ELECTROMAGNETIC SPECTRUM, from high frequency / short wavelength to low frequency / long wavelength:**

```
gamma rays → X-rays → UV → VISIBLE → infrared → microwaves → radio waves
    high energy                                              low energy
```

| Region | What it does at Earth |
|---|---|
| **Gamma rays and X-rays** | **very high energy, harmful to life** — **mostly filtered out by the upper atmosphere** |
| **Ultraviolet (UV)** | short-wavelength UV is **mostly absorbed by the OZONE layer**, protecting life and contributing some atmospheric heating |
| **Visible** | reaches the surface; **provides energy for PHOTOSYNTHESIS** — the primary source of food for most organisms — and partly warms land and water |
| **Infrared (IR)** | **warms the Earth's surface**, which then **re-radiates the heat** into the atmosphere |
| **Microwaves and radio waves** | **carry too little energy to warm the Earth significantly** |

**About 99% of the Sun's energy reaching Earth is in the UV, visible and IR range — and these three shape the Earth's climate and support life.**

> [!NOTE]
> **UV rays** have wavelengths from **100 nm to 400 nm** and **much higher energy than visible light**. Prolonged exposure can **damage eyes and skin and increase the risk of cancer** — hence UV-protective glasses and sunscreen.
>
> **They are also useful:** killing germs in **water purifiers**, and powering **fluorescent lights**.

### Insolation and the solar constant

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **INSOLATION = the amount of the Sun's radiation that reaches the Earth's surface.** It warms the surface and the atmosphere.
>
> **SOLAR CONSTANT = the average solar energy received per unit time per unit area PERPENDICULAR to the Sun's rays, AT THE TOP OF THE ATMOSPHERE.**
> ```
> solar constant ≈ 1.4 kW/m²  =  about 1400 J s⁻¹ m⁻²
> ```
> **It is the energy available BEFORE any absorption, scattering or reflection by the atmosphere.**
>
> **Maximum insolation at the SURFACE is lower — about 1 kW/m² under clear skies** — because gases, clouds and dust absorb and scatter some of it on the way down.

**Worked — energy on 1 m² in one hour at 1 kW/m²:**
```
E = intensity × area × time
  = 1000 J s⁻¹ m⁻² × 1 m² × 3600 s
  = 3,600,000 J = 3.6 × 10⁶ J
```
> [!TIP]
> **3.6 × 10⁶ J is roughly the energy needed to melt 5 kg of ice and heat the water to 100 °C** — and it is exactly **one unit of electricity** as shown on a household bill.
>
> **That is one square metre for one hour.** Scale it up and the reason solar power is worth pursuing becomes obvious: **even a fraction of the Thar desert covered with panels could supply India's electricity needs.**

**India, lying in the tropical and sub-tropical regions, receives abundant sunlight year-round** — which **drives the southwest monsoon** (shaping climate and agriculture) and gives **immense potential for solar energy**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Anna Mani**, India's pioneering atmospheric scientist, **mapped solar insolation across India in the 1950s**, and with **S. Rangarajan** published ***Solar Radiation Over India* in 1982** — the country's **first insolation atlas**. Her measurements showed India's vast solar potential, now being realised through large-scale solar deployment.

### 13.1.1 Albedo

**Different materials absorb and heat differently under sunlight.** Land heats faster than water; **dark surfaces absorb more sunlight while light-coloured surfaces reflect more and stay cooler** — which is why dark roads heat quickly and dark clothes feel hotter in summer.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ALBEDO = the fraction of solar radiation REFLECTED by a surface.** (From the Latin for *whiteness*.)
>
> **HIGH albedo → reflects more → stays COOL.**
> **LOW albedo → reflects less, absorbs more → heats up QUICKLY.**

| Material | Albedo |
|---|---|
| **Snow** | **0.80 – 0.90** |
| **Ice** | **0.50 – 0.70** |
| Crushed rock | 0.25 – 0.30 |
| Light-coloured soil | moderate |
| **Black soil** | **low** |
| **Ocean water** | **low** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Snow and ice reflect a large proportion of incoming radiation, which is a major reason the polar regions are so cold.** Black soil and ocean water absorb far more and are relatively warmer.
>
> **And this creates a feedback:** warming melts ice → the ice's **high albedo is replaced by the ocean's LOW albedo** → more radiation is absorbed → more warming. **A change that amplifies itself.**

**All objects radiate heat.** A **concrete house feels hot at night in summer** because the concrete **re-radiates** the heat it absorbed by day. **Traditional houses with thick mud and wooden walls stay cool** because they re-radiate far less.

> [!NOTE]
> **The URBAN HEAT ISLAND EFFECT.** Cities are **warmer than surrounding rural areas**, especially in summer and at night.
>
> **Why:** cities have more built-up area — **steel, concrete, brick, and roads of concrete and asphalt** — all of which **absorb solar radiation and retain heat**, then **re-radiate it**, warming the city. **Rural areas and forests stay cool through shade and plant transpiration.**
>
> **The consequence: increased energy demand for air conditioning**, further stressing urban ecosystems. **It shows how human land use can alter local climate.**

### 13.1.2 Latitude and the Earth's shape

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Because the Earth is SPHERICAL, the Sun's rays strike different latitudes at different ANGLES.**
>
> **At the equator the radiation is concentrated over a SMALLER area; at the poles the same amount is spread over a LARGER area.**
>
> **That is why equatorial regions stay relatively warm all year while polar regions are much colder** — and this **uneven heating creates the temperature difference between equator and poles that drives global winds and ocean currents.**

**The Earth's spherical shape and the TILT of its axis also give rise to the SEASONS and the changing length of daytime through the year.**

### 13.1.3 The atmosphere

**Composition: nitrogen 78%, oxygen 21%**, plus small amounts of **argon, carbon dioxide, water vapour** and other gases. **It is held in place by the Earth's gravity.**

| Layer | Altitude | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **TROPOSPHERE** | **0 – 12 km** | **all weather formation**; **temperature DECREASES with height** (about 6.5 °C per km) |
| **STRATOSPHERE** | **12 – 50 km** | contains the **OZONE layer**, which absorbs UV; **temperature INCREASES with height** |
| Mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere | above 50 km | minor role in surface climate; **outer space begins about 100 km up** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why weather is confined to the troposphere.** It is **heated FROM THE EARTH'S SURFACE**, so the air is **warmest at the bottom** — warm air rises, and that vertical mixing drives winds and storms.
>
> **In the stratosphere the temperature INCREASES with height**, so the warm air is already on top and there is **no vertical mixing**. That calms the layer and **keeps weather below it**.
>
> **The troposphere is highest above the equator and lowest above the poles** — because the stronger heating at the equator pushes it further up.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The atmosphere protects life in TWO ways:**
> 1. **It partly ABSORBS incoming solar radiation** — the ozone layer blocks harmful UV, and clouds and gases absorb some sunlight before it reaches the surface.
> 2. **It TRAPS outgoing heat.** The surface absorbs sunlight and **re-radiates it as INFRARED**; **greenhouse gases — CO₂, CH₄ and water vapour — absorb this re-radiated heat**, preventing it escaping to space.
>
> **Without the atmosphere the Earth would be too COLD for life.**
>
> **But excess CO₂ from human activities ENHANCES the greenhouse effect, causing global warming** — which unchecked could make the Earth uninhabitable.

> [!TIP]
> **Venus is HOTTER than Mercury even though Mercury is closer to the Sun** — because Venus has a thick atmosphere and an **uncontrolled greenhouse effect**. It is the clearest possible demonstration that **atmosphere matters more than distance**.

> [!NOTE]
> **The ozone layer and the Montreal Protocol.** Ozone acts as a **protective shield absorbing harmful UV radiation**. When ozone molecules are destroyed faster than they form, the layer thins.
>
> **In the late 20th century, human-made CHLOROFLUOROCARBONS (CFCs)** — used in refrigerators and aerosols — **caused severe ozone loss over Antarctica**, the **ozone hole**.
>
> **The MONTREAL PROTOCOL, a global agreement, reduced CFC use, and the ozone layer is now slowly recovering** — showing the power of international scientific cooperation.

> [!NOTE]
> **K. R. Ramanathan**, India's atmospheric scientist, **climbed to 18,000 feet in the Himalayas in 1934 to measure ozone levels** and found them **lower than expected**. His work laid the foundation for understanding **how UV absorption varies with altitude and pollution**, and he later led early monsoon forecasting efforts.

## 13.2 Uneven heating causes wind and ocean currents

**Wind is the movement of air from HIGH pressure to LOW pressure, and those pressure differences are mainly caused by the UNEVEN HEATING of the Earth's surface.**

### 13.2.1 Local winds

**In mountainous regions the slopes and the valley floor do not heat and cool at the same rate.**

| | **VALLEY BREEZE** (day) | **MOUNTAIN BREEZE** (night) |
|---|---|---|
| What heats/cools faster | **slopes facing the Sun heat more rapidly** than the valley floor | **slopes LOSE heat faster**, while the valley floor stays warmer |
| Air over the slopes | becomes **warm and RISES**, creating **low pressure** | becomes **cooler and DENSER** |
| Result | **cooler valley air moves UP the slopes** | **cool dense air flows DOWN into the valley** |

**These daily reversals are experienced in hilly regions like Shimla, Dehradun and other Himalayan valleys.** They **regulate temperature and moisture, and support soil and crop health.**

### 13.2.2 Planetary winds

**Uneven heating between the equator and the poles creates belts of low and high pressure, setting air in motion over long distances.**

```
POLAR HIGH (90°)     very low temperature → cold dense air SINKS
   ↓ air flows toward the sub-polar belt
SUB-POLAR LOW (60°)  air rises where poleward surface air meets cold polar air
   ↑
SUB-TROPICAL HIGH (30°)  air that rose at the equator has cooled, become denser
                          and SINKS here
   ↓ part flows back to the equator along the surface, part moves poleward
EQUATORIAL LOW (0°)  intense heating → warm air RISES, moves poleward aloft
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE CORIOLIS DEFLECTION: the Earth's ROTATION causes these winds to be deflected from their straight paths.**
>
> **To the RIGHT in the Northern Hemisphere; to the LEFT in the Southern Hemisphere.**
>
> **So planetary winds follow CURVED paths rather than moving directly from high to low pressure.**

### 13.2.3 Ocean currents

**OCEAN CURRENTS are the continuous movement of large masses of ocean water.**

**What drives them:**
- **Strong planetary winds DRAG the surface water because of FRICTION**, setting surface currents in motion
- **TEMPERATURE differences** — **warm equatorial water travels along the surface towards the poles**, while **colder, denser water flows back towards the equator through DEEPER levels**
- **SALINITY differences** — **lower-salinity water is less dense and stays near the surface; higher-salinity, denser water sinks** and moves at depth
- **The Earth's ROTATION**, which deflects the moving water into large circular patterns called **GYRES** — **clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere, anticlockwise in the Southern**
- **The distribution of LAND MASSES**, which blocks and redirects currents

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why ocean currents matter: by TRANSPORTING HEAT from the equator towards the poles, they REDUCE TEMPERATURE DIFFERENCES across the planet.**
>
> **Example: the NORTH ATLANTIC DRIFT**, an extension of the **Gulf Stream** (which carries warm water from the southern part of the North American east coast across the Atlantic), **flows towards north-western Europe and keeps many ports ICE-FREE in winter, even at high latitudes.**
>
> **They also support massive ecosystems by transporting NUTRIENTS.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Scientists at the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM), Pune** run advanced computer models that **couple the energy flows between atmosphere, oceans, land and ice** to simulate the **Indian monsoon**. They use data from **satellites, buoys in the Indian Ocean and stations in Antarctica** to improve seasonal forecasts and study how global warming may change monsoon rainfall across India.

## 13.3 Biogeochemical cycles

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A BIOGEOCHEMICAL CYCLE is the cyclic movement of matter and energy between the ABIOTIC (non-living) and BIOTIC (living) components of the Earth.**
>
> **Why cycles are necessary:** the Earth receives a constant flow of **energy** from the Sun, but it does **not** receive a supply of **matter**. **The atoms available are all the atoms there will ever be** — so carbon, nitrogen and oxygen must be **recycled** to remain available to support life.

### 13.3.1 The water cycle

```
EVAPORATION from oceans, rivers, lakes  +  TRANSPIRATION from plants
   → CONDENSATION into clouds
   → PRECIPITATION as rain, hail or snow
   → RUN OFF back to the ocean, and INFILTRATION into soil and rock as GROUNDWATER
```

**Water dissolves minerals from soil and rocks, supports all terrestrial organisms, and transports nutrients to the oceans to support marine life.**

> [!WARNING]
> **How climate change is disrupting it:**
> - **a warmer atmosphere holds MORE MOISTURE** → **heavier rains in some areas** (intensified monsoons) and **droughts elsewhere**
> - **melting glaciers add more water to rivers**, raising sea levels and threatening coastal cities such as **Mumbai and Chennai**
> - **sudden bursts of intense rainfall** cause **more RUN OFF, which erodes soil**, and **less INFILTRATION, which reduces groundwater recharge** — making agriculture harder in dry months
>
> **Notice this single cycle links ALL FIVE SPHERES:** cryosphere (glaciers), hydrosphere (rivers and oceans), atmosphere (moisture), geosphere (soil erosion and infiltration) and biosphere (crops and fisheries).

### 13.3.2 The carbon cycle

**Carbon forms the BACKBONE of life — every protein, carbohydrate, fat and DNA molecule contains it.** It circulates between the **atmosphere** (CO₂ gas), **biosphere** (plants and animals), **geosphere** (carbonate rocks and fossil fuels) and **hydrosphere** (dissolved CO₂ and marine shells).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Different parts of the carbon cycle operate on VERY DIFFERENT TIME SCALES — and that mismatch is the whole problem.**
>
> **THE FAST CYCLE — days to years:**
> ```
> plants convert atmospheric CO₂ into glucose by PHOTOSYNTHESIS
> RESPIRATION releases CO₂ back
> animals eat plants and other animals
> when organisms die, DECOMPOSITION returns CO₂ to the air
> ```
> **THE SLOW CYCLE — millions of years:**
> ```
> dead plants and animals are BURIED and converted into FOSSIL FUELS — coal, oil, gas
> ```
> **The imbalance: fossil fuels took MILLIONS OF YEARS to form, and we burn them on a VERY SHORT time scale**, releasing carbon back as CO₂ far faster than it was ever stored.

**The ocean's role:** atmosphere and ocean **continuously exchange CO₂**. **Ocean water absorbs atmospheric CO₂ to form carbonate and bicarbonate ions**; **phytoplankton use them for photosynthesis**, and **some marine organisms use them to form shells**. When those organisms die they **sink to the ocean floor, storing carbon for a long period**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Carbon makes up about 49% of the dry weight of living organisms.** Of the total global carbon, **71% is in the OCEANS** — which is why the oceanic reservoir regulates atmospheric CO₂. **The atmosphere holds only about 1% of the global total.**

> [!WARNING]
> **Human activities — burning fossil fuels and deforestation — have raised atmospheric CO₂ by about 35% since 1960**, from about **315 ppm to 420 ppm** (parts per million). **This is an unprecedented rise in the history of human civilisation.** The record is the **KEELING CURVE**, whose **sawtooth pattern** comes from **seasonal plant growth in the Northern Hemisphere absorbing CO₂ each year**.
>
> **Some CO₂ is necessary to keep the Earth warm enough for life — the BALANCE is what is critical.** Excess CO₂ intensifies the greenhouse effect, causing **global warming, melting glaciers and Arctic sea ice, rising sea levels and more extreme weather.** In India this may mean **more intense monsoons** (warmer air holds more moisture) and **threats to agriculture from changing rainfall patterns**.

### 13.3.3 The nitrogen cycle

**Nitrogen is essential for making PROTEINS and NUCLEIC ACIDS in all living organisms, and the atmosphere is its largest reservoir.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The problem: nitrogen gas (N₂) is rather NON-REACTIVE and CANNOT be used directly by plants and animals.** (Recall the triple bond from Chapter 9 — it takes a great deal of energy to break.)
>
> **It must first be converted into SOLUBLE COMPOUNDS that living beings can absorb.**

| Step | What happens | Organisms |
|---|---|---|
| **NITROGEN FIXATION** | atmospheric **N₂ → ammonia (NH₃)** | **Rhizobium** in the root nodules of legumes; **Azotobacter** in soil |
| **NITRIFICATION** | **ammonia → nitrite (NO₂⁻) → nitrate (NO₃⁻)** | **Nitrosomonas** then **Nitrobacter** |
| **ASSIMILATION** | plants **absorb** nitrogen compounds from soil; animals get nitrogen by **eating** plants or other animals | — |
| **AMMONIFICATION** | dead organisms and waste are broken down, **returning ammonia to the soil** | decomposer bacteria and fungi |
| **DENITRIFICATION** | **nitrates → nitrogen gas**, completing the cycle | **Pseudomonas** |

**LIGHTNING also fixes a small amount of nitrogen, producing nitrogen oxides.**

> [!NOTE]
> **The Haber–Bosch process** (early 1900s) makes **ammonia from atmospheric nitrogen** artificially, and produces **most of the fertilisers used today**. This **"Bread from Air"** revolutionised agriculture, **enabling India's Green Revolution and feeding billions** — **more than half the nitrogen atoms in the human body come from it.**
>
> **The costs:** it is **energy-intensive, using about 1–2% of global energy**, and the **overuse of fertilisers has degraded soil and water.**

### 13.3.4 The oxygen cycle

**Oxygen is one of the Earth's most abundant elements — about 21% of the atmosphere is free O₂**, and it is an essential component of **carbohydrates, proteins, nucleic acids and fats**. It also exists **combined** — as **metal oxides and minerals in the crust**, and as **carbon dioxide in the air**.

```
CONSUMED by:  RESPIRATION (plants and animals)  +  COMBUSTION of fuels
                                   ↓ releasing CO₂
PRODUCED by:  PHOTOSYNTHESIS — plants use sunlight, water and CO₂ to
              form glucose and release O₂
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **This balance between CONSUMPTION (respiration and combustion) and PRODUCTION (photosynthesis) circulates oxygen between the atmosphere, land, oceans and living organisms.**
>
> **Notice the carbon and oxygen cycles are the SAME two reactions read in opposite directions** — photosynthesis takes CO₂ and gives O₂; respiration and combustion take O₂ and give CO₂. **You cannot disturb one without disturbing the other.**

## 13.4 Human impact on Earth's processes

| Activity | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Burning fossil fuels, deforestation** | **saturate natural carbon sinks** like forests and oceans; excess CO₂ **intensifies greenhouse warming**, disrupting the carbon cycle |
| **Excess atmospheric CO₂** | increases ocean absorption, making **sea water more ACIDIC** — threatening **plankton and coral reefs** and disrupting marine ecosystems |
| **Warmer ocean water** | **REDUCES the ocean's capacity to absorb CO₂**, weakening it as a carbon sink |
| **Overuse of fertilisers** | excess **nitrates reach rivers and lakes**, causing **algal blooms that DEPLETE OXYGEN and kill fish** — **EUTROPHICATION**, threatening water bodies and coastal fisheries |
| **Deforestation** | **less photosynthesis**, **less transpiration → decline in local rainfall**, **altered surface albedo**, **more soil erosion** without roots to hold the soil, **habitat destruction and biodiversity loss** |
| **Vehicular emissions** | react with sunlight to form **ground-level SMOG** and **ground-level OZONE, which is harmful to health** |

> [!WARNING]
> **Two feedbacks that make things worse, not better:**
> 1. **Warmer oceans absorb LESS CO₂** — so warming reduces the very process that was slowing it.
> 2. **Melting ice replaces high-albedo surfaces with low-albedo ones** — so warming increases the absorption that causes warming.
>
> **A feedback that amplifies itself is far more dangerous than a one-off change.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Ozone is protective in the STRATOSPHERE and harmful at GROUND LEVEL.**
>
> **The same molecule, opposite verdicts** — because in the stratosphere it **blocks UV radiation far above us**, while at ground level we **breathe it**.

### What can be done

- **Conserving energy and energy resources**
- **Switching to renewable energy** — solar and wind
- **Planting trees**
- **Saving water** and practising **sustainable farming**
- **Reducing waste, reusing and recycling**

**India has planted billions of trees, expanded solar and renewable energy significantly, and promoted sustainable farming practices.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **What international cooperation has and has not achieved:**
> - **The MONTREAL PROTOCOL** reduced CFCs and **started the recovery of the ozone layer** — a genuine success.
> - **The KYOTO PROTOCOL and the PARIS AGREEMENT**, under which countries were to reduce CO₂ emissions, **have been LESS SUCCESSFUL**.
>
> **Why the difference is worth thinking about:** CFCs came from a handful of specific products with available substitutes. **CO₂ comes from almost everything we do**, which makes agreement far harder to reach and far harder to keep.

> [!NOTE]
> **Mission LiFE (Lifestyle for Environment)**, an India-led global initiative introduced at the **United Nations Climate Change Conference in 2021**, encourages **mindful, eco-friendly lifestyles**.
>
> **Traditional practices and ancient texts in India have long recognised that the Earth functions as an INTERCONNECTED SYSTEM**, working through the **flow of energy and the cycling of matter across spheres**. **Unsustainable consumption disturbs that balance.** Mission LiFE highlights how the actions of individuals and communities can help build a sustainable future.
