## Why reproduction matters

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **REPRODUCTION is the biological process by which living beings produce new individuals of their own kind.**
>
> **Every organism has a definite life span** — born, grows, matures, reproduces, dies. **Reproduction is how life on Earth continues** despite that. A mango tree dies, but its seeds grow into new mango plants.

| | **ASEXUAL** | **SEXUAL** |
|---|---|---|
| Parents | **ONE** | **TWO** |
| Offspring | **almost exact copies** of the parent | inherit a **mix** of characteristics |
| Variation | **none** | **plenty** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why variation matters:** the small differences between parents and offspring, **accumulated over many generations, help living beings ADAPT to changing environments** — and sometimes give rise to **new species**.

## 11.1 Asexual reproduction

**Seen in unicellular organisms — bacteria, amoeba, yeast — in simple multicellular animals like hydra and sponges, and in many plants.**

### Vegetative propagation

**New plants arise from the VEGETATIVE (growing) parts of an existing plant**, without seeds:
- **potato and ginger** — fleshy underground stems sprout new plants
- **money plant and sugarcane** — stem cuttings grow into new plants
- **Bryophyllum** — **leaves** sprout tiny plantlets

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **It involves only ONE parent, so it produces GENETICALLY IDENTICAL individuals — CLONES.**

### 11.1.1 Methods used in agriculture

| Method | What is done | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **CUTTING** | cut overgrown branches at the end of the growing season; remove leaves from the lower half; insert about half the length into compost-rich soil at **45–60°** | the cutting must carry **nodes**, since new shoots and roots arise from them |
| **GRAFTING** | make a **slit** in a twig of a healthy rooted plant (A), **insert a stem cutting** of the desired plant (B), and **wrap it** with cloth or film until it heals | A supplies the **roots**, B supplies the **desired variety** — so you get a hardy root system carrying good fruit or flowers |
| **LAYERING** | **bury the middle part** of a flexible twig under the soil while it is still attached; after **10–15 days roots develop**; then cut it free | the twig is fed by the parent plant while it grows roots, so it cannot die of thirst in the meantime |
| **TISSUE CULTURE** | mass-produce plantlets from the **shoot tip (apical meristem)** | the meristem is usually **virus-free**, so it **eliminates virus-infected plants** and ensures high yields — as in banana farming |

> [!TIP]
> **Why these methods are so useful:** every plant produced is **genetically identical** to a parent chosen for its desirable characters. A farmer who finds one outstanding mango tree can reproduce **exactly that tree** thousands of times — something seeds could never guarantee.
>
> **Wrap the graft** to keep pests out until the wound heals, and **cut the other branches of plant A** so its energy goes into the graft.

> [!NOTE]
> **Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs)** under the **Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR)** train farmers in modern grafting, helping them grow high-yield fruits and boost income.

### Budding

**In yeast and hydra, repeated cell division at a specific site on the parent's body produces a small outgrowth — a BUD. The bud enlarges and SEPARATES from the parent to live independently.**

**In hydra, many buds often grow on the parent at the same time.** Yeast in warm sugar solution shows **small round outgrowths** emerging from the parent cells under a microscope.

### Spore formation

**Moist bread kept warm and dark grows MOULD in about three days.** Under a microscope you see **thread-like HYPHAE with a round SAC at the tip**, and **tiny round SPORES inside the sac**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Where the mould came from: SPORES already present in the AIR** settled on the moist bread and began reproducing rapidly.
>
> **Why spores work so well:**
> - produced in **huge numbers** — millions from one mould colony
> - **lightweight and usually single-celled**, so they **float easily on air currents**
> - they simply **wait for moisture and nutrients**, then germinate quickly

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Louis Pasteur's experiments proved that NEW LIFE ALWAYS COMES FROM PRE-EXISTING LIFE.** For a long time people believed living things arose **spontaneously** from non-living matter.
>
> His work founded the **germ theory of disease**, strengthened the **cell theory** (all cells arise from pre-existing cells), and led to **food and instrument sterilisation**.

> [!TIP]
> **Why we refrigerate food.** Mould spores need **warmth and moisture** to grow — 25–35 °C is ideal. **Lower temperatures slow or stop their reproduction.** Before refrigerators became common about 100 years ago, fresh food lasted only **1–2 days**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Fungi are not only spoilers.** They grow very fast by spore formation and **degrade organic wastes and pollutants**, help **remove heavy metals from industrial wastes**, and give us many **antibiotics** — penicillin and amoxicillin among them.

### What all asexual reproduction has in common

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The central process behind ALL asexual reproduction is MITOSIS** — the division that produces two daughter cells with **the same number of chromosomes, identical to the parent cell**.
>
> **That is exactly why the offspring are genetically identical to the parent and are called CLONES.**
>
> **The advantage: it is FAST**, and lets an organism increase its population quickly when conditions are favourable — no partner needed, no gametes to make, no waiting.

## 11.2 Sexual reproduction

**Two parents, both contributing genetic material.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The problem this creates:** if each generation received the **full set** of chromosomes from **both** parents, **the chromosome number would DOUBLE every generation**.
>
> **The solution: MEIOSIS**, which halves the chromosome number in the gametes.

### 11.2.1 How meiosis creates variation

**Each species has a fixed number of chromosomes. Humans have 23 PAIRS — 46 in all — with one chromosome of each pair coming from each parent.**

**In meiosis, the chromosomes of each pair SEPARATE, so each gamete receives only ONE chromosome from each pair. Every human gamete therefore has 23 chromosomes.**

- **Animals:** male gametes are **sperm**, female gametes are **eggs**
- **Plants:** the **pollen grain** carries male gametes to an **ovule**, which contains the egg

**How many combinations?** With just **3 pairs** of contrasting characters, picking one from each pair gives:

```
2 × 2 × 2 = 8 possible combinations
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Now scale that up to 23 pairs, each carrying information for many characters.** The number of possible combinations is astronomical.
>
> **This random mixing makes children genetically different from their parents AND from their siblings. Each of us receives a unique combination of chromosomes.**
>
> **Why it matters: variation helps some individuals adapt better to changing environments**, and over time this contributes to **evolution**. Examples in humans: tolerating **low oxygen at high altitudes**, or being able to **digest milk in adulthood**.

### 11.2.2 Sexual reproduction in flowering plants

**Flowering plants — ANGIOSPERMS — are the most diverse group of plants on Earth, and the FLOWER is their reproductive organ.**

**The four whorls of a complete flower, from outside in:**

| Part | Description | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **SEPAL** | the outermost whorl, thin, flat, **green** | **protects the flower in the bud stage**, and the other parts when it blooms |
| **PETAL** | the coloured projections | **attract pollinators** — colour and fragrance are advertising |
| **STAMEN** — the **MALE** part | **filament** + **anther** | the **anther produces POLLEN GRAINS containing male gametes** |
| **PISTIL** — the **FEMALE** part | **stigma** + **style** + **ovary** | receives pollen and holds the eggs |

**Inside the pistil:**
- **STIGMA** — at the tip, often **flat and/or sticky**, to catch and hold pollen
- **STYLE** — a thin long tube connecting stigma to ovary
- **OVARY** — contains **OVULES**, and **each ovule has an egg cell (female gamete)**

### The pollination experiment

**On one pea plant, treat five flowers differently and see which form fruit:**

| Treatment | Fruit formed? |
|---|---|
| Flower **bud** wrapped in muslin | **YES** |
| Flower **bud with stamens REMOVED**, wrapped in muslin | **NO** |
| Open **flower with stamens removed**, wrapped in muslin | **YES** |
| Open **flower** wrapped in muslin | **YES** |
| Open **flower left uncovered** | **YES** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Only ONE treatment fails — the BUD whose stamens were removed BEFORE it opened.**
>
> **Why that one and not the open flower with stamens removed?** In pea, **pollination happens inside the bud, before the flower opens**. Removing the stamens from an already-open flower is too late — pollination has already occurred. Removing them from a bud prevents it entirely.
>
> **Conclusion: the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma is NECESSARY for fruit formation.** That transfer is **POLLINATION**.

### 11.2.3 Self- and cross-pollination

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **SELF-POLLINATION:** pollen is transferred to the stigma of the **same flower**, or another flower **of the same plant**.
> - **CROSS-POLLINATION:** pollen is transferred from a flower of **one plant** to the stigma of a flower of **another plant of the same kind**.
>
> **Cross-pollination generates far more variation**, because it combines genetic material from two different individuals.

### 11.2.4 Pollination strategies

**Pollination depends on external agents called POLLINATORS.**

| Agent | Examples | Flower adaptations |
|---|---|---|
| **WIND** | wheat, maize, rice | pollen **light, small and produced in huge numbers**; stigma **long and feathery** to trap it |
| **WATER** | Vallisneria, Hydrilla | currents carry the pollen from flower to flower |
| **INSECTS** (bees, butterflies) | sunflower, hibiscus, marigold | **brightly coloured**, produce **nectar** and **fragrance**; pollen **large, sticky or spiny** to cling to the insect; stigma **sticky** to receive it |
| **BIRDS** | coral tree, hibiscus — visited by Indian white-eye and sunbirds | large, sturdy, brightly coloured flowers |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Every adaptation follows from the agent.** Wind cannot aim, so wind-pollinated flowers make **enormous amounts of light pollen** and spread out big feathery nets to catch it — and have no need of petals, scent or nectar. Insects can aim, so insect-pollinated flowers **pay them with nectar** and **advertise with colour and scent**, and need far less pollen.

**Comparing the two strategies:**

| Strategy | Pollen grains per flower | Average seeds formed |
|---|---|---|
| **Wind-pollinated grasses** (maize, wheat) | **500,000 – 1,000,000** | **50 – 200** |
| **Insect-pollinated plants** (sunflower) | **20,000 – 40,000** | **800 – 1,000** |

> [!TIP]
> **The pollen-to-seed ratio is staggeringly different** — roughly **5000 grains per seed** for wind against about **30 per seed** for insects. **Insect pollination is far more EFFICIENT.**
>
> **So why does wind pollination survive at all?** Because **producing vast quantities of pollen is still an effective strategy** when the plant does not have to make nectar, scent or showy petals, and does not depend on the presence of any animal. **Waste is cheap if the alternative is expensive.**

### 11.2.5 Fertilisation and seed formation

```
pollen lands on a compatible stigma
   → the pollen grain produces a POLLEN TUBE
   → the tube grows down through the STYLE into the OVARY
   → the male gamete travels down it to the OVULE
   → it FUSES with the egg cell           ← FERTILISATION
   → the fertilised egg is a ZYGOTE, which develops into an EMBRYO
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **What each part becomes:**
> ```
> OVULE  →  SEED
> OVARY  →  FRUIT
> ```
> **Seeds are dispersed by wind, water or animals.** When water, air and temperature are favourable, the seed **germinates** into a new plant.

> [!NOTE]
> **P. Maheshwari**, known as the **"Father of Indian Embryology"**, developed the technique of **in-vitro fertilisation in flowering plants**, successfully fusing an egg and a male gamete **in a test tube** to create new hybrid plants. He was among the first to grow **plant embryos on artificial nutrient media**, and his 1950 book *An Introduction to the Embryology of Angiosperms* is still used worldwide.

> [!NOTE]
> **Plant breeding** applies all this. In **selective breeding**, farmers choose plants with desirable characters to reproduce. **Artificial hybridisation** means **removing the stamens, bagging the flower to prevent self-pollination, and manually transferring the desired pollen**. **Genetic engineering** inserts genetic material for desired characters directly into the DNA. These give **high-yielding and disease-resistant varieties**.

## 11.3 Sexual reproduction in animals

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **EXTERNAL fertilisation:** the female releases eggs into the water and the male releases sperm over them — **frogs and most fish**.
> - **INTERNAL fertilisation:** fertilisation occurs **inside the female's body** — **reptiles, birds and mammals**.
>
> **Survival is generally higher with internal fertilisation**, because the fertilised egg or embryo is **better protected**.

| Animal | Habitat | Fertilisation | Eggs produced | Survival of young |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fish** | water | **external** | 100s–1000s at a time | **low** |
| **Frog** | water/land | **external** | **5,000–50,000** at a time | **low** |
| **Lizard** | land | **internal** | 2–20 at a time | moderate |
| **Bird** | water/land | **internal** | **1–15** at a time | **moderate to high** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why external fertilisation means so many eggs.** In open water, **many eggs are destroyed by currents or eaten** before they can be fertilised, and many more afterwards. **A very low survival rate is compensated by very large numbers.**
>
> **Internal fertilisation protects the embryo, so far fewer eggs are needed.** The strategy shifts from **quantity** to **care**.

## 11.4 Variations in reproductive strategy

**All animals face the same two challenges: getting the male and female gametes to MEET, and getting the young to SURVIVE long enough to reproduce.**

| Group | Strategy |
|---|---|
| **Fish, amphibians, insects** | eggs contain **just enough yolk to produce a LARVA**. The larva hatches and **feeds itself** on organic wastes, growing until enough nutrition is accumulated; then it **transforms into the adult body** — as in butterflies and frogs |
| **Reptiles and birds** | each egg contains **enough yolk to nourish the embryo all the way to hatching** |
| **Mammals** | the zygote **grows and develops inside the female body**, and the young are fed on **breast milk** after birth |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why a larval stage exists at all:** the mother's body **cannot provide enough yolk for thousands of eggs**. So the egg carries only enough to make a **feeding stage** — the larva — which then gathers its own nutrition. **The larva is an intermediate feeding stage in development, not a small adult.**

**In some species the young are ready to find their own food at birth; in others an extended period of post-hatching or post-birth feeding and care is needed** — depending largely on how long the embryo developed inside the egg or the mother.

## 11.5 Reproduction in human beings

### 11.5.2 The male reproductive system

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **TESTES** (two oval organs) | produce **SPERM**, and a **hormone** that controls sperm production and causes the physical changes of puberty |
| **SCROTUM** (pouch of skin holding the testes) | keeps the testes **slightly COOLER than normal body temperature**, which is **necessary for sperm formation** |
| **VAS DEFERENS** | a long tube carrying sperm from the testes, opening into the urethra |
| **URETHRA** | the **common passage** for urine and sperm |
| **SEMINAL VESICLES and PROSTATE GLAND** | add **fluids that nourish the sperm** and help them stay active and move |

**Each sperm has a HEAD containing genetic material and a long TAIL that helps it swim towards the egg.**

### 11.5.3 The female reproductive system

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| **OVARIES** (a pair) | produce **EGGS** and release **hormones** that bring about the changes of puberty |
| **OVIDUCTS (fallopian tubes)** | connect each ovary to the uterus |
| **UTERUS** | a bag-like structure where the **foetus develops** |
| **CERVIX** | the narrow passage through which the uterus opens into the vagina |
| **VAGINA** | the passage to the outside |

### 11.5.4 Gametogenesis

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **GAMETOGENESIS = the formation of gametes**, taking place in the **testes and the ovaries**, by **MEIOSIS**.
>
> **Human cells have 46 chromosomes; sperm and eggs have only 23.** That halving is essential so that **when they combine, the zygote has the same number as the parents — 46.**

| Feature | **Sperm** | **Egg** |
|---|---|---|
| Size | **very small** | **large** |
| Number produced | **millions** | **few** |
| Stored nutrients | **absent** | **present** |
| Motility | **actively motile** | **non-motile** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **This asymmetry is seen across most animals, and it makes sense of itself:** the sperm's whole job is **to travel**, so it is stripped down to genetic material plus a tail. The egg's job is **to nourish the early embryo**, so it is large and stocked with nutrients — and there is no point making millions of expensive ones.

### 11.5.5 When a sperm meets an egg

**At birth, a girl's ovaries already contain millions of immature eggs.**

```
from puberty, usually ONE mature egg is released each month  ← OVULATION
before ovulation the uterus lining becomes THICK
the egg travels from the ovary into the oviduct
millions of sperm enter through the vagina and swim up the tract
if one fuses with the egg in the oviduct → a ZYGOTE forms   ← FERTILISATION
the uterus lining becomes thicker and richer in blood vessels
the zygote divides by MITOSIS while travelling to the uterus
it IMPLANTS into the uterus lining to receive nourishment
                                        ← this marks the beginning of PREGNANCY
```

### 11.5.6 When the egg is not fertilised

**An unfertilised egg remains viable for about a day and then degenerates.** The thickened uterine lining is **no longer needed, so it SHEDS** — leaving the body through the vagina along with some blood.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **This is MENSTRUATION (a period), usually lasting 3 to 7 days.**
>
> **The cycle of ovulation → uterine preparation → menstruation repeats every 21–35 days (often about 28)**, beginning at puberty between about **10–14 years** and continuing until **menopause, around age 50**.

**A typical 28-day cycle:**

| Days | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1–5** | **MENSTRUATION** — the uterine lining is shed |
| **6–14** | the lining **gradually rebuilds**; an egg starts maturing in the ovary |
| **14** | **OVULATION** — the ovary releases a mature egg |
| **15–28** | the lining becomes **thicker and rich with blood vessels**; if no fertilisation happens it starts breaking down around day 28 and the cycle repeats |

> [!NOTE]
> **What determines a baby's biological sex.** Everyone has two sex chromosomes: **females XX, males XY**. **The mother always contributes an X**; **the father contributes either an X (giving XX, female) or a Y (giving XY, male)**.
>
> **So the father's gamete determines the sex of the baby.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Menstrual hygiene.** Use menstrual products (preferably biodegradable pads, or cups); **clean the genital area with water — avoid soap**, which disrupts the natural bacterial balance; **wash hands with soap before and after** changing a pad; **wrap used products in paper and bin them — never flush them**; dry reusable pads completely before reuse; and **change pads every 4–6 hours**, or more often with heavy flow.
>
> **Menstruation is a sign of a healthy reproductive system, not something to be ashamed of.**

### 11.5.7 Pregnancy and childbirth

**Human pregnancy lasts about NINE MONTHS, divided into three TRIMESTERS.**

| Trimester | What happens |
|---|---|
| **First** | the fertilised egg develops into an **EMBRYO** in the first two months, and **major organs start forming**. From about the **ninth week** it is called a **FOETUS** |
| **Second** | the foetus **grows bigger and stronger**; the mother can usually **feel its movements** |
| **Third** | the baby **grows rapidly** and prepares for life outside the womb |

**At childbirth, strong contractions of the uterine muscles push the foetus out through the birth canal.** If a normal vaginal birth is not possible or safe for mother or foetus, doctors may use **medical or surgical procedures** to deliver the baby safely.

**After birth: BREASTFEEDING is essential** — a mother's milk provides **complete nutrition and protects the infant from many diseases**. Newborns must be **kept warm, vaccinated on time and handled gently**.

### 11.5.8 The mother's health

**A balanced diet rich in proteins, vitamins and minerals; regular medical check-ups; light exercise and rest as advised.** She should **avoid smoking, alcohol and any medicine without medical advice**. **Emotional well-being matters too**, and family support helps.

> [!NOTE]
> **Post-partum depression** — a form of anxiety and fatigue some mothers experience after delivery. **It is a recognised condition that can be treated.** Mothers who feel low or overwhelmed should be encouraged to talk to a doctor, nurse or ASHA worker.
>
> **More than 10 lakh ASHA (Accredited Social Health Activist) workers** across India — community health workers trained under the **National Health Mission** — promote hygiene, immunisation and family planning, especially in rural areas, and advise on maternal care, safe deliveries and contraception.

> [!NOTE]
> **IVF (In-Vitro Fertilisation)** combines an egg and a sperm **outside the body**, usually in a laboratory dish; the fertilised egg is then implanted in the uterus. A baby born this way is commonly called a **test tube baby**, though the process happens in a culture dish, not a test tube.
>
> **In 1978, Subhash Mukhopadhyay of Kolkata pioneered India's first test tube baby, Kanupriya Agarwal (nicknamed Durga).**

### 11.5.9 Sexual and emotional maturity

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SEXUAL MATURITY — the production of sperm in boys and menstrual cycles in girls — happens GRADUALLY during adolescence. EMOTIONAL MATURITY takes LONGER.**
>
> **Being emotionally mature means handling feelings, communicating clearly and making thoughtful decisions.** **Being physically capable of reproduction does NOT mean being ready for adult responsibilities.**

### 11.5.10 Preventing unwanted pregnancy and infection

**SEXUALLY TRANSMITTED INFECTIONS (STIs)** pass from an infected person to an uninfected one through close physical contact. They include **gonorrhoea, herpes, syphilis, genital warts, and HIV** (which can eventually lead to AIDS). **Some are not curable yet.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Using CONDOMS can prevent the transmission of STIs AND prevent pregnancy — the only method that does both.**

| Contraceptive method | How it works | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Barrier** — condoms, vaginal covers | **stop sperm reaching the egg** | also prevent STIs |
| **Oral pills** | **alter hormones** so the release of eggs changes | may have side effects |
| **IUDs** such as the **copper-T** | placed in the uterus to prevent pregnancy | may sometimes irritate the uterus |
| **Surgical** | **blocking the vas deferens** in males or the **fallopian tubes** in females | so sperm and egg cannot meet |

**ABORTION** — surgical removal of an unwanted pregnancy, generally only within the **first trimester**, when the embryo is very small.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Sex-selective abortion, driven by a preference for a particular gender, causes a marked IMBALANCE in the societal SEX RATIO.**
>
> **This is why PRENATAL SEX DETERMINATION IS STRICTLY PROHIBITED BY LAW IN INDIA** — to maintain a healthy sex ratio.

> [!NOTE]
> **Indian scientists at the Central Drug Research Institute, Lucknow, developed the world's first NON-STEROIDAL, NON-HORMONAL oral contraceptive pill.** Taken **once weekly**, it avoids side effects like weight gain, nausea or headaches, and is provided **free** through the National Family Planning Programme.
