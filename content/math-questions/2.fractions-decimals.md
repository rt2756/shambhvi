<!-- ===== What a fraction means ===== -->

A pizza is cut into **8 equal slices** and you eat **3**. Write the fraction you ate, and say what the top and bottom numbers mean.

> [!ANSWER]
> You ate **3/8** of the pizza.
>
> **Numerator 3** (top) = how many slices you took. **Denominator 8** (bottom) = how many equal slices make **one whole** pizza.

---

The fraction bar means **divide**. Write **12/4** as a whole number.

> [!ANSWER]
> `12/4` means `12 ÷ 4` → **3**.
>
> Twelve slices shared into groups of 4 gives 3 whole pizzas.

---

Find **1/2 of 10**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **"of" means multiply:** `1/2 × 10 = 10/2 = ` **5**.
>
> Half of 10 slices is 5 slices.

---

Find **3/4 of 20**.

> [!ANSWER]
> `3/4 × 20 = (3 × 20)/4 = 60/4 = ` **15**.
>
> One quarter of 20 is 5, and we want **3** quarters → `3 × 5 = 15`.

---

<!-- ===== Types of fractions ===== -->

Sort these into **proper**, **improper**, or **mixed**: `3/5`, `7/4`, `2 1/3`.

> [!ANSWER]
> **`3/5` — proper:** top < bottom, so it's **less than one** whole pizza.
>
> **`7/4` — improper:** top > bottom, so it's **more than one** pizza.
>
> **`2 1/3` — mixed:** a whole number **plus** a fraction.

---

Are **2/7** and **5/7** *like* or *unlike* fractions? Why?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Like fractions** — same denominator (**7**).
>
> Both pizzas are cut into 7 equal slices, so the **slice size matches** and the slices can be counted together.

---

Are **2/3** and **3/4** *like* or *unlike* fractions?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Unlike fractions** — different denominators (3 and 4).
>
> The pizzas are cut into **different-size slices**, so you can't just count them together until the slices are made equal.

---

Write **two fractions equivalent to 1/2**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Multiply top and bottom by the **same** number (that keeps the value): `1/2 = ` **2/4** `= ` **3/6**.
>
> All three are still **half a pizza** — just cut into more slices.

---

<!-- ===== Improper ⇄ mixed ===== -->

Convert **11/4** to a mixed fraction.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Divide, then write `Q R/D`** (Quotient Remainder / Divisor).
>
> `11 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 3` → **2 3/4**.

---

Convert **17/5** to a mixed fraction.

> [!ANSWER]
> `17 ÷ 5 = 3 remainder 2` → **3 2/5**.
>
> 3 whole pizzas, with 2 of the 5 slices left over.

---

Convert **2 3/4** back to an improper fraction.

> [!ANSWER]
> **`(whole × denominator + numerator) / denominator`:**
>
> `(2 × 4 + 3)/4 = 11/4`.
>
> The 2 whole pizzas are `2 × 4 = 8` quarter-slices, plus 3 more = 11 quarters. (This just reverses the question above ✓.)

---

Convert **3 2/5** back to an improper fraction.

> [!ANSWER]
> `(3 × 5 + 2)/5 = 17/5`.

---

<!-- ===== Comparing fractions ===== -->

Which is bigger, **5/9 or 3/9**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Like fractions** — same slice size, so just **compare the numerators**: `5 > 3` → **5/9** is bigger.

---

Which is bigger, **2/3 or 3/4**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Unlike** — first make the slice sizes equal. LCM of 3 and 4 is **12**:
>
> `2/3 = 8/12` · `3/4 = 9/12`. Now compare 8 and 9 → **3/4** is bigger.

---

Which is bigger, **3/5 or 2/3**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Unlike — LCM of 5 and 3 is **15**:
>
> `3/5 = 9/15` · `2/3 = 10/15`. Compare 9 and 10 → **2/3** is bigger.

---

<!-- ===== Adding & subtracting ===== -->

Work out **3/4 + 2/4**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Like fractions** — add only the numerators, keep the denominator:
>
> `3/4 + 2/4 = 5/4 = ` **1 1/4** pizzas.

---

Work out **5/6 − 1/6**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Like — subtract numerators: `5/6 − 1/6 = 4/6`.
>
> Then **simplify** (÷2 top and bottom) → **2/3**.

---

Work out **1/4 + 1/6**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Unlike** — make denominators the same first. LCM of 4 and 6 is **12**:
>
> `1/4 = 3/12` · `1/6 = 2/12` → `3/12 + 2/12 = ` **5/12**.

---

Work out **3/4 − 1/6**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Unlike — LCM of 4 and 6 is **12**:
>
> `3/4 = 9/12` · `1/6 = 2/12` → `9/12 − 2/12 = ` **7/12**.

---

Work out **2/3 + 1/5**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Unlike — LCM of 3 and 5 is **15**:
>
> `2/3 = 10/15` · `1/5 = 3/15` → `10/15 + 3/15 = ` **13/15**.

---

Shambhvi ate **1/4** of a pizza and her friend ate **3/8**. How much did they eat **together**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Add the fractions — but the slice sizes differ, so match them first. LCM of 4 and 8 is **8**:
>
> `1/4 = 2/8`, so `2/8 + 3/8 = ` **5/8** of the pizza.

---

<!-- ===== Simplest form ===== -->

Write **6/12** in its simplest form.

> [!ANSWER]
> Top and bottom can both be divided by **6**: `6/12 = ` **1/2**.
>
> Six slices out of twelve is exactly **half** the pizza.

---

Write **75/100** in its simplest form.

> [!ANSWER]
> Divide top and bottom by **25**: `75/100 = ` **3/4**.

---

Write **18/24** in its simplest form.

> [!ANSWER]
> Top and bottom share the factor **6**: `18/24 = ` **3/4**.
>
> (Same value, fewer slices — the fraction is easier to read.)

---

<!-- ===== Multiplying ===== -->

Work out **2/3 × 4/5**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Top × top** and **bottom × bottom:**
>
> `(2 × 4)/(3 × 5) = ` **8/15**.

---

Work out **3/4 × 2/9**.

> [!ANSWER]
> `(3 × 2)/(4 × 9) = 6/36`, then **simplify** (÷6) → **1/6**.

---

Work out **2/5 × 10**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Write the whole number as a fraction: `10 = 10/1`.
>
> `(2 × 10)/(5 × 1) = 20/5 = ` **4**.

---

<!-- ===== Dividing (Keep-Change-Flip) ===== -->

Work out **2/3 ÷ 4/5**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Keep-Change-Flip:** keep `2/3`, change `÷` to `×`, flip `4/5` to `5/4`:
>
> `2/3 × 5/4 = 10/12 = ` **5/6** (simplified by 2).

---

Work out **5/6 ÷ 1/2**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Keep-Change-Flip: `5/6 × 2/1 = 10/6`.
>
> Simplify (÷2) → `5/3 = ` **1 2/3**.

---

Work out **3/4 ÷ 6/8**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Keep-Change-Flip: `3/4 × 8/6 = 24/24 = ` **1**.
>
> That makes sense — `6/8` **is** `3/4`, and anything divided by itself is **1**.
