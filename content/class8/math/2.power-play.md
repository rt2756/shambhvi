## Exponential growth — the folded paper

Fold a sheet of paper (thickness 0.001 cm) again and again. **The thickness doubles with every fold.**

| Folds | Thickness | Compare with |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 1.024 cm | just over 1 cm |
| 17 | ≈ 131 cm | a little over 4 feet |
| 26 | ≈ 670 m | Burj Khalifa is 830 m |
| 30 | ≈ 10.7 km | height planes fly; Mariana Trench is 11 km deep |
| **46** | **more than 7,00,000 km** | **reaches the Moon** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **This is exponential (multiplicative) growth, and our intuition is terrible at it.** After 10 folds you have barely a centimetre — 36 folds later you have crossed to the Moon. **Every 10 folds multiplies the thickness by 1024** (= 2 multiplied by itself 10 times), no matter where you start.

## Exponential notation

`n × n × n × … (a times) = nᵃ`

- In `5⁴ = 625`: **5 is the base**, **4 is the exponent (power)**. Read "5 raised to the power 4".
- `a × a × a × b × b = a³b²`

> [!WARNING]
> **`4 + 4 + 4 = 3 × 4 = 12`, but `4 × 4 × 4 = 4³ = 64`.** Repeated **addition** is multiplication; repeated **multiplication** is a power. Don't blur the two.

**Prime factorisation in exponential form:** `32400 = 2⁴ × 3⁴ × 5²`

**Signs:** `(−4)³ = −64` (odd power → negative) · `(−2)⁴ = 16` (even power → positive) · `(−1)⁵ = −1`, `(−1)⁵⁶ = 1`.

## The laws of exponents

| Law | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **`nᵃ × nᵇ = nᵃ⁺ᵇ`** | a copies of n followed by b more copies is **a + b copies in a row** |
| **`(nᵃ)ᵇ = (nᵇ)ᵃ = nᵃᵇ`** | b groups of a copies each — **a × b copies altogether** |
| **`nᵃ ÷ nᵇ = nᵃ⁻ᵇ`** (n ≠ 0) | b of the copies on top **cancel** with the b below, leaving a − b |
| **`mᵃ × nᵃ = (mn)ᵃ`** | regroup `(m×m×m…) × (n×n×n…)` into `(mn) × (mn) × …` |
| **`mᵃ ÷ nᵃ = (m/n)ᵃ`** | the same regrouping, dividing instead |

**Example (power of a power two ways):** `4⁶ = (4³)² = (4²)³` — both give 4096.

## Zero and negative exponents

These aren't arbitrary definitions — **they are forced by making the division law keep working**.

**Why `x⁰ = 1`:**
`2⁰ = 2⁴⁻⁴ = 2⁴ ÷ 2⁴ = 16 ÷ 16 = 1`
In general `xᵃ ÷ xᵃ = xᵃ⁻ᵃ = x⁰`, and anything divided by itself is **1**. So **`x⁰ = 1` for x ≠ 0**.

**Why negative exponents mean reciprocals:**
`2⁴ ÷ 2⁵ = 2⁴⁻⁵ = 2⁻¹` — but written out, `2⁴/2⁵ = 1/2`. So **`2⁻¹ = 1/2`**.

> **`n⁻ᵃ = 1/nᵃ`** and **`nᵃ = 1/n⁻ᵃ`** (n ≠ 0)

Examples: `10⁻³ = 1/10³` · `7⁻² = 1/7²` · `2⁻⁶ = 1/64`

> [!TIP]
> **All three laws still hold when a and b are ANY integers**, not just counting numbers. That's exactly why zero and negative powers were defined this way — so that no rule needs an exception.

## Counting combinations

**Multiply the number of choices at each step.**

- 4 dresses × 3 caps = **12** outfits.
- 7 dresses × 2 hats × 3 pairs of shoes = **42** ways.
- **A 5-digit password:** 10 choices per digit → `10 × 10 × 10 × 10 × 10 = 10⁵ = ` **1,00,000** possibilities.
- **6 slots with letters A–Z:** `26⁶` possibilities — far more, which is why letters make a safer lock.

> [!TIP]
> **Stuck? Solve a simpler version first.** For the 5-digit lock, start with a 2-digit one (10 × 10 = 100), then 3-digit (1000) — the pattern becomes obvious. This technique is worth reaching for on any hard problem.

**Doubling puzzles:** lotuses double daily and fill a pond on day 30. **On day 29 the pond is half full** — because the very last doubling takes it from half to full.

## Powers of 10

**Expanded form:**
`47561 = (4 × 10⁴) + (7 × 10³) + (5 × 10²) + (6 × 10¹) + (1 × 10⁰)`

**Decimals use negative powers:**
`561.903 = (5 × 10²) + (6 × 10¹) + (1 × 10⁰) + (9 × 10⁻¹) + (0 × 10⁻²) + (3 × 10⁻³)`

### Scientific notation

Huge numbers are hard to read — **you can miscount the zeros**, and getting ₹5,000 instead of ₹50,000 is exactly that mistake. **In many cases the number of zeros matters more than the leading digits.**

`5900 = 590 × 10¹ = 59 × 10² = ` **`5.9 × 10³`** ` = 0.59 × 10⁴`

**Standard scientific notation puts one non-zero digit before the point** — `5.9 × 10³`.

**Examples:**
- Sun's distance from the Milky Way's centre ≈ **3 × 10²⁰ m**
- Number of stars in our galaxy ≈ **10¹¹**
- Mass of the Earth ≈ **5.976 × 10²⁴ kg**
