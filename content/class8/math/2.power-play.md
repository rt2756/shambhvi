## Probe and ponder

- If you could fold a sheet of paper **46 times**, how thick would it be?
- Why is `2⁰ = 1` — not 0?
- Which is bigger: `2¹⁰⁰` or `100²`?

## An impossible venture — the folded paper

Take a sheet **0.001 cm** thick. Each fold **doubles** the thickness.

| Folds | Thickness | Compare with |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.002 cm | |
| 10 | 1.024 cm | just over 1 cm |
| 17 | ≈ 131 cm | a little over 4 feet |
| 20 | ≈ 10.5 m | a 3-storey house |
| 26 | ≈ 670 m | Burj Khalifa is 830 m |
| 30 | ≈ 10.7 km | where planes fly; the Mariana Trench is 11 km deep |
| 40 | ≈ 11,000 km | almost Earth's diameter |
| **46** | **≈ 7,00,000 km** | **past the Moon (3,84,400 km)** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Doubling is MULTIPLICATIVE (exponential) growth.** Every 3 folds multiply the thickness by `2 × 2 × 2 = 8`; **every 10 folds by `2¹⁰ = 1024`** — whatever the starting thickness. That is why 10 folds give 1 cm but 36 more give the Moon: each block of 10 folds multiplies by about a thousand, and 1 cm × 1000 × 1000 × 1000 × … grows terrifyingly fast.

(In real life you can fold paper only about 7 times — it gets too thick and too small. This is a **thought experiment**.)

## Exponential notation

Thickness after 7 folds = `0.001 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 0.001 × 2⁷`. Writing seven 2s is tiring, so:

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **nᵃ means n multiplied by itself a times.** In `5⁴ = 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 = 625`, **5 is the base**, **4 is the exponent (power)**; read "5 raised to the power 4". 5¹, 5², 5³, … are the **powers of 5**.
> After 10 folds the thickness, starting from v, is **2¹⁰v** (not 10v, not 10 + v).

- `a × a × a × b × b = a³b²`
- `4 × 4 × 4 = 4³ = 64`, and `(−4) × (−4) × (−4) = (−4)³ = −64`

> [!WARNING]
> **`4 + 4 + 4 = 3 × 4 = 12`, but `4 × 4 × 4 = 4³ = 64`.** Repeated **addition** is multiplication; repeated **multiplication** is a power.

**Signs of powers:** a pair of negatives makes a positive, so
- **even** power of a negative → positive: `(−2)⁴ = 16`, `(−1)⁵⁶ = 1`
- **odd** power → one negative is left over → negative: `(−1)⁵ = −1`

**Powers of 0:** `0² = 0`, `0⁵ = 0`, `0ⁿ = 0` for any counting number n (anything × 0 = 0).

**Prime factorisation in exponential form:** `32400 = 2⁴ × 3⁴ × 5²`.

2. `648 = 2³×3⁴`; `405 = 3⁴×5`; `540 = 2²×3³×5`; `3600 = 2⁴×3²×5²`.
3. `2×10³ = 2000`; `7²×2³ = 49×8 = 392`; `3×4⁴ = 3×256 = 768`; `(−3)²×(−5)² = 9×25 = 225`; `3²×10⁴ = 90000`; `(−2)⁵×(−10)⁶ = −32 × 1000000 = −32000000`.

## The laws of exponents — and WHY

### Law 1: multiplying powers of the same base

**The stones that shine:** 3 daughters, each with 3 baskets, each holding 3 keys, each opening 3 rooms, each with 3 tables, each with 3 necklaces, each with 3 diamonds.
- Rooms = `3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 3⁴ = 81`
- Diamonds = seven 3s = `3⁷`. We already know `3⁴ = 81`, so just multiply by the remaining three 3s: `3⁷ = 3⁴ × 3³ = 81 × 27 = 2187`.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **nᵃ × nᵇ = nᵃ⁺ᵇ**
> **Why it works:** a copies of n followed by b more copies is simply a + b copies in a row. `p⁴ × p⁶ = (p·p·p·p)(p·p·p·p·p·p) = p¹⁰`. (And `3⁷ = 3² × 3⁵` too — any split of the seven 3s works.)

### Law 2: power of a power

`4⁶` two ways:
- `(4×4×4) × (4×4×4) = 4³ × 4³ = (4³)²` = 64 × 64 = 4096
- `(4×4) × (4×4) × (4×4) = (4²)³` = 16 × 16 × 16 = 4096

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **(nᵃ)ᵇ = (nᵇ)ᵃ = nᵃᵇ**
> **Why it works:** (nᵃ)ᵇ is b groups with a copies of n in each — a × b copies altogether. Grouping them the other way round gives the same pile.

**Power of a power two ways:** `8⁶ = (8²)³ = (2³)⁶ = 2¹⁸`; `7¹⁵ = (7³)⁵ = (7⁵)³`; `9¹⁴ = (9²)⁷ = 3²⁸`; `5⁸ = (5²)⁴ = (5⁴)²`.

### Law 3: same power, different bases

**Magical pond:** lotuses **double** daily and fill the pond on day 30. **On day 29 it's half full** — the last doubling takes half to full. Full = `2³⁰` lotuses (starting from 1), half = `2²⁹`.

Damayanti puts 1 lotus in the doubling pond for 4 days (→ `2⁴`), then moves them to a **tripling** pond for 4 days (→ `2⁴ × 3⁴`). Regroup the 2s and 3s in pairs:

`2⁴ × 3⁴ = (2×3)(2×3)(2×3)(2×3) = 6⁴ = 1296`

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **mᵃ × nᵃ = (mn)ᵃ** and **mᵃ ÷ nᵃ = (m/n)ᵃ**
> **Why it works:** there are a copies of m and a copies of n; pair each m with an n and you get a copies of (mn).
> `2⁵ × 5⁵ = 10⁵ = 100000`; `10⁴ ÷ 5⁴ = 2⁴ = 16`.

### Counting combinations

Estu has **4 dresses and 3 caps**. For each cap, 4 dresses → `4 + 4 + 4 = 4 × 3 = 12` outfits. Roxie has 7 dresses, 2 hats, 3 pairs of shoes → `7 × 2 × 3 = 42`.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **When you make choices one after another, MULTIPLY the number of options at each step.** Why: every option at step 1 can be followed by every option at step 2 — a tree that branches again at each step.

**The 5-digit lock.** Too big to imagine? **Solve a smaller version first.** A 2-digit lock: 10 first digits × 10 second digits = 100 (00 to 99). A 3-digit lock: each of those 100 gets 10 endings → 1000. So 5 digits → `10⁵ = 1,00,000` passwords (00000 to 99999).

A lock with **6 slots of letters A–Z**: `26⁶ = 30,89,15,776` — over 300 times more than the 5-digit lock, so much safer.

### Law 4: dividing powers

A line 16 = `2⁴` units long, halved 3 times: `2⁴ ÷ 2³ = (2·2·2·2)/(2·2·2) = 2 = 2¹`.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **nᵃ ÷ nᵇ = nᵃ⁻ᵇ** (n ≠ 0)
> **Why it works:** b copies on the bottom cancel b copies on top, leaving a − b. E.g. `2¹⁰⁰ ÷ 2²⁵ = 2⁷⁵`.
> **Why n ≠ 0:** dividing by 0 has no meaning.

## Zero and negative exponents — forced by the laws

These aren't random rules. They are the **only** values that keep Law 4 working.

**Why x⁰ = 1:** `2⁴ ÷ 2⁴` is 16 ÷ 16 = **1**. Law 4 says it is `2⁴⁻⁴ = 2⁰`. So **2⁰ must be 1** — and the same for any x ≠ 0.

**Why negative exponents are reciprocals:** halve 16 = 2⁴ five times: `2⁴ ÷ 2⁵ = (2·2·2·2)/(2·2·2·2·2) = 1/2`. Law 4 says it is `2⁻¹`. So **2⁻¹ = 1/2**. Halving 10 times: `2⁴ ÷ 2¹⁰ = 2⁻⁶ = 1/2⁶ = 1/64`.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **n⁻ᵃ = 1/nᵃ** and **nᵃ = 1/n⁻ᵃ** (n ≠ 0).
> `10⁻³ = 1/10³`, `7⁻² = 1/49`, `(−5)⁻³ = 1/(−5)³ = −1/125`.
> **With these definitions all the laws work for ANY integers a and b** — that's the whole point.

**Simplify (book):** `2⁻⁴ × 2⁷ = 2³`; `3² × 3⁻⁵ × 3⁶ = 3³`; `p³ × p⁻¹⁰ = p⁻⁷`; `2⁴ × 4⁻² = 2⁴ × 2⁻⁴ = 2⁰ = 1`; `8ᵖ × 8ᑫ = 8ᵖ⁺ᑫ`.

### Power lines

Write the powers of 4 on a ladder: … `4⁻² = 1/16`, `4⁻¹ = 1/4`, `4⁰ = 1`, `4¹ = 4`, `4² = 16`, … `4⁸ = 65536`. **Each step up is ×4, each step down is ÷4.** Moving 2 steps = ×16.

- Is 16384 (4⁷) 16 times 1024 (4⁵)? Yes: `4⁷ ÷ 4⁵ = 4²`.
- How many times bigger is 4² than 4⁻²? `4² ÷ 4⁻² = 4⁴ = 256` times.
- On the 7-ladder: `2401 × 49 = 7⁴ × 7² = 7⁶`; `49³ = 7⁶`; `343 × 2401 = 7⁷`; `1/343 × 1/343 = 7⁻⁶`.

## Powers of 10

`47561 = 4×10⁴ + 7×10³ + 5×10² + 6×10¹ + 1×10⁰`

**Each place is a power of 10**, and the places after the decimal point continue the ladder downwards:

`561.903 = 5×10² + 6×10¹ + 1×10⁰ + 9×10⁻¹ + 0×10⁻² + 3×10⁻³`

(`10⁻¹ = 1/10` is the tenths place, `10⁻²` hundredths, `10⁻³` thousandths.)

### Scientific notation (standard form)

The Sun is `30,00,00,00,00,00,00,00,00,000 m` from the centre of our galaxy. Easy to miscount zeros — like getting ₹5,000 instead of ₹50,000!

`5900 = 590 × 10¹ = 59 × 10² = 5.9 × 10³ = 0.59 × 10⁴` — all equal. We choose one:

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Standard form: x × 10ʸ, with 1 ≤ x < 10 and y an integer.**
> `5900 = 5.9 × 10³`, `20800 = 2.08 × 10⁴`, `80,00,000 = 8 × 10⁶`, `308100000 = 3.081 × 10⁸`.
> **How:** put the point after the first non-zero digit, then count how many places it moved — that's y.

**Why the exponent matters most:** Mumbai's 2 crore people = `2 × 10⁷`. Change the 2 to 3 → 50% more. Change the 7 to 8 → **ten times** more. **The exponent tells the size; the first digit fine-tunes it.**

**Digits show how sure you are:** writing Kohima's population as 1,42,395 claims you know it to the last person. If you only know "about 1 lakh 42 thousand", write `1.42 × 10⁵`; "about 1 lakh 40 thousand" → `1.4 × 10⁵`.

**Comparing:** Sun–Saturn `1.4335 × 10¹²` m, Saturn–Uranus `1.439 × 10¹²` m, Sun–Earth `1.496 × 10¹¹` m. **Compare exponents first** — Sun–Earth has the smaller exponent, so it's the smallest (about a tenth of Sun–Saturn), even though 1.496 looks big.

**Book practice:** `59,853 = 5.9853 × 10⁴`; `65,950 = 6.595 × 10⁴`; `34,30,000 = 3.43 × 10⁶`; `70,04,00,00,000 = 7.004 × 10⁹` (it has 10 digits, so the exponent is 9).

## Did you ever wonder? — estimating big things

**The method:** (1) make a quick **guess**; (2) write the **relationship** between the quantities; (3) make **reasonable assumptions** for unknowns; (4) **compute**, and compare with your guess. Answers will differ with assumptions — that's fine; the modelling is what matters.

- **Tulābhāra** (offering goods equal to your weight): Roxie 45 kg × jaggery ₹70/kg = **₹3150**; Estu 50 kg × wheat ₹50/kg = **₹2500**.
- **Ladder to the Moon:** 3,84,400 km with steps 20 cm apart → `3.844 × 10¹⁰ cm ÷ 20 cm = 1.922 × 10⁹` ≈ **192 crore steps**. Folding paper needs just **46** folds!

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Linear growth is ADDITIVE** (+20 cm each step). **Exponential growth is MULTIPLICATIVE** (×2 each fold). Additive needs billions of steps where multiplicative needs dozens.

### A sense of large numbers

Lakh `10⁵`, crore `10⁷`, arab `10⁹`, kharab `10¹¹`, neel `10¹³`, padma `10¹⁵`, shankh `10¹⁷` — **each is 100 × the previous** (two more zeros). Million `10⁶`, billion `10⁹`, trillion `10¹²`, quadrillion `10¹⁵` — **each is 1000 × the previous**.

Some book facts: humans ≈ `8.2 × 10⁹`; African elephants ≈ `4 × 10⁵`; trees ≈ `3 × 10¹²`; ants ≈ `2 × 10¹⁶`; stars in the observable universe ≈ `2 × 10²³`.

**Worked (book):**
- Ants per human: `2 × 10¹⁶ ÷ 8 × 10⁹ = 0.25 × 10⁷ =` **2.5 × 10⁶**
- Starling flocks of 10,000: `1.3 × 10⁹ ÷ 10⁴ =` **1.3 × 10⁵**
- Leaves, if each tree has 10⁴: `3 × 10¹² × 10⁴ =` **3 × 10¹⁶**
- Sheets of paper (0.001 cm = 10⁻⁵ m) to the Moon: `3.844 × 10⁸ m ÷ 10⁻⁵ m =` **3.844 × 10¹³**
- Can we say there are about 20,000 people per African elephant? `8 × 10⁹ ÷ 4 × 10⁵ = 2 × 10⁴ = 20,000` — **yes**.

### Time in powers of 10

- `10⁶ s ≈ 11.6 days` — so **a million seconds is under 2 weeks**
- `10⁹ s ≈ 31.7 years` — **a billion seconds is half a lifetime!**
- `10¹² s ≈ 31,700 years`; dinosaurs died out ≈ `2 × 10¹⁵ s` ago; the universe is ≈ `4 × 10¹⁷ s` old.

Roxie is 4840 days old → `4840 × 24 = 1,16,160` hours.

## A pinch of history

The Buddhist *Lalitavistara* (1st century BCE) names odd powers of ten up to **10⁵³** (tallakshana). Mahaviracharya listed 24 names (to 10²³); a Jaina text named powers up to **10⁹⁶**; a Pali grammar went to **10¹⁴⁰** (asaṅkhyeya). **10¹⁰⁰ is a googol**; atoms in the universe ≈ 10⁷⁸ to 10⁸².

## Figure it Out — worked (end of chapter)

1. **Units digit of 2²²⁴ ÷ 4³²:** `4³² = (2²)³² = 2⁶⁴`, so it's `2¹⁶⁰`. Units digits of powers of 2 repeat **2, 4, 8, 6**; 160 is a multiple of 4 → **6**.
2. 5 bottles per container, 1 new container a day, 40 days → `40 × 5 =` **200 = 2 × 10²** (linear growth).
3. `64³ = 2¹⁸ = 2¹⁰ × 2⁸ = 4⁵ × 4⁴ = 8³ × 8³`; `192⁸ = 2⁴⁸ × 3⁸ = 2⁴⁰ × 6⁸`; `32⁻⁵ = 2⁻²⁵ = 2⁻¹⁰ × 2⁻¹⁵`.
4. Always / sometimes / never:
   - Cubes are squares — **sometimes** (only sixth powers: 64 = 4³ = 8², but 8 isn't a square).
   - Fourth powers are squares — **always**: `n⁴ = (n²)²`.
   - n⁵ divisible by n³ — **always** (n ≠ 0): `n⁵ ÷ n³ = n²`, a whole number.
   - Product of two cubes is a cube — **always**: `a³ × b³ = (ab)³`.
   - q⁴⁶ (q prime) is a 4th and 6th power — **never**: 46 isn't divisible by 4 or by 6.
5. `10⁻² × 10⁻⁵ = 10⁻⁷`; `5⁷ ÷ 5⁴ = 5³`; `9⁻⁷ ÷ 9⁴ = 9⁻¹¹`; `(13⁻²)⁻³ = 13⁶`; `m⁵n¹²(mn)⁹ = m¹⁴n²¹`.
6. From 12² = 144: `1.2² = 1.44`, `0.12² = 0.0144`, `0.012² = 0.000144`, `120² = 14400` (**decimal places double** when squaring; zeros double too).
7. Equal: `2⁴×3⁶`, `6⁴×3²` and `18²×6²` — all equal **2⁴ × 3⁶ = 11664**. (`6⁴×3² = 2⁴3⁴×3²`; `18²×6² = (2·3²)²(2·3)² = 2⁴3⁶`.) 6¹⁰ and 6²⁴ are different.
8. `3⁴ = 81 > 4³ = 64`; `2⁸ = 256 > 8² = 64`; `2¹⁰⁰` is astronomically bigger than `100² = 10⁴` (2¹⁰⁰ > 10³⁰ since 2¹⁰ > 10³).
9. 8.5 billion = `8.5 × 10⁹` codes. n digits give `10ⁿ` codes; `10⁹` is too few, `10¹⁰` enough → **10 digits**.
10. Numbers that are both squares and cubes are **sixth powers** (1, 64, 729, 4096, …) — the exponent of every prime must be divisible by both 2 and 3.
11. 26 letters + 10 digits = 36 symbols per slot → `36⁵ = 6,04,66,176` codes.
12. `10⁹ + 10⁹ = 2 × 10⁹` — options (v) and (vi) (not 20⁹ or 10¹⁸: adding doesn't add exponents!).
13. Clothes: `8.2×10⁹ × 30 = 2.46 × 10¹¹`; bees: `10⁸ × 5×10⁴ = 5 × 10¹²`; bacteria: `3.8×10¹³ × 8.2×10⁹ ≈ 3.1 × 10²³`; eating time (1.5 h a day for 70 years) ≈ `1.4 × 10⁸ s`.
14. A billion seconds ≈ 31.7 years ago — around the mid-1990s.

**Tremendous in Ten (game):** `10¹³` beats `999999 × 999999` (which is less than `(10⁶)² = 10¹²`). And `4 × 10¹⁰⁰⁰` is tiny next to `10¹⁰⁰⁰⁰⁰⁰ × 9000`: **exponents beat everything**.

## In a nutshell

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - `nᵃ` = n multiplied by itself a times; base n, exponent a.
> - `nᵃ × nᵇ = nᵃ⁺ᵇ` • `nᵃ ÷ nᵇ = nᵃ⁻ᵇ` • `(nᵃ)ᵇ = nᵃᵇ` • `mᵃnᵃ = (mn)ᵃ` • `mᵃ ÷ nᵃ = (m/n)ᵃ`
> - `n⁰ = 1`, `n⁻ᵃ = 1/nᵃ` — chosen so the division law never breaks.
> - Choices in a row → **multiply**: 5-digit lock = 10⁵ codes.
> - Standard form `x × 10ʸ`, 1 ≤ x < 10; the exponent shows the size.
> - Exponential (×) growth crushes linear (+) growth: 46 folds vs 192 crore ladder steps.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** `4³` is 64, not 12 • `2⁰ = 1`, not 0 • `2⁻³` is 1/8, not −8 • `10⁹ + 10⁹ = 2 × 10⁹`, not 10¹⁸ • `(2³)² = 2⁶` but `2³ × 2² = 2⁵` • `(−1)` to an even power is +1 • in standard form x must be **at least 1 and below 10** (59 × 10² is not standard).
