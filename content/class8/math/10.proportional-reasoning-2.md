## Quick recap

**Two ratios are proportional when both terms change by the same factor.**

`6 : 3` and `4 : 2` — check by **cross multiplication**: `6 × 2 = 12` and `3 × 4 = 12` ✓ proportional.

**In general, `a : b :: c : d` exactly when `ad = bc`, equivalently `a/c = b/d`.**

## Ratios in maps

Every proper map carries a **Representative Fraction (RF)** — the ratio of a distance **on the map** to the matching distance **on the ground**.

`RF 1 : 60,00,000` means **1 cm on the map = 60,00,000 cm on the ground**.

**Convert:** `60,00,000 cm = 60,000 m = ` **60 km**.

So 3.5 cm on such a map is `3.5 × 60 =` **210 km**.

> [!WARNING]
> This is **geographical (straight-line) distance, not road distance**. Roads bend, so the road distance is always longer.

> [!TIP]
> **Different maps have different scales, but the ground distance must come out the same.** A larger-scale map (say 1 : 20,00,000) shows the same two cities further apart on paper — 3 times further — but `× 20 km` per cm instead of `× 60`, so the answer matches.

## Ratios with more than two terms

A spice mix uses coriander : chillies : toor dal : fenugreek in the ratio **8 : 4 : 2 : 1**.

To keep the taste, **every** quantity must change by the **same** factor. With only 2 chillies (half of 4), halve everything: **4 : 2 : 1 : 0.5**.

**In general, `a : b : c : d :: p : q : r : s` means `a/p = b/q = c/r = d/s`** — one shared factor throughout.

**Worked:** purple paint is red : blue : white = `2 : 3 : 5`, and you have 10 L of white.
White is 5 parts, so **1 part = 2 L**. Red `= 2 × 2 =` **4 L**, blue `= 3 × 2 =` **6 L**. Total **20 L**.

**Worked:** concrete is cement : sand : gravel = `1 : 1.5 : 3`. With 3 bags of cement, multiply by 3: **3 : 4.5 : 9**, giving `3 + 4.5 + 9 =` **16.5 bags** of concrete.

## Dividing a whole in a multi-term ratio

**Same method as before — count the groups, but now there are more terms.**

```
each part of x in the ratio a : b : c : … is
    x × a/(a + b + c + …),  x × b/(a + b + c + …),  …
```

**Worked:** 110 units of concrete at `1 : 1.5 : 3`.
Total parts `= 5.5`. Each part `= 110 ÷ 5.5 = 20`.
**Cement 20, sand 30, gravel 60.** ✓ (adds to 110)

**Worked:** 50 mL of purple at `2 : 3 : 5`.
Total parts 10, each 5 mL → **red 10 mL, blue 15 mL, white 25 mL**.

**Worked (angles):** a triangle with angles in ratio `1 : 3 : 5`.
Angles sum to **180°**; total parts 9, each `180 ÷ 9 = 20°`.
→ **20°, 60°, 100°** ✓

> [!TIP]
> **The "whole" changes with the context.** For a pie chart it's **360°**; for a triangle's angles, **180°**; for a mixture, the total volume. Always ask what the whole actually is before dividing.

## Pie charts

**A pie chart shows proportions of a whole as slices of a circle.** Since the whole circle is **360°**, each slice's angle must be **proportional to its share**.

**Worked:** grades A–E scored by 12, 10, 8, 6, 4 students (40 total).

Simplify `12 : 10 : 8 : 6 : 4` by HCF 2 → `6 : 5 : 4 : 3 : 2`. Total parts 20, so **each part = `360 ÷ 20 = 18°`**.

| Grade | Students | Angle |
|---|---:|---:|
| A | 12 | `6 × 18 =` **108°** |
| B | 10 | `5 × 18 =` **90°** |
| C | 8 | `4 × 18 =` **72°** |
| D | 6 | `3 × 18 =` **54°** |
| E | 4 | `2 × 18 =` **36°** |
| | **40** | **360°** ✓ |

**Drawing it:** draw a circle with one radius. From that radius, measure 108° and draw the next radius; from *that* one measure 90°; and so on. Each new angle is measured **from the previous line**, not from the start.

> [!TIP]
> **Always check your angles add to 360°.** If they don't, you've made an arithmetic slip.

**From percentages:** `1% = 3.6°`. So Entertainment 50% → **180°**, Sports 25% → **90°**, News 15% → **54°**, Information 10% → **36°**.

**Reading a pie chart backwards:** a 60° slice is `60/360 = 1/6` of the whole. If that slice is 18 children, the survey covered `18 × 6 =` **108 children**.

## Inverse proportion

**Direct proportion:** one goes up, the other goes up by the **same factor**. Their **quotient** `x/y` stays constant.

**Inverse proportion:** one goes up by a factor n, the other goes **down** by `1/n`. Their **product** `xy` stays constant.

**Lucknow to Kanpur (90 km):**

| | Walk | Bicycle | Motorcycle | Car |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Speed (km/h) | 5 | 15 | 30 | 60 |
| Time (h) | 18 | 6 | 3 | 1.5 |
| **Speed × Time** | 90 | 90 | 90 | 90 |

Bicycle is `15 ÷ 5 =` **3 times** the speed of walking, and takes `18 ÷ 6 =` **⅓** the time. Same factor, opposite direction.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **`x × y = k` (a constant) is the test for inverse proportion.**
>
> So `x₁y₁ = x₂y₂`, which rearranges to **`x₁/x₂ = y₂/y₁`** — notice the subscripts **swap sides**. That flip is the whole difference from direct proportion.

### Direct or inverse?

| Situation | Type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| taps filling a tank ↔ time taken | **inverse** | more taps, less time |
| painters ↔ days to paint a wall | **inverse** | more painters, fewer days |
| petrol in the tank ↔ distance the car goes | **direct** | more petrol, more distance |
| cyclist's speed ↔ time for a fixed route | **inverse** | speed × time = distance, fixed |
| metres of cloth ↔ price at a fixed rate | **direct** | more cloth, more money |
| pages in a book ↔ reading time | **direct** | more pages, more time |

> [!TIP]
> **The one-second test: does the product stay fixed, or the quotient?**
> Speed × time = distance → **product fixed** → inverse.
> Price ÷ length = rate → **quotient fixed** → direct.

### Worked inverse problems

**20 workers take 4 days; how long for 10 workers?**
`20 × 4 = 10 × y` → `y =` **8 days**. Halve the workers, double the days.

**2 pumps fill a tank in 18 hours; how long with 4 pumps?**
`2 × 18 = 4 × x` → `x =` **9 hours**.

**Provisions for 80 students last 15 days; 20 more students join.**
`80 × 15 = 100 × x` → `x =` **12 days**.

**42 machines make the toys in 63 days; how many for 54 days?**
`42 × 63 = m × 54` → `m = 2646/54 =` **49 machines**.

**A car takes 2 h at 60 km/h; how long at 80 km/h?**
`60 × 2 = 80 × t` → `t = 1.5 h =` **1 hour 30 minutes**.

**25 rows of 12 chairs, rearranged into rows of 20.**
`25 × 12 = 300` chairs, `300 ÷ 20 =` **15 rows**.

**8 periods of 45 min become 9 periods.**
`8 × 45 = 360` min, `360 ÷ 9 =` **40 minutes each**.

> [!WARNING]
> **These all assume everything else stays the same** — every worker works at the same rate, every student eats the same amount, every machine is identical. Real life is messier; the assumption is part of the answer.

### Careful — some "more workers" problems are direct

**It takes 6 hours to fill 2 tanks; how long for 5 tanks (same pump)?**

**More tanks means MORE time** — this is **direct**, not inverse.
`6/2 = 3` hours per tank, so 5 tanks take **15 hours**.

## Work problems — add the rates, not the times

**Ram takes 1 hour to cut the vegetables; Shyam takes 1.5 hours. Together?**

**Wrong instinct:** average the times. **Right method: add what each does in one hour.**

- Ram does **1** unit of work per hour.
- Shyam does `1/1.5 =` **2/3** of a unit per hour.
- Together: `1 + 2/3 =` **5/3** units per hour.

To do **1** unit at `5/3` units per hour takes `1 ÷ 5/3 =` **3/5 hour = 36 minutes**.

**Worked:** a small pump fills a tank in 3 h, a large one in 2 h. Together?
Rates `1/3 + 1/2 = 5/6` tanks per hour → time `= 6/5 =` **1.2 hours = 1 h 12 min**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Rates add; times don't.**
>
> **Why:** "work done" is what actually accumulates when two people work side by side, and work done per hour is the rate. Times can't be added because both people are working during the *same* hour, not one after the other.
>
> **Sanity check: the joint time is always LESS than the faster person's time alone.** If your answer is bigger, you've added times by mistake.

## Which is which — a summary

| | Direct proportion | Inverse proportion |
|---|---|---|
| Behaviour | both up, or both down | one up, the other down |
| Constant | **quotient** `x/y = k` | **product** `xy = k` |
| Equation | `x₁/y₁ = x₂/y₂` | `x₁y₁ = x₂y₂` |
| Cross form | `x₁/x₂ = y₁/y₂` | `x₁/x₂ = y₂/y₁` |
| Example | cloth and cost | speed and time |
