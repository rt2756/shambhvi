## What this chapter is about

In Proportional Reasoning — 1, two quantities went up **together**: twice the lemonade needs twice the sugar. This chapter goes further:

- What does **1 : 60,00,000** on a map mean, and how do you find real distances from it?
- How do ratios with **three or more terms** work (cement : sand : gravel = 1 : 1.5 : 3)?
- How do you share a whole among **several** parts, and draw a **pie chart**?
- What about quantities that go **opposite ways**: the faster you go, the less time it takes? (**Inverse proportion**.)

> [!NOTE]
> **Words we will use.** Two quantities are in **direct proportion** when they change by the same factor in the **same** direction (one doubles, the other doubles). They are in **inverse proportion** when one changes by a factor n and the other by 1/n (one doubles, the other halves). A **constant** is a number that stays the same while other things change.

## 10.1 A quick recap

Idli batter needs rice and urad dal in the ratio **2 : 1**. Viswanath uses 6 cups of rice with 3 of dal (6 : 3); Puneet uses 4 with 2 (4 : 2). Are these the same?

**Cross multiply:** 6 × 2 = 12 and 3 × 4 = 12. The products match, so **6 : 3 :: 4 : 2**, and both batters (cooked the same way) should taste the same.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **a : b :: c : d exactly when a × d = b × c**, i.e. a/c = b/d.
> **Why:** proportional means c and d are a and b multiplied by the same factor f. Then a × d = a × fb = fa × b = c × b.

## 10.2 Ratios in maps

A map is a small copy of the land, so every distance is shrunk by the **same factor**. The factor is printed as a ratio called the **Representative Fraction (RF)**, often in a corner.

**RF 1 : 60,00,000** means 1 cm on the map stands for 60,00,000 cm on the ground. And 60,00,000 cm = 60,000 m = **60 km**. (This is the straight-line distance "as the crow flies", not the distance by road.)

![A map at RF 1 : 60,00,000: Mangaluru and Chennai 9.7 cm apart, Bengaluru 5.0 cm from Mangaluru and 4.8 cm from Chennai; a scale bar shows 0, 60, 120, 180 km](assets/img/class8/math/10/map_scale.svg)

**Example 1: real distances from a map.** On this map Bengaluru and Chennai are **4.8 cm** apart. 1 cm : 60 km :: 4.8 cm : d, so d = 4.8 × 60 = **288 km**.

> [!TIP]
> To use a map scale: **measure on the map, multiply by the scale.** Bigger maps of the same area (say 1 : 30,00,000) show the same distance as a longer line, but the real distance comes out the same.

**Try this.** Find two cities near you in an atlas and on another map with a different RF. Measure, convert, and compare the two answers. They should agree roughly; small differences come from measuring.

**Try this.** Draw your classroom to the scale 1 : 50 (1 cm on paper = 50 cm in the room). Measure the room and the main objects (board, teacher's desk, door, fans), divide each length by 50 and draw them in place, using simple symbols.

### Check yourself

**1.** Convert 60,00,000 cm to kilometres.

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. 100 cm = 1 m, so 60,00,000 cm = 60,00,000 ÷ 100 = 60,000 m.
> 2. 1000 m = 1 km, so 60,000 m = 60,000 ÷ 1000 = **60 km**.
>
> In one step: 1 km = 1,00,000 cm, and 60,00,000 ÷ 1,00,000 = 60.

**2.** On the map above, find the real distance (as the crow flies) from Bengaluru to Chennai and from Mangaluru to Chennai.

> [!ANSWER]
> Each map centimetre is 60 km.
> - **Bengaluru–Chennai:** 4.8 cm × 60 = **288 km**.
> - **Mangaluru–Chennai:** 9.7 cm × 60 = **582 km**.
>
> (Bengaluru–Mangaluru: 5.0 × 60 = 300 km.) Road distances are longer, because roads bend.

## 10.3 Ratios with more than two terms

Viswanath grinds a spice powder from 8 spoons of coriander seeds, 4 red chillies, 2 spoons of toor dal and 1 spoon of fenugreek. The ratio is **8 : 4 : 2 : 1**.

Puneet has only **2** chillies, half of 4. For the same taste he halves **everything**: 4 spoons coriander, 2 chillies, 1 spoon toor dal, ½ spoon fenugreek, i.e. **4 : 2 : 1 : 0.5**. So 8 : 4 : 2 : 1 :: 4 : 2 : 1 : 0.5.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **a : b : c : d :: p : q : r : s** when **a/p = b/q = c/r = d/s**: every term changes by the same factor.
> **Why all the terms:** the taste (or colour, or strength) depends on how each ingredient compares with every other one. Changing one term by a different factor changes those comparisons.

**Example 2: purple paint.** Red : blue : white = 2 : 3 : 5. Yasmin has 10 litres of white.
1. White is 5 parts = 10 litres, so 1 part = 2 litres.
2. Red = 2 × 2 = **4 L**, blue = 3 × 2 = **6 L**. Total = 4 + 6 + 10 = **20 L** of purple.

**Example 3: concrete.** For strong pillars and roofs, cement : sand : gravel = 1 : 1.5 : 3. With 3 bags of cement, multiply every term by 3: 3 : 4.5 : 9. That makes 3 + 4.5 + 9 = **16.5 bags** of concrete.

## 10.4 Dividing a whole in a given ratio

To divide 12 in the ratio 2 : 1: add the terms (3), divide (12 ÷ 3 = 4), multiply back (8 and 4). The same works for any number of terms.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **To divide x in the ratio a : b : c : …**, the parts are **x × a/(a + b + c + …)**, **x × b/(a + b + c + …)**, and so on.
> **Why:** the ratio says the whole is made of a + b + c + … equal "parts"; each share is its number of parts times the size of one part, x ÷ (a + b + c + …).

**Example 4: 110 units of concrete at 1 : 1.5 : 3.** One "batch" of 1 + 1.5 + 3 = 5.5 units; 110 ÷ 5.5 = 20 batches. Cement 1 × 20 = **20**, sand 1.5 × 20 = **30**, gravel 3 × 20 = **60** units.

**Example 5: 50 mL of purple at 2 : 3 : 5.** Total parts 10; each part is 5 mL. Red **10 mL**, blue **15 mL**, white **25 mL**.

**Example 6: a triangle with angles 1 : 3 : 5.** The angles add to 180°, and 1 + 3 + 5 = 9 parts, so 1 part = 20°. The angles are **20°, 60°, 100°**.

### Check yourself: Figure it out (10.3, 10.4)

**1.** A coach divides each 150-minute session as warm-up/cool-down : batting : bowling : fielding = 3 : 4 : 3 : 5. How long is each?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. Total parts: 3 + 4 + 3 + 5 = 15. One part = 150 ÷ 15 = 10 minutes.
> 2. Warm-up/cool-down **30 min**, batting **40 min**, bowling **30 min**, fielding **50 min**.
>
> Check: 30 + 40 + 30 + 50 = 150 ✓.

**2.** A library has Odia : Hindi : English books = 3 : 2 : 1. There are 288 Odia books. How many Hindi and English?

> [!ANSWER]
> Odia is 3 parts = 288, so 1 part = 96. **Hindi = 2 × 96 = 192**, **English = 96**. (Total 576 books.)

**3.** I have 100 coins: ₹10 : ₹5 : ₹2 : ₹1 coins = 4 : 3 : 2 : 1 by number. How much money is that?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. Parts: 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10, so 1 part = 10 coins: **40** ten-rupee, **30** five-rupee, **20** two-rupee and **10** one-rupee coins.
> 2. Money: 40 × 10 + 30 × 5 + 20 × 2 + 10 × 1 = 400 + 150 + 40 + 10 = **₹600**.
>
> Careful: the ratio is of the **number** of coins, not of their value.

**4.** Construct a triangle with sides in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5. Will every such triangle be congruent to yours?

> [!ANSWER]
> Choose a unit, say 1 cm: draw a 4 cm base, then arcs of 3 cm and 5 cm from its ends (as in constructing a triangle from three sides). You get a **right-angled** triangle (3² + 4² = 5²).
>
> ![3-4-5 cm and 6-8-10 cm right triangles: same shape, different sizes](assets/img/class8/math/10/triangle_345.svg)
>
> **Not all are congruent.** A friend using 2 cm units gets 6, 8, 10 cm: the same **shape** (same angles) but a different **size**. Congruent triangles must have equal sides, not just sides in the same ratio. Triangles with sides in the same ratio are **similar**.

**5.** Can you construct a triangle with sides in the ratio 1 : 3 : 5?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No.** In any triangle, the two shorter sides together must be longer than the third (the straight path is the shortest). Here 1 + 3 = 4, which is **less than 5**, whatever unit you choose. Drawn from the ends of the 5-unit side, the 1-unit and 3-unit sides can't meet.
>
> ![A base of 5 with circles of radius 1 and 3 at its ends: they don't meet](assets/img/class8/math/10/triangle_135_sides.svg)

## 10.5 A slice of the pie

A **pie chart** shows how a whole is shared out. 40 students got these grades:

| Grade | A | B | C | D | E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Students | 12 | 10 | 8 | 6 | 4 |

Each slice's **angle** must be in proportion to its number, and the angles fill the whole circle, **360°**. So we divide 360° in the ratio 12 : 10 : 8 : 6 : 4.

1. Simplify by the HCF, 2: **6 : 5 : 4 : 3 : 2**, with 6 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 = 20 parts.
2. One part = 360° ÷ 20 = 18°.
3. Angles: A 6 × 18 = **108°**, B **90°**, C **72°**, D **54°**, E **36°**. (Check: 108 + 90 + 72 + 54 + 36 = 360 ✓.)

![A pie chart: A 108°, B 90°, C 72°, D 54°, E 36°](assets/img/class8/math/10/pie_grades.svg)

**How to draw it:** draw a circle and one radius. Measure 108° from that radius with a protractor and draw the next radius: that's slice A. From the new radius measure 90° for B, then 72° for C, and so on. The last slice should close up exactly. Colour and label each slice.

> [!TIP]
> **Angle of a slice = (its amount ÷ total) × 360°.** Percentages work too: 25% is a quarter of the circle, 25% × 360° = 90°.

**Try this.** Fill a bucket with one mug, timing how long it takes. Now fill it with two mugs (two people pouring together). Does the time halve? This is inverse proportion in action, as long as both mugs pour at the same rate.

**Try this.** Ask each classmate their favourite subject (only one each) and make a pie chart of the answers. Is any slice bigger than half the pie? Is the angle sum 360°?

## 10.6 Inverse proportion

**Direct proportion** (the Rule of Three): if 5 workers move 4500 bricks in a day, then 18000 bricks (4 times as many) need 4 times the workers: 4500 : 18000 :: 5 : x, so x = **20** workers.

But not everything goes up together. Lucknow to Kanpur is 90 km:

| | Walk | Bicycle | Motorcycle | Car |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Speed (km/h) | 5 | 15 | 30 | 60 |
| Time (hours) | 18 | 6 | 3 | 1.5 |

The faster you go, the **less** time you take. And by the same factor: walking → bicycle is **3 times** the speed and **⅓** of the time (18 → 6). Speed and time change by the same factor in **opposite** directions. In every column, **speed × time = 90**, the distance.

![Time against speed: the points (5, 18), (15, 6), (30, 3), (60, 1.5) lie on a curve where speed × time = 90](assets/img/class8/math/10/speed_time.svg)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> x and y are in **inverse proportion** when **x × y = k**, a constant. Then **x₁y₁ = x₂y₂**, or x₁/x₂ = y₂/y₁: if x is multiplied by n, y is multiplied by **1/n**.
> **Why the product stays the same:** here it is the fixed distance (speed × time = distance). In work problems it is the fixed job (workers × days = total worker-days).

> [!WARNING]
> Puneeth's father rode 3 hours at 30 km/h. At 60 km/h, the proportion 30 : 60 :: 3 : x would give x = 6 hours, **more** time for a faster ride. That is wrong: this is an **inverse** proportion. Correct: 30 × 3 = 60 × x, so x = **1.5 hours**. Always ask first: "if one doubles, does the other double, or halve?"

**Example 7: workers and days.** 20 workers lay a road in 4 days. How long for 10 workers? Fewer workers → more days: inverse. 20 × 4 = 10 × y, so y = **8 days**.

**Example 8: pumps.** 2 pumps fill a tank in 18 hours. With 4 pumps: 2 × 18 = 4 × x, so x = **9 hours**.

**Example 9: food supplies.** Food for 80 students for 15 days. With 20 more students: 80 × 15 = 100 × x, so x = **12 days**.

**Example 10: working together.** Ram cuts some vegetables in 1 hour; Shyam takes 1.5 hours. Together?
1. In 1 hour Ram does the **whole** job (1 unit); Shyam does 1/1.5 = **2/3** of it.
2. Together, in 1 hour they do 1 + 2/3 = **5/3** jobs.
3. Work done and time are **directly** proportional: 5/3 : 1 :: 1 : x, so x = 1 ÷ (5/3) = **3/5 hour = 36 minutes**.

> [!TIP]
> **For "together" problems, add the work done per hour**, not the times. Adding the times (1 + 1.5 = 2.5 hours) would mean that a helper slows you down!

### Check yourself

**1.** Check that speed and time change by the same factor (in opposite directions) for the other modes of transport too.

> [!ANSWER]
> | From → to | Speed × | Time × |
> |---|---|---|
> | Bicycle → motorcycle | 30 ÷ 15 = **2** | 3 ÷ 6 = **1/2** |
> | Motorcycle → car | 60 ÷ 30 = **2** | 1.5 ÷ 3 = **1/2** |
> | Walk → car | 60 ÷ 5 = **12** | 1.5 ÷ 18 = **1/12** |
>
> Every time, the time is multiplied by the **reciprocal** of the speed factor, because speed × time stays 90.

**2.** Puneeth's father rode 3 hours at 30 km/h. Can the car trip at 60 km/h be written 30 : 60 :: 3 : x? Will the time increase or decrease?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No.** That proportion says the time doubles when the speed doubles (x = 6 hours). In fact the time **decreases**: doubling the speed halves the time. Using speed × time = constant: 30 × 3 = 60 × x, so **x = 1.5 hours**.

**3.** In the vegetable-cutting problem, are the quantity of work and the time taken directly or inversely proportional?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Directly.** Working at the same speed, twice the work takes twice the time. That is why 5/3 : 1 :: 1 : x is a direct proportion. (It is the number of **workers** and the time that are inversely proportional.)

### Check yourself: Figure it out (inverse proportion)

**1.** Which tables show inverse proportion? (i) x: 40, 80, 25, 16; y: 20, 10, 32, 50. (ii) x: 40, 80, 25, 16; y: 20, 10, 12.5, 8. (iii) x: 30, 90, 150, 10; y: 15, 5, 3, 45.

> [!ANSWER]
> Multiply each pair x × y; for inverse proportion all the products must be equal.
> 1. **(i)** 40 × 20 = 800, 80 × 10 = 800, 25 × 32 = 800, 16 × 50 = 800: all equal, so **inverse** (k = 800).
> 2. **(ii)** 800, 800, 25 × 12.5 = 312.5, 16 × 8 = 128: **not** inverse. (Not direct either: x/y is 2, 8, 2, 2.)
> 3. **(iii)** 30 × 15 = 450, 90 × 5 = 450, 150 × 3 = 450, 10 × 45 = 450: **inverse** (k = 450).

**2.** x and y are in inverse proportion. Fill in the blanks: x = 16, 12, __, 36 and y = 9, __, 48, __.

> [!ANSWER]
> k = 16 × 9 = **144**, so y = 144 ÷ x and x = 144 ÷ y:
> - x = 12 → y = 144 ÷ 12 = **12**
> - y = 48 → x = 144 ÷ 48 = **3**
> - x = 36 → y = 144 ÷ 36 = **4**

## Exercises — try first

### 1. Which pairs are in inverse proportion? (i) taps filling a tank and the time to fill it; (ii) painters and the days to paint a fixed wall; (iii) the distance a car can travel and the petrol in its tank; (iv) a cyclist's speed and the time for a fixed route; (v) length of cloth and its price at a fixed rate per metre; (vi) pages in a book and the time to read it at a fixed speed.

> [!ANSWER]
> Ask of each pair: "if one doubles, does the other **double** (direct) or **halve** (inverse)?"
>
> | | Pair | If the first doubles… | Kind |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | (i) | taps, time | twice the taps fill it in half the time | **Inverse** |
> | (ii) | painters, days | twice the painters need half the days | **Inverse** |
> | (iii) | petrol, distance | twice the petrol goes twice as far | Direct |
> | (iv) | speed, time | twice the speed takes half the time | **Inverse** |
> | (v) | length, price | twice the cloth costs twice as much | Direct |
> | (vi) | pages, time | twice the pages take twice as long | Direct |
>
> So **(i), (ii) and (iv)** are inverse. In each, a product stays fixed: taps × time, painters × days and speed × time (the distance).

### 2. 24 pencils cost ₹120. How much do 20 cost?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. More pencils cost more, in the same ratio: **direct** proportion.
> 2. One pencil costs 120 ÷ 24 = ₹5.
> 3. 20 pencils cost 20 × 5 = **₹100**. (Or 24 : 120 :: 20 : x, x = 120 × 20 ÷ 24 = 100.)

### 3. A water tank supplies 20 families for 6 days. If 10 more families move in, how long will it last? What must you assume?

> [!ANSWER]
> More families → fewer days: **inverse**. 20 × 6 = 30 × x, so x = **4 days**. (The tank holds 120 "family-days" of water.)
>
> **Assumptions:** every family uses the same amount each day, the tank is not refilled during this time, and no water is lost.

### 5. A pie chart shows how children travel to school: walk 90°, bus 120°, two-wheeler 60°, cycle 60°, and car (angle not marked).

![Pie chart: walk 90°, bus 120°, two-wheeler 60°, cycle 60°, car unmarked](assets/img/class8/math/10/pie_transport.svg)

**(i)** What is the most common way?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Bus**: it has the biggest slice, 120°.

**(ii)** What fraction travel by car?

> [!ANSWER]
> Car = 360° − 90° − 120° − 60° − 60° = **30°**, and 30/360 = **1/12** of the children.

**(iii)** If 18 children come by car, how many were surveyed? How many use taxis?

### 6. Three workers paint a fence in 4 days. If one more joins, how long will it take? What must you assume?

> [!ANSWER]
> Inverse: 3 × 4 = 4 × x, so x = **3 days**. (The job is 12 worker-days.)
>
> **Assumptions:** every worker paints at the same rate, they don't get in each other's way, and the rate stays the same each day.

### 7. A pump fills 2 tanks of the same size in 6 hours. How long for 5 tanks?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. More tanks take more time, in the same ratio: **direct** (not inverse, though it is a pump question).
> 2. 2 tanks in 6 hours means 3 hours per tank.
> 3. 5 tanks take 5 × 3 = **15 hours**. (2 : 6 :: 5 : x, x = 15.)

### 8. Chairs are in 25 rows of 12. If rearranged in rows of 20, how many rows?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. The number of chairs is fixed: 25 × 12 = **300**.
> 2. Rows × chairs per row = 300 always, so rows and chairs per row are **inversely** proportional.
> 3. Rows = 300 ÷ 20 = **15**.

### 9. A school has 8 periods of 45 minutes. If it has 9 periods in the same school day, how long is each?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. The school day is fixed: 8 × 45 = **360 minutes**.
> 2. Periods × length = 360, so they are inversely proportional.
> 3. Each period = 360 ÷ 9 = **40 minutes**.

### 10. A small pump fills a tank in 3 hours and a large one in 2 hours. Together?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. Per hour: small pump 1/3 of the tank, large pump 1/2.
> 2. Together: 1/3 + 1/2 = 5/6 of the tank per hour.
> 3. Time = 1 ÷ (5/6) = 6/5 hours = **1 hour 12 minutes**.
>
> Sense check: faster than the large pump alone (2 h), as it must be.

### 11. 42 machines make some toys in 63 days. How many machines make the same toys in 54 days?

> [!ANSWER]
> Inverse: 42 × 63 = x × 54, so x = 2646 ÷ 54 = **49 machines**. (Fewer days need more machines.)

### 12. A car takes 2 hours at 60 km/h. How long at 80 km/h?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. Distance = 60 × 2 = **120 km**, and it stays the same.
> 2. Speed × time = 120, so time = 120 ÷ 80 = **1.5 hours** (1 hour 30 minutes).
>
> Sense check: faster, so less than 2 hours ✓.

## In a nutshell

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **a : b :: c : d ⇔ ad = bc.** A ratio can have many terms: **a : b : c :: p : q : r** when a/p = b/q = c/r.
> - **Map scale (RF) 1 : n:** real distance = map distance × n (convert units!).
> - **Divide x in a : b : c:** each part = x × (its term) ÷ (a + b + c).
> - **Pie chart:** angle = (amount ÷ total) × 360°; the angles total 360°.
> - **Direct proportion:** x/y constant (both double together). **Inverse proportion:** x × y constant (one doubles, the other halves).
> - **Working together:** add the **work per hour**, then time = 1 ÷ (total work per hour).
> - Sides in the ratio 1 : 3 : 5 make no triangle (1 + 3 < 5); sides 3 : 4 : 5 make a right triangle of any size.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** check **direct or inverse** before setting up a proportion (speed and time are inverse) • a ratio of **numbers** of coins is not a ratio of their **values** • map distances are straight lines, not roads • "together" times are not added • triangles with sides in the same ratio are similar, **not** necessarily congruent.
