## Why some resized photos look wrong

Five copies of one photo, all different sizes:

| Image | Width (mm) | Height (mm) | Width : Height | Simplest form |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| A | 60 | 40 | 60 : 40 | **3 : 2** |
| B | 40 | 20 | 40 : 20 | 2 : 1 |
| C | 30 | 20 | 30 : 20 | **3 : 2** |
| D | 90 | 60 | 90 : 60 | **3 : 2** |
| E | 60 | 60 | 60 : 60 | 1 : 1 |

**A, C and D look right. B looks stretched, E looks squashed.**

**Why:** going from A to C, width **and** height are both **multiplied by ½** — the same factor. Going from A to B, both dropped by **20 mm** — the same *difference*, but the factors are `40/60 = 2/3` and `20/40 = ½`. **Different factors, distorted picture.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Proportional change means multiplying by the same factor — not adding the same amount.**
>
> This is the whole chapter in one line. Adding a fixed amount to both terms of a ratio **changes** the ratio.

## Ratios

**`a : b` means: for every a units of the first quantity there are b units of the second.**

The numbers a and b are the **terms** of the ratio.

**Simplest form: divide both terms by their HCF.**
`60 : 40` → HCF 20 → **3 : 2**
`90 : 60` → HCF 30 → **3 : 2**

**Two ratios are in proportion when their simplest forms match**, written `a : b :: c : d`.

`60 : 40 :: 30 : 20 :: 90 : 60` ✓ (all reduce to 3 : 2)

> [!WARNING]
> **Units must match before you compare.** 200 g for ₹200 versus 1 kg for ₹800 is **not** `200 : 200` vs `1 : 800` — convert first: `200 : 200` vs `1000 : 800`, i.e. `1 : 1` vs `5 : 4`. **Not proportional.**

### Adding to a ratio destroys it

Neelima is 3, her mother 30 → `3 : 30 = 1 : 10`.
Nine years later she is 12 and her mother 39 → `12 : 39 = 4 : 13`.

**Not proportional.** Both ages went up by the *same amount*, not by the same *factor*.

> [!TIP]
> This is why "the ratio of our ages will always be 1 : 10" is wrong. Age ratios **always drift towards 1 : 1**.

## Finding a missing term

Ratios proportional to `14 : 21`:

- **`__ : 42`** — the second term doubled (`42 ÷ 21 = 2`), so double the first → **28 : 42**
- **`6 : __`** — the factor is `6 ÷ 14 = 3/7` (a fraction is fine!), so `21 × 3/7 = 9` → **6 : 9**
- **`2 : __`** — divide both by their HCF 7 → **2 : 3**

> [!TIP]
> **The factor need not be a whole number.** `3/7` works exactly as well as `2`. What matters is that **both** terms use the *same* factor.

## Making things stronger or weaker

Manjunath's filter coffee: **decoction : milk**.

| Decoction (mL) | Milk (mL) | Simplest | Compared with 15 : 35 = 3 : 7 |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| 15 | 35 | 3 : 7 | **regular** |
| 20 | 30 | 2 : 3 | **stronger** (more decoction per milk) |
| 10 | 40 | 1 : 4 | **lighter** |
| 300 | 600 | 1 : 2 | **stronger** |
| 150 | 500 | 3 : 10 | **lighter** |
| 200 | 400 | 1 : 2 | **stronger** |
| 24 | 56 | 3 : 7 | **regular** |
| 100 | 300 | 1 : 3 | **lighter** |

**How to compare quickly:** turn both into "milk per 1 mL of decoction".
`3 : 7` → 7 · `2 : 3` → 3.5 → **less milk per decoction = stronger**.

## The Rule of Three (Trairasika)

**The standard shape of a proportion problem: four linked quantities, three known.**

**Worked:** 120 students need 15 kg rice. How much for 80 students?

`120 : 15 :: 80 : ?`
Factor: `80 ÷ 120 = 2/3`. Apply it to 15: `15 × 2/3 =` **10 kg**.

### Cross multiplication — and why it works

If `a : b :: c : d`, then **`ad = bc`**.

**Why:** proportionality means `c = fa` and `d = fb` for one shared factor f. So

```
f = c/a   and   f = d/b
⇒ c/a = d/b
⇒ multiply both sides by ab:  bc = ad
```

Hence the missing term is **`d = bc / a`**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Āryabhaṭa** (5th century CE) named the three known quantities **pramāṇa** (measure), **phala** (fruit) and **ichchhā** (requisition), with the answer the **ichchhāphala** (yield). His rule:
>
> *"Multiply the phala by the ichchhā and divide by the pramāṇa"* — exactly `d = bc/a`.

**Worked:** a car goes 90 km in 150 minutes. How far in 4 hours?

**First fix the units** — 4 hours = **240 minutes**.
`150 : 90 :: 240 : x` → `150x = 240 × 90` → `x = 21600/150 =` **144 km**.

> [!WARNING]
> **The Rule of Three does NOT apply when one quantity goes DOWN as the other goes up.**
>
> A 2-hour ride at 50 km/h becomes **shorter**, not longer, at 75 km/h. Writing `50 : 2 :: 75 : ?` would give a *bigger* time — nonsense. That's **inverse proportion**, covered in Part II.
>
> **Always ask first: as one goes up, does the other go up too?**

### Not everything in life is proportional

| Container | Volume | Price | Price per mL |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sachet | 6 mL | ₹2 | ₹0.33 |
| Small bottle | 180 mL | ₹154 | ₹0.86 |
| Medium bottle | 340 mL | ₹276 | ₹0.81 |
| Large bottle | 1000 mL | ₹540 | ₹0.54 |

**Price is not proportional to volume.** Packaging, transport and shop margins don't scale with the contents — and companies price sachets to be affordable per unit, not per mL.

## Sharing in a given ratio

**Divide 42 counters in the ratio 4 : 3.**

**The method: count the groups.**

1. Total groups = `4 + 3 =` **7**
2. Size of each group = `42 ÷ 7 =` **6**
3. Parts = `4 × 6 =` **24** and `3 × 6 =` **18**

**Why it works:** a ratio 4 : 3 is really "cut the whole into 7 equal groups; one person takes 4 of them, the other takes 3". The `m + n` is the number of groups, not a random sum.

**General rule:** to divide x in the ratio `m : n`,

```
first part  = m × x/(m + n)
second part = n × x/(m + n)
```

**Worked (profit sharing):** Prashanti invests ₹75,000 and Bhuvan ₹25,000; profit ₹4,000 shared in the investment ratio.
`75000 : 25000 = 3 : 1`. Groups = 4. Each group = `4000 ÷ 4 = 1000`.
**Prashanti ₹3,000, Bhuvan ₹1,000.**

**Worked (changing a mixture):** 40 kg of sand + cement in ratio 3 : 1. How much cement to add to make it 5 : 2?

1. Original: sand `= 3/4 × 40 = 30 kg`, cement `= 1/4 × 40 = 10 kg`.
2. **The sand doesn't change** — it stays 30 kg.
3. New ratio `5 : 2 :: 30 : ?` → `? = 30 × 2/5 =` **12 kg** cement needed.
4. Already have 10 kg → **add 2 kg**.

> [!TIP]
> **When something is added to a mixture, find what stayed the same first.** That fixed quantity anchors the new ratio.

## Unit conversions to keep handy

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 1 metre = **3.281 feet** |
| Area | 1 sq metre = **10.764 sq feet** |
| | 1 acre = **43,560 sq feet** |
| | 1 hectare = **10,000 sq metres** = **2.471 acres** |
| Volume | 1 mL = **1 cubic centimetre (cc)** |
| | 1 litre = **1,000 mL** |
| Temperature | `F = (9/5) × C + 32` and `C = (5/9) × (F − 32)` |

> [!WARNING]
> **Temperature is NOT a ratio problem.** Because of the `+32`, doubling the Celsius does **not** double the Fahrenheit. `0°C = 32°F`, but `0°F` is well below freezing — the two scales don't share a zero, so no single multiplying factor connects them.
>
> Check: `25°C = (9/5)(25) + 32 = 45 + 32 =` **77°F** ✓

## Worked problems

**Earth's orbit.** 940 million km per year. Per week: `940,000,000 × 7/365 ≈` **18 million km**.

**Crowding.** Delhi: 30 million people in 1,484 sq km → **~20,200 per sq km**. Mumbai: 20 million in 550 sq km → **~36,400 per sq km**. **Mumbai is far more crowded** — you must compare people **per unit area**, not raw populations.

**Buses.** 3 buses carried 162 people, all full → `162 ÷ 3 =` **54 per bus**. For 204 people: `204 ÷ 54 = 3.78` → **4 buses**, and the last one will **not** be full (it carries 204 − 162 = 42).

**Gold.** Equal volumes of gold and water have masses in ratio 37 : 2. 1 litre of water is 1 kg, so 1 litre of gold is `37/2 × 1 =` **18.5 kg**.

**Filling time.** A tap fills a 500 mL mug in 15 s. A 10-litre bucket is `10000 ÷ 500 = 20` mugs → `20 × 15 =` **300 s = 5 minutes**.

**Land cost.** 1 acre = 43,560 sq ft costs ₹15,00,000. Then 2,400 sq ft costs
`1500000 × 2400/43560 ≈` **₹82,645**.

**Manure.** A 200 ft × 500 ft plot is 100,000 sq ft = `100000 ÷ 43560 ≈ 2.3 acres`. At 10 tonnes per acre → **about 23 tonnes**.

**Ploughing.** A pair of oxen takes 6 h per acre → 20 acres take **120 hours**. A tractor is 4× faster → **30 hours**.

**Lilavati's saffron.** 2½ palas cost 37 niskas. For 9 niskas:
`37 : 2.5 :: 9 : x` → `x = 2.5 × 9 / 37 =` **22.5/37 ≈ 0.61 palas**.

**Harmain's age.** She is 1, her brother 5 — a 4-year gap that **never changes**. When her age is half his, `x` and `x + 4` with `2x = x + 4` → `x =` **4 years old**.

> [!TIP]
> Age problems are proportion traps. **The difference stays fixed; the ratio moves.** Set up the constant difference and solve, don't scale the ratio.
