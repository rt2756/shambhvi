## What this chapter is about

Make a photo bigger on a phone and it still looks right. Stretch it only sideways and everyone looks too fat. Add 10 spoons of sugar to 6 glasses of lemonade, and you need 30 spoons for 18 glasses to keep the same taste. These are all about **proportion**: two quantities changing **by the same factor**.

- When do two rectangles, two mixtures or two prices "match"?
- How do you find a missing number so that two ratios match (the **Rule of Three**)?
- How do you share something **unequally** in a given ratio?
- When does proportional reasoning **not** work?

> [!NOTE]
> **Words we will use.** A **ratio** a : b compares two quantities: "for every a of the first, there are b of the second". a and b are its **terms**. A **factor** is the number we multiply by. The **HCF** is the highest common factor. **Proportional** means changing by the same factor.

## 7.1 Same change, same look

Here are five pictures of a face, of different sizes:

![Five pictures A to E: A 60 × 40 mm, B 40 × 20, C 30 × 20, D 90 × 60, E 60 × 60; the faces in A, C, D look the same, B and E look squashed or stretched](assets/img/class8/math/7/five_images.svg)

A, C and D look the same; B looks squashed and E looks stretched. Why?

| Picture | Width (mm) | Height (mm) |
|---|---|---|
| A | 60 | 40 |
| B | 40 | 20 |
| C | 30 | 20 |
| D | 90 | 60 |
| E | 60 | 60 |

- **A to C:** the width is halved (60 → 30) **and** the height is halved (40 → 20). Both changed by the **same factor**, ½. C looks like A.
- **A to B:** the width went down by 20 mm, and so did the height. The same **difference**, but not the same **factor**: the height was halved (40 → 20) while the width was multiplied by only ⅔ (60 → 40). B looks wrong.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Two shapes look alike when **every length changes by the same factor** (multiplying), not when the same amount is added or taken away. Such changes are called **proportional**.
> **Why multiplying, not adding:** taking 20 mm from 40 mm halves the height, but taking 20 mm from 60 mm removes only a third of the width. The same amount means a different *share* of each length, so the shape changes.

### Check yourself

**1.** By what factors do the width and the height change from picture A to picture D? Are the factors the same?

> [!ANSWER]
> - Width: 60 → 90, factor 90 ÷ 60 = **1.5** (or 3/2).
> - Height: 40 → 60, factor 60 ÷ 40 = **1.5**.
>
> **The same factor**, so D is just a bigger copy of A and looks the same.

## 7.2 Ratios and their simplest form

The ratio of width to height of picture A is **60 : 40**: for every 60 mm of width there are 40 mm of height. Picture C's is 30 : 20 and D's is 90 : 60.

Multiplying both terms of 60 : 40 by ½ gives 30 : 20, C's ratio. Ratios whose terms change by the same factor are **proportional**.

A neater way to compare: **reduce each ratio to its simplest form** by dividing both terms by their HCF.
- A: HCF(60, 40) = 20, so 60 : 40 = **3 : 2**.
- D: HCF(90, 60) = 30, so 90 : 60 = **3 : 2**.
- B: 40 : 20 = **2 : 1**. E: 60 : 60 = **1 : 1**.

A, C and D all simplify to 3 : 2; B and E don't. That matches what our eyes told us.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Two ratios are **in proportion** when they have the **same simplest form**. We write **a : b :: c : d** ("a is to b as c is to d"). So 60 : 40 :: 30 : 20 :: 90 : 60.
> **Why the simplest form works:** dividing both terms by the same number is undoing a "multiply both by the same factor". Every ratio in a family of proportional ratios shrinks down to the same smallest pair.

**Example 1: are 3 : 4 and 72 : 96 proportional?** HCF(72, 96) = 24, and 72 ÷ 24 = 3, 96 ÷ 24 = 4. So 72 : 96 = 3 : 4: **yes, proportional**.

**Example 2: lemonade.** Kesang adds 10 spoons of sugar to 6 glasses of lemonade. How much sugar for 18 glasses, just as sweet?
1. The ratio glasses : spoons must stay the same: 6 : 10 :: 18 : ?
2. Glasses went from 6 to 18, a factor of 18 ÷ 6 = 3.
3. Sugar must grow by the same factor: 10 × 3 = **30 spoons**.

**Example 3: is Hari's wall weaker?** Nitin built 60 ft of wall with 3 bags of cement; Hari built 40 ft with 2 bags. Nitin: 60 : 3 = 20 : 1. Hari: 40 : 2 = 20 : 1. Both used one bag per 20 ft, so the walls are **equally strong**. Less cement doesn't mean weaker; what matters is the ratio.

**Example 4: ages.** When Neelima was 3, her mother was 30 (ten times as old): 3 : 30 = 1 : 10. When Neelima is 12, nine years later, her mother is 39: 12 : 39 = **4 : 13**, a different ratio.

> [!WARNING]
> **Adding the same number to both terms changes a ratio.** 3 : 30 is not proportional to (3 + 9) : (30 + 9). Only **multiplying or dividing** both terms by the same number keeps a ratio the same.

**Example 5: fill in ratios proportional to 14 : 21.**
- **__ : 42**: 42 = 21 × 2, so the first term is 14 × 2 = **28**. (28 : 42)
- **6 : __**: the factor taking 14 to 6 is 6/14 = 3/7. So the second term is 21 × 3/7 = **9**. (6 : 9)
- **2 : __**: 14 ÷ 7 = 2 (7 is the HCF), so 21 ÷ 7 = **3**. (2 : 3)

All three simplify to 2 : 3, like 14 : 21.

**Try this.** Count the teachers and students in your school (or your class and one other). Write the ratio teachers : students in its simplest form. One school has 5 teachers for 170 students, which is 1 : 34. Is your school's ratio the same, better or worse?

**Try this.** Measure the width and height of your classroom board to the nearest cm. Write the ratio. Now draw a rectangle in your notebook with the same ratio (divide both by the same number). Do your friends' rectangles look the same as yours, even if they are a different size?

**1.** By what factor do you multiply 60 : 40 (picture A) to get 90 : 60 (picture D)?

> [!ANSWER]
> 60 × f = 90 gives f = 90 ÷ 60 = **3/2** (= 1.5). Check the second term: 40 × 3/2 = 60 ✓. So 60 : 40 :: 90 : 60.

## 7.3 Stronger and lighter coffee

Manjunath makes filter coffee by mixing coffee decoction with milk.

![Bars for 50 mL cups: regular 15 decoction + 35 milk; strong 20 + 30; light 10 + 40](assets/img/class8/math/7/coffee_bars.svg)

- Regular: 15 mL decoction to 35 mL milk, **15 : 35 = 3 : 7**.
- Strong: 20 : 30 = **2 : 3**.
- Light: 10 : 40 = **1 : 4**.

To compare strengths, compare the **share of the cup that is decoction**: regular 15/50 = 3/10, strong 20/50 = 2/5 = 4/10, light 10/50 = 1/5 = 2/10.

> [!TIP]
> To compare two ratios a : b and c : d, turn them into fractions of the whole (a/(a + b) and c/(c + d)) or "per one" amounts (a/b and c/d), then compare those numbers. Bigger share of decoction = stronger coffee.

### Check yourself

**1.** Why is the 20 : 30 coffee stronger than the regular 15 : 35?

> [!ANSWER]
> In the 20 : 30 cup, decoction is 20 out of 50 mL = **2/5 = 0.4** of the cup. In the regular cup it is 15 out of 50 = **3/10 = 0.3**. More of the cup is decoction, so it tastes **stronger**.
>
> Another way: per 1 mL of milk, the strong cup has 20 ÷ 30 ≈ 0.67 mL decoction, and the regular cup 15 ÷ 35 ≈ 0.43 mL.

**2.** Why is the 10 : 40 coffee lighter?

> [!ANSWER]
> Decoction is 10 out of 50 mL = **1/5 = 0.2** of the cup, less than the regular 0.3. So there is less coffee taste in each sip: **lighter**.

**3.** Is each mixture below regular, strong or light? 300 : 600, 150 : 500, 200 : 400, 24 : 56, 100 : 300 (decoction : milk, in mL)

> [!ANSWER]
> Compare each decoction share with the regular **3/10 = 0.30**:
>
> | Decoction : milk | Simplest | Decoction share | Verdict |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 300 : 600 | 1 : 2 | 1/3 ≈ 0.33 | **Strong** |
> | 150 : 500 | 3 : 10 | 3/13 ≈ 0.23 | **Light** |
> | 200 : 400 | 1 : 2 | 1/3 ≈ 0.33 | **Strong** |
> | 24 : 56 | 3 : 7 | 3/10 = 0.30 | **Regular** |
> | 100 : 300 | 1 : 3 | 1/4 = 0.25 | **Light** |
>
> Only 24 : 56 simplifies to exactly 3 : 7, the regular ratio.

> [!ANSWER]

**(ii)** 8 : 3 :: 24 : 6

> [!ANSWER]

> [!ANSWER]
> **False.** 7 : 12 means the first is smaller; 12 : 7 means the first is bigger. Swapping the terms makes a different ratio (unless they are equal).

**(iv)** 21 : 6 :: 35 : 10

> [!ANSWER]
> **True.** 21 : 6 = 7 : 2 (÷ 3) and 35 : 10 = 7 : 2 (÷ 5). Same simplest form.

**(v)** 12 : 18 :: 28 : 12

> [!ANSWER]
> **False.** 12 : 18 = 2 : 3 but 28 : 12 = 7 : 3.

**(vi)** 24 : 8 :: 9 : 3

> [!ANSWER]
> **True.** Both simplify to **3 : 1**.

## 7.4 The Rule of Three

**Example 6: mid-day meal.** A cook makes 15 kg of rice for 120 students. On a rainy day only 80 come. How much rice?
1. Students : rice must stay proportional: 120 : 15 :: 80 : ?
2. Factor for students: 80 ÷ 120 = **2/3**.
3. Rice: 15 × 2/3 = **10 kg**.

In such problems **four** quantities are linked proportionally, three are known, and we find the fourth. Algebra gives a short-cut.

If **a : b :: c : d**, then c = f × a and d = f × b for the same factor f. So f = c/a and f = d/b, hence c/a = d/b. Multiply both sides by ab:

**bc = ad**, so **d = (b × c)/a**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Cross multiplication:** a : b :: c : d exactly when **a × d = b × c** (the outside terms multiply to the same as the inside terms). So the missing fourth term is **d = b × c ÷ a**.
> **Why:** proportional means c/a = d/b (the same factor); multiplying both sides by ab clears the fractions.

> [!NOTE]
> **Trairāśika, the Rule of Three.** Āryabhaṭa (born 476 CE) and later Indian mathematicians called this the "Rule of Three". The three known numbers are the *pramāṇa* (measure, our a), the *phala* (fruit, b) and the *icchā* (requisition, c). The answer is the *icchāphala* (yield, d): "multiply the phala by the icchā and divide by the pramāṇa", which is exactly d = bc/a.

**Example 7: a car's distance.** A car goes 90 km in 150 minutes. At the same speed, how far does it go in 4 hours?
1. **Units first:** 150 is in minutes, so write 4 hours as **240 minutes**. (150 : 90 :: 4 : ? would mix minutes and hours.)
2. 150 : 90 :: 240 : x
3. Cross multiply: 150x = 240 × 90, so x = 21600 ÷ 150 = **144 km**.

A second way: 150 minutes for 90 km means 30 minutes for 18 km. 240 minutes is 8 lots of 30 minutes: 8 × 18 = 144 km ✓.

**Example 8: which tea costs more?** A small farmer sells 200 g of tea for ₹200; a big estate sells 1 kg for ₹800.
1. Use the **same unit**: 1 kg = 1000 g. Weight : price is 200 : 200 = **1 : 1** and 1000 : 800 = **5 : 4**. Not proportional.
2. Compare the price of the **same weight**. 200 g is 1/5 of 1 kg, so 1 kg of the farmer's tea costs 200 × 5 = **₹1000**.
3. ₹1000 > ₹800, so the **small farmer's tea is more expensive** per kg. (Small farms often pick and process by hand, which costs more.)

> [!WARNING]
> **Not everything is proportional.** Puneeth's father rides to Kanpur in 2 hours at 50 km/h. At 75 km/h, will it take **more** time? No: going faster takes **less** time. So 50 : 2 :: 75 : ? is the **wrong** model. The distance is 50 × 2 = 100 km, and at 75 km/h it takes 100 ÷ 75 = 1⅓ hours = **1 hour 20 minutes**. Before using the Rule of Three, ask: "if one quantity doubles, does the other double too?"

### Check yourself: Figure it out (Rule of Three)

**1.** The Earth travels about 940 million km around the Sun in a year. How far does it travel in a week?

> [!ANSWER]
> A year has about **52 weeks**. year : distance :: week : x, so 52 : 940 million :: 1 : x.
>
> x = 940,000,000 ÷ 52 ≈ **18,076,923 km**, about **1.8 crore km** (18 million km) a week.
>
> (Using 365 days instead: 940,000,000 × 7 ÷ 365 ≈ 18,027,397 km. A year is 52 weeks and 1 day, so the two answers differ slightly; "about 18 million km" is a fair answer either way, since 940 million is itself approximate.)

**2.** A mason needs about 1450 bricks for 10 ft of wall. How many bricks for this house (outer walls and the inner wall between rooms, all the same height and thickness)?

![House plan: room 1 is 9 ft by 12 ft and room 2 is 15 ft by 12 ft side by side; room 3, 6 ft by 9 ft, is below room 2](assets/img/class8/math/7/house_plan.svg)

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. **Total wall length.** Top: 9 + 15 = 24 ft. Bottom of the two big rooms: 24 ft. The left, middle and right walls: 12 + 12 + 12 = 36 ft. Room 3: two sides of 9 ft and a bottom of 6 ft = 24 ft (its top is already the bottom wall of room 2). Total = 24 + 24 + 36 + 24 = **108 ft**.
> 2. **Rule of Three:** 10 : 1450 :: 108 : x, so x = 1450 × 108 ÷ 10 = **15,660 bricks**.
>
> (Every 1 ft needs 145 bricks, and 108 × 145 = 15,660.)

**3.** Puneeth's father rides from Lucknow to Kanpur in 2 hours at 50 km/h. How long at 75 km/h? Can this be written as 50 : 2 :: 75 : __?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No.** That proportion says that a higher speed needs *more* time, which is wrong. Here, when one quantity goes up the other goes **down**.
>
> 1. Distance = speed × time = 50 × 2 = **100 km**.
> 2. At 75 km/h: time = 100 ÷ 75 = 4/3 hours = **1 hour 20 minutes**.
>
> Notice that speed × time stays the same (50 × 2 = 75 × 4/3 = 100). Quantities like this are not proportional.

**4.** A shop sells one shampoo in different sizes: sachet 6 mL for ₹2, small 180 mL for ₹154, medium 340 mL for ₹276, large 1000 mL for ₹540. Is the volume proportional to the price? Why might it not be?

> [!ANSWER]
> Compare sachet and small bottle: volumes 6 : 180 = **1 : 30**, prices 2 : 154 = **1 : 77**. **Not proportional.** The clearest check is the price for 1 mL:
>
> | Size | ₹ per mL |
> |---|---|
> | Sachet | 2 ÷ 6 ≈ 0.33 |
> | Small | 154 ÷ 180 ≈ 0.86 |
> | Medium | 276 ÷ 340 ≈ 0.81 |
> | Large | 540 ÷ 1000 = 0.54 |
>
> **Why not proportional:** the bottle, cap, label, transport and shop's share all cost money, and they don't grow in the same ratio as the shampoo inside. Companies also set prices to sell more of certain sizes; sachets are kept cheap so that people with little cash can buy them.

**5.** What are the good and bad points of the different sizes, for the company and for customers? What would you recommend to reduce waste?

> [!ANSWER]
> - **Sachets:** cheap to buy at once and easy to carry, but each one is a small plastic packet that is hard to recycle, so they make a lot of waste for very little shampoo.
> - **Large bottles:** less plastic for each mL and (here) a low price per mL, but they cost more at once and may not suit someone who needs only a little.
> - **Recommendation:** customers who can should buy the large size or refill packs; companies could offer refill stations and recyclable packs and stop making tiny sachets where possible.
>
> **Try this.** Visit a shop and make a table of size and price for rice, atta or soap. Work out the price per kg (or per 100 g) for each size. Which products are priced proportionally?

## 7.5 Sharing, but not equally

Two friends share 12 counters. Equally, each gets 6: ratio 6 : 6 = 1 : 1. If one gets 5, the other gets 7: ratio 5 : 7.

To share 12 in the ratio **3 : 1**, the partner takes 3 and you take 1, again and again, until none are left:

![Three rounds: the partner takes 3 counters and you take 1 each round; 9 and 3 counters, ratio 3 : 1](assets/img/class8/math/7/share_12.svg)

Each round uses 3 + 1 = 4 counters, and 12 ÷ 4 = 3 rounds. So the partner gets 3 × 3 = **9** and you get 3 × 1 = **3**.

For big numbers, count rounds by division instead. To share **42 in the ratio 4 : 3**, split 42 into 4 + 3 = 7 equal **groups** of 42 ÷ 7 = 6. The partner gets 4 groups = **24**, you get 3 groups = **18**.

![A bar of 7 equal groups of 6: four blue groups (24) and three orange groups (18)](assets/img/class8/math/7/share_42.svg)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **To divide x in the ratio m : n:** make m + n equal groups, each of size x ÷ (m + n). The parts are **m × x/(m + n)** and **n × x/(m + n)**.
> **Why:** the ratio m : n means "m groups to every n groups" of the same size. Together that is m + n groups, which must make up the whole x.

**Example 9: sharing a profit.** Prashanti invested ₹75,000 and Bhuvan ₹25,000. They share a profit of ₹4,000 in the same ratio.
1. 75000 : 25000 = **3 : 1**.
2. 3 + 1 = 4 groups; 4000 ÷ 4 = ₹1000 each.
3. Prashanti: 3 × 1000 = **₹3,000**; Bhuvan: **₹1,000**.

**Example 10: sand and cement.** 40 kg of mixture has sand : cement = 3 : 1. How much cement must be added to make the ratio 5 : 2?
1. Now: 4 groups of 10 kg, so sand = **30 kg**, cement = **10 kg**.
2. The sand stays 30 kg. The new ratio 5 : 2 :: 30 : ? → the factor is 30 ÷ 5 = 6, so cement = 2 × 6 = **12 kg**.
3. Add 12 − 10 = **2 kg** of cement.

![Top: 40 kg as 3 parts of sand and 1 of cement, 10 kg each. Bottom: 30 kg of sand is 5 parts of 6 kg, so cement must be 2 parts = 12 kg](assets/img/class8/math/7/sand_cement.svg)

**Try this.** With 12 counters and a partner, share them in the ratios 1 : 1, 1 : 2, 1 : 3, 1 : 5 and 2 : 1. Which ratios work out exactly and which don't? (Hint: look at m + n and whether it divides 12.)

**1.** Divide ₹4,500 in the ratio 2 : 3.

> [!ANSWER]

> 2. Parts: 2 × 900 = **₹1,800** and 3 × 900 = **₹2,700**.

**2.** Acid and water are mixed in the ratio 1 : 5. How much of each is in 240 mL of solution?

> [!ANSWER]

**3.** Blue and yellow paint are mixed 3 : 5 to make green. How much of each for 40 mL? If 20 mL more yellow is added, what is the new ratio?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. 3 + 5 = 8 groups; 40 ÷ 8 = 5 mL each. **Blue 15 mL, yellow 25 mL.**
> 2. Add 20 mL yellow: yellow = 45 mL, blue still 15 mL.
> 3. New ratio blue : yellow = 15 : 45 = **1 : 3**. More yellow, so a lighter green.

**4.** Soft idlis need rice and urad dal in the ratio 2 : 1. How much of each for 6 cups of mixture?

> [!ANSWER]
> 2 + 1 = 3 groups; 6 ÷ 3 = 2 cups each. **Rice 4 cups, urad dal 2 cups.**

**5.** A bucket of orange paint is red and yellow mixed 3 : 5. Another (same-sized) bucket of yellow is added. What is the new ratio of red to yellow?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. Split one bucket into 3 + 5 = 8 equal parts: red is 3/8 of a bucket, yellow 5/8.
> 2. Adding a whole bucket of yellow adds 8/8: yellow = 5/8 + 8/8 = 13/8.
> 3. Red : yellow = 3/8 : 13/8 = **3 : 13**.
>
> **Why count in eighths:** the extra bucket is the same size as the whole first bucket, which is 8 of the parts.

## 7.6 Unit conversions

Proportion problems often need units to match first (minutes with minutes, grams with grams). Some useful conversions:

| Quantity | Conversion |
|---|---|
| Length | 1 metre ≈ 3.281 feet |
| Area | 1 square metre ≈ 10.764 square feet; 1 acre = 43,560 square feet; 1 hectare = 10,000 square metres ≈ 2.471 acres |
| Volume | 1 mL = 1 cubic centimetre (cc); 1 litre = 1000 mL |
| Temperature | °F = (9/5) × °C + 32; °C = (5/9) × (°F − 32) |

For instance 25 °C = (9/5) × 25 + 32 = 45 + 32 = **77 °F**.

> [!NOTE]
> Temperature is **not** proportional: 0 °C is 32 °F, not 0 °F, so doubling the °C does not double the °F. That is why its formula has "+ 32" and is not just a multiplication.

### 1. Anagh mixes 600 mL of orange juice with 900 mL of apple juice. Write the ratio of orange to apple juice in simplest form.

> [!ANSWER]
> 600 : 900. HCF(600, 900) = 300, so the ratio is **2 : 3**.

### 2. Last year 3 full buses carried 162 students and teachers. This year 204 are going. How many buses are needed? Will they all be full?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. One bus holds 162 ÷ 3 = **54** people.
> 2. 204 ÷ 54 = 3.78…, so 3 buses (162 seats) are not enough: we need **4 buses**.
> 3. 4 buses have 4 × 54 = 216 seats, so **12 seats stay empty**: not all buses will be full.

### 3. Delhi has an area of 1,484 sq km and about 30 million people; Mumbai has 550 sq km and about 20 million. Which is more crowded? Why?

> [!ANSWER]
> Compare people **per square km** (the same area for both):
> - Delhi: 30,000,000 ÷ 1484 ≈ **20,216** per sq km
> - Mumbai: 20,000,000 ÷ 550 ≈ **36,364** per sq km
>
> **Mumbai is more crowded**: it has fewer people in total, but they live on much less land, so each sq km holds almost twice as many.

### 4. A crane 155 cm tall has neck : rest of body = 4 : 6. If your neck and the rest of your body had this ratio, how long would your neck be?

> [!ANSWER]
> 4 + 6 = 10 parts, and the neck is 4 of them: **neck = 4/10 of your height**.
>
> For a height of 150 cm: neck = 150 × 4/10 = **60 cm**. (A real human neck is about 10 cm, which shows how long a crane's neck is! For the crane itself: 155 × 4/10 = 62 cm.)

### 5. A problem from Līlāvatī: "If 2½ palas of saffron cost 3/7 of a niṣka, O expert merchant, tell me quickly how much saffron 9 niṣkas will buy." (Pala was a weight, niṣka a coin.)

> [!ANSWER]
> Rule of Three, price : saffron :: price : saffron:
>
> 3/7 : 5/2 :: 9 : x
>
> 1. Cross multiply: (3/7) × x = (5/2) × 9 = 45/2.
> 2. x = (45/2) ÷ (3/7) = (45/2) × (7/3) = 315/6 = **52½ palas**.
>
> Check: 9 niṣkas is 9 ÷ (3/7) = 21 times the price, and 21 × 2½ = 52½ ✓.

### 6. Harmain is 1 year old and her brother is 5. How old will Harmain be when her age : her brother's age = 1 : 2?

> [!ANSWER]
> After x years they are 1 + x and 5 + x. We want (1 + x) : (5 + x) = 1 : 2.
> 1. Cross multiply: 2(1 + x) = 5 + x
> 2. 2 + 2x = 5 + x, so x = 3.
>
> **Harmain will be 4** (and her brother 8). The difference in their ages is always 4 years, and 4 : 8 is the only time it is 1 : 2. (Notice adding the same years to both ages changed the ratio from 1 : 5 to 1 : 2.)

### 7. Equal volumes of gold and water have masses in the ratio 37 : 2. One litre of water has mass 1 kg. What is the mass of 1 litre of gold?

> [!ANSWER]
> gold : water :: 37 : 2, so x : 1 :: 37 : 2. Cross multiply: 2x = 37, **x = 18.5 kg**. One litre of gold weighs 18.5 kg, 18.5 times as much as water.

### 8. A farmer should use 10 tonnes of cow manure per acre. His tomato plot is 200 ft by 500 ft. How much manure should he buy?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. Plot area = 200 × 500 = **1,00,000 sq ft**.
> 2. 1 acre = 43,560 sq ft, so the plot is 100000 ÷ 43560 ≈ **2.296 acres**.
> 3. Manure = 10 × 2.296 ≈ **23 tonnes** (22.96 tonnes, about 22,957 kg).

### 9. A tap fills a 500 mL mug in 15 seconds. How long does it take to fill a 10-litre bucket?

> [!ANSWER]
> 10 L = 10,000 mL = 20 mugs of 500 mL. Time = 20 × 15 = **300 seconds = 5 minutes**.
> (Rule of Three: 500 : 15 :: 10000 : x, x = 15 × 10000 ÷ 500 = 300.)

### 10. One acre of land costs ₹15,00,000. What do 2,400 sq ft of the same land cost?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1 acre = 43,560 sq ft. 43560 : 1500000 :: 2400 : x, so x = 1500000 × 2400 ÷ 43560 ≈ **₹82,645**.

### 11. A tractor ploughs 4 times as fast as a pair of oxen. Oxen take 6 hours per acre. How long to plough a 20-acre field with oxen? With the tractor?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. **Oxen:** 6 hours per acre, so 20 acres take 20 × 6 = **120 hours**.
> 2. **Tractor:** 4 times as fast means it takes **a quarter** of the time: 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5 hours per acre, and 20 × 1.5 = **30 hours**.
>
> Careful: "4 times as fast" means the time is divided by 4, not multiplied. Faster work takes less time.

### 12. A ₹10 coin is cupro-nickel: copper and nickel in the ratio 3 : 1, total mass 7.74 g. Copper costs ₹906 per kg and nickel ₹1,341 per kg. What is the metal in one coin worth?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. **Split the mass** 3 : 1: 7.74 ÷ 4 = 1.935 g per part. Copper = 3 × 1.935 = **5.805 g**, nickel = **1.935 g**.
> 2. **Copper cost:** 906 ÷ 1000 = ₹0.906 per g; 5.805 × 0.906 ≈ **₹5.26**.
> 3. **Nickel cost:** ₹1.341 per g; 1.935 × 1.341 ≈ **₹2.59**.
> 4. **Total ≈ ₹7.85** of metal in a ₹10 coin.

## In a nutshell

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - A **ratio** a : b means "for every a of the first, b of the second". Simplify by dividing both terms by their **HCF**.
> - Two ratios are **proportional** (a : b :: c : d) when their terms change by the **same factor**, i.e. they have the **same simplest form**.
> - Shapes look alike when all lengths are **multiplied** by the same factor, not when the same amount is added.
> - **Cross multiplication:** a : b :: c : d exactly when **ad = bc**, so the fourth term is **d = bc ÷ a** (the Rule of Three, *trairāśika*).
> - **Match the units** before setting up a proportion (minutes with minutes, grams with grams).
> - **Divide x in the ratio m : n:** m + n groups of x ÷ (m + n); the parts are m and n groups.
> - To compare mixtures or prices, compare the **same amount** (share of the cup, price per kg, people per sq km).

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** adding the same number to both terms **changes** the ratio • 7 : 12 is **not** 12 : 7 • convert units **first** (150 minutes vs 4 hours) • faster speed means **less** time: not every pair of quantities is proportional • "4 times as fast" divides the time by 4 • when sharing in a ratio m : n, divide by **m + n**, not by m or n.
