<!-- ===== Proportional change ===== -->

A 60 mm × 40 mm photo is resized. Copy C is 30 × 20; copy B is 40 × 20. One looks fine, one looks stretched. Which, and why?

> [!ANSWER]
> **C looks fine; B looks stretched.**
>
> - C: width `30/60 = ½`, height `20/40 = ½` — **the same factor**, so the shape is preserved.
> - B: width `40/60 = ⅔`, height `20/40 = ½` — **different factors**, so the tiger gets elongated.
>
> Both B's dimensions dropped by 20 mm, but **equal differences are not equal factors**.

---

State the one-line rule that decides whether a resize looks right.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Multiply both dimensions by the same factor.**
>
> Adding or subtracting the same amount from both will **not** preserve shape — that's the trap.

<!-- ===== Ratios and simplest form ===== -->

What does `a : b` actually mean, and what are a and b called?

> [!ANSWER]
> **For every a units of the first quantity there are b units of the second.**
>
> a and b are the **terms** of the ratio.

---

Reduce 90 : 60, 40 : 20 and 60 : 60 to simplest form.

> [!ANSWER]
> Divide by the HCF each time:
>
> - `90 : 60` ÷30 → **3 : 2**
> - `40 : 20` ÷20 → **2 : 1**
> - `60 : 60` ÷60 → **1 : 1**

---

When are two ratios **in proportion**? Write the symbol.

> [!ANSWER]
> When their **simplest forms are identical**.
>
> Written **`a : b :: c : d`**.
>
> `60 : 40 :: 90 : 60` because both reduce to **3 : 2**.

---

Which of these are true proportions: `4 : 7 :: 12 : 21`, `8 : 3 :: 24 : 6`, `7 : 12 :: 12 : 7`, `21 : 6 :: 35 : 10`?

> [!ANSWER]
> - `4 : 7 :: 12 : 21` — **true** (both × 3; cross-check `4×21 = 7×12 = 84`)
> - `8 : 3 :: 24 : 6` — **false** (8 tripled but 3 only doubled)
> - `7 : 12 :: 12 : 7` — **false** (a ratio is not the same reversed)
> - `21 : 6 :: 35 : 10` — **true** (both reduce to **7 : 2**)

---

Give three ratios proportional to 4 : 9.

> [!ANSWER]
> **8 : 18**, **12 : 27**, **40 : 90** — multiply *both* terms by 2, 3 and 10.
>
> Fractional factors work too: `2 : 4.5` is also proportional.

---

Fill in the blanks so each is proportional to 18 : 24: `3 : __`, `12 : __`, `27 : __`.

> [!ANSWER]
> `18 : 24` reduces to **3 : 4**.
>
> - `3 : ` **4** (factor 1/6)
> - `12 : ` **16** (factor 4 on 3 : 4)
> - `27 : ` **36** (factor 9 on 3 : 4)

---

Fill in the blanks so each is proportional to 14 : 21: `__ : 42`, `6 : __`, `2 : __`.

> [!ANSWER]
> - `__ : 42` — the second term doubled, so double 14 → **28 : 42**
> - `6 : __` — the factor is `6/14 = 3/7`, so `21 × 3/7 = 9` → **6 : 9**
> - `2 : __` — divide both by HCF 7 → **2 : 3**
>
> **Note the second one:** the factor is a **fraction**, and that's perfectly fine — it just has to be the *same* fraction for both terms.

<!-- ===== Ratios that drift ===== -->

Neelima is 3 and her mother is 30. Nine years later, is the age ratio still the same?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No.** Now `12 : 39 = 4 : 13`, not `1 : 10`.
>
> **Why:** both ages went up by the **same amount** (9), not by the same **factor**. Adding a constant to both terms always changes a ratio.
>
> Age ratios always creep towards **1 : 1** as people get older.

---

Harmain is 1, her brother is 5. How old will she be when their ages are in the ratio 1 : 2?

> [!ANSWER]
> The **age gap of 4 years never changes**. Let her age then be x, so his is `x + 4`.
>
> `x : (x + 4) = 1 : 2` → `2x = x + 4` → `x =` **4 years old** (he'll be 8).
>
> **The lesson:** in age problems, anchor to the **constant difference**, not the changing ratio.

<!-- ===== Stronger and lighter ===== -->

Filter coffee is usually 15 mL decoction to 35 mL milk. Is 20 : 30 stronger or lighter? What about 10 : 40?

> [!ANSWER]
> Reduce everything: regular is **3 : 7**.
>
> - `20 : 30 = 2 : 3` — for each part of decoction there is **1.5** of milk, versus 2.33 normally → **stronger**
> - `10 : 40 = 1 : 4` — 4 parts milk per decoction → **lighter**
>
> **The trick:** convert to "milk per 1 of decoction" and compare a single number.

---

Sort these decoction : milk mixes into stronger, regular or lighter than 15 : 35 — `300 : 600`, `150 : 500`, `24 : 56`, `100 : 300`.

> [!ANSWER]
> Regular = `3 : 7` = 2.33 milk per decoction.
>
> - `300 : 600 = 1 : 2` → 2.0 → **stronger**
> - `150 : 500 = 3 : 10` → 3.33 → **lighter**
> - `24 : 56 = 3 : 7` → 2.33 → **regular**
> - `100 : 300 = 1 : 3` → 3.0 → **lighter**

<!-- ===== Rule of Three ===== -->

Kesang uses 10 spoons of sugar for 6 glasses of lemonade. How much sugar for 18 glasses at the same sweetness?

> [!ANSWER]
> `6 : 10 :: 18 : ?`
>
> The factor is `18 ÷ 6 = 3`, so the sugar must also be tripled: `10 × 3 =` **30 spoons**.
>
> **Why the same factor:** "same sweetness" means the sugar-per-glass must not change, and only a shared factor leaves a ratio untouched.

---

Nitin built a 60 ft wall with 3 bags of cement; Hari built a 40 ft wall with 2 bags. Is Hari's wall weaker?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No — they're equally strong.**
>
> Nitin: `60 : 3 = 20 : 1`. Hari: `40 : 2 = 20 : 1`.
>
> Both used **1 bag per 20 ft**. Hari used less cement because she built less wall.

---

Prove the cross-multiplication rule: if `a : b :: c : d` then `ad = bc`.

> [!ANSWER]
> Proportional means there's **one shared factor f** with `c = fa` and `d = fb`.
>
> So `f = c/a` and `f = d/b`, giving `c/a = d/b`.
>
> Multiply both sides by `ab`: **`bc = ad`** ✓
>
> Rearranged, the missing term is **`d = bc/a`**.

---

What did Āryabhaṭa call the three known quantities in a Rule of Three problem, and what was his instruction?

> [!ANSWER]
> **pramāṇa** (measure, = a), **phala** (fruit, = b), **ichchhā** (requisition, = c); the answer is the **ichchhāphala** (yield, = d).
>
> His rule: *"Multiply the phala by the ichchhā and divide by the pramāṇa"* — exactly **`d = bc/a`**.

---

A cook makes 15 kg of rice for 120 students. On a rainy day only 80 come. How much rice?

> [!ANSWER]
> `120 : 15 :: 80 : ?`
>
> Factor `= 80/120 = 2/3`, so `15 × 2/3 =` **10 kg**.

---

A car covers 90 km in 150 minutes. How far in 4 hours?

> [!ANSWER]
> **First convert:** 4 hours = **240 minutes** (mixing units is the commonest error here).
>
> `150 : 90 :: 240 : x` → `150x = 240 × 90 = 21600` → `x =` **144 km**.

---

Puneeth's father takes 2 hours at 50 km/h. Can you write `50 : 2 :: 75 : ?` to find the time at 75 km/h?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No — and this is the key warning of the chapter.**
>
> The Rule of Three needs both quantities to move the **same way**. Here, **going faster takes LESS time**, so the setup would give a bigger answer — nonsense.
>
> This is **inverse proportion**: `speed × time` is constant. `50 × 2 = 100`, so at 75 km/h the time is `100/75 = 1⅓ hours =` **1 hour 20 minutes**.

---

Before using the Rule of Three, what one question must you ask?

> [!ANSWER]
> **"As the first quantity goes up, does the second go up too?"**
>
> If yes → direct proportion, Rule of Three applies.
> If it goes **down** → inverse proportion, and the product (not the ratio) is what stays constant.

---

Shampoo: a 6 mL sachet is ₹2, a 180 mL bottle ₹154, a 340 mL bottle ₹276, a 1000 mL bottle ₹540. Is price proportional to volume?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No.** Work out the price per mL:
>
> | Size | Price per mL |
> |---|---:|
> | 6 mL sachet | ₹0.33 |
> | 180 mL | ₹0.86 |
> | 340 mL | ₹0.81 |
> | 1000 mL | ₹0.54 |
>
> If it were proportional, all four numbers would be identical. They range from 33 paise to 86 paise.
>
> **Why:** packaging, transport and shop margins don't scale with the contents, and sachets are deliberately priced to be affordable per **packet**, not per mL.

<!-- ===== Sharing in a ratio ===== -->

Share 42 counters in the ratio 4 : 3. Explain the method.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Count the groups.**
>
> 1. Total groups `= 4 + 3 =` 7
> 2. Each group `= 42 ÷ 7 =` 6
> 3. Parts `= 4 × 6 =` **24** and `3 × 6 =` **18**
>
> **Why:** "4 : 3" means cut the whole into **7 equal groups** and take 4 and 3 of them. The `m + n` is the **number of groups**, which is why you divide by it.

---

Write the general formula for dividing x in the ratio `m : n`.

> [!ANSWER]
> **First part `= m × x/(m + n)`, second part `= n × x/(m + n)`.**
>
> `x/(m + n)` is the size of one group; multiply by how many groups each side gets.

---

Divide ₹4,500 in the ratio 2 : 3.

> [!ANSWER]
> Groups `= 5`, each group `= 4500 ÷ 5 = 900`.
>
> Parts: `2 × 900 =` **₹1,800** and `3 × 900 =` **₹2,700**. ✓ (They add to 4,500.)

---

Prashanti invested ₹75,000, Bhuvan ₹25,000. They split a ₹4,000 profit in that ratio. Who gets what?

> [!ANSWER]
> `75000 : 25000 = 3 : 1`. Groups `= 4`, each `= 4000 ÷ 4 = 1000`.
>
> **Prashanti ₹3,000, Bhuvan ₹1,000.**

---

A 240 mL solution is acid and water in the ratio 1 : 5. How much of each?

> [!ANSWER]
> Groups `= 6`, each `= 240 ÷ 6 = 40 mL`.
>
> **Acid 40 mL, water 200 mL.**

---

Blue and yellow paint mix 3 : 5 to make green. For 40 mL of green, how much of each? Then 20 mL more yellow is added — what's the new ratio?

> [!ANSWER]
> Groups `= 8`, each `= 5 mL` → **blue 15 mL, yellow 25 mL**.
>
> After adding 20 mL yellow: `15 : 45` → **1 : 3**.
>
> **Note:** the blue stayed fixed. Anchoring to what doesn't change makes the new ratio easy.

---

Idli batter needs rice and urad dal in ratio 2 : 1. For 6 cups of mixture, how much of each?

> [!ANSWER]
> Groups `= 3`, each `= 2 cups`.
>
> **Rice 4 cups, urad dal 2 cups.**

---

A 40 kg mixture is sand and cement in ratio 3 : 1. How much cement must be added to make it 5 : 2?

> [!ANSWER]
> 1. Original: sand `= 3/4 × 40 =` 30 kg, cement `= 1/4 × 40 =` 10 kg.
> 2. **The sand doesn't change** — still 30 kg.
> 3. Need `5 : 2 :: 30 : ?` → `? = 30 × 2/5 =` 12 kg cement.
> 4. Already have 10 kg → **add 2 kg**.
>
> **The key move:** find what stays constant first, then rebuild the new ratio around it.

---

One bucket of orange paint is red : yellow = 3 : 5. A whole extra bucket of yellow is added. What's the new ratio?

> [!ANSWER]
> A bucket holds `3 + 5 = 8` parts. Adding a full bucket of yellow adds **8 more parts of yellow**.
>
> New ratio `3 : (5 + 8) =` **3 : 13**.

<!-- ===== Unit conversions ===== -->

Why is Fahrenheit-to-Celsius **not** a proportion problem?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because of the **+32**: `F = (9/5)C + 32`.
>
> The two scales **don't share a zero** (0 °C is 32 °F), so no single multiplying factor connects them. Doubling the Celsius does **not** double the Fahrenheit.
>
> Check: `25 °C = 45 + 32 =` **77 °F**, but `50 °C = 90 + 32 = 122 °F`, not 154.

---

Convert 200 ft × 500 ft into acres. (1 acre = 43,560 sq ft.)

> [!ANSWER]
> Area `= 100,000 sq ft`.
>
> `100000 ÷ 43560 ≈` **2.3 acres**.

---

Good practice is 10 tonnes of manure per acre. How much for a 200 ft × 500 ft plot?

> [!ANSWER]
> The plot is ≈ 2.3 acres, so `2.3 × 10 ≈` **23 tonnes**.

---

One acre costs ₹15,00,000. What do 2,400 sq ft cost?

> [!ANSWER]
> `1500000 × 2400/43560 ≈` **₹82,645**.
>
> **Why the fraction:** 2,400 sq ft is `2400/43560` of an acre, so it costs that fraction of the price.

<!-- ===== Worked problems ===== -->

Earth travels about 940 million km around the Sun in a year. How far in a week?

> [!ANSWER]
> `940,000,000 × 7/365 ≈` **18 million km**.
>
> (Distance is proportional to time at a steady speed — the Rule of Three applies cleanly.)

---

Delhi: 30 million people in 1,484 sq km. Mumbai: 20 million in 550 sq km. Which is more crowded?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Mumbai, by a lot.**
>
> - Delhi: `30,000,000 ÷ 1484 ≈` **20,200 people per sq km**
> - Mumbai: `20,000,000 ÷ 550 ≈` **36,400 people per sq km**
>
> **Why the raw populations mislead:** Delhi has more people but nearly three times the area. Crowding is people **per unit area**.

---

Last year 3 full buses carried 162 people. This year there are 204. How many buses, and will they all be full?

> [!ANSWER]
> One bus holds `162 ÷ 3 =` **54**.
>
> `204 ÷ 54 = 3.78` → **4 buses**, and the fourth carries only `204 − 162 = 42` — **not full**.
>
> **Why you round up:** you can't hire part of a bus.

---

Equal volumes of gold and water have masses in ratio 37 : 2. If 1 litre of water is 1 kg, what is 1 litre of gold?

> [!ANSWER]
> `2 : 37 :: 1 : x` → `x = 37/2 =` **18.5 kg**.

---

A tap fills a 500 mL mug in 15 s. How long to fill a 10-litre bucket?

> [!ANSWER]
> 10 litres = 10,000 mL = `10000 ÷ 500 =` **20 mugs**.
>
> `20 × 15 = 300 s =` **5 minutes**.

---

A pair of oxen ploughs an acre in 6 hours; a tractor is 4 times faster. How long for a 20-acre field, each way?

> [!ANSWER]
> - **Oxen:** `20 × 6 =` **120 hours**
> - **Tractor:** 4 times faster ⇒ **one-quarter** the time ⇒ `120 ÷ 4 =` **30 hours**
>
> **Note:** "4 times faster" divides the time — a small **inverse** proportion hiding inside a direct one.

---

From the *Lilavati*: if 2½ palas of saffron cost 37 niskas, how much saffron for 9 niskas?

> [!ANSWER]
> `37 : 2.5 :: 9 : x` → `x = 2.5 × 9 / 37 = 22.5/37 ≈` **0.61 palas**.
>
> (Roughly three-fifths of a pala.)

---

A ₹10 coin is cupro-nickel, copper : nickel = 3 : 1. In a 7.7 g coin, how much of each metal?

> [!ANSWER]
> Groups `= 4`, each `= 7.7 ÷ 4 = 1.925 g`.
>
> **Copper ≈ 5.78 g, nickel ≈ 1.93 g.**

---

A 155 cm crane has neck : rest-of-body = 4 : 6. If your height is 150 cm and you had the same ratio, how long would your neck be?

> [!ANSWER]
> `4 : 6 = 2 : 3`, so groups `= 5` and each group `= 150 ÷ 5 = 30 cm`.
>
> Neck `= 2 × 30 =` **60 cm** — which is why we are not cranes.

---

Why does a drawn human figure look realistic only when head : torso : legs are kept in proportion?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because our eyes recognise a body by the **relative** sizes of its parts, not by absolute measurements — the same reason a stretched photo looks wrong.
>
> Scale every part by the **same factor** and the figure stays believable at any size.
