<!-- ===== What a percentage is ===== -->

Where does the word "per cent" come from, and what does 25% literally mean?

> [!ANSWER]
> Latin **per centum** — "by the hundred".
>
> `25%` means **25 out of every 100**: 25 rupees per ₹100, 25 marks per 100 marks, 25 people per 100.

---

Why is a percentage described as "a fraction in disguise"?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because **a percentage is exactly a fraction with denominator 100**.
>
> `20% = 20/100 = 1/5`, `33% = 33/100`. The `%` sign is shorthand for "÷ 100".

---

Express 3/5, 7/14, 9/20 and 72/150 as percentages.

> [!ANSWER]
> Multiply each by 100:
>
> - `3/5 × 100 =` **60%**
> - `7/14 = 1/2 → ` **50%**
> - `9/20 × 100 =` **45%**
> - `72/150 × 100 =` **48%**

---

Why does multiplying a fraction by 100 turn it into a percentage?

> [!ANSWER]
> A fraction says what share of **1** you have; a percentage says what share of **100**.
>
> Scaling the whole from 1 up to 100 scales your share by the same factor — hence **× 100**.

---

Express 24% as a fraction in three different equivalent forms.

> [!ANSWER]
> `24% = 24/100`, and equivalently **12/50**, **6/25**, **48/200**.
>
> The simplest form is **6/25**.

---

Complete: 5%, 43% and 1% as fractions and decimals.

> [!ANSWER]
> | | Fraction | Decimal |
> |---|---|---|
> | 5% | 5/100 = 1/20 | 0.05 |
> | 43% | 43/100 | 0.43 |
> | 1% | 1/100 | 0.01 |

---

Why is 100 chosen as the denominator rather than 10 or 1000?

> [!ANSWER]
> - Our number system is **base 10**, so 100 converts to decimals instantly (`31% = 0.31`).
> - 100 is **big enough for useful detail** — "per 10" would round away too much — and **small enough to picture mentally**.
>
> "Per thousand" and "per lakh" do exist in statistics, but 100 hits the sweet spot for everyday use.

---

Sugar is 9/34 of one biscuit and 13/45 of another. Which is sweeter, and what does this show about percentages?

> [!ANSWER]
> `9/34 = 26.47%` and `13/45 = 28.88%` → **the second is sweeter**.
>
> **What it shows:** the fractions are almost impossible to compare by eye. Forcing a **common denominator of 100** makes the comparison instant. That's the practical point of percentages.

---

Nandini has 25 marbles, 15 of them white. What percentage are white?

> [!ANSWER]
> `15/25 × 100 =` **60%**.

---

15 of 80 students walk to school. What percentage is that?

> [!ANSWER]
> `15/80 × 100 = 18.75%` — just under a fifth.

<!-- ===== Percentage of a quantity ===== -->

Find 25% of 160, 16% of 250, 62% of 360 and 140% of 40.

> [!ANSWER]
> - `25% of 160 = 160/4 =` **40**
> - `16% of 250 = 0.16 × 250 =` **40**
> - `62% of 360 = 0.62 × 360 =` **223.2**
> - `140% of 40 = 1.4 × 40 =` **56** (more than the whole — that's fine)

---

Find 1% of 1 hour and 7% of 10 kg.

> [!ANSWER]
> - 1 hour = 3600 s, so `1% = ` **36 seconds**
> - `7% of 10 kg = 0.7 kg =` **700 g**
>
> **Tip:** convert to the smallest sensible unit first, or you'll be left with awkward fractions of an hour.

---

Madhu ate 120 g of biscuits with 25% sugar; Madhav ate 95 g with 35% sugar. Who ate more sugar?

> [!ANSWER]
> - Madhu: `0.25 × 120 =` **30 g**
> - Madhav: `0.35 × 95 =` **33.25 g**
>
> **Madhav ate more.** Note that the bigger percentage won here — but only because the amounts were close. Never assume.

---

Why is it wrong to compare two percentages without knowing what they're percentages *of*?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because a percentage is a **share of a base**, and different bases give different amounts.
>
> `25% of 120 g = 30 g` beats `35% of 70 g = 24.5 g`. **A percentage without its base is meaningless.**

---

Mentally find 15% of 287, and explain the method.

> [!ANSWER]
> `15% = 10% + 5%`.
>
> - 10% of 287 = 28.7 (move the decimal one place)
> - 5% is half of that = 14.35
> - Sum = **43.05**
>
> **Why adding works:** `10/100 + 5/100 = 15/100` — same denominator, so numerators add.

---

Give a mental method for 75%, 90% and 70% of a number.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **75%** = 50% + 25% → halve, then halve again, then add
> - **90%** = whole − 10% → knock off one-tenth
> - **70%** = 7 × 10% → take 10%, multiply by 7 (or do 50% + 20%)

---

What is 5% of 40? What is 40% of 5? Explain the pattern with algebra.

> [!ANSWER]
> Both are **2**.
>
> **Why:** `(x/100) × y = xy/100 = (y/100) × x`. Multiplication doesn't care about order, so **x% of y always equals y% of x**.
>
> **Use it:** `4% of 75` is awkward, but `75% of 4 = 3` is instant.

---

Use the flip trick on 12% of 25 and 60% of 15.

> [!ANSWER]
> - `12% of 25 = 25% of 12 = 12/4 =` **3**
> - `60% of 15 = 15% of 60 = 9` — check: `0.6 × 15 =` **9** ✓

---

Fill in: 30% of k is 70, so 60% of k is ___, 90% of k is ___, 120% of k is ___.

> [!ANSWER]
> **140, 210, 280.**
>
> **Why:** 60% is twice 30%, 90% is three times, 120% is four times. You never need to find k itself.

---

Fill in: 3 is ___% of 300; ___ is 40% of 4; 40 is 80% of ___.

> [!ANSWER]
> - `3/300 × 100 =` **1%**
> - `0.4 × 4 =` **1.6**
> - `40/0.8 =` **50**

---

Is 10% of a day longer than 1% of a week?

> [!ANSWER]
> - 10% of a day = `0.1 × 24 =` **2.4 hours**
> - 1% of a week = `0.01 × 168 =` **1.68 hours**
>
> **Yes**, 10% of a day is longer — by about 43 minutes.

<!-- ===== Ratios to percentages ===== -->

Millet and water are mixed 2 : 7. What percentage is millet? For 500 mL, how much millet?

> [!ANSWER]
> The **whole** is `2 + 7 = 9` parts, so millet is `2/9`.
>
> `2/9 × 100 =` **22.22%** millet (water is 77.78%).
>
> For 500 mL: `0.2222 × 500 =` **111.1 mL of millet**.

---

Why is `2/7 × 100` the wrong way to turn the ratio 2 : 7 into a percentage?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because ratio terms are **part-to-part**, while a percentage is **part-to-whole**.
>
> `2 : 7` means 2 parts millet in a total of **9** parts — so the fraction is `2/9`, not `2/7`. **Add the terms first.**

---

A training session splits warm-up : play : cool-down as 10% : 80% : 10%. For a 90-minute session, how long is each?

> [!ANSWER]
> - Warm-up `= 0.1 × 90 =` **9 min**
> - Play `= 0.8 × 90 =` **72 min**
> - Cool-down `= 0.1 × 90 =` **9 min**
>
> Check: `9 + 72 + 9 = 90` ✓

---

A halwa recipe for 4 people is rava 40%, sugar 40%, ghee 20%. What are the proportions for 8 people? If the total weight is 2 kg, how much of each?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The percentages don't change** — 40%, 40%, 20%. Doubling the people doubles every ingredient, so the *shares* stay the same.
>
> For 2 kg: rava **800 g**, sugar **800 g**, ghee **400 g**.

A shop's daily target is ₹5,000. Express sales of ₹2,000, ₹6,000 and ₹9,550 as percentages of target.

> [!ANSWER]
> - `2000/5000 × 100 =` **40%** (60% short)
> - `6000/5000 × 100 =` **120%** (20% over target)
> - `9550/5000 × 100 =` **191%**

---

What does a percentage greater than 100 mean?

> [!ANSWER]
> **More than the whole you're comparing to.**
>
> `250%` = **2.5 times** the original. `120%` = the original plus a fifth again.
>
> There's nothing strange about it — you can score 150% of a target, even if you can't score 150% in an exam.

---

Last year a farmer harvested 260 kg of wheat; this year 650 kg. Express this year's harvest as a percentage of last year's.

> [!ANSWER]
> `650/260 × 100 =` **250%** — i.e. **2.5 times** last year's harvest.

<!-- ===== Increase and decrease ===== -->

Tomato prices went from ₹30 to ₹42 per kg. What is the percentage increase?

> [!ANSWER]
> Increase `= 12`. Base = the **original** ₹30.
>
> `12/30 × 100 =` **40% increase**.

---

Cinema footfall fell from 160 to 100. What is the percentage decrease?

> [!ANSWER]
> Decrease `= 60`, base `= 160`.
>
> `60/160 × 100 =` **37.5% decrease**.

---

Prices go up 40% then come back down to the original. Is that a 40% fall?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No.** ₹30 → ₹42 is +40%. Coming back ₹42 → ₹30 is a fall of `12/42 × 100 =` **28.6%**.
>
> **Why:** the base changed. A rise uses the small number as base; the matching fall uses the big one. **Up r% and down r% never cancel.**

---

Do these mean the same? (i) the 1991 population is 165% of the 1961 population; (ii) the population increased by 65% from 1961 to 1991.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Yes.**
>
> (i) `q = 1.65p`. (ii) `q = p + 0.65p = 1.65p`. Identical.
>
> "**165% of**" and "**increased by 65%**" are two phrasings of the multiplier **1.65**.

---

Give the multiplier for: up 5%, down 15%, up 120%, down 3%.

> [!ANSWER]
> - up 5% → **× 1.05**
> - down 15% → **× 0.85**
> - up 120% → **× 2.20**
> - down 3% → **× 0.97**
>
> **The rule:** up r% → `1 + r/100`; down r% → `1 − r/100`.

---

Rice cost ₹38 per kg in 2024 and ₹42 in 2025. What is the rate of inflation?

> [!ANSWER]
> `4/38 × 100 =` **10.53%**.

---

Petrol was ₹60 in 2015 and ₹100 in 2025. What is the percentage increase?

> [!ANSWER]
> `40/60 × 100 =` **66.66%**.
>
> (Not 40% — the base is the **original** ₹60, not the new ₹100.)

---

A number increased by 20% becomes 90. What was the number?

> [!ANSWER]
> `1.2x = 90` → `x =` **75**.
>
> **Not 72** — subtracting 20% of 90 would be using the wrong base.

---

An elephant population p grew by 5%. Which expression gives the new population: `p × 0.05`, `p × 1.5`, `p × 1.05`, `p + 1.50`?

> [!ANSWER]
> **`p × 1.05`**.
>
> `p × 0.05` is only the *increase*, not the total. `p × 1.5` would be a 50% rise.

---

"Demand for cameras has fallen 85% in a decade." Which is equivalent: (a) demand now is 85% of before, (b) demand now is 15% of before, (c) demand before was 185% of now?

> [!ANSWER]
> **(b) — demand now is 15% of what it was.**
>
> A fall of 85% leaves `100 − 85 =` **15%**.
>
> (a) describes a 15% fall; (c) would be a fall of about 46%.

<!-- ===== Profit and loss ===== -->

Define cost price, marked price and selling price, and say which is the base for profit % and which for discount %.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **CP** — what the seller paid
> - **MP** — the price displayed (MRP)
> - **SP** — what the customer actually pays
>
> **Profit % and loss % use the COST price as base. Discount % uses the MARKED price.**
>
> Mixing these two up is the commonest error in the chapter.

---

A retailer buys a sweater at ₹300, marks it ₹480, sells at ₹430. Find the profit and the profit percentage.

> [!ANSWER]
> Profit `= 430 − 300 =` **₹130**.
>
> `130/300 × 100 =` **43.3% profit** (on cost price).

---

A shopkeeper buys a geometry box for ₹75 and sells for ₹110. What is the profit margin on cost?

> [!ANSWER]
> Profit `= ₹35`. `35/75 × 100 =` **46.67%**.

---

Rice cost ₹35/kg; 10 kg was sold for ₹300. Find the percentage loss. Would working per kg give the same answer?

> [!ANSWER]
> CP `= 350`, SP `= 300`, loss `= 50`. `50/350 × 100 =` **14.28% loss**.
>
> **Per kg gives the same answer:** CP ₹35, SP ₹30, loss ₹5 → `5/35 × 100 = 14.28%`. **Percentages don't change when you scale both numbers equally** — that's the whole point of them.

---

A carpenter's materials cost ₹475 and he wants a 50% profit margin. What price should he sell at?

> [!ANSWER]
> `475 × 1.5 =` **₹712.50**.

---

Vases cost ₹2,650 each. One is sold at an 18% loss. What is the selling price?

> [!ANSWER]
> SP is `100 − 18 = 82%` of CP.
>
> `0.82 × 2650 =` **₹2,173**.
>
> (Or compute the loss `0.18 × 2650 = 477` and subtract.)

---

Strawberries are sold at ₹80/kg at a 12% loss. What was the cost price?

> [!ANSWER]
> `0.88 × CP = 80` → `CP = 80/0.88 =` **₹90.91**.
>
> **Work backwards through the multiplier** — don't add 12% of 80.

---

A ₹1,800 cooker is discounted 35%. What is the selling price? If the cost price was ₹900, what is the profit percentage?

> [!ANSWER]
> SP `= 0.65 × 1800 =` **₹1,170**.
>
> Profit `= 1170 − 900 = 270`, so `270/900 × 100 =` **30% profit**.

---

A shirt priced ₹300 has a 25% discount. What does the customer pay?

> [!ANSWER]
> `0.75 × 300 =` **₹225**.

---

Samson paid ₹4,40,000 for a car after a 15% discount. What was the original price?

> [!ANSWER]
> `0.85 × MP = 440000` → `MP =` **₹5,17,647** (approximately).

---

A company's revenue was ₹2.5 crore with a 25% profit margin **on revenue**. What was the total expenditure?

> [!ANSWER]
> Profit `= 0.25 × 2.5 crore = ₹62.5 lakh`.
>
> Expenditure `= 2.5 crore − 62.5 lakh =` **₹1.875 crore**.

---

Explain the difference between gross profit and net profit.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **Gross profit** = sales − cost of the goods sold. (₹80,000 − ₹48,000 = ₹32,000)
> - **Net profit** = gross profit − all other expenses (transport, salaries, electricity). (₹32,000 − ₹8,000 = ₹24,000)
>
> Gross profit ignores the cost of *running* the business; net profit is what's actually left.

---

A trader buys 50 kg fertiliser bags at ₹500 and sells at ₹750. Is her profit 50% or 33.33%?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Both — they answer different questions.**
>
> - On **cost**: `250/500 =` **50%** — "how much did I gain compared with what I invested?"
> - On **revenue**: `250/750 =` **33.33%** — "what share of my sales is profit?"
>
> Always state which base you're using.

---

A pencil seller finds that the SP of 3 pencils equals the CP of 5 pencils. Profit or loss, and by what percentage?

> [!ANSWER]
> Take CP = ₹1 per pencil. Then 3 pencils sell for ₹5 but cost ₹3.
>
> Profit `= 2` on a cost of `3` → `2/3 × 100 =` **66.67% profit**.

<!-- ===== Compounding ===== -->

What does "10% p.a." mean, and what are the principal and the interest?

> [!ANSWER]
> **p.a. = per annum**, i.e. per year.
>
> The **principal** is the money deposited or borrowed; the **interest** is the extra money paid for its use. At 10% p.a., ₹6,000 earns `0.10 × 6000 =` **₹600** in a year.

---

Write the formulas for the final amount with and without compounding.

> [!ANSWER]
> With principal p, rate r (as a decimal) and t terms:
>
> - **Without compounding:** `p(1 + rt)` — the interest `p × r` is the same every year because the principal never changes.
> - **With compounding:** `p(1 + r)^t` — each year's interest is added back, so next year's interest is earned on a bigger amount.

---

₹6,000 at 10% p.a. for 3 years. Compare the two options.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **Simple:** `6000 × (1 + 0.1×3) = 6000 × 1.3 =` **₹7,800**
> - **Compound:** `6000 × 1.1³ = 6000 × 1.331 =` **₹7,986**
>
> Compounding gains **₹186 more** — because in years 2 and 3 you earn interest **on the interest**.

---

₹20,000 at 10% p.a. for 2 years — compare simple and compound.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **Simple:** `20000 × 1.2 =` **₹24,000**
> - **Compound:** `20000 × 1.1² = 20000 × 1.21 =` **₹24,200**
>
> The extra ₹200 is exactly 10% of the first year's ₹2,000 interest.

---

₹50,000 at 7% p.a. for 3 years. Find the simple interest, and how much more compounding gives.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **Simple interest:** `50000 × 0.07 × 3 =` **₹10,500**
> - **Compound amount:** `50000 × 1.07³ = 50000 × 1.225043 =` ₹61,252
> - Compound interest = **₹11,252**, so compounding gives **₹752 more**.

---

Giridhar borrows ₹12,500 at 12% p.a. simple for 3 years; Raghava borrows the same at 10% p.a. compounded annually for 3 years. Who pays more?

> [!ANSWER]
> - **Giridhar:** `12500 × 0.12 × 3 =` **₹4,500** interest
> - **Raghava:** `12500 × 1.1³ = 12500 × 1.331 = ₹16,637.50` → interest **₹4,137.50**
>
> **Giridhar pays ₹362.50 more** — the higher rate beats the compounding over this short a period.

---

Why is compounding called exponential growth and simple interest linear growth?

> [!ANSWER]
> - **Simple:** you **add** a fixed amount `pr` each year — the graph is a **straight line**.
> - **Compound:** you **multiply** by a fixed factor `(1 + r)` each year — the graph **curves upward**, getting steeper.
>
> Over long periods the curve leaves the line far behind, because the compound version earns interest on interest.

---

A city's population is 1.5 crore and rises 3% a year. What is it after 3 years?

> [!ANSWER]
> `1.5 crore × 1.03³ = 1.5 × 1.092727 =` **about 1.64 crore**.

---

Bacteria grow 2.5% per hour from an initial count of 5,06,000. How many after 2 hours?

> [!ANSWER]
> `506000 × 1.025² = 506000 × 1.050625 =` **about 5,31,616**.

<!-- ===== Depreciation ===== -->

A ₹21,000 TV depreciates 5% in a year. What is it worth?

> [!ANSWER]
> `0.95 × 21000 =` **₹19,950**.
>
> **Depreciation is compounding with a multiplier below 1.**

---

A village of 1,250 people loses 10% each decade. What is the population after 3 decades? Why isn't the answer 875?

> [!ANSWER]
> `1250 × 0.9 × 0.9 × 0.9 = 911.25 ≈` **910 people**.
>
> **Why not 875:** each 10% is taken off a **smaller** number than the last. Subtracting 30% in one go would take 10% of 1,250 three times over, which is too much.

<!-- ===== Tricky percentages ===== -->

Bakery A offers "30% + 20% off"; Bakery B offers "50% off". On a ₹200 cake, which is cheaper?

> [!ANSWER]
> - **A:** `200 → 140 → 112` (the 20% comes off the already-reduced ₹140)
> - **B:** `200 → 100`
>
> **B is cheaper.** `0.7 × 0.8 = 0.56`, so "30% + 20%" is really a **44% discount**, not 50%.
>
> **The lesson: successive discounts multiply, they don't add.**

---

A shopkeeper marks up 50% on cost, then offers a 50% discount. Does she break even?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No — she loses 25%.**
>
> Cost x → marked `1.5x` → discounted `0.5 × 1.5x =` **0.75x**.
>
> **Why:** the 50% markup is on the **small** number x, but the 50% discount is on the **bigger** number 1.5x. The same percentage of a bigger base is a bigger amount.

---

Following on: if she sold goods for ₹12,000 after that discount, how much did she lose, and what discount should she have offered to break even?

> [!ANSWER]
> `0.75x = 12000` → `x = ₹16,000`, so she **lost ₹4,000** — a **25% loss**.
>
> To break even: `1.5x(1 − d) = x` → `1 − d = 2/3` → `d =` **33.33%**.

---

A shop offers "20% off **or** ₹50 off". Which is better for purchases of ₹180, ₹225 and ₹300?

> [!ANSWER]
> | Purchase | 20% off | ₹50 off | Better |
> |---:|---:|---:|---|
> | ₹180 | ₹36 | ₹50 | **₹50 off** |
> | ₹225 | ₹45 | ₹50 | **₹50 off** |
> | ₹300 | ₹60 | ₹50 | **20% off** |
>
> **Break-even at ₹250**, where 20% is exactly ₹50. Above that, the percentage wins.

---

Bus fares rose 3% last year and 4% this year. What is the overall increase?

> [!ANSWER]
> `1.03 × 1.04 = 1.0712` → **7.12%**, not 7%.
>
> The extra 0.12% is the 4% charged on last year's 3% rise.

---

A mouse population changes +5%, −2%, −3% over three months. Is the final population more or less than the start?

> [!ANSWER]
> `p × 1.05 × 0.98 × 0.97 = 0.9981p` → **slightly less than p**.
>
> **Why chaining beats adding:** `+5 − 2 − 3 = 0` suggests no change, but each percentage acts on a different base, so the answer isn't exactly zero.

---

A shopkeeper sets a 35% profit margin then offers a 30% discount. Profit or loss?

> [!ANSWER]
> Cost x → marked `1.35x` → sold at `0.7 × 1.35x =` **0.945x**.
>
> **A loss of 5.5%.** The 30% is taken off a bigger number than the 35% was added to.

---

100 people are in a room, 99% left-handed. How many left-handers must leave to make it 98%?

> [!ANSWER]
> **49.**
>
> **The trick: anchor on what doesn't change.** There is exactly **1 right-hander**, and he never leaves. He must become **2%** of the room, so the room must hold `1 ÷ 0.02 =` **50 people**.
>
> From 99 left-handers down to 49 means **49 leave**.

---

A rectangle's length increases 10% but the area stays the same. By exactly what percentage does the breadth decrease?

> [!ANSWER]
> `1.1 × m = 1` → `m = 1/1.1 = 0.9090…`
>
> The breadth falls by **9.09%** (exactly `1/11`), **not 10%**.
>
> **Why not 10%:** `1.1 × 0.9 = 0.99`, which would shrink the area by 1%.

---

Which is the better deal: deposit ₹100 and get back ₹300, or deposit ₹1,000 and get back ₹1,500?

> [!ANSWER]
> - Option A: gain ₹200 on ₹100 → **200% gain**
> - Option B: gain ₹500 on ₹1,000 → **50% gain**
>
> **A has the better percentage; B gives more money.** Which you'd choose depends on whether you have ₹1,000 spare — the point is that **percentages compare proportions, not amounts**.

---

Ariba says "I have 120% of the marbles Arun has." What can Arun say?

> [!ANSWER]
> Arun has `1/1.2 = 0.8333…` of Ariba's, so **"I have about 83.3% of the marbles Ariba has"** — equivalently, about **16.7% fewer**.
>
> **Note it isn't 80%.** Reversing a percentage comparison means taking the **reciprocal** of the multiplier, not subtracting.

<!-- ===== More worked problems ===== -->

Maximum marks are 75 and an A grade needs 80%. What is the minimum score?

> [!ANSWER]
> `0.8 × 75 =` **60 marks**.

---

A cyclist has completed 40% of the Delhi–Agra journey, covering 92 km. How much further?

> [!ANSWER]
> If 40% is 92 km, then 20% is 46 km, so **60% is `92 + 46 =` 138 km**.
>
> (Or: total `= 92 × 100/40 = 230 km`, remaining `= 230 − 92 = 138 km`.)
