<!-- ===== Recap ===== -->

Viswanath mixes 6 cups rice with 3 cups urad dal; Puneet mixes 4 with 2. Will their idlis taste the same?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Yes** (assuming everything else is the same).
>
> Cross multiply `6 : 3` and `4 : 2`: `6 × 2 = 12` and `3 × 4 = 12`. **Equal, so the ratios are proportional** — both are 2 : 1.

---

State the two equivalent tests for `a : b :: c : d`.

> [!ANSWER]
> **`ad = bc`** (cross multiplication), equivalently **`a/c = b/d`**.
>
> Both say the same thing: one shared factor takes the first ratio to the second.

---

<!-- ===== Maps ===== -->

What does "RF 1 : 60,00,000" on a map mean?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Representative Fraction** — the ratio of a distance **on the map** to the matching distance **on the ground**.
>
> `1 cm` on the map = `60,00,000 cm` on the ground = `60,000 m` = **60 km**.

---

Two cities are 3.5 cm apart on a 1 : 60,00,000 map. How far apart are they?

> [!ANSWER]
> `3.5 × 60 =` **210 km**.

---

Why is the map distance called *geographical* distance rather than road distance?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because the map measures the **straight line** between two points.
>
> Roads bend around hills, rivers and towns, so the **road distance is always longer** than the geographical distance.

---

Two maps of the same region have scales 1 : 60,00,000 and 1 : 20,00,000. Will they give the same ground distance between two cities?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Yes — approximately.**
>
> The second map is **three times bigger**, so the cities appear 3 times further apart on paper. But each centimetre now stands for `20 km` instead of `60 km`.
>
> `3 × d × 20 = d × 60` ✓ The two effects cancel exactly.

---

You are drawing your classroom to a scale of 1 : 50. A 4 m blackboard is how long on your sketch?

> [!ANSWER]
> `4 m = 400 cm`, so on paper it is `400 ÷ 50 =` **8 cm**.

---

<!-- ===== Multi-term ratios ===== -->

A spice mix uses coriander : chillies : toor dal : fenugreek = 8 : 4 : 2 : 1. With only 2 chillies, how much of everything else?

> [!ANSWER]
> 2 chillies is **half** of 4, so **halve every term**:
>
> **4 spoons coriander, 2 chillies, 1 spoon toor dal, ½ spoon fenugreek.**
>
> **Why all of them:** the ratio is only preserved if **every** quantity changes by the **same** factor.

---

Purple paint is red : blue : white = 2 : 3 : 5. With 10 L of white, how much red and blue, and what is the total?

> [!ANSWER]
> White is **5 parts** = 10 L, so **1 part = 2 L**.
>
> Red `= 2 × 2 =` **4 L**, blue `= 3 × 2 =` **6 L**.
>
> Total `= 4 + 6 + 10 =` **20 L**.

---

Concrete is cement : sand : gravel = 1 : 1.5 : 3. With 3 bags of cement, how much concrete can you make?

> [!ANSWER]
> Multiply everything by 3 → **3 : 4.5 : 9**.
>
> Total `= 3 + 4.5 + 9 =` **16.5 bags of concrete**.

---

<!-- ===== Dividing a whole ===== -->

110 units of concrete are needed at 1 : 1.5 : 3. How much of each ingredient?

> [!ANSWER]
> Total parts `= 1 + 1.5 + 3 = 5.5`. Each part `= 110 ÷ 5.5 =` **20**.
>
> **Cement 20, sand 30, gravel 60** ✓ (they add to 110)

---

Write the general rule for dividing x in a ratio `a : b : c : …`.

> [!ANSWER]
> Each part is
>
> `x × a/(a+b+c+…)`, `x × b/(a+b+c+…)`, `x × c/(a+b+c+…)`, …
>
> **Why:** the denominators count the **total number of groups**; each share takes its own number of groups.

---

You need 50 mL of purple paint at 2 : 3 : 5. How much of each colour?

> [!ANSWER]
> Total parts 10, each `= 5 mL`.
>
> **Red 10 mL, blue 15 mL, white 25 mL.**

---

Construct a triangle with angles in the ratio 1 : 3 : 5.

> [!ANSWER]
> **The whole here is 180°**, not 360 — angles of a triangle sum to 180°.
>
> Total parts 9, each `= 180 ÷ 9 = 20°`.
>
> Angles: **20°, 60°, 100°** ✓

---

A 150-minute cricket session splits warm-up : batting : bowling : fielding = 3 : 4 : 3 : 5. How long is each?

> [!ANSWER]
> Total parts `= 15`, each `= 150 ÷ 15 =` **10 min**.
>
> **Warm-up 30, batting 40, bowling 30, fielding 50 minutes** ✓

---

A library has Odiya : Hindi : English books = 3 : 2 : 1, with 288 Odiya books. How many Hindi and English?

> [!ANSWER]
> 3 parts = 288, so **1 part = 96**.
>
> **Hindi `= 2 × 96 =` 192**, **English `= 1 × 96 =` 96**.

---

I have 100 coins in the ratio ₹10 : ₹5 : ₹2 : ₹1 = 4 : 3 : 2 : 1. How much money is that?

> [!ANSWER]
> Total parts `= 10`, so each part `= 100 ÷ 10 =` **10 coins**.
>
> 40 ten-rupee, 30 five-rupee, 20 two-rupee, 10 one-rupee coins.
>
> Value `= 400 + 150 + 40 + 10 =` **₹600**.

---

Can you construct a triangle with **side lengths** in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5? Will all such triangles be congruent?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Yes you can** — and it will be a **right-angled** triangle (a Baudhāyana triple).
>
> **But they are NOT all congruent.** Sides 3–4–5 and 6–8–10 have the same *shape* but different *sizes*. Ratios fix shape, not size — such triangles are **similar**, not congruent.

---

Can you construct a triangle with sides in the ratio 1 : 3 : 5?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No.**
>
> **Why:** the two shorter sides must together be **longer** than the third. Here `1 + 3 = 4`, which is **less than 5** — the two short sides can't reach across. No such triangle exists at any scale.

<!-- ===== Pie charts ===== -->

Why must a pie-chart slice's angle be proportional to its share?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because the **whole circle is 360°** and represents the whole data set.
>
> A slice that is one-quarter of the data must be one-quarter of the circle — `90°` — or the picture would mislead the eye.

---

Grades A–E were scored by 12, 10, 8, 6 and 4 students. Find each slice's angle.

> [!ANSWER]
> Simplify `12 : 10 : 8 : 6 : 4` by HCF 2 → `6 : 5 : 4 : 3 : 2`. Total parts 20, so each part is `360 ÷ 20 =` **18°**.
>
> | Grade | Angle |
> |---|---:|
> | A | **108°** |
> | B | **90°** |
> | C | **72°** |
> | D | **54°** |
> | E | **36°** |
>
> Check: they add to **360°** ✓

---

360 people voted on their favourite season: 90 summer, 120 rainy, the rest winter. Find the pie-chart angles.

> [!ANSWER]
> Winter `= 360 − 90 − 120 =` **150 people**.
>
> Conveniently there are **360 people and 360°**, so **1 person = 1°**:
>
> **Summer 90°, rainy 120°, winter 150°** ✓

---

Draw a pie chart for TV channel preference: Entertainment 50%, Sports 25%, News 15%, Information 10%.

> [!ANSWER]
> **`1% = 3.6°`** (since `360 ÷ 100 = 3.6`).
>
> - Entertainment `= 50 × 3.6 =` **180°** (half the circle)
> - Sports `= 25 × 3.6 =` **90°**
> - News `= 15 × 3.6 =` **54°**
> - Information `= 10 × 3.6 =` **36°**
>
> Total **360°** ✓

---

When drawing a pie chart, from where do you measure each new angle?

> [!ANSWER]
> **From the radius you drew last**, not from the starting line.
>
> Each slice sits next to the previous one, so its angle is measured off the shared edge. Measuring from the start every time would overlap the slices.

---

A transport pie chart has slices: bus 120°, walk 90°, cycle 60°, two-wheeler 60°, car 30°. Which mode is commonest, and what fraction travel by car?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Bus is commonest** — the biggest slice at 120°.
>
> Car is `30/360 =` **1/12** of the children.
>
> **Cycle and two-wheeler are equal** (60° each).

---

In that survey, 18 children travel by car (a 30° slice). How many children took part?

> [!ANSWER]
> The car slice is `30/360 = 1/12` of the whole.
>
> So the total is `18 × 12 =` **216 children**.
>
> **Taxis: zero** — there is no taxi slice in the chart.

<!-- ===== Inverse proportion ===== -->

Puneeth's father takes 3 hours at 30 km/h. Can you write `30 : 60 :: 3 : x` to find the time at 60 km/h?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No.** Going faster takes **less** time, so that setup would give the wrong direction.
>
> Speed and time are **inversely** proportional: `30 × 3 = 60 × x` → `x =` **1.5 hours**.

---

Complete the table and find what stays constant: walk 5 km/h → 18 h; bicycle 15 km/h → 6 h; motorcycle 30 km/h → 3 h; car 60 km/h → 1.5 h.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Speed × time = 90 in every column** — that's the distance, which never changes.
>
> `5×18 = 15×6 = 30×3 = 60×1.5 = ` **90 km** ✓

---

Define inverse proportion, and state the equation.

> [!ANSWER]
> Two quantities are in **inverse proportion** when one changes by a factor n and the other changes by **1/n**.
>
> **Their PRODUCT stays constant: `xy = k`.**
>
> So `x₁y₁ = x₂y₂`, which rearranges to `x₁/x₂ = y₂/y₁` — **note the subscripts swap sides**. That flip is the whole difference from direct proportion.

---

How can you tell direct from inverse in one step?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Ask whether the PRODUCT or the QUOTIENT stays fixed.**
>
> - `speed × time = distance` — product fixed → **inverse**
> - `price ÷ length = rate` — quotient fixed → **direct**

---

Which of these tables show inverse proportion? (i) x = 40, 80, 25, 16 with y = 20, 10, 32, 50. (ii) x = 40, 80, 25, 16 with y = 20, 10, 12.5, 8. (iii) x = 30, 90, 150, 10 with y = 15, 5, 3, 45.

> [!ANSWER]
> - **(i) Yes** — products `800, 800, 800, 800` ✓
> - **(ii) No** — products `800, 800, 312.5, 128` ✗. (It's **direct**: `x/y = 2` throughout.)
> - **(iii) Yes** — products `450, 450, 450, 450` ✓

---

x and y are inversely proportional. If x = 16 gives y = 9, complete: x = 12 → y = ?, y = 48 → x = ?, x = 36 → y = ?

> [!ANSWER]
> The constant is `16 × 9 =` **144**.
>
> - `x = 12` → `y = 144/12 =` **12**
> - `y = 48` → `x = 144/48 =` **3**
> - `x = 36` → `y = 144/36 =` **4**

---

Which of these pairs are inversely proportional: (a) taps and filling time; (b) painters and days; (c) petrol in tank and distance travelled; (d) cyclist's speed and time; (e) cloth bought and price; (f) pages in a book and reading time?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Inverse: (a), (b), (d)** — more taps → less time; more painters → fewer days; more speed → less time.
>
> **Direct: (c), (e), (f)** — more petrol → more distance; more cloth → more cost; more pages → more reading time.

---

20 workers take 4 days to lay a road. How long for 10 workers?

> [!ANSWER]
> `20 × 4 = 10 × y` → `y =` **8 days**.
>
> Halving the workers **doubles** the days — the factors are exact inverses.

---

2 pumps fill a tank in 18 hours. How long if 2 more identical pumps are added?

> [!ANSWER]
> Now 4 pumps. `2 × 18 = 4 × x` → `x =` **9 hours**.

---

A school has food for 80 students for 15 days. 20 more students join. How long will it last, and what must you assume?

> [!ANSWER]
> `80 × 15 = 100 × x` → `x =` **12 days**.
>
> **Assumption:** every student eats the same amount every day, and nothing is wasted or added. Without that, the product wouldn't stay constant.

---

A tank supplies 20 families for 6 days. 10 more families move in. How long will the water last?

> [!ANSWER]
> `20 × 6 = 30 × x` → `x =` **4 days**.
>
> **Assumptions:** every family uses the same amount of water each day, and no more water enters the tank.

---

3 workers paint a fence in 4 days. How long with 4 workers?

> [!ANSWER]
> `3 × 4 = 4 × x` → `x =` **3 days**.
>
> **Assumptions:** all workers work at the same rate, and they don't get in each other's way — a fence can genuinely be split four ways.

---

42 machines produce a batch of toys in 63 days. How many machines to do it in 54 days?

> [!ANSWER]
> `42 × 63 = m × 54` → `m = 2646 ÷ 54 =` **49 machines**.

---

25 rows of 12 chairs are rearranged into rows of 20. How many rows now?

> [!ANSWER]
> Total chairs `= 25 × 12 =` 300 — **that's the constant**.
>
> `300 ÷ 20 =` **15 rows**.

---

A school day has 8 periods of 45 minutes. If it moves to 9 periods with the same total time, how long is each?

> [!ANSWER]
> Total `= 8 × 45 =` **360 minutes** — the constant.
>
> `360 ÷ 9 =` **40 minutes** per period.

---

A car takes 2 hours at 60 km/h. How long at 80 km/h?

> [!ANSWER]
> `60 × 2 = 80 × t` → `t = 120/80 =` **1.5 hours**, i.e. 1 hour 30 minutes.

---

24 pencils cost ₹120. What do 20 cost?

> [!ANSWER]
> **This is DIRECT, not inverse** — fewer pencils cost less.
>
> One pencil `= 120 ÷ 24 = ₹5`, so 20 cost **₹100**.

---

It takes 6 hours to fill 2 tanks with one pump. How long for 5 tanks?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Direct, not inverse** — more tanks need **more** time.
>
> `6 ÷ 2 = 3` hours per tank, so 5 tanks take **15 hours**.
>
> **Watch out:** "pumps and time" is inverse, but "tanks and time" is direct. Read which quantity is changing.

<!-- ===== Work problems ===== -->

Ram cuts the vegetables in 1 hour, Shyam in 1.5 hours. How long together?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Add the rates, not the times.**
>
> - Ram: **1** unit of work per hour
> - Shyam: `1 ÷ 1.5 =` **2/3** unit per hour
> - Together: `1 + 2/3 =` **5/3** units per hour
>
> Time for 1 unit `= 1 ÷ 5/3 =` **3/5 hour = 36 minutes**.

---

A small pump fills a tank in 3 hours, a large one in 2 hours. How long together?

> [!ANSWER]
> Rates: `1/3 + 1/2 = 2/6 + 3/6 =` **5/6** tanks per hour.
>
> Time `= 6/5 =` **1.2 hours = 1 hour 12 minutes**.

---

Why can't you just average the two times in a work problem?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because **both people work during the SAME hour**, not one after the other. What adds up is the **work done per hour** — the rate.
>
> **Sanity check:** the joint time must be **less than the faster person's time alone**. Averaging 3 h and 2 h gives 2.5 h, which is *slower* than the big pump on its own — obviously wrong.

---

Summarise the difference between direct and inverse proportion in a table.

> [!ANSWER]
> | | Direct | Inverse |
> |---|---|---|
> | Behaviour | both up together | one up, one down |
> | Constant | **quotient** `x/y = k` | **product** `xy = k` |
> | Equation | `x₁/y₁ = x₂/y₂` | `x₁y₁ = x₂y₂` |
> | Cross form | `x₁/x₂ = y₁/y₂` | `x₁/x₂ = y₂/y₁` |
> | Example | cloth and cost | speed and time |
