## Probe and ponder

- Which things in a picture are **matter**, and which are **not**?
- How can **elements combine** to make a **compound**?
- How might a compound that **absorbs carbon dioxide** from the air help the environment?

Stairs, air, water, food, clothes, books, trees, a ball — **all matter**, all made of tiny **particles**. Most things are **not one substance** but **several mixed together**.

## Mixtures

**Poha** and a **sprout salad** are made by mixing ingredients; so are **sugar water, soup, lemonade**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **MIXTURE = two or more substances mixed together, where EACH KEEPS ITS OWN PROPERTIES.**
> - The substances in it are its **COMPONENTS**.
> - Components **do NOT react chemically** with each other.
> - They can be in **any proportion** and are separated by **physical methods**.

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **NON-UNIFORM** | components **visible** (by eye or a magnifier), **unevenly** spread | **sprout salad**, sand + water, oil + water, muddy water |
| **UNIFORM** | components **evenly spread**, **can't be told apart** even under a microscope | **sugar water**, salt water, **air**, vinegar, **alloys** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ALLOYS are uniform mixtures of metals** (sometimes with a non-metal):
> - **Stainless steel** = **iron + nickel + chromium** + a little **carbon**
> - **Brass** = **copper + zinc**
> - **Bronze** = **copper + tin**

> [!NOTE]
> **Mishraloha** — ancient India's name for a mixture of metals with properties **different from its metals**. The *Charaka Samhita*, *Susruta Samhita* and *Rasaratna Samucchaya* mention alloys in medicine — e.g. **Kamsya (bronze) = Tamra (copper) 4 parts + Vanga (tin) 1 part**, used to aid digestion and immunity.

### Is air a mixture?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Air is a UNIFORM mixture** of mainly **nitrogen (~78%), oxygen, argon, carbon dioxide and water vapour**.
> - **Oxygen** is needed by most living things and **supports burning**; **nitrogen doesn't** take part in burning.
> - **Water vapour** shows up as droplets on a **cool surface**.

**Activity 8.1 — proving CO₂ is in air:** add **calcium oxide (quick lime)** to water carefully (it reacts **vigorously** and gets **hot**), stir, **filter** → clear **lime water** (calcium hydroxide). Leave it in a petri dish for a few hours → it turns **MILKY**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Calcium hydroxide + carbon dioxide → calcium carbonate + water**
> Calcium carbonate is **insoluble** — tiny **white particles** make it look milky. **Air contains CO₂.**

**Activity 8.2:** leave a **black sheet** near an open window for a few hours → **dust particles** settle on it (also seen as **sparkling specks in a sunbeam**).

> [!NOTE]
> **Dust isn't a real part of air** — it's a **pollutant**, varying from place to place and time to time. Major air pollutants: **particulate matter (dust, soot), carbon monoxide, ozone, nitrogen dioxide, sulfur dioxide**. The **Air Quality Index (AQI)** describes air quality.

### Types of mixture by state

| Mixture | Example | Uniform? |
|---|---|---|
| gas + gas | **air** | uniform |
| gas + liquid | **soda water**; oxygen in water | uniform |
| solid + gas | **carbon (soot) particles in air** | non-uniform |
| liquid + liquid | **vinegar** (acetic acid in water) | uniform |
| liquid + liquid | **oil and water** | non-uniform |
| solid + liquid | **sand and water** | non-uniform |
| solid + liquid | **seawater** | uniform |
| solid + solid | **baking powder** (baking soda + tartaric acid) | (non-uniform — two powders) |
| solid + solid | **alloys** | uniform |

> [!WARNING]
> **In science, every component of a mixture must be a PURE SUBSTANCE.** In everyday life we separate mixtures to **get what we want and throw the rest**; in science we separate them to **get PURE substances**.

## Pure substances

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **"Pure" means different things:**
> - **Everyday:** "pure ghee / milk / spices" = **not ADULTERATED**. (**Adulteration** = illegally adding **cheaper or poor-quality** stuff to increase quantity or cut cost — it lowers quality and can be **harmful**.)
> - **Science:** a **PURE SUBSTANCE** has **no other substance** in it — it's made of **only ONE type of particle**, and it **can't be separated into other kinds of matter by any physical process**.
>
> So scientifically, **milk, packed fruit juice and soil are MIXTURES**, while **baking soda and sugar are pure substances** (compounds).

## Breaking water down — elements

Ice ↔ water ↔ vapour: the **particles stay the same** (physical change). But what if we pass **electricity** through water?

**Activity 8.3 (teacher demo):** water with a few drops of **dilute sulfuric acid**; a **9 V battery** in the beaker; a water-filled test tube upside-down over **each terminal**.
- **Bubbles** form at both terminals; gas collects in both tubes — **unequal volumes** (one tube gets about **twice** as much).
- **Burning candle at the tubes:** one gives a **POP** → **HYDROGEN**; the other makes the flame **burn brighter** → **OXYGEN**.
- They **aren't water vapour** — vapour would just condense back into water.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WATER → HYDROGEN + OXYGEN** — new substances form, so this is a **CHEMICAL change**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ELEMENT = a pure substance that CANNOT be broken down into anything simpler.** The **building blocks of all matter**.
> - Made of **identical particles called ATOMS**, different from any other element's atoms.
> - e.g. **hydrogen, oxygen, gold, silver, sulfur, carbon**.
> - Many atoms can't exist alone and join into **MOLECULES** — **2 hydrogen atoms → a hydrogen molecule**; **2 oxygen atoms → an oxygen molecule**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Elements are METALS** (gold, silver, magnesium, iron, aluminium), **NON-METALS** (carbon, sulfur, hydrogen, oxygen), or **METALLOIDS** — in-between properties (**silicon, boron**).

> [!NOTE]
> - **118 elements** are known; **most are solids**.
> - **11 are gases** at room temperature — **all non-metals** (oxygen, helium, nitrogen…).
> - **Only 2 are liquids:** **mercury** (a metal) and **bromine** (a non-metal).
> - **Gallium and caesium** are solids that **melt around 30 °C**.
> - A **mobile phone** uses **over 45 elements** (aluminium, copper, silicon, cobalt, lithium, gold, silver…).

## Compounds

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **COMPOUND = two or more ELEMENTS CHEMICALLY COMBINED in a FIXED RATIO**, forming something **entirely new**.
> - Its **properties are DIFFERENT** from those of its elements.
> - Its elements **CANNOT be separated by physical methods** (only by chemical ones).
>
> **Water:** hydrogen and oxygen atoms in a **fixed ratio of 2 : 1**. **Hydrogen is a FUEL, oxygen SUPPORTS burning — yet water PUTS OUT fire!** Completely new properties.
>
> **Sodium chloride (common salt):** **sodium** (a soft, reactive metal) + **chlorine** (a **hazardous gas**) in **1 : 1** → a **harmless, tasty** substance we need. (Evaporation separates salt **from water** — a mixture — but it **can't** split salt into sodium and chlorine.)

**Activity 8.4 — heating sugar (with a teacher):** sugar **turns brown**, then **chars black**; **water droplets** form near the cool mouth of the tube; **black charcoal (carbon)** is left.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The water must come **from the sugar** (we're heating the tube, so air's vapour couldn't condense inside it). Water = hydrogen + oxygen, and carbon is left behind → **SUGAR is a COMPOUND of CARBON, HYDROGEN and OXYGEN** — not an element.

## Mixture vs compound — iron and sulfur

**Activity 8.5 (demo, well-ventilated):** mix **5.6 g iron filings + 3.2 g sulfur powder** → **Sample A**. Heat half of it in a china dish, stirring, until it forms a **black mass**; cool and grind → **Sample B**.

| Test | **Sample A** (iron + sulfur **mixture**) | **Sample B** (**iron sulfide**, a **compound**) |
|---|---|---|
| **Appearance** | **black and yellow** bits visible — **non-uniform** | **black, same throughout** |
| **Magnet** | **iron filings are pulled out** — easily separated | **not attracted** — iron can't be pulled out |
| **+ dilute HCl** | iron reacts → **hydrogen**: **colourless, odourless**, burns with a **POP**; **yellow sulfur left** unreacted | **hydrogen sulfide**: colourless, **ROTTEN-EGG smell** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **A:** iron + dilute hydrochloric acid → iron chloride + **hydrogen**. Sulfur doesn't react.
> - **B:** **iron sulfide** + dilute hydrochloric acid → iron chloride + **hydrogen sulfide**.
> - **Iron + sulfur —(heat)→ iron sulfide**
>
> **Why doesn't the magnet attract Sample B?** The iron has **chemically combined** — iron sulfide is a **new substance** with **its own properties**, and it isn't magnetic.

> [!WARNING]
> **Smelling a gas safely:** **never sniff directly** — **waft** it towards your nose with your hand.

| | **MIXTURE** | **COMPOUND** |
|---|---|---|
| Made by | **physically mixing** | **chemical reaction** |
| Proportion | **any** | **fixed ratio** |
| Properties | components **keep** theirs | **new** properties |
| Separation | **physical methods** (magnet, filtering, evaporation) | **only chemical methods** |
| Composition | can be uniform or non-uniform | always uniform |
| Example | iron + sulfur powder, air | iron sulfide, water |

## Using elements, compounds and mixtures

- **Air** (mixture), **water** (compound), **iron and aluminium** (elements) in **bridges, buildings, vehicles**.
- Chemists combine elements to make **medicines, vaccines, fertilisers**.
- **Stainless steel** is **stronger and more durable than pure iron**. **Wood, steel and concrete** are all mixtures.

> [!NOTE]
> **Graphene aerogel** — made of carbon, said to be the **lightest material on Earth** (a blade of grass can hold it). Very **porous** → could soak up **oil spills**; useful in energy-saving devices and coatings.

## Minerals

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **MINERALS = natural solid substances with a FIXED chemical composition.** **Most rocks are MIXTURES of minerals.**
> - **Most minerals are COMPOUNDS** — **quartz, calcite, mica, pyroxene, olivine, talc**.
> - **NATIVE minerals** are **pure ELEMENTS** — metals like **gold, silver, copper**, or non-metals like **sulfur, carbon**.
> - **Cement** comes from calcite, quartz, alumina, iron oxide; **talcum powder** from **talc**.
>
> **Gold is both a mineral and a metal** — it occurs **naturally as a pure element** (a native mineral), and that element is a **metal**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Dhokra art (Bihar, Odisha):** shape a figure in **beeswax** → cover in **clay** → when the clay hardens, **melt the wax out** → pour **molten brass or bronze** into the hollow. Shiny golden figures of animals, people and nature.

> [!WARNING]
> **Not everything is matter.** **Light, heat, electricity, thoughts and emotions** are real, but they **have no mass and take up no space** — they aren't matter.

## Think-it-through questions

1. **A + B → C, and A and B can't be broken down** → **A and B are elements; C is a compound with a fixed composition.**
2. **"Air is a mixture" because "a mixture forms when substances mix without a chemical change"** → **both true, and the reason explains the assertion**.
3. **Which list is right?**
   - Elements — water, nitrogen, iron, air → **wrong** (water is a compound, air a mixture).
   - Uniform mixtures — minerals, seawater, bronze, air → **wrong** (minerals have a fixed composition).
   - **Pure substances — carbon dioxide, iron, oxygen, sugar → CORRECT** (each is one kind of particle).
   - Non-uniform — air, sand, brass, muddy water → **wrong** (air and brass are uniform).
4. **Iron + moist air → iron oxide (rust); magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide:** **iron, magnesium, oxygen → elements**; **iron oxide, magnesium oxide, water → compounds**; **moist air → mixture**.
5. **Sort them:**
   - **Elements:** aluminium, gold, oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur, hydrogen
   - **Compounds:** carbon dioxide, magnesium oxide, rust, iron sulfide, glucose, water, sodium chloride, baking soda
   - **Mixtures:** sand, seawater, muddy water, air, fruit juice
   - **Pure substances** = all the elements + all the compounds.
6. **Can something be both an element and a compound?** **No** — an element **can't** be broken down; a compound **can** (chemically). It's one or the other.
7. **If water were a MIXTURE of hydrogen and oxygen**, it would keep their properties — it would **burn explosively** and couldn't put out fires or be drunk safely.
8. **Iron filings + dilute HCl → Gas A** = **hydrogen**: iron + hydrochloric acid → iron chloride + hydrogen.
9. **Compounds of non-metals only:** **water** (H, O — drinking, cleaning) and **carbon dioxide** (C, O — fizzy drinks, fire extinguishers; plants use it in photosynthesis).

## In a nutshell

- **Mixture:** components keep their properties, any proportion, separated physically; **uniform** (air, alloys, sugar water) or **non-uniform** (salad, muddy water).
- **Pure substance:** one kind of particle — an **element** or a **compound**.
- **Element:** can't be broken down; made of **atoms**; metals, non-metals, metalloids; **118** known.
- **Compound:** elements **chemically combined** in a **fixed ratio**, with **new properties** (water 2:1, salt 1:1, sugar = C + H + O).
- Iron + sulfur **mixture** vs **iron sulfide** compound. **Minerals** are mostly compounds; native ones are elements.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** alloys are mixtures, not compounds · air is a mixture · "pure" ghee ≠ scientifically pure · water's 2:1 is the ratio of atoms · hydrogen pops, oxygen brightens a flame · iron sulfide isn't magnetic · mercury and bromine are the only liquid elements.
