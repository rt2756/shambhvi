## Mixtures

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A MIXTURE is formed when two or more substances are mixed and EACH SUBSTANCE RETAINS ITS OWN PROPERTIES.**
>
> The individual substances are the **components**, and **they do NOT react chemically with each other.**

**Poha, sprout salad, soup, lemonade, sugar in water** — all mixtures.

### Uniform and non-uniform

| | **Non-uniform** | **Uniform** |
|---|---|---|
| Components | **visible** with the eye or a magnifier | **cannot be seen separately**, even with a microscope |
| Distribution | uneven | **evenly distributed throughout** |
| Examples | sprout salad, poha, sand in water, oil and water | sugar in water, air, seawater, vinegar, alloys |

> [!NOTE]
> **Alloys are uniform mixtures of metals.**
> **Stainless steel** = iron + nickel + chromium + a little carbon. **Brass** = copper + zinc. **Bronze** = copper + tin.
>
> In ancient India such mixtures were called **Mishraloha** — metal mixtures with properties **distinct from their constituents**. The **Charaka Samhita, Susruta Samhita, Rasaratna Samucchaya** and **Rasa Jala Nidhi** describe their medicinal use. **Bronze (Kamsya)** — copper (**Tamra**) 4 parts to tin (**Vanga**) 1 part — was used to improve digestion and boost immunity.

### Types of mixtures by state

| Type | Example | Uniform? |
|---|---|---|
| Gas + gas | **air** | uniform |
| Gas + liquid | soda water, oxygen dissolved in water | uniform |
| Solid + gas | carbon particles (soot) in air | **non-uniform** |
| Liquid + liquid | vinegar (acetic acid in water) | uniform |
| Liquid + liquid | **oil and water** | **non-uniform** |
| Solid + liquid | seawater | uniform |
| Solid + liquid | **sand and water** | **non-uniform** |
| Solid + solid | alloys | uniform |
| Solid + solid | baking powder (baking soda + tartaric acid) | **non-uniform** |

> [!TIP]
> **In science, the components of a mixture must be PURE SUBSTANCES.** In everyday speech we call poha a mixture too — but its "components" (rice flakes, onion) are themselves mixtures.

### Air is a mixture

**Uniform mixture of mainly nitrogen (~78%), oxygen, argon, carbon dioxide and water vapour.**

- **Oxygen** is needed by most living beings and **supports combustion**
- **Nitrogen** does **not** take part in combustion
- **Water vapour** turns into liquid droplets when warm air meets a cool surface

**Testing for carbon dioxide in air:**

```
calcium oxide (quick lime) + water → calcium hydroxide + HEAT
                                       ↓ (filtered = LIME WATER, colourless)
lime water left exposed to air
                                       ↓
calcium hydroxide + carbon dioxide → calcium carbonate + water
                                       ↓
                             tiny insoluble white particles → MILKY
```

**Lime water turning milky on exposure to air proves carbon dioxide is present in the air.**

> [!WARNING]
> **Dust is NOT part of air.** Leave a clean black sheet near an open window for a few hours and tiny particles settle on it.
>
> Dust particles are **suspended** in air, are considered **pollutants**, and their number **varies with time and place**. Air's actual composition is the gas list above.

**Major air pollutants:** **particulate matter** (dust, soot) and gases like **carbon monoxide, ozone, nitrogen dioxide, sulfur dioxide**. The **Air Quality Index (AQI)** describes air quality.

## Pure substances

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **"Pure" means two different things.**
>
> **In everyday use:** unadulterated — nothing cheap or poor-quality has been added. **Adulteration** is the **illegal** addition of cheaper or poorer substances to increase quantity or cut cost; it degrades quality and can be **hazardous to health**.
>
> **In science:** **a pure substance is one that contains NO other substance — it consists of the SAME TYPE OF PARTICLES throughout, and cannot be separated into other kinds of matter by any physical process.**

**So a packet of "pure" milk is scientifically a mixture** — it contains water, fats, proteins and more.

**Classify:** milk (**mixture**), packed fruit juice (**mixture**), soil (**mixture**), baking soda (**pure substance**), sugar (**pure substance**).

## Two kinds of pure substance

### Splitting water with electricity

Fill two test tubes with water (plus a few drops of dilute sulfuric acid), invert them over the terminals of a **9 V battery**, and wait.

**Gas bubbles collect in both — and one collects TWICE as much as the other.**

**Testing them with a burning candle:**

| Test tube | Result | Gas |
|---|---|---|
| the one with **more** gas | a **"pop" sound** | **hydrogen** |
| the one with **less** gas | the flame **glows brighter** | **oxygen** |

> [!TIP]
> **Why they can't be water vapour:** water vapour would have **condensed back into water** in the test tube. These stayed as gases.

**Conclusion: `Water → Hydrogen + Oxygen`** — water is composed of two different constituents.

**This is a CHEMICAL change** — new substances with entirely different properties are formed, and you can't reverse it by simply cooling.

### Elements

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **An ELEMENT is a pure substance that cannot be broken down into simpler substances.** Elements are the **building blocks of all matter**.
>
> **Each element is made of identical particles called ATOMS**, and its atoms are **different from those of every other element**.

**Examples:** hydrogen, oxygen, gold, silver, sulfur, carbon.

**Molecules.** The atoms of most elements **cannot exist independently**. Two or more combine into a stable particle called a **molecule** — two hydrogen atoms make one hydrogen molecule; two oxygen atoms make one oxygen molecule.

**Elements are classified as:**

- **Metals** — gold, silver, magnesium, iron, aluminium
- **Non-metals** — carbon, sulfur, hydrogen, oxygen
- **Metalloids** — silicon, boron; **intermediate properties** between metals and non-metals

**Facts worth knowing:**

- **118 elements** are known at present; **most are solid**
- **11 are gases** at room temperature — all **non-metals** (oxygen, helium, nitrogen, …)
- **Only 2 are liquid** at room temperature: **mercury** (a metal) and **bromine** (a non-metal)
- **Gallium and caesium** are solid, but melt at only about **30 °C** — they'd melt in your hand
- **More than 45 different elements** go into making a single mobile phone

### Compounds

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A COMPOUND is formed when different elements combine CHEMICALLY in a FIXED RATIO to form something entirely new.**
>
> **Its properties are completely different from those of the elements that formed it**, and **its constituent elements CANNOT be separated by any physical method.**

**Water:** hydrogen atoms to oxygen atoms in the ratio **2 : 1**.

> [!TIP]
> **Hydrogen is a fuel. Oxygen supports combustion. Water puts out fire.**
>
> That is the clearest possible demonstration that a compound's properties bear no relation to its elements'.

**Sodium chloride (common salt):** **sodium**, a soft metal, plus **chlorine**, a hazardous gas, combine in a **1 : 1** ratio to give a harmless, essential, taste-enhancing substance.

> [!WARNING]
> **Dissolved salt CAN be recovered from water by evaporation** — because salt-in-water is a **mixture**.
>
> **But salt cannot be split into sodium and chlorine by any physical process** — because salt itself is a **compound**. Don't confuse the two separations.

**Sugar is a compound too.** Heat sugar in a boiling tube: it turns **brown**, then **chars black**, and **water droplets appear near the open end**.

**The water must have come from the sugar itself** — the tube was being heated, so nothing could condense from the air onto it.

`Sugar → carbon (charcoal) + water`, and water is hydrogen + oxygen. **So sugar contains carbon, hydrogen and oxygen — it cannot be an element.**

## The iron + sulfur experiment — mixture vs compound

Take **5.6 g iron filings** and **3.2 g sulfur powder**.

**Sample A** — simply **mixed** on a watch glass.
**Sample B** — half of Sample A **heated with stirring** until a black mass forms, then cooled and ground.

| Test | **Sample A (mixture)** | **Sample B (compound)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Appearance** | black and yellow particles **visible separately** — non-uniform | **same colour and texture throughout** — uniform black |
| **Magnet** | **iron filings are attracted** and can be separated | **not attracted at all** |
| **With dilute HCl** | `Iron + HCl → iron chloride + HYDROGEN` — colourless, **odourless**, burns with a **"pop"**. Sulfur is left as a **yellow solid** at the bottom — it does not react. | `Iron sulfide + HCl → iron chloride + HYDROGEN SULFIDE` — colourless, **rotten-egg smell** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Sample A is a MIXTURE:** the components keep their own properties (iron is still magnetic, sulfur still yellow and unreactive), and can be separated physically.
>
> **Sample B is a COMPOUND — iron sulfide:** a **new substance** with completely different properties. It is **not magnetic**, and iron and sulfur can **no longer be separated**.
>
> **Why the magnet no longer works:** the iron is no longer present as iron. It has chemically combined into an entirely new substance.

> [!WARNING]
> **Never smell a gas directly.** Waft it towards your nose with your hand.

## Mixture vs compound — the summary

| | **Mixture** | **Compound** |
|---|---|---|
| Formation | components simply mixed | elements **chemically combined** |
| Ratio | **any** ratio | **fixed** ratio |
| Properties | components **keep** their own | **entirely new** properties |
| Separation | by **physical** methods | **not** by physical methods |
| Change | physical | **chemical** |
| Example | iron + sulfur powder | iron sulfide |

## Why this matters

- **Chemists** study how elements combine into compounds to invent **life-saving medicines and vaccines**, and **fertilisers** that raise crop production.
- **Engineers and material scientists** design materials with useful properties — **stainless steel** is stronger and more durable than pure iron. **Wood, steel and concrete** are all mixtures.

> [!NOTE]
> **Graphene aerogel**, made from carbon, is said to be the **lightest material on Earth** — light enough for a blade of grass to hold. Being **highly porous** it absorbs enormously, so it could clean up **oil spills** at sea and on land, and is useful in **energy-saving devices** and building coatings.

## Minerals

**Most rocks are MIXTURES of minerals**, visible by eye, magnifier or microscope.

- **Native minerals** are **pure elements**, not compounds — metals like **gold, silver, copper**, or non-metals like **sulfur, carbon**.
- **Most minerals are compounds** of more than one element — **quartz, calcite, mica, pyroxene, olivine, talc**.

**Everyday things from minerals:** **cement** from calcite, quartz, alumina and iron oxide; **talcum powder** from the mineral **talc**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Dhokra art** — an old craft from Bihar and Odisha. A design is shaped in **beeswax**, covered in **clay** to form a mould; when the clay hardens the wax is **melted out**, leaving a hollow that is filled with **molten brass or bronze**. The alloy makes the figures **strong** and gives them a **shiny golden colour**.
>
> Elements, compounds and mixtures — all three, in one traditional craft.
