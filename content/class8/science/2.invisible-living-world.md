## Seeing the unseen

Our eyes can only see things **above a certain size**. Long ago people found that a **curved piece of glass** makes small things look bigger. It was shaped like a **lentil seed** — thick in the middle, thin at the edge — so it was called a **LENS** (Latin for lentil). Better lenses led to **magnifying glasses**, then **microscopes**, which opened up a hidden world.

**Activity 2.1:** fill a **round-bottom glass flask with water**, cork it, and put it on an open book. **The letters look bigger** — the water-filled flask acts as a **magnifying glass** (it's curved like a lens).

> [!NOTE]
> **Robert Hooke (1665)** published ***Micrographia*** — detailed drawings of tiny things seen through his microscope (magnifying **200–300 times**). Looking at a thin slice of **cork**, he saw many small **empty compartments** like a **honeycomb** and called each one a **CELL** — the first use of the word in science.
>
> **Antonie van Leeuwenhoek** (Dutch, 1660s) made **better lenses** and was the **first to clearly see and describe bacteria and blood cells** — the **Father of Microbiology**.

## The cell — the basic unit of life

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **All living beings (ORGANISMS) are made of CELLS** — just as a **wall is made of bricks**.

### Onion peel cells (Activity 2.2 — teacher demonstration)

1. Pull the **thin transparent layer** (the **onion peel**) from inside an onion piece with **forceps**.
2. Stain it in **safranin** (red stain) for 30 seconds → cells turn **pinkish**, easier to see.
3. **Rinse** in water to remove extra stain.
4. Lay it flat on a **glass slide**, add a drop of **glycerin** (stops the cells **drying out** and improves clarity).
5. Lower a **coverslip** at an angle with a needle so **no air bubbles** are trapped; wipe extra glycerin with **blotting paper**.
6. Look under a microscope (a **foldable paper microscope** will do).

→ **Nearly rectangular cells, packed closely with no gaps** — like bricks in a wall.

### Cheek cells (Activity 2.3)

Gently scrape the inside of your cheek with the **blunt end of a clean toothpick**, spread it in a drop of water on a slide, add **methylene blue** (blue stain — increases **contrast**), then glycerin and a coverslip.

→ **Thin, flat, polygon-shaped** cells — they form the **lining of your mouth**.

### Parts of a cell

> [!IMPORTANT]
> | Part | Where | What it does |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Cell membrane** | outer boundary of every cell | **separates** one cell from another; **porous** — lets **useful materials in** and **wastes out** |
> | **Cytoplasm** | fills the space between membrane and nucleus | holds the cell's components and compounds (carbohydrates, proteins, fats, salts); **most life processes happen here** |
> | **Nucleus** | round structure, usually central, with its own **membrane** | **controls all the cell's activities**, including **growth** |
> | **Cell wall** | an **extra outer layer** in **plant** cells (also fungi, bacteria) | gives **rigidity and strength** — that's why plant cells look **firm and compact** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Plant cells also have:**
> - **PLASTIDS** — tiny rod-shaped bodies. **Chloroplasts** contain green **chlorophyll** for **photosynthesis**; in non-green parts plastids **store** substances.
> - **A large VACUOLE** — stores substances, removes waste, and **keeps the cell's shape** (supports the plant).
>
> **Animal cells:** **no cell wall, no chloroplasts**; vacuoles are **absent or small**.

| Feature | Plant cell | Animal cell |
|---|---|---|
| Cell membrane | ✓ | ✓ |
| Cytoplasm, nucleus | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Cell wall** | ✓ | ✗ |
| **Chloroplasts** | ✓ | ✗ |
| **Vacuole** | **large** | small or none |
| Shape | regular, often rectangular | irregular |

> [!TIP]
> **Why does a plant cell need a wall but an animal cell doesn't?** A plant has **no skeleton** — the stiff walls of millions of cells hold it **upright**. Animals get support from bones and muscles, and their cells need to be **flexible** to move.

### Shape fits the job

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A cell's shape, size and structure suit its FUNCTION.**
>
> | Cell | Shape | Why that shape |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Cheek cell** | thin, flat | forms a **protective lining** |
> | **Nerve cell (neuron)** | **very long, branched** | carries **messages** quickly to far parts of the body |
> | **Muscle cell** | **spindle-shaped**, thin, flexible | **contracts and relaxes** — e.g. waves in the food pipe push food to the stomach |
> | **Plant tube cells** | long tubes | carry **water** through the plant |
>
> In the **stomach**, **muscle cells churn** food while **lining cells make digestive juices and acid** — different cells working together.

## Levels of organisation

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **CELL → TISSUE → ORGAN → ORGAN SYSTEM → ORGANISM**
>
> - **Tissue** = a group of **similar cells** doing one job.
> - **Organ** = different tissues working together (stomach, leaf).
> - **Organ system** = several organs doing a **major function** (digestive system).
> - **Organism** = all the organ systems together — a whole plant or animal.

> [!NOTE]
> **Every complex organism begins as ONE cell — the EGG**, which divides again and again. Living things made of many cells are **MULTICELLULAR** (all plants and animals).
>
> **The ostrich egg's yolk is a single cell** — the **largest known cell**, about **130–170 mm** across. The shell and the white are **non-cellular** (protection and nourishment).

## Microorganisms

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **MICROORGANISMS (MICROBES)** = living things **too small to see with the naked eye** (*micro* = very small).
> - **UNICELLULAR** (one cell): **bacteria, Amoeba, Paramecium**, **yeast**.
> - **MULTICELLULAR**: some **fungi** (moulds) and **algae**.
> - Found **everywhere** — water, soil, air, inside our bodies, even **hot springs and icy zones**.
> - A school microscope magnifies **100–400 times**. Low-cost **foldable paper microscopes** bring the micro-world to many more people.

**Activities 2.4 and 2.5:** look at a drop of **pond water** and the top of a settled **soil suspension** (stir moist soil in water — don't touch soil with bare hands).

| Found in | Microorganism | Group | What you see |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pond water | **Amoeba** | protozoa | one cell, **irregular shape**, moving |
| Pond water | **Paramecium** | protozoa | one cell, moves using **special structures** |
| Pond water / soil | **Algae** | algae | **green** (chlorophyll); spherical or moving |
| Soil | **Bread mould** | fungi | **branched threads**, no chlorophyll, **sac-like** tips |
| Soil | **Mould** | fungi | branched threads, **brush-like** tips |
| Soil | **Bacteria** | bacteria | **spherical, rod, comma or spiral**; hair-like projections |

> [!NOTE]
> **VIRUSES** are microscopic but **acellular** (not made of cells). They **multiply only inside a living cell** — of a plant, animal or bacterium — and may cause disease.

> [!NOTE]
> **Our scientific heritage:** the **Vedas** (including the **Atharvaveda**) mention **"Krimi"** — tiny beings, both **Drishya (visible)** and **Adrishya (invisible)** — and their good and harmful effects.

## Why does food rot — but not pickles?

A lemon, tomato or orange left out grows a **powdery or cotton-like** coat — **microbes** from the air have landed and grown on it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Pickles and murabbas don't spoil** because **lots of salt or sugar** (plus spices) acts as a **PRESERVATIVE** — microbes **can't grow** in such high concentrations.

## Microbes clean the environment

**Activity 2.7:** half-fill a container with garden soil, add **fruit and vegetable peels**, cover with soil. After **2–3 weeks** the peels have become **dark, crumbly MANURE**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **DECOMPOSITION** = **bacteria and fungi** break down complex substances in **dead plants, animals and dung** into **simpler, nutrient-rich** substances, which **return to the soil**.
> - That's why gardeners put **dry leaves into pits** — to make **natural manure**.
> - It needs the **right temperature and moisture**.
> - **Without decomposers, dead things and waste would pile up**, and nutrients would never return to the soil.

> [!NOTE]
> **Biogas:** some bacteria live **without oxygen** and break down plant/animal waste and sewage, giving off **BIOGAS** — mainly **methane** plus carbon dioxide — used for **cooking, heating, electricity and vehicles**.
>
> **Ananda Mohan Chakrabarty (1938–2020)** made a bacterium (1971) that could **break down oil spills**; it was **patented in 1980**. (A **patent** stops others copying or selling an invention without permission.)

## Microbes in the kitchen

### Yeast makes dough rise (Activity 2.8)

Two bowls of 200 g flour with a pinch of sugar; add **yeast to bowl A only**; knead both with **warm water**, cover with a damp cloth, keep warm for 4–5 hours.

→ **Bowl A rises, turns fluffy and smells different.** Bowl B doesn't change. (Bowl B is the **comparison** — it shows the change is due to yeast.)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **YEAST is a FUNGUS** (unicellular). It **respires**, breaking down **sugar** for energy and releasing **CARBON DIOXIDE** — the gas **bubbles make the dough soft and fluffy**. It also makes a little **alcohol** — the smell.
> - **Why sugar?** Food for the yeast. **Why warm water/place?** Yeast **grows best warm**.
> - Used for **bread, cakes, pastries**. Bacteria such as ***Lactobacillus*** ferment **idli/dosa batter** and **bhatura dough**.

> [!TIP]
> **Aanandi's balloons:** sugar solution in two test tubes, **yeast in B only**, half-blown balloons on top, kept warm. **B's balloon inflates** → **yeast produced a gas**. To find out **which** gas, she bubbles it through **lime water** — if the lime water turns **milky**, it's **carbon dioxide**.

### Bacteria make curd (Activity 2.9)

Add a spoon of curd to **lukewarm milk (A, kept warm)** and **cold milk (B, in the fridge)**.

→ **A sets into curd** (and turns slightly sour); **B stays as milk**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> ***LACTOBACILLUS*** (a bacterium in curd) feeds on **lactose** (milk sugar), **multiplies**, and **ferments** the milk into curd, making **LACTIC ACID** — which makes curd **sour**.
> It **grows well when warm**, so curd forms in A but not in B.

> [!TIP]
> **Curd left out for a day gets more sour** → the bacteria **keep growing** and making **more lactic acid** (faster in the warmth).
>
> **Bread near the sink vs in the fridge for 3 days:** the sink slice gets **mould** (warm, moist, air); the fridge slice stays fresh longer — **cold slows microbes**.

## Microbes on the farm — *Rhizobium*

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Legumes** — **beans, peas, lentils, cowpea** — have swollen **ROOT NODULES** containing ***RHIZOBIUM*** bacteria. They **trap nitrogen from the air** and make it usable by the plant.
> - So legumes grow well **without chemical nitrogen fertiliser**.
> - Farmers **rotate** legumes with other crops to **add nitrogen** to the soil for the next crop.

> [!TIP]
> **Why does the wheat farmer add nitrogen fertiliser but the bean farmer doesn't?** Beans have **Rhizobium** in their root nodules supplying nitrogen; wheat **doesn't**.

## Microalgae — tiny helpers in water

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **MICROALGAE** are microscopic plant-like organisms (in water, soil, air, on trees) that **make their own food from sunlight** — and in doing so produce **MORE THAN HALF OF EARTH'S OXYGEN**.
> - **Food** for many aquatic animals.
> - ***Spirulina, Chlorella*, diatoms** — health **supplements** and medicines.
> - **Clean water**; used to make **biofuel**.
> - Threatened by **pollution, climate change and habitat loss** — must be **conserved**.

> [!NOTE]
> ***Spirulina*** — a "**superfood**": over **60% protein**, a source of **vitamin B12**, little fat or sugar. Grow it in a **clear glass tank of pond water** in a **bright place out of direct sun**; stir twice a week; **harvest after 3–6 weeks** by filtering through fine cloth — a **livelihood** opportunity.

## Microbe cells compared

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - All have a **cell membrane**.
> - **Fungi** have a **cell wall** but **no chloroplasts** — they **can't photosynthesise**. Yeast is unicellular; mould is multicellular.
> - **BACTERIA have NO well-defined nucleus** and no nuclear membrane — just a **NUCLEOID** region. This sets them apart from yeast, protozoa, algae, fungi, plants and animals.
> - An **electron microscope** magnifies about **10,00,000 times**, revealing even more parts.

| Part | Plant cell | Animal cell | Bacterial cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cell membrane, cytoplasm | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Nucleus | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ (**nucleoid**) |
| Cell wall | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ |
| Chloroplast | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ |

## Think-it-through questions

1. **Snehal's two compost pits** — peels **with** dry leaves vs **without** → she's testing whether **adding dry leaves** changes how fast/well **manure forms**.
2. **Who am I?** live everywhere including your gut → **bacteria**; make bread fluffy → **yeast**; live in roots of pulse crops → ***Rhizobium***.
3. **Test that microbes need warmth, air and moisture:** set up identical bread slices (or peel pots) and change **one** condition at a time — **warm vs cold**, **sealed airtight vs open**, **dry vs moist** — and compare mould growth.
4. **Flask of warm sugar solution + yeast, tube to lime water:** the sugar is **fermented**; the **lime water turns milky** (CO₂); **without yeast**, nothing happens.

## In a nutshell

- **Cells** are the basic unit of life (Hooke named them; Leeuwenhoek first saw bacteria).
- Every cell: **membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus**; plants add **cell wall, chloroplasts, big vacuole**.
- **Cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism.** Shape fits function.
- **Microorganisms:** bacteria, protozoa, fungi, algae (viruses are acellular). Uni- or multicellular.
- **Helpful microbes:** decomposers (manure, biogas), yeast (bread), *Lactobacillus* (curd), *Rhizobium* (nitrogen), microalgae (oxygen, food).
- Salt and sugar **preserve** food.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** animal cells have no cell wall · bacteria have a nucleoid, not a nucleus · yeast is a fungus, not a bacterium · yeast gives CO₂ (+ alcohol); *Lactobacillus* gives lactic acid · viruses aren't cells · microalgae make over half of Earth's oxygen.
