## Health is more than "not being sick"

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHO definition:** health is *"a state of complete **physical, mental and social** well-being, and not merely the absence of disease."*

Three parts, all needed:

| Aspect | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Physical** | the body works well; you can do tasks efficiently |
| **Mental** | a positive mindset; you cope in difficult situations |
| **Social** | strong relationships; you adjust well with peers and society |

**Case in point:** a student moves to a new city, has no friends and busy parents, retreats into his phone — and develops **headaches, weight loss and sleeplessness**. Nothing infected him. **His health failed on the social and mental sides, and the body followed.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Ayurveda** says the same thing in older words: true health is a **balance of body, mind and surroundings**. It prescribes **dinacharya** (daily routine) and **ritucharya** (seasonal routine), fresh wholesome food suited to one's **prakriti** (body constitution), regular exercise, cleanliness, restful sleep and a calm mind — supported by yoga, meditation and mindfulness.

## Staying healthy

**Good habits**

- Eat a **balanced diet** — plenty of fruits, vegetables, whole grains
- Stay **physically active** — play outdoors, walk, run, cycle
- **Limit screen time**; spend time in nature
- Get **enough sleep** so body and mind can recover
- Practise **yoga or pranayama**
- Keep **personal hygiene**
- Say **NO** to tobacco, alcohol and addictive drugs

**Habits that harm**

- Too much time on phones and screens
- Daily fast food and junk food
- Sleeping very late; too little sleep
- Skipping meals, especially breakfast

> [!TIP]
> **Two big factors decide your health: your lifestyle (how you live) and your environment (your surroundings).** Neither one alone is enough.

**A clean environment matters too.** Air pollution from vehicles and factories causes coughing and asthma; the **Air Quality Index (AQI)** tells you how clean the air is. A dirty, fly-and-mosquito-ridden neighbourhood makes people fall sick more often.

**And so do relationships.** Even with good food and a clean home, loneliness makes people unwell.

## Signs and symptoms

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A SYMPTOM is what you FEEL** — pain, tiredness, dizziness.
> **A SIGN is what can be SEEN or MEASURED** — fever, rash, high blood pressure, swelling.
>
> The distinction matters because **signs can be checked by someone else**; symptoms have to be reported by you. Doctors use both.

## Diseases: two big families

**A disease is a condition that affects the normal working of the body or mind**, usually because one or more organs or systems stop functioning properly.

| | **Communicable** | **Non-communicable (NCD)** |
|---|---|---|
| Caused by | **pathogens** — bacteria, viruses, fungi, worms, protozoa | lifestyle, diet, environment, deficiency |
| Spreads person to person? | **Yes** | **No** |
| Examples | typhoid, dengue, flu, chickenpox, COVID-19, TB, malaria | cancer, diabetes, asthma, obesity, high blood pressure, scurvy, anaemia, goitre |

**A pathogen is a disease-causing organism.**

> [!WARNING]
> **NCDs now cause most deaths in India** — because people eat more processed food, exercise less, and live longer. India has one of the **highest numbers of people with diabetes in the world**.
>
> **A disease that isn't catching is not therefore a small problem.**

**Chronic diseases** last more than about 3 months and need long-term care — cancer, diabetes, asthma.

**Deficiency diseases** — scurvy, anaemia, goitre — come from lacking specific nutrients. They are **non-communicable** too.

## How communicable diseases spread

| Route | How | Example diseases |
|---|---|---|
| **Air** | coughing, sneezing | common cold, influenza, chickenpox, measles, TB |
| **Direct contact** | shaking hands, touching | many skin and respiratory infections |
| **Indirect contact** | sharing towels, handkerchiefs, personal items | |
| **Contaminated food and water** | drinking or eating unclean things | cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A, ascariasis |
| **Vectors** — insects that carry pathogens | mosquito or housefly bites | malaria, dengue |
| **Animal bite** | rabid animal | rabies |

**A vector is an insect (or other animal) that carries a pathogen from one person to another** — the mosquito doesn't cause malaria, it delivers the protozoan that does.

### The table worth knowing

| Disease | Caused by | Site | Key symptoms | Prevention |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Common cold / influenza** | **Virus** | respiratory tract | congestion, sore throat, fever, cough, body ache | wash hands, don't share personal items, cover mouth and nose |
| **Chickenpox** | **Virus** | respiratory tract, skin | mild fever, itchy skin, rashes, blisters | isolation, cover mouth and nose, **vaccination** |
| **Measles** | **Virus** | skin, respiratory tract | fever, sore throat, reddish rashes | isolation, hygiene, **vaccination** |
| **Tuberculosis (TB)** | **Bacteria** | lungs | cough, fever, fatigue, loss of appetite, night sweats | avoid close contact, cover mouth, hygiene, **vaccination** |
| **Hepatitis A** | **Virus** | liver | fatigue, fever, nausea, vomiting, **jaundice** | boiled water, **vaccination** |
| **Cholera** | **Bacteria** | intestine | diarrhoea, dehydration | hygiene, sanitation, cooked food, boiled water, vaccination |
| **Typhoid** | **Bacteria** | intestine | headache, abdominal discomfort, fever, diarrhoea | hygiene, sanitation, cooked food, boiled water, vaccination |
| **Ascariasis (roundworm)** | **Worms** | intestine | worms in stool, poor growth, weight loss, anaemia | hygiene, sanitation, cooked food, boiled water |
| **Malaria** | **Protozoa** | skin, blood | high fever, profuse sweating, **periodic chills** | mosquito nets and repellents, long sleeves, control breeding |
| **Dengue** ("break bone fever") | **Virus** | skin, blood | fever, headache, severe muscle and joint pain, nausea | mosquito nets and repellents, long sleeves, **remove still water** |

> [!TIP]
> **Notice the pattern in the prevention column.** Air-borne diseases → cover the mouth. Water-borne → boil water and cook food. Vector-borne → stop the insect. **The prevention always attacks the route, not the germ.**

**Worms** (like roundworm) live inside the digestive system as **parasites** — organisms that live in or on another living being and feed on its nutrients.

## Preventing and controlling disease

**Simple, powerful precautions:**

- Keep yourself and your surroundings clean
- **Wash hands with soap and water** — this removes pathogens
- Cover your mouth and nose when coughing or sneezing
- Wear a mask in crowded places
- Don't share towels or handkerchiefs
- Keep home, food and water clean
- **Stay home and rest when unwell** — this both helps you recover and stops you infecting others

> [!NOTE]
> **Bhadrak district, Odisha** ran a community sanitation campaign that got far more people building and using toilets. **Open defecation dropped sharply**, and child health improved with **fewer cases of diarrhoea and infection**. Simple sanitation, done at community scale, prevents disease better than any medicine.

## Immunity

**Immunity is the body's natural ability to fight disease.** The **immune system** is what does it.

> [!TIP]
> **This is why two people in the same environment don't fall ill equally often** — their immune systems differ.

**Acquired immunity** is protection developed **after** exposure to a pathogen or a vaccine.

### Vaccines

**A vaccine trains the immune system to recognise and attack a particular germ, without you having to catch the disease.**

**Vaccines can be made from:**
- **weakened or dead** pathogens
- **inactive or harmless parts** of the pathogen (a tetanus shot uses an **inactivated bacterial toxin**)
- newer designs that **instruct your own cells** to make a harmless piece of the germ for the immune system to practise on

> [!WARNING]
> **Vaccines are PREVENTIVE, not CURATIVE.** They stop a disease before it happens; they do **not** treat someone already ill.
>
> **And getting vaccinated protects the people around you too**, not just yourself — fewer infected people means fewer chances for the germ to spread.

> [!NOTE]
> **Edward Jenner and the first vaccine.** Smallpox killed millions. Jenner **observed** that milkmaids who had caught the much milder **cowpox** never got smallpox. His **hypothesis**: something in cowpox blisters protects against smallpox. He **tested** it by injecting cowpox material into a boy, who then resisted smallpox. **Result:** cowpox exposure gave protection. **Application:** mass vaccination eventually **eradicated smallpox worldwide in 1979**.
>
> That five-step chain — observation → hypothesis → experiment → result → application — is exactly the investigative method of Chapter 1.

> [!NOTE]
> **India had a traditional method long before modern vaccines: variolation.** Material from a smallpox sore was used to scratch the skin, creating a mild infection that built immunity. The practitioners were called **teekedaars**.
>
> **India is today one of the world's largest vaccine producers**, supplying many countries — and Indian companies played a key role during the COVID-19 pandemic.

**Two Indian scientists worth knowing:**
- **Kamal Ranadive** (1917–2001) — studied how hormones and certain viruses link to **cancer**, and showed how tobacco, diet and pollution raise the risk.
- **Maharaj Kishan Bhan** — played a key role in developing the **Rotavirus vaccine**, which protects children from diarrhoea, and pushed for affordable healthcare.

## Antibiotics

**Antibiotics are medicines that kill bacteria.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Antibiotics work ONLY against bacterial infections.** They **do not work against viruses or protozoa**.
>
> **Why:** they target parts of **bacterial cells** that are different from human and other animal cells. A virus isn't a cell at all, so there's nothing for the antibiotic to attack — which is why taking an antibiotic for a cold is useless.

> [!NOTE]
> **Alexander Fleming, 1928.** While studying harmful bacteria in London, he noticed that a **mould** growing on a discarded petri dish had **stopped the bacteria growing around it**. He realised the mould released a bacteria-killing substance. That chance observation gave the world **penicillin**, the first antibiotic.
>
> The discovery was accidental — but only because **he noticed and investigated** instead of throwing the dish away.

### Antibiotic resistance

**Bacteria that were once killed by an antibiotic survive and multiply despite it.**

**How it develops:**

1. A **few** bacteria happen to be resistant.
2. The antibiotic kills the vulnerable ones — **including the good bacteria that protect the body**.
3. The **resistant** ones now have no competition, so they **grow and take over**.
4. Some bacteria even **transfer resistance to other bacteria**.

**How it spreads through the community:** resistant bacteria pass from person to person, through hospitals, through **animals given antibiotics**, through **animal food products**, and through **soil in crop fields** via manure — eventually reaching people through crops.

> [!WARNING]
> **Two behaviours cause it: taking antibiotics when they aren't needed, and not finishing the prescribed course.**
>
> Stopping early leaves the toughest bacteria alive to multiply. That's why "I felt better so I stopped" is dangerous.

**To reduce resistance:** use antibiotics **only when a doctor prescribes them**, at the **correct dose**, for the **full duration** — and don't give them to healthy cattle.

## Treatment beyond antibiotics

**Traditional systems** — **Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani** — have long been used in India, using herbs, oils and minerals alongside a healthy lifestyle and balanced diet. **They help with some conditions and with everyday well-being, but are not effective for all diseases at all stages.**

**For non-communicable diseases**, treatment focuses on **managing symptoms and improving quality of life** through medication, lifestyle change and rehabilitation. **Early diagnosis and continuous care** are what stop the disease progressing.

> [!TIP]
> **"Prevention is better than cure" is not a slogan — it's a conclusion.** Nearly every disease in this chapter is far cheaper and easier to avoid (clean water, a mask, a mosquito net, a vaccine, a balanced diet) than to treat.
