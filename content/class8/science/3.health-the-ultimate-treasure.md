## Probe and ponder

- How does your body respond to an infection like a **common cold**?
- We rarely see **smallpox or polio** now, but **diabetes and heart problems** are more common. **Why?**
- Could **climate change** bring new diseases? How do **stress and worry** make us sick?

The chapter opens with a **notice board of news clippings**: more than **100 million Indians are diabetic**; **air pollution** is a leading health risk; the **monsoon** brings **malaria and dengue**; **regular handwashing cuts infection by 50%**; **too much screen time** causes dry eyes, obesity, poor sleep and anxiety; **yoga** reduces anxiety.

## Health is more than not being sick

**Activity 3.1 — a story:** a Grade 8 boy moves to a new city. No friends, busy parents — he feels **lonely**, spends more and more time on his **phone and social media**, which makes it **worse**. He gets **headaches**, **loses weight**, can't **sleep**. A doctor advises **less screen time** and a **counsellor**, who helps him make friends — and his health improves.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHO definition: HEALTH = "a state of complete PHYSICAL, MENTAL and SOCIAL well-being, and not merely the absence of disease."**
>
> The boy had **no germ** — his illness came from **loneliness and habits** (mental and social health), and it showed up in his **body**. All three parts are linked.
> A healthy person works **efficiently**, **copes** with difficult situations and **gets along** with others.

> [!NOTE]
> **Ayurveda:** true health is a **balance of body, mind and surroundings** — **dinacharya** (daily routine), **ritucharya** (seasonal routine), fresh food suited to one's **prakriti** (body constitution), exercise, cleanliness, sleep, and a calm mind through **yoga, meditation, mindfulness**.

## How to stay healthy

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Our health depends on our LIFESTYLE (how we live) and our ENVIRONMENT (surroundings).**
>
> | Do | Avoid |
> |---|---|
> | **balanced diet** — fruits, vegetables, whole grains | processed, fatty, sugary food; **daily junk food** |
> | **stay active** — play outdoors, walk, cycle | **too much screen time** |
> | **enough sleep** | sleeping very late |
> | **eat breakfast** | **skipping meals** |
> | **yoga, pranayama**, relax or meditate daily; **manage stress** | — |
> | **personal hygiene** | — |
> | say **NO** to **tobacco, alcohol, drugs** | — |
> | spend time with **family and friends** | **isolation** |

**A clean environment:** a **clean playground** vs a **dirty one full of flies and mosquitoes** — people near dirt **fall sick more often**. **Smoke and dust** from vehicles and factories cause **coughing and asthma**; the **Air Quality Index (AQI)** tells us how clean the air is.

## Signs and symptoms

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **SYMPTOM = what YOU FEEL** — pain, tiredness, dizziness.
> - **SIGN = what can be SEEN or MEASURED** — fever (a thermometer reading), rash, high blood pressure, swelling.
>
> Both help a **doctor** work out what's wrong.

## Disease and its types

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **DISEASE** = a condition that affects the **normal working of the body or mind** — one or more organs or systems stop working properly.
> **PATHOGENS** = **disease-causing organisms**: bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, worms.

| | **COMMUNICABLE** (infectious) | **NON-COMMUNICABLE (NCDs)** |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | **pathogens** | **lifestyle, diet, environment**, hormones, deficiency — **no germ** |
| Spreads person to person? | **YES** | **NO** |
| Examples | typhoid, dengue, flu, chickenpox, COVID-19 | **diabetes, heart disease, cancer, asthma**, obesity, high blood pressure |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why are NCDs rising in India?** People eat **more processed food**, **exercise less**, and **live longer**. **Today MOST deaths in India are from NCDs.** (That answers the opening question: vaccines wiped out smallpox and nearly polio, but lifestyle diseases grew.)
>
> **CHRONIC disease** = lasts a **long time (over 3 months)** — cancer, diabetes, asthma.
> **DEFICIENCY diseases** (scurvy, anaemia, goitre) are **non-communicable** too — caused by **lack of a nutrient**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Diabetes** — India has **one of the highest numbers of diabetics in the world**. Causes: **hormonal imbalance**, **unhealthy eating**, **little exercise**, **being overweight**. Signs: **frequent urination, excessive thirst, weight loss, tiredness, slow healing of wounds**.

## How communicable diseases spread

> [!IMPORTANT]
> | Route | How |
> |---|---|
> | **Air** | an infected person **coughs or sneezes** |
> | **Direct contact** | **shaking hands**, touching |
> | **Indirect contact** | **sharing personal items** (towels, handkerchiefs) |
> | **Contaminated food and water** | eating/drinking germs |
> | **VECTORS** | **insects** like **mosquitoes and houseflies** that **carry** pathogens |
> | Animal bites | e.g. a **rabid** animal |

### Common communicable diseases

| Disease | Cause | Where it attacks | Main symptoms | Prevention |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Common cold / influenza** | virus | respiratory tract | blocked/runny nose, sore throat, fever, cough | handwashing, **mask**, don't share items |
| **Chickenpox** | virus | respiratory tract, skin | mild fever, **itchy blisters** | **isolate**, cover mouth, **vaccine** |
| **Measles** | virus | skin, respiratory tract | fever, sore throat, **reddish rash** | isolate, hygiene, **vaccine** |
| **Tuberculosis (TB)** | **bacteria** | **lungs** | cough, fever, fatigue, **night sweats** | avoid close contact, cover mouth, **vaccine** |
| **Hepatitis A** | virus | **liver** | fatigue, nausea, **jaundice** | **boiled water**, vaccine |
| **Cholera** | **bacteria** | intestine | **diarrhoea, dehydration** | hygiene, cooked food, **boiled water**, vaccine |
| **Typhoid** | **bacteria** | intestine | headache, fever, diarrhoea | hygiene, cooked food, boiled water, vaccine |
| **Ascariasis** | **roundworms** | intestine | worms in stool, poor growth, **anaemia** | hygiene, cooked food, boiled water |
| **Malaria** | **protozoa** | blood | **high fever, sweating, periodic chills** | **mosquito nets, repellents**, full sleeves, **stop breeding** |
| **Dengue** ("break-bone fever") | virus | blood | fever, headache, **muscle and joint pain** | nets, repellents, **no still water** |

> [!TIP]
> **Group them by route:** **air** — cold, flu, chickenpox, measles, TB · **food/water** — hepatitis A, cholera, typhoid, roundworms (and **polio**) · **insects** — malaria, dengue.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Simple precautions:** keep yourself and surroundings **clean** · **wash hands with soap** (removes pathogens) · **cover mouth and nose** when coughing or sneezing · **mask** in crowds · **don't share** towels or handkerchiefs · keep **food and water clean** · **stay home when sick** (helps you recover and **stops spreading** it).

> [!NOTE]
> **PARASITES** = organisms that live **in or on another living being** and feed on it — e.g. **worms in the intestine** stealing nutrients.

> [!NOTE]
> **Kamal Ranadive (1917–2001)** — biomedical researcher who studied how **hormones and certain viruses** are linked to **cancer**, and how **tobacco, diet and pollution** raise cancer risk.

## Prevention is better than cure

> [!TIP]
> **Bhadrak district, Odisha:** a **community sanitation campaign** got more people to **build and use toilets**, ending much **open defecation** → **fewer cases of diarrhoea and infection** in children. **Simple sanitation stops many communicable diseases.**

### Immunity

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **IMMUNITY** = the body's **natural ability to fight disease**, through the **IMMUNE SYSTEM**. That's why some people fall sick more often than others in the same place.

### Vaccines

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A VACCINE trains the immune system to recognise and attack a particular germ** — giving **ACQUIRED IMMUNITY** (protection developed after exposure to a pathogen or a vaccine).
>
> Made from:
> - **weakened or dead** pathogens, or
> - **inactive / harmless parts** of the pathogen, or
> - (newer vaccines) instructions for **our own cells to make a harmless part** of the germ.
>
> e.g. a **tetanus shot** after an injury contains an **inactivated bacterial toxin**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why does the body respond more strongly the SECOND time it meets a germ?** The first time, the immune system is **learning** to recognise it — a slow, weak response. It **remembers**, so the next time it **recognises the germ at once** and fights it **fast and hard**. **A vaccine gives you that "first meeting" safely**, without the disease.

> [!WARNING]
> **Vaccines PREVENT; they don't CURE.** They protect **before** you're ill, not once you're sick. Vaccinating also **protects the people around you** — they're carefully **tested for safety**.

### The first vaccine — Edward Jenner and smallpox

| Step | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Observation** | **Milkmaids** who caught mild **cowpox** didn't get deadly **smallpox** |
| **Hypothesis** | something in **cowpox pus** protects against smallpox |
| **Experiment** | Jenner (late 1700s) put cowpox material into a **boy**, who later showed **no illness** when exposed to smallpox |
| **Result** | cowpox-infected people were **resistant** to smallpox |
| **Application** | mass vaccination (campaign from **1965**) → **smallpox eradicated in 1979** |

> [!NOTE]
> **India's earlier method — VARIOLATION:** material from a **smallpox sore** was scratched into the skin to cause a **mild infection** and build immunity. The people who did it were **teekedaars**.
>
> **India is one of the world's largest vaccine producers** — vital during **COVID-19**.
> **Maharaj Kishan Bhan** helped develop the **Rotavirus vaccine**, protecting children from **diarrhoea**.

### Antibiotics

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ANTIBIOTICS kill BACTERIA.** They target parts of **bacterial cells that differ from our cells**.
> **They DO NOT work against VIRUSES or PROTOZOA** — so **no antibiotics for a cold, cough or flu** (these are **viral**).

> [!NOTE]
> **Penicillin (1928), Alexander Fleming:** a **mould** on a discarded petri dish had **stopped bacteria growing** around it. It released a bacteria-killing substance — the **first antibiotic**. A chance observation, noticed by a careful scientist.

> [!WARNING]
> **ANTIBIOTIC RESISTANCE** = bacteria once killed by an antibiotic **survive and multiply** despite it — making infections **harder to treat**.
>
> **How it develops:** taking antibiotics **when not needed** or **not finishing the dose** leaves the **toughest bacteria alive**; they **multiply** and can **pass resistance to other bacteria**. Antibiotics given to **cattle** spread resistant bacteria through **manure, soil, crops and animal food**; hospitals spread them via **dirty surfaces and healthcare workers**.
>
> **Use antibiotics wisely:** **only on a doctor's prescription**, the **right dose**, for the **full duration**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani** use herbs, oils and minerals and stress lifestyle and diet — helpful for everyday well-being, but **not effective for every disease at every stage**.
> **NCD treatment** focuses on **managing symptoms**: medicine, **lifestyle change**, rehabilitation — **early diagnosis** is key.

## Think-it-through questions

1. **Communicable or not?** cold and flu, typhoid, chickenpox → **communicable**; diabetes, asthma → **non-communicable**.
2. **Flu outbreak at school** → sick students **stay home**; masks, handwashing, ventilated rooms; if your bench-mate is sick, **kindly suggest** they rest and see a doctor, and wear a mask yourself.
3. **Travelling to a malaria area** → carry **nets and repellent**, wear **long sleeves**, avoid **still water**, see a doctor if fever follows.
4. **Saniya: "Antibiotics cure any infection."** Vinita can ask: *"Do antibiotics work on viruses — like a cold or COVID? What about antibiotic resistance?"*
5. **Dengue cases by month:** highest in **July, August, September** (65 each); lowest in **January** (10). **Why?** The **monsoon** leaves **still water** where mosquitoes **breed**. **Prevent:** clear stagnant water **before** the rains, fogging, awareness, nets.
6. **Water contaminated by excreta spreads** → **hepatitis A, polio, cholera** (not TB or chickenpox, which spread through air).

## In a nutshell

- **Health = physical + mental + social well-being**, not just no disease.
- **Symptom** = felt; **sign** = seen or measured.
- **Communicable** diseases (pathogens) spread by **air, contact, food/water, vectors**; **non-communicable** ones come from **lifestyle, diet, environment** — and now cause **most deaths in India**.
- **Immunity** fights disease; **vaccines** give acquired immunity (**Jenner**, smallpox eradicated 1979).
- **Antibiotics** (Fleming, penicillin) kill **bacteria only**; misuse → **antibiotic resistance**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** antibiotics don't work on viruses · vaccines prevent, they don't cure · malaria is caused by a protozoan (the mosquito is only the vector) · TB is bacterial, dengue is viral · symptom ≠ sign · deficiency diseases are non-communicable.
