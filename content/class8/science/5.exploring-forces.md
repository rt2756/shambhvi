## Probe and ponder

- Why is it **harder to pedal uphill** than on flat ground?
- Why is it **easier to slip on a wet surface**?
- Why do you feel **"light"** just as a swing starts coming down from its highest point?

## Sonali and Ragini's bicycle ride

On a windy day **Sonali and Ragini** pump up their tyres and ride out. **Against the wind** they must **pedal harder**. **Rough** stretches of road are harder to pedal than **smooth** ones. They ring their **bells** and **turn the handles** to steer round a herd of sheep. Coming **down the hill**, the bicycles **speed up without pedalling** — *"something is pulling us downhill!"* Every one of these is about **forces**.

## What is a force?

**Activity 5.1:** move a big cardboard box in every way you can — **push** it, **pull** it, **lift** it (pull up), **carry** it. Every way needs a **push or a pull**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **FORCE = a push or a pull on an object, resulting from its INTERACTION with another object.**
> - **At least TWO objects** must interact. Push a table → your **hand** and the **table** interact.
> - **SI unit: newton**, symbol **N** (small *n* in the name, capital **N** as the symbol).

> [!NOTE]
> **Forces come in pairs.** When you push a table, you **feel a force on your hand too**. Whenever two objects interact, **each feels a force from the other**; stop the interaction and **both forces disappear**.

## What can a force do?

| Action | Push / pull | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Friend holds your moving bicycle from behind | pull | **slows / stops** it |
| Bat hits a moving ball | push | **changes direction** |
| Pressing an inflated balloon | push | **changes shape** |
| Kicking a football at rest | push | **starts motion** |
| Rolling a chapati, stretching a rubber band | push / pull | **changes shape** |
| Turning an autorickshaw's handle | push/pull | **changes direction** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A force can:**
> 1. **start** an object moving from rest
> 2. **change its speed**
> 3. **change its direction of motion**
> 4. **change its shape**
> 5. do **several of these at once**
>
> **And the reverse is true: whenever speed, direction or shape changes, a force MUST be acting.** Nothing changes on its own.

> [!TIP]
> **An object at rest — no force on it?** **No** — the forces on it **balance each other**. A book on a table is pulled down by gravity and pushed up by the table.
>
> **An object in non-uniform motion** (speed or direction changing) **always** has an (unbalanced) force on it.

## Contact forces

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **CONTACT FORCES act only when objects TOUCH** — directly (hand) or indirectly (a stick, a rope).

### Muscular force

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **MUSCULAR FORCE** = the force from our **muscles contracting and relaxing** — walking, running, lifting, pushing, jumping. Animals, birds, fish and insects use it too, and humans have long used **animals' muscular force** (bullocks ploughing, horses pulling carts).

> [!NOTE]
> **Inside you,** muscular force **chews food** and **pushes it along the alimentary canal**, and the **heart's muscles contracting and expanding** keep **blood circulating**.

### Friction

A ball rolling on flat ground **stops by itself**; a bicycle you stop pedalling **slows down** — sooner on a **rough** road. **Speed changes, so some force must be acting.**

**Activity 5.3:** push a **lunch box** across a table — it slides a bit and stops. Push it the **opposite way** — it still stops. The stopping force always **opposes** the motion.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **FRICTION = the force that comes into play when an object MOVES, or TRIES to move, over another surface.**
> - It always acts **OPPOSITE to the direction of motion** (or attempted motion).
> - It's a **contact force**.
>
> **Why it happens:** even **smooth-looking surfaces have tiny bumps (irregularities)**. When two surfaces touch, the bumps **lock into each other** and resist sliding.

**Activity 5.4:** slide the same box on **glass, cloth, wood, tile, sand** — it stops after **different distances**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Friction depends on the NATURE OF THE SURFACES.** **Rougher surfaces → more friction** (bigger bumps lock more). That's why rough roads are harder to cycle on.

> [!TIP]
> **Why do we slip on ice, polished floors or wet surfaces?** These surfaces are **very smooth** (or water fills the bumps), so there's **very little friction** to grip our feet.
>
> **A ball rolled down a slope from P stops at A on the floor.** To stop it **sooner**, make the floor **rougher** (spread sand or cloth). To make it go **further**, make it **smoother** (glass, polish).

> [!NOTE]
> **Air and water cause friction too.** That's why **aeroplanes, ships, boats and high-speed trains** have **streamlined shapes** — to reduce the drag from the air or water.

## Non-contact forces

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **NON-CONTACT FORCES act from a DISTANCE** — the objects don't have to touch.

### Magnetic force

**Activity 5.5:** slide two **ring magnets** onto a vertical wooden stick with **like poles facing** — the top one **floats** above the bottom one; push it down and you **feel it push back**. Flip both magnets and it still floats.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **MAGNETIC FORCE** = the force a magnet exerts on another magnet or a magnetic material. **Like poles repel, unlike poles attract** — it can **pull or push**, **without touching**.

### Electrostatic force

**Activity 5.6:** rub a **plastic scale or straw** hard with **polythene**, then hold it near **small paper pieces** (don't touch the rubbed part) — the bits **jump up and stick** to it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Rubbing certain materials together builds up **electric charges** on their surfaces — **STATIC CHARGES** (they don't move on their own). The object is now **CHARGED**, and it **attracts uncharged** light objects like paper bits — **from a distance**.

**Activity 5.7:** hang **two balloons** on threads, **rub both with a woollen cloth**:
- the two balloons **move APART** (repel)
- a balloon and the **woollen cloth** **move TOGETHER** (attract)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Two kinds of charge: POSITIVE and NEGATIVE.**
> - Both balloons were charged the **same way** → **same charge** → they **repel**. **LIKE charges REPEL.**
> - Rubbing gives the **rubbing object and the rubbed object OPPOSITE charges** → balloon and cloth **attract**. **UNLIKE charges ATTRACT.**
>
> **ELECTROSTATIC FORCE** = the force a charged body exerts on another charged **or uncharged** body. **Non-contact.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Moving charges = ELECTRIC CURRENT** — the same current that lights a lamp and gives the heating and magnetic effects.

### Gravitational force

**Activity 5.8:** throw a ball up — it comes down. Throw it **harder** — it **still** comes down. Everything thrown up falls back.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **GRAVITATIONAL FORCE (GRAVITY) = the force with which the EARTH ATTRACTS objects towards itself.**
> - **Non-contact.**
> - **ALWAYS ATTRACTIVE** — unlike magnetic and electrostatic forces, which can attract **or** repel.
>
> **This is what pulled Sonali and Ragini downhill.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A ball thrown straight up:** it moves up in a straight line, **slows down**, **stops for an instant at the top**, then comes **straight down, speeding up**. Gravity acts **DOWNWARDS the whole time** — going up, at the top, and coming down. **It's what slows the ball on the way up and speeds it up on the way down.**

## Weight and how to measure it

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WEIGHT = the force with which the Earth pulls an object towards itself.** Since weight is a **force**, its SI unit is the **newton (N)**.

**Activity 5.9:** hang a pencil box, a tiffin box and a stone from a **spring** in turn — each stretches it **by a different amount**. **The Earth pulls different objects with different forces.**

### The spring balance

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A **SPRING BALANCE** measures **weight (force)**: a spring fixed at one end with a **hook** at the other; the **stretch** shows the weight on a scale **in newtons**. (Often a second scale shows **mass in grams**, marked assuming you're on Earth.)
>
> **Reading it (like a thermometer):**
> - **Range:** 0 to the maximum, e.g. **0–10 N**. Never hang anything heavier — it can **damage** the spring.
> - **Smallest division** = (gap between two big marks) ÷ (number of small divisions): **1 N ÷ 5 = 0.2 N**.

### Mass vs weight

> [!IMPORTANT]
> | | **MASS** | **WEIGHT** |
> |---|---|---|
> | What it is | **amount of matter** in an object | **gravitational pull** on it |
> | Unit | **g, kg** | **newton (N)** |
> | Changes with place? | **NO — same everywhere** | **YES** — slightly across Earth, a lot on other planets |
> | Measured with | beam balance (comparing) | spring balance |
>
> | | Earth | Moon | Mars | Venus | Jupiter |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | **Mass** | 1 kg | 1 kg | 1 kg | 1 kg | 1 kg |
> | **Weight** | **10 N** | **1.6 N** | 3.8 N | 9 N | **25.4 N** |

> [!TIP]
> **On the Moon your weight is about ONE-SIXTH of Earth's — because the Moon's gravity is weaker.** Your **mass doesn't change** — you're made of the same amount of matter.
>
> **Everyday trap:** "the wheat bag **weighs** 10 kg" is everyday talk; scientifically **10 kg is its MASS** (its weight is about 100 N).

## Floating and sinking

A **mug feels lighter inside the water** in a bucket. **Activity 5.13:** push a **closed empty bottle** under water — you **feel an upward push**, and when you let go it **bounces up**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **UPTHRUST (BUOYANT FORCE) = the UPWARD force a liquid applies on an object in it.**
>
> Two forces act on an object in a liquid:
> - **gravity (weight) — DOWN**
> - **buoyant force — UP**
>
> | | Result |
> |---|---|
> | **weight > buoyant force** | **SINKS** |
> | **weight = buoyant force** | **FLOATS** |
>
> Buoyant force depends on the **density of the liquid** (Chapter 6).

> [!NOTE]
> **ARCHIMEDES' PRINCIPLE:** an object fully or partly in a liquid feels an **upward force equal to the WEIGHT OF THE LIQUID IT DISPLACES** (pushes aside).
> - weight of liquid displaced **< object's weight** → **sinks**
> - weight of liquid displaced **= object's weight** → **floats**

> [!TIP]
> **A small coin sinks; a bigger wooden block floats.** The coin is **heavy for its size** — the little water it displaces weighs **less** than the coin. The block is **light for its size** — it sinks only until the water it pushes aside **equals its weight**, then floats.
>
> **Same-size blocks floating at different depths:** the one that **sinks deepest is the heaviest** — it must displace more water to balance its weight.

> [!NOTE]
> **PUMICE** — a rock that **floats**! Formed when gassy **lava cools quickly**, trapping tiny **gas bubbles** — light, porous, and **less dense than water**.

## Think-it-through questions

1. **Match:** child lifting a bag → **muscular**; compass needle pointing north → **magnetic**; cricket ball stopping before the boundary → **friction**; fruit falling → **gravitational**; rubbed balloon attracting hair → **electrostatic**.
2. **True or false?**
   - *A force is always needed to change speed.* → **True.**
   - *Friction makes a rolling ball speed up.* → **False** — it **slows** it.
   - *No force between two charged objects a little apart.* → **False** — **electrostatic force** acts at a distance.
3. **Two balloons rubbed with wool brought near each other** → they **repel** (same kind of charge).
4. **Forces on a ball thrown up:** going up — **gravity down** (+ a little air friction down); at the top — **gravity down**; coming down — **gravity down** (+ air friction up).
5. **Why feel "light" on a swing as it comes down?** You and the seat are **falling together**, so the seat **pushes up on you less** — gravity is still acting, but you don't feel the seat's support.

## In a nutshell

- **Force** = push or pull from an interaction; unit **newton (N)**. It can start/stop motion, change speed, direction or shape.
- **Contact forces:** **muscular**, **friction** (opposes motion; more on rough surfaces).
- **Non-contact forces:** **magnetic**, **electrostatic** (like charges repel, unlike attract), **gravitational** (always attractive).
- **Weight** = Earth's pull (N), varies with place; **mass** (kg) never changes. Moon weight ≈ ⅙.
- **Buoyant force** pushes up; sink if weight > upthrust, float if equal (**Archimedes**).

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** weight is measured in newtons, not kg · mass doesn't change on the Moon · gravity only attracts · friction acts opposite to motion · a resting object can have forces on it (balanced) · gravity acts on a ball at the top of its throw too.
