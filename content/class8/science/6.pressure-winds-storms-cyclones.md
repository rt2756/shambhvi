## Pressure = force spread over an area

Two identical bags, equally heavy. One has **narrow** straps and hurts the shoulders; the other has **broad** straps and doesn't.

**Why:** the same weight acts on a **smaller area** with narrow straps and a **larger area** with broad ones.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **`Pressure = Force ÷ Area`**
>
> **SI unit: newton per square metre (N/m²), also called the PASCAL (Pa).**
>
> (We consider only forces acting **perpendicular** to the surface.)

**Worked:** a force of 100 N on a board of area 2 m² gives
`100 ÷ 2 =` **50 N/m² = 50 Pa**

### Reducing pressure — spread the force out

- **Broad straps** on a school bag
- **Broad handles** on a water bucket
- A **round cloth pad** under a pot or basket carried on the head

**All three increase the area, so the same weight produces less pressure.**

### Increasing pressure — concentrate the force

- A nail's **pointed end** drives in easily; its flat head does not
- A knife's **sharp edge** cuts an apple; its blunt edge does not

**Small area → high pressure → the job gets done.**

> [!TIP]
> **Weight didn't change in any of these examples — only the area.** That's the whole idea: pressure is not force, it's force *per unit area*.

## Liquids exert pressure

### Pressure depends on the HEIGHT of the column, not the amount of liquid

**Experiment:** two transparent pipes of the **same length but different diameters**, each with a balloon on the bottom end. Fill both with water to the **same level**.

**Both balloons bulge the SAME amount** — even though the wide pipe holds much more water, and therefore much more weight.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **So the weight of the water is NOT what causes the bulge — the pressure is.** And that pressure depends on the **height of the column**, not on the quantity.

**Add more water to one pipe and its balloon bulges more.** Higher column → higher pressure.

> [!TIP]
> **This is why overhead water tanks are placed at a height.** The taller the column above the tap, the greater the pressure, and the stronger the stream.
>
> **And why a tap on the first floor runs harder than one on the second floor**, when the tank is on the roof — there's more column height above it.

### Liquids push sideways too

**Experiment:** poke four holes at the **same height** near the bottom of a bottle, tape them, fill with water, then remove all the tape at once.

**Water spurts out of all four holes.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Liquids exert pressure not just downwards on the bottom, but on the sides too — in fact in ALL directions.**

**That's why water fountains out of leaking pipe joints.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Why the base of a dam is much broader than its top.** The stored water pushes **horizontally on the walls** and **vertically on the floor**, and the horizontal pressure is **greatest near the bottom** because the water column above is tallest there. A broad base is needed to withstand it.

## Air exerts pressure

**The atmosphere** is the envelope of air surrounding the Earth — **nitrogen, oxygen, argon, carbon dioxide** and other gases in small quantities, extending many kilometres up.

**Experiment:** an inverted paper plate with a stick, covered first with a **folded** chart-paper sheet and then with the **same sheet unfolded**.

**It is much harder to lift with the unfolded sheet — although the sheet weighs exactly the same.**

**Why:** the unfolded sheet covers a **larger area**, so **air pushes down on more of it**. More area × the same pressure = more force.

**The pressure exerted by the air around us is ATMOSPHERIC PRESSURE**, and air exerts it **in all directions** — which is why a balloon expands in every direction when you blow into it.

### How big is atmospheric pressure?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The air column over an area of just 15 cm × 15 cm pushes with about 2250 N** — the same as the weight of a **225 kg** object.
>
> **Why aren't we crushed?** Because **the pressure inside our bodies equals the atmospheric pressure**, balancing it from within. That internal pressure comes from the movement of fluids and gases in our tissues and organs.

**The rubber sucker.** Press it against a smooth flat surface and most of the air underneath is **pushed out**, lowering the pressure inside.

**It sticks because the surrounding air pressure is now HIGHER than the pressure inside.** To pull it off you must overcome that difference.

> [!NOTE]
> **Practical units:** the **millibar (mb)** and the **hectopascal (hPa)** are both equal to **100 Pa**. Weather maps use them.

## How winds form

**Experiment:** join an inflated balloon and an uninflated one with a straw.

**Air flows from the inflated into the uninflated one** until both are about the same size — then the flow **stops**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **AIR MOVES FROM A REGION OF HIGH PRESSURE TO A REGION OF LOW PRESSURE.**
>
> **The flow stops when the pressures become equal.** And **the bigger the pressure difference, the faster the air moves** — which is why winds are stronger on some days than others.

### Sea breeze and land breeze

| | **Day** | **Night** |
|---|---|---|
| Which heats faster | **land** | (land cools faster) |
| Warm air rises over | **land** → low pressure over land | **sea** → low pressure over sea |
| Wind blows | **sea → land** = **sea breeze** | **land → sea** = **land breeze** |

**Why the air rises:** warm air is **lighter**, so it rises, leaving a **low-pressure** region behind, and cooler high-pressure air rushes in to fill it.

## High-speed winds LOWER the pressure

**Experiment:** hang two inflated balloons 6–10 cm apart and blow air into the gap between them.

**They move TOWARDS each other** — and blowing harder makes them rush together faster.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **High-speed winds are accompanied by REDUCED air pressure.**
>
> Blowing between the balloons creates a **low-pressure region** there, and the **higher surrounding pressure pushes the balloons in**.

### Why roofs blow off — and how to prevent it

When high-speed winds pass **over** a roof, the pressure **above** it drops. The pressure **below** (inside the house) stays normal.

**If the difference is large and the roof is weak, the roof is pushed UP and blown away.**

> [!TIP]
> **This is why you should keep doors and windows OPEN during a high-wind storm** — counter-intuitive but correct.
>
> With them open, wind flows **through** the house too, so the inside pressure drops as well. **The difference between inside and above the roof is greatly reduced**, and the roof stays put.

## Storms, thunderstorms and lightning

### How a storm forms

```
land gets heated
    ↓
warm, moist air (lighter) RISES → low-pressure area
    ↓
cooler air from surrounding high-pressure areas rushes in
    ↓  (and it too gets heated and rises — a continuous circulation)
rising air EXPANDS and COOLS
    ↓
moisture CONDENSES into water droplets → clouds
    ↓
droplets merge into heavier drops → rain, hail or snow
```

**Strong winds accompanied by rain make a STORM.** They are more frequent in hot, humid, tropical regions like India.

### How lightning happens

If the warm air rises high enough, the **low temperature up there turns water droplets into ice particles**.

```
strong winds blowing up and down
    ↓
water droplets and ice particles RUB against each other
    ↓  (rubbing charges objects — Chapter 5)
static charges develop in the cloud
    ↓
lighter POSITIVE ice particles rise to the TOP of the cloud
heavier NEGATIVE water droplets sink to the BOTTOM
    ↓  = charge separation
the negative cloud base makes the ground and nearby
trees and buildings become POSITIVELY charged
```

**Normally air is an electrical INSULATOR and keeps the opposite charges apart.**

**But when the charge build-up becomes very large, the insulating property of air breaks down.** A sudden flow of charge occurs, giving a bright flash — **LIGHTNING**.

**Lightning can occur within a cloud, between clouds, or between a cloud and the ground.**

**Thunder** is the sound made when lightning **rapidly heats the surrounding air**, causing it to **expand** violently.

**A storm accompanied by lightning and thunder is a THUNDERSTORM.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Local Indian thunderstorms have their own names:** **Kalboishakhi** in West Bengal, Bihar and Jharkhand; **Bordoisila** in Assam — both arriving before the monsoon and helping **kharif crops**. In Kerala, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu they are called **mango showers** because they help mangoes ripen; in Karnataka they also help coffee plants grow.

### Staying safe in lightning

**Lightning can start fires, damage buildings and cause severe burns or death.**

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Find a **low-lying open area** and **crouch down**, minimising contact with the ground | **Don't lie flat** — that increases contact with the ground |
| Stay **inside a bus or car** — comparatively safe | **Don't stand near tall objects** — trees, poles |
| | **Don't use an umbrella with a metallic rod** |
| Get **out of water** immediately | |

> [!NOTE]
> **A lightning conductor** is a metallic rod fitted along a building's walls during construction. Its **pointed upper end sits above the highest point of the building** and its lower end is **buried deep in the ground**.
>
> It gives the charge an **easy path into the ground**, so it travels harmlessly down the rod instead of through the building.

## Cyclones

**Cyclones are large storms that form over WARM OCEAN WATERS.**

```
ocean water is heated → warm moist air above it RISES
    ↓
rising vapour CONDENSES into raindrops
    ↓
condensation RELEASES the heat taken up during evaporation
    ↓
that heat warms the rising air FURTHER, so it rises even more
    ↓
pressure at the centre drops even LOWER
    ↓
surrounding air rushes in, and it too begins to rise
    ↓
Earth's ROTATION makes the moving air SPIN
    ↓
a very low-pressure area with high-speed winds revolving around it
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The released heat of condensation is the engine of a cyclone.** Evaporation takes heat from the ocean; condensation gives it back to the air. That is why cyclones need **warm ocean water** — and why they **weaken over land**, where the source of moist air is cut off.
>
> **And the Earth's rotation is what makes it spin.** Without rotation you'd get a rising column of air, but not a revolving cyclone.

**The EYE of the cyclone** is the region of **lowest pressure at the centre**, where the **wind is calm** — while the surrounding region has strong winds and heavy rainfall.

### What cyclones do

- **Storm surge:** strong winds push ocean water towards the shore, creating a **wall of water 3–12 metres high** that floods coastal areas and inland regions.
- **Heavy rainfall** makes rivers overflow and can trigger **landslides**.
- **Seawater rushing inland contaminates drinking water** and damages farmland — the **salt makes soil less fertile**, hurting crops for a long time afterwards.
- **Fallen trees and debris block roads**, so help can't reach affected areas.
- **Power outages** lasting days disrupt emergency services and daily life.

**Cyclone Amphan (2020)** had peak wind speeds of **270 km/h**.

**Even after a cyclone weakens over land, it leaves destruction that takes months or years to repair.**

### Protecting ourselves

- Follow **weather reports, alerts and warnings from the India Meteorological Department (IMD)**
- **Weather monitoring satellites** let us track cyclones and predict their path, greatly reducing loss of life
- If you live in a cyclone-prone area, keep an **emergency kit ready**
- **Move quickly to a designated cyclone shelter**

## The whole chain in one place

```
warm air rises → low pressure
cool air rushes in to replace it
rising air cools → vapour condenses → clouds
drops grow heavy → rain, hail or snow
up-and-down winds rub droplets and ice → charges in the cloud
charges meet → lightning (and thunder)
over warm ocean, with Earth's spin → cyclone
```
