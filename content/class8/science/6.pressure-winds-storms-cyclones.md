## Probe and ponder

- Why are winds **stronger on some days** than others?
- Why are **water tanks put up high**?
- Can **air pressure crush us**?
- What causes **storms and cyclones**?

Wind lifts fallen leaves, bends trees, **slams doors**, rattles windows, flutters clothes — the force of wind creates **wind pressure**.

## Pressure

**Megha and Pawan** walk to a picnic with **equally heavy** bags. Pawan's bag **hurts his shoulders**; Megha's doesn't. The difference: **Pawan's straps are narrow, Megha's are broad.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The bag's **weight (force)** is the same, but **narrow straps press it onto a SMALL area** of the shoulder; **broad straps spread it over a LARGE area**.
>
> **PRESSURE = FORCE ÷ AREA** (force per unit area; here, force acting **perpendicular** to the surface)
>
> - **SI unit:** newton per square metre, **N/m²**, also called the **pascal (Pa)**.
> - e.g. 100 N on 2 m² → 100 ÷ 2 = **50 N/m² = 50 Pa**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Same force, SMALLER area → HIGHER pressure. Same force, LARGER area → LOWER pressure.**
>
> | Want LOW pressure (spread the force) | Want HIGH pressure (concentrate it) |
> |---|---|
> | **broad bag straps** | **pointed end** of a nail |
> | **broad bucket handle** | **sharp edge** of a knife |
> | a **cloth ring** under a head-load of pots | needles, pins |
> | broad base of a dam (see below) | |

> [!TIP]
> **Worked examples:**
> - **Elephant, weight 20 000 N, on 4 feet of 0.25 m² each:** area = 4 × 0.25 = **1 m²** → pressure = 20 000 ÷ 1 = **20 000 Pa**.
> - **Boat A:** 5 people × 700 N = 3500 N on 7 m² → **500 Pa**. **Boat B:** 3 × 700 = 2100 N on 3.5 m² → **600 Pa**. **B** has more pressure, by **100 Pa**.
> - **A boy on loose sand sinks more STANDING than LYING** — same weight, but standing puts it on the **small area of his feet → higher pressure**.

## Pressure in liquids

### Depends on the HEIGHT of the liquid (Activity 6.1)

Tie **balloons** to the bottom of a **narrow pipe and a broad pipe**, and fill both with water to the **same height**.

→ **Both balloons bulge EQUALLY** — even though the broad pipe holds **more water (more weight)**.
→ Add more water to one pipe → its balloon bulges **more**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The pressure of a liquid depends on the HEIGHT (depth) of its column** — **not** on how much liquid there is or the width of the container. **Taller column → more pressure at the bottom.**
>
> **That's why water tanks are put HIGH:** a taller column of water gives **more pressure** at the taps → a **good stream**. A friend on the **first floor** gets a **stronger stream** than you on the **second** — there's a **taller column of water above** their tap.

> [!TIP]
> **Two vessels filled to the same level, A with a small base and B with a big base:** the **pressure** at the bottom is the **same** (same height), but the **force** on B's bottom is **bigger** (force = pressure × area, and B's area is bigger). So **P_A = P_B, F_A < F_B**.
>
> **Connected vessels of different shapes** fill to the **same level** — water flows until the **pressure is equal** everywhere at the bottom.

### Liquids press in ALL directions (Activity 6.2)

Poke **four holes at the same height** around a plastic bottle, tape them, fill it, then pull off the tape → **water spurts out of all four sides**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Liquids exert pressure on the bottom AND the SIDES — in fact in ALL directions.** That's why water **spurts like a fountain** from a leaky pipe joint.

> [!NOTE]
> **Why is a dam's base much broader than its top?** Water presses **sideways** on the dam wall, and that pressure is **greatest near the bottom** (the deepest water). The **broad base** withstands it.

## Air pressure

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The **ATMOSPHERE** = the envelope of air (nitrogen, oxygen, argon, CO₂…) round the Earth, many kilometres high. **Air presses on everything** — **ATMOSPHERIC PRESSURE**.

**Activity 6.3:** stick a handle through an **upside-down paper plate**, cover it with **folded** chart paper and lift — easy. Cover it with the **same sheet unfolded** (much bigger area) — **much harder**, though the paper weighs the same.
→ **Air pushes down on the sheet; bigger area → bigger total force.** Air exerts **pressure**.

> [!TIP]
> **A balloon swells in ALL directions** when you blow into it — air pressure acts in **all directions**. Leave its mouth open and the air **rushes out** (from high pressure inside to lower pressure outside).

### How big is it? (Activity 6.4 — the rubber sucker)

Press a rubber **sucker** onto a smooth surface → it **sticks**; it's **hard to pull off**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Pressing **pushes out most of the air** inside the cup, so the **pressure inside is LOW**. The **higher air pressure outside** pushes it onto the surface. To pull it off you must overcome that **pressure difference**.
>
> **A sucker on a ROUGH surface won't stick** — air **leaks in** through the gaps, so the pressure inside and out stays equal.

> [!NOTE]
> **Air pressure is HUGE:** on just **15 cm × 15 cm**, the air above pushes with about **2250 N** — like a **225 kg** mass!
> **Why aren't we crushed?** The **pressure inside our bodies** (from fluids and gases in our tissues) **equals** atmospheric pressure and **balances** it.
>
> **Units:** besides pascals, air pressure is given in **millibars (mb)** or **hectopascals (hPa)** — both **= 100 Pa**.

## How winds form

**Activity 6.5:** join an **inflated balloon** to an **empty one** with a straw → air flows from the full one into the empty one until **both are about the same size**, then **stops**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **AIR MOVES FROM HIGH PRESSURE TO LOW PRESSURE** — and keeps flowing **until the pressures are equal**.
> **WIND = air moving because of a pressure difference.** The **bigger the difference, the faster the wind** — that's why winds are **stronger on some days**.
> (Same reason air escapes a **punctured tyre**.)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Sea breeze and land breeze (from Class 7), explained by pressure:**
> - **Day:** land heats faster → air above it gets **warm, light, rises** → **LOW pressure over land** → cooler air blows **from sea to land** = **SEA BREEZE**.
> - **Night:** the sea stays warmer → **low pressure over the sea** → wind blows **from land to sea** = **LAND BREEZE**.

> [!TIP]
> **Coastal trees on a summer afternoon bend one way** — the **sea breeze** blows **from sea to land**, so the trees lean **away from the sea, towards the land**.

## Fast winds mean LOW pressure

**Activity 6.6:** hang two balloons 6–10 cm apart and **blow between them** → they **move TOWARDS each other** (faster when you blow harder).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **HIGH-SPEED AIR → LOWER pressure.** Blowing makes **low pressure between** the balloons; the **higher pressure outside pushes them together**.
>
> (Blow over a hanging **strip of paper** → it **rises** — the same effect.)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why roofs blow off in storms:** fast wind over a roof makes **low pressure ABOVE it**, while the still air inside the house keeps **higher pressure BELOW**. A big difference can **lift a weak roof off**.
> **Keep doors and windows OPEN** in a windstorm — wind flows **through** the house too, so the pressure difference is **much smaller**.

> [!TIP]
> **Why are holes cut in banners and hoardings?** Wind can **pass through** the holes, so there's **less pressure difference** across the banner — it isn't torn or blown down.

## Storms, thunderstorms and lightning

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **How a storm forms:**
> 1. Land gets hot → **warm, moist air rises** → **low pressure**.
> 2. **Cooler air rushes in** from surrounding high-pressure areas; it too heats and rises → a continuous **wind circulation**.
> 3. Rising air **expands and cools**; its moisture **condenses** into droplets → **clouds**.
> 4. Droplets merge into heavy drops → **rain, hail or snow**.
>
> **STORM = strong winds with rain.** Common in **hot, humid, tropical** places like **India**.

### Lightning

> [!IMPORTANT]
> 1. Warm air rises **so high** that droplets **freeze into ice particles**.
> 2. **Strong winds blowing up and down** make **ice particles and water droplets rub** together → **static charges** build up (like rubbing a balloon).
> 3. **Light, POSITIVE ice particles** go to the **top** of the cloud; **heavy, NEGATIVE water droplets** collect at the **bottom** — **charge separation**.
> 4. The negative cloud base makes the **ground, trees and buildings below POSITIVE**.
> 5. **Air is normally an INSULATOR**, keeping the charges apart — but when the charge gets **very large**, air's insulation **breaks down** → a sudden **flow of charge** = a bright flash: **LIGHTNING**.
>
> It can strike **within a cloud, between clouds, or cloud to ground**.
> **THUNDER:** lightning **heats the air suddenly** → the air **expands violently** → a loud **sound**.
> **THUNDERSTORM = a storm with lightning and thunder.**

> [!TIP]
> **If air and clouds were good conductors, would there be lightning?** **No** — charges would **leak away steadily** and never build up to a huge, sudden discharge.

> [!NOTE]
> **India's local thunderstorms:** **Kalbaishakhi** (West Bengal, Bihar, Jharkhand), **Bordoisila** (Assam) — come **before the monsoon** and help **kharif** crops. **Mango showers** (Kerala, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu) help **mangoes ripen**; Karnataka's storms help **coffee**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Lightning safety:**
> - stay **away from tall objects** (trees, poles)
> - in the open, find a **low-lying area** and **CROUCH** — **don't lie flat** (less contact with the ground)
> - **no umbrella with a metal rod**
> - **get out of water**
> - inside a **car or bus** you're **relatively safe**
>
> **LIGHTNING CONDUCTOR:** a **metal rod** along a building, its **pointed top above the highest point**, its other end **buried deep in the ground** → gives charge an **easy path into the ground**, protecting the building.

## Cyclones

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **How a cyclone forms (over WARM OCEANS):**
> 1. Warm ocean water heats the air above → **warm, moist air rises**.
> 2. Its vapour **condenses into raindrops**, **releasing heat** (the heat taken in during evaporation comes back out).
> 3. This **heats the rising air even more** → it rises further → **even lower pressure**.
> 4. Air from all around **rushes in** and rises too; **the Earth's rotation makes it SPIN**.
> 5. The cycle repeats → a **very low-pressure centre** with **high-speed winds spinning round it**.
>
> **CYCLONE = a huge spinning system of clouds, winds and rain.**
> **EYE** = the centre, **lowest pressure**, **CALM**; the area around it has the **strongest winds and heaviest rain**.
>
> Over **land**, it loses its supply of **moist air** and slowly **weakens** — but not before doing great damage.

> [!WARNING]
> **Damage:** **Cyclone Amphan (2020)** — peak winds **270 km/h**.
> - **STORM SURGE:** winds push a **wall of seawater 3–12 m high** onto the coast → **floods**
> - heavy rain → **rivers overflow, landslides**
> - **seawater** spoils **drinking water** and makes farmland **salty and less fertile**
> - **fallen trees block roads**; **power cuts** for days

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Staying safe:** follow alerts from the **India Meteorological Department (IMD)** — **weather satellites** now **track cyclones and predict their path**. Keep an **emergency kit**; move to a **designated cyclone shelter**.

> [!TIP]
> **Storm → cyclone:** when a storm forms over **warm ocean water**, the **heat released by condensation** keeps powering the rising air, the pressure keeps dropping, more air rushes in, and the **Earth's rotation sets it spinning** — it grows into a cyclone.

## Think-it-through questions

1. **True or false?**
   - *Air flows from high to low pressure.* → **True.**
   - *Liquids press only on the bottom.* → **False** — **all directions**.
   - *Weather is stormy at the eye of a cyclone.* → **False** — the eye is **calm**.
   - *In a thunderstorm a car is safer.* → **True.**
2. **More pressure on the ground floor from the roof tank?** → **Raise the tank higher** (a bigger tank at the same height won't help).
3. **Two balloons tied at different heights on a bottle's side, bottle filled:** both **bulge**, but the **lower one bulges more** — it's **deeper**, with a taller column of water above it.

## In a nutshell

- **Pressure = force ÷ area** (N/m² = Pa). Smaller area → more pressure.
- **Liquid pressure** grows with **depth** and acts in **all directions** (high tanks, broad dams).
- **Air pressure** is huge but balanced by pressure inside us (suckers, balloons).
- **Wind:** air flows **high → low pressure**; **fast air = low pressure** (roofs, balloons).
- **Thunderstorm:** rising moist air → clouds → rubbing ice and droplets separates charge → **lightning** (+ thunder).
- **Cyclone:** over **warm ocean**; condensation heat + Earth's rotation → spinning low; **calm eye**; storm surge.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** liquid pressure depends on height, not amount · fast-moving air has lower pressure · open windows in a windstorm · crouch, don't lie flat, in lightning · the eye of a cyclone is calm · cyclones form over warm oceans and weaken over land.
