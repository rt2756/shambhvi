## Probe and ponder

- Why can you **pile up stones or sand** but not **water**?
- Why does water take the shape of your **cupped hands** — and lose it when you let go?
- We can't see air, so how does it **add weight** to a balloon?

## Where does sand come from?

In the mountains, rocks break down by **erosion**. Rivers carry the pieces and keep breaking them into **pebbles, stones, sand** and, finally, fine **clay**. **Is a sand grain the smallest bit of rock — or can it be broken further?**

## Matter is made of particles

**Activity 7.1:** break a chalk stick again and again, then **grind** the bits in a mortar and pestle. Under a magnifying glass, **every tiny speck is still chalk** — grinding is a **physical change** (no new substance, only smaller pieces).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Imagine grinding forever. Eventually you'd reach pieces that **can't be broken any further** — the basic building blocks of chalk.
> **CONSTITUENT PARTICLE = the basic unit that makes up a larger piece of a substance.** A piece of chalk is a huge number of them. Sand and clay grains, too, are made of countless constituent particles.

**Activity 7.2:** drop two spoons of sugar into water **without stirring** — the **top layer isn't sweet**. **Stir** till it dissolves — now the top layer **is sweet**, though **no sugar can be seen**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Dissolving breaks sugar into its constituent particles**, far too small to see (even with an ordinary microscope) — but **taste proves they're there**.
> **Where did they go?** Into the **INTERPARTICLE SPACES** — the gaps **between water particles**.
>
> **So: matter is made of a very large number of extremely small particles, with spaces between them.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Acharya Kanad**, an ancient Indian philosopher, proposed that matter is made of tiny, **indivisible, eternal** particles called **Parmanu** — in his ***Vaisheshika Sutras***.

## What holds particles together?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **INTERPARTICLE ATTRACTIONS** = attractive forces holding particles together.
> - Their strength depends on the **substance** and the **distance between particles**.
> - **Even a slight increase in distance weakens them drastically.**
> - **The strength of these attractions decides the STATE** — solid, liquid or gas.

## Solids

**Activity 7.3:** try hammering an **iron nail, rock salt, a stone, wood, a key, aluminium** — they keep their shape.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SOLIDS have a definite SHAPE and a definite VOLUME** because:
> - particles are **tightly packed** (least space between them)
> - **interparticle attractions are VERY STRONG**
> - particles are held in **fixed positions** — they can only **VIBRATE** about them, not move past each other.

### Melting

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Heat a solid → its particles vibrate more and more vigorously → they break free of their fixed positions** → attractions weaken → it becomes a **LIQUID**.
> **MELTING POINT** = the **minimum temperature** at which a solid melts into a liquid at **atmospheric pressure**.
>
> | Solid | Melting point | Attractions |
> |---|---|---|
> | Ice | **0 °C** | weak |
> | Urea | 133 °C | |
> | Iron | **1538 °C** | very strong |
>
> **Weak attractions → low melting point; strong attractions → high melting point.**

> [!NOTE]
> Usually particles in a liquid are a little **farther apart** than in its solid. **Ice is an exception** — its particles are **farther apart than in water** (that's why ice floats).

## Liquids

**Activity 7.4:** pour **200 mL** of water from container A to B to C (all different shapes) — it **takes each container's shape**, but stays at **200 mL**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **LIQUIDS have NO fixed shape but a FIXED VOLUME.**
> - Particles can **move past each other** — so a liquid **flows** and takes the container's shape.
> - But they're still **close together** — attractions are **slightly weaker than in solids**, still strong enough to keep the volume fixed.
>
> **Run a finger through water** — it moves aside and **closes back** behind you. You can't do that with a solid.

> [!TIP]
> **Why can you pile sand but not water?** Each **sand grain is a solid** that keeps its shape, so grains rest on each other. **Water particles slide past each other** and spread out.
>
> **Rice grains and rice flour take the jar's shape — are they liquids?** **No — each grain is a SOLID** with its own fixed shape and volume. A **heap** of small solid pieces just settles into the jar.

### Boiling and evaporation

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **BOILING POINT** = the temperature at which a liquid boils and turns into vapour at atmospheric pressure. Particles move so **vigorously** that they escape — **fast**, from the **surface AND from inside** (seen as **bubbles**).
>
> **EVAPORATION** = vapour formation at **any temperature below** the boiling point — **slow**, and **only at the surface**. That's why spilled water **dries at room temperature**.

## Gases

**Activity 7.5:** trap **incense smoke** in gas jar A, cover it with a glass plate, put an empty jar B upside down on top, **slide the plate out** → the smoke **spreads to fill BOTH jars**. (Iodine vapour works too — handle with care, it irritates.)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **GASES have NO fixed shape and NO fixed volume** — they **fill all the space** available.
> - Interparticle attractions are **negligible**.
> - Particles **move freely in all directions** at high speed.
>
> (The smoke specks are **pushed around by invisible air particles** hitting them — they let us **see** the motion of gas particles.)

> [!NOTE]
> **Liquids and gases both FLOW** and don't keep a fixed shape → together they're called **FLUIDS**.

## Spaces between particles

### Gases can be squeezed (Activity 7.6)

Pull a needleless **syringe** fully out, **block the nozzle with your thumb**, and push the plunger → the air **squashes into a smaller volume**. Let go → the plunger **springs back**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Gases are easily COMPRESSED** — there's **a lot of space** between their particles, which pressure can reduce.
> **Water in the syringe? Practically INCOMPRESSIBLE** — liquid particles have **little space** between them.

### Liquids have spaces (Activity 7.7)

Mark the level of half a glass of water (**A**). Add 2 spoons of sugar → level rises (**B**). **Stir till it dissolves** → the level **drops a little** (**C**, below B).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The solution's volume is LESS than water + sugar added separately** → **sugar particles slipped into the spaces between water particles**.
>
> **Sand** doesn't dissolve — it just **settles** and takes up its own space, so the level **rises and stays**.
> **Why sugar dissolves but sand doesn't:** water particles can **pull sugar particles out** of the crystal; **sand's particles are held too strongly**.

> [!WARNING]
> **The spaces between particles are EMPTY** — not filled with air. There's **nothing** there.

> [!NOTE]
> **"Particle" means different things in different places.** **Suspended Particulate Matter (SPM)** in air pollution means **dust specks** — huge compared with constituent particles; each speck is itself made of countless atoms and molecules.

## Particles are always moving

### In liquids (Activity 7.8)

Drop a few grains of **potassium permanganate** into water (use a spatula — don't touch it) → **pink streaks** spread out; later the **whole glass turns evenly pink**, with no stirring.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Water particles are constantly moving.** They **pull particles out** of the grain and **bump them along** until they're spread evenly.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Hot vs room-temperature vs ice-cold water:** the colour spreads **fastest in HOT water**, slower at room temperature, **slowest in ice-cold** water.
> **Heat makes particles move FASTER.**

### In gases (Activity 7.9)

Light an **incense stick** in one corner of a room → at first you smell it only nearby; soon, **throughout the room**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Air particles are constantly moving**, hitting the fragrance particles and **spreading them** through the room — just like **perfume** reaches you, or **food smells** reach you from the kitchen.
>
> **Why gases mix easily but solids don't:** gas particles have **lots of space** and **move freely**, so they spread into each other. Solid particles are **fixed in place**.

> [!NOTE]
> **How soap cleans:** soap particles surround **oil** on the cloth — **one end grips the oil**, the **other end mixes with water** — lifting the oil off to be **washed away**.

## The big picture — thermal energy decides the state

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THERMAL (HEAT) ENERGY of the particles decides the state of matter:**
> - **Solid:** low energy → particles **close**, strong attractions → only **vibrate**.
> - **At the melting point:** energy overcomes the attractions enough for particles to **leave fixed positions** → **liquid** (move around, but within a limited space).
> - **Gas:** enough energy to **overcome the attractions completely** → particles move **freely everywhere**.

| | **Solid** | **Liquid** | **Gas** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interparticle space | **minimum** | a little more | **maximum** |
| Packing | **closely packed** | a little looser | particles **free** |
| Attraction | **maximum** | slightly weaker | **negligible** |
| Movement | **only vibrations** | within a **limited space** | in **all the available space** |
| Shape | **fixed** | **not fixed** | **not fixed** |
| Volume | **fixed** | **fixed** | **not fixed** |
| Compressible? | no | practically no | **yes, easily** |

> [!NOTE]
> **Atoms and molecules:** the tiny particles of matter are **atoms** and **molecules**. **Iron** is iron atoms; **gold** is gold atoms. Some atoms (**hydrogen, oxygen, sulfur**) can't exist alone and join into **molecules** — two hydrogen atoms make a **hydrogen molecule**; **water = 2 hydrogen atoms + 1 oxygen atom**. (More in higher grades.)

## Think-it-through questions

1. **Main difference between solids and liquids:** particles are **closely packed in solids** and **move past each other in liquids**.
2. **True or false — correct the false ones:**
   - *Melting ice is solid → liquid.* → **True.**
   - *Melting involves a decrease in interparticle attractions.* → **True.**
   - *Solids have fixed shape and volume.* → **True.**
   - *In solids attractions are very strong and spaces very small.* → **True.**
   - *Heated camphor's fragrance reaches all corners.* → **True** — particles move and spread.
   - *Heating adds energy that is released as smell.* → **False** — heating makes camphor **turn to vapour faster**; its **particles** spread and we smell them.
3. **Remove all the constituent particles from a chair?** → **Nothing of the chair would remain** — the chair **is** its particles.
4. **Spilled milk spreads; the glass keeps its shape** → milk is a **liquid** (particles move past each other); glass is a **solid** (particles fixed).
5. **Why does seawater taste salty though you can't see salt?** → salt has **dissolved** into constituent particles in the **spaces between water particles** — too small to see, but **tasted**.
6. **A just-blown-out candle:** the **hard wax** is solid, the **pool of molten wax** is liquid, and the **wax vapour/smoke** above is gas.

## In a nutshell

- **Matter = huge numbers of tiny particles** with **spaces** between them, held by **interparticle attractions** (Kanad's *Parmanu*).
- **Solid:** fixed shape and volume, particles vibrate. **Liquid:** fixed volume, flows. **Gas:** no fixed shape or volume, fills all space, compressible.
- **Melting point** and **boiling point**: heat overcomes attractions. **Evaporation** happens at any temperature, at the surface.
- Particles **move constantly** (KMnO₄ spreading, incense smell) — **faster when hot**.
- **Thermal energy** decides the state.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** spaces between particles are empty, not air · sugar dissolving lowers the level slightly (fills gaps) · ice particles are farther apart than water's · evaporation ≠ boiling · rice and flour are solids · heat speeds up particles.
