## Probe and ponder

- Have you seen the **Moon in the daytime**? Why is it up when the Sun is?
- If you lived **on the Moon**, what would a day, a month or a year mean?
- Without **clocks or calendars**, how could we measure time?

## Meera sees a daytime Moon

On **Makar Sankranti**, at Ahmedabad's **Patang Mahotsav** (kite festival), **Meera** spots the **Moon shining in the daytime** — and it isn't a full circle. She knows the Moon is a **sphere** that **shines by reflected sunlight**. So **why don't we see all of it every night?** (Not an eclipse — those are rare and short.)

## The phases of the Moon

**Activity 11.1 (a month-long observation):** starting the morning after a full Moon, look for the Moon **at sunrise** (in the west), shade its dark part in a circle each day, and note whether the bright part is **growing or shrinking** and whether the Moon looks **closer to or farther from the Sun**. After about 15 days, switch to **sunset**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **FULL MOON (Purnima)** | the whole bright **circle** is seen |
> | **NEW MOON (Amavasya)** | the Moon **can't be seen** |
> | **WANING** — **Krishna Paksha** | bright part **SHRINKS** — full → half (about a week) → none (another week) |
> | **WAXING** — **Shukla Paksha** | bright part **GROWS** — none → half (about a week) → full (another week) |
> | **CRESCENT** | **less than half** of the lit part seen (a thin curve) |
> | **GIBBOUS** | **more than half** seen |
> | **PHASES** | the changing shapes of the bright part, day to day |
>
> **One full cycle (full Moon to full Moon) ≈ 29.5 days — about a MONTH.**

### Where to look for the Moon

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **Full Moon:** **opposite the Sun** — as the Sun **rises in the east**, the full Moon is **setting in the west**.
> - As it **wanes**, the Moon at sunrise appears **closer to the Sun** each day. At **half Moon** it's **overhead at sunrise**. Then a thin crescent, even closer.
> - **Waxing Moon → easiest to see at SUNSET; waning Moon → at SUNRISE.**
> - **New Moon:** **closest to the Sun** in the sky; **full Moon:** **farthest**.

> [!NOTE]
> **The Moon rises about 50 MINUTES LATER each day.** It doesn't always rise at sunset — sometimes it rises in the **afternoon (2–4 p.m.)**, so you can see it in the **eastern sky in daylight**. (That's Meera's daytime Moon!) Moonrise times are published by the **Positional Astronomy Centre (IMD)**.

### Why phases happen

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Moon's shape never changes — only how much of its LIT side we can see.**
> 1. The Moon makes **no light**; it **reflects sunlight**.
> 2. **The half facing the Sun is always lit**; the other half is dark.
> 3. As the Moon **revolves round the Earth** (about a month), the **part of that lit half facing us changes**.
>    - Moon **opposite** the Sun → we see the **whole lit half** → **FULL MOON**.
>    - Moon **between** Earth and Sun → we face only its **dark half** → **NEW MOON**.
>    - In between → part lit, part dark → **crescent, half, gibbous**.

**Activity 11.2 — ball on a stick:** stand in a dark place with a lamp or torch about 3 m away (the **Sun**); your head is the **Earth**; hold a ball (the **Moon**) at arm's length, slightly above your head. **Turn slowly anticlockwise** and watch the ball:
- ball **away from** the lamp → **fully lit face** → full Moon
- ball **towards** the lamp → **dark face** → new Moon
- in between → the line between light and dark is **curved** → crescent and gibbous shapes

> [!TIP]
> **People all over the Earth see nearly the SAME phase on a given day** — the Earth spins once a day, but the Moon takes a month to go round, so its phase barely changes in a day.
>
> **Why does the Moon come back 50 minutes later?** In the **24 hours** the Earth takes to spin once, the Moon has **moved further along its orbit**, so the Earth must turn **a bit more** (~50 min) to bring it back to the same place in the sky.

> [!WARNING]
> **Phases are NOT caused by the Earth's shadow.** That's a common wrong idea. The **Earth's shadow on the Moon** causes a **LUNAR ECLIPSE**.
> - **Lunar eclipse** → only on a **full Moon** day. **Solar eclipse** → only on a **new Moon** day.
> - They **don't happen every month** because the Moon's orbit is **slightly tilted** to the Earth's orbit — usually the three bodies don't line up exactly.

> [!TIP]
> **A crescent always "points" its lit side towards the Sun.** Point from the Sun to the crescent — your finger crosses the **lit edge first**. Proof that we're seeing **reflected sunlight**.

## How calendars came about

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Three natural cycles → three units of time:**
>
> | Unit | Based on | Length |
> |---|---|---|
> | **DAY** | **Earth ROTATING** on its axis — the Sun returning to its **highest point** | **24 hours** (mean solar day) |
> | **MONTH** | **Moon REVOLVING** round the Earth — one cycle of **phases** | **≈ 29.5 days** |
> | **YEAR** | **Earth REVOLVING** round the Sun — one cycle of **seasons** | **≈ 365¼ days** |

**Activity 11.3 — measure a day:** stand a **1 m stick** upright in a sunny spot; from **11:00 a.m. to about 1:10 p.m.**, mark the tip of its shadow **every minute**. The **shortest shadow** = the Sun at its **highest point**. Repeat on following days — e.g. 12:20, 12:20, 12:19 → each day ≈ **24 hours**.

### Lunar calendars

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **About 12 Moon cycles fit in one cycle of seasons** → **lunar calendar**: months of ≈ 29.5 days, **12 lunar months = a lunar year of 354 days**.
> **Problem:** seasons repeat every **≈ 365 days**, so a lunar year is **~11 days SHORT** — the **seasons drift** through the lunar months year after year.

### Solar calendars

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Farmers need to know when seasons come** → **SOLAR calendars**, kept in step with the seasons. The **Gregorian calendar** (the one we use) is solar: months of **30, 31, or 28 (February)** days, adding up to **365**.
>
> **LEAP YEAR:** the Earth takes about **365¼** days, so the extra **¼ day × 4 years ≈ 1 day**. → **Every 4 years, February gets a 29th day.** (A year divisible by 4 is a leap year.)

> [!NOTE]
> **Finer corrections:** a year is **slightly less** than 365¼ days, so adding a day every 4 years **over-corrects**. So **century years are NOT leap years** (1700, 1800, 1900) — **unless divisible by 400** (1600, **2000**).
>
> **Tropical year** = spring equinox to spring equinox (Gregorian calendar uses it). **Sidereal year** = time for the **same stars to rise at sunset** again — about **20 minutes longer**. Astronomers use the sidereal year to track Earth's position.

> [!TIP]
> **No leap years at all?** The calendar would slip about **¼ day a year** against the seasons — about **1 day every 4 years**. For **15 August** to shift by **half a year** (from summer into winter), that's ~182 days × 4 ≈ **730 years**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Ancient Indian sky-watchers** found the year was ≈ 365 days **without telescopes** — by patient observation. The Sun **rises a little north of east in summer** and **south of east in winter**; the extremes are the **solstices (~21 June, ~21 December)**.
> - **Uttarayan** — the Sun's apparent **northward** shift, **December → June**.
> - **Dakshinayan** — **southward**, **June → December**.
> - The ***Taittirīya Saṁhitā*** says the Sun moves **south for six months and north for six months**; the ***Surya Siddhanta*** links Uttarayana to the Sun entering **Capricorn (Makar)**.

### Luni-solar calendars

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Count days and months **by the Moon**, but **stay in step with the seasons**: 12 lunar months are **~11 days short**, so every **2–3 years** the gap reaches about a **month** → add an **extra month — ADHIKA MAASA (intercalary month)**. Used in many parts of India.
>
> Month names: **Chaitra, Vaisakha, Jyeshtha, Ashadha, Shravana, Bhadrapada, Ashwin, Kartika, Margashirsha (Agrahayan), Pausha, Magha, Phalguna**.
> - **Amant** calendars: a month **ends on the new Moon**.
> - **Purnimant** calendars: a month **ends on the full Moon**.

> [!TIP]
> **The Moon is slowly moving away and slowing down** → the lunar month gets **longer** → 12 lunar months get **closer to a solar year** → the extra month will be needed **LESS often**.

### The Indian National Calendar

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A **solar** calendar of **365 days**, used officially alongside the Gregorian:
> - Year starts on **22 March** — the day after the **spring equinox** (**21 March** in leap years).
> - Months have **30 or 31 days**; months **2–6 have 31**, the rest 30.
> - **Leap day** goes into **Chaitra**, the first month.
> - Era: **Shaka** (e.g. 22 March 2025 = **1 Chaitra 1947**). Adopted **21 March 1956 (1 Chaitra 1878 Saka)**, on the recommendation of the **Calendar Reform Committee (1952)**; follows the principles of the *Surya Siddhanta*.

> [!NOTE]
> **Meghnad Saha (1893–1956)** — astrophysicist known for the **Saha equation** (temperatures of stars); **chaired the Calendar Reform Committee**. The **Saha Institute of Nuclear Physics, Kolkata** is named after him.

## Festivals and the sky

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why do most Indian festivals fall on different Gregorian dates each year?** They follow the **Moon** (lunar or luni-solar calendars):
> - **Diwali** — **new Moon** of **Kartika**
> - **Holi** — **full Moon** of **Phalguna**
> - **Buddha Purnima** — full Moon of **Vaisakha**
> - **Dussehra** — **10th day of Ashwin**
> - **Eid-ul-Fitr** — on sighting the **crescent** after **Ramazan**
>
> **Luni-solar** festivals (Diwali) shift by **less than a month** — the extra month keeps them near the same season.
> **Purely lunar** festivals (Eid-ul-Fitr) come **~11 days earlier every year**, moving through **all the seasons**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Solar sidereal festivals** — **Makar Sankranti, Pongal, Bihu, Vaisakhi, Poila Baisakh, Puthandu** — fall on **almost the same Gregorian date** every year. Long ago they matched a solstice or equinox, but a slow **wobble of Earth's axis** makes them drift: **Makar Sankranti moves 1 day later every 71 years**.
>
> Festival dates depend on the **lunar phase at sunrise**, which differs between east and west India, so the **Positional Astronomy Centre** publishes the **Rashtriya Panchang** each year to fix national holiday dates.
>
> The Moon inspires **ragas** (Chandrakauns, Chandranandan, Shubhapantuvarali), **dance mudras** (Chandrakala, Ardhachandran), and **Madhubani, Warli, Gond, Saura** art.

## Artificial satellites

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The Moon is Earth's **natural** satellite. **ARTIFICIAL satellites** are **human-made** and launched into orbit. Most orbit about **800 km up**, taking about **100 minutes** per orbit — seen as **fast-moving points of light** just after sunset or before sunrise.
>
> **Uses:** **communication, navigation, weather monitoring, disaster management, scientific research**.

> [!NOTE]
> **ISRO's satellites and missions:**
> - **Cartosat** — high-quality images for **maps, city planning, disasters**; shown on the **Bhuvan** platform
> - **AstroSat** — observes **stars and celestial objects**
> - **Chandrayaan 1, 2, 3** (Moon), **Aditya L1** (Sun), **Mangalyaan** (Mars)
> - student-built satellites: **AzaadiSat, InspireSat-1, Jugnu**
>
> **Vikram Sarabhai (1919–1971)** — **Father of the Indian Space Programme**; the **Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre**, Thiruvananthapuram, is named after him.

> [!WARNING]
> **SPACE JUNK (debris):** dead satellites and rocket parts **crowd space** and can **crash into working satellites**. Small pieces burn up in the atmosphere; **large ones can hit the ground**. Countries are working together to remove it.

> [!NOTE]
> **Tides** also come about **50 minutes later each day** — like moonrise. Tide levels are closely linked to the **Moon's position and phase**.

## Think-it-through questions

1. **True or false?**
   - *We see only the part of the Moon reflecting sunlight towards us.* → **True.**
   - *The Earth's shadow causes phases.* → **False** — it causes **lunar eclipses**.
   - *Calendars are based on repeating astronomical cycles.* → **True.**
   - *The Moon is seen only at night.* → **False** — it's often visible by **day**.
2. **Amol was born on 6 May, a full Moon. Is every birthday a full Moon?** → **No** — 12 lunar cycles take **354 days**, not 365, so the phase on 6 May **changes** each year.
3. **Malini sees the Moon overhead at sunset** → a **half Moon**, lit on the **west side** (towards the Sun) → **waxing** (first quarter).
4. **Ravi: "a crescent rising in the east at sunset"; Kaushalya: "a gibbous Moon in the east in the afternoon."** → **Kaushalya** is right (a waxing gibbous rises in the afternoon). The Moon rising at sunset is the **full Moon** — a crescent is always **near the Sun** in the sky.
5. **Vaishali sees the Moon all night, sunset to sunrise** → **full Moon** (rises at sunset, sets at sunrise).
6. **37 full Moons in 3 years (36 months)** → there are **more full Moons than months**, so **at least one month must have two** (the **pigeonhole** idea).
7. **Day, month, year are based on** → Earth's **rotation**; the Moon's **revolution** (phases); Earth's **revolution** round the Sun (seasons).

## In a nutshell

- **Phases** happen because we see **different amounts of the Moon's sunlit half** as it orbits; a cycle takes ≈ **29.5 days**. **Waxing = Shukla Paksha, waning = Krishna Paksha.**
- The Moon rises **~50 minutes later** each day; it can be seen in **daylight**.
- **Day** (rotation), **month** (Moon's orbit), **year** (Earth's orbit, ≈ 365¼ days).
- **Lunar** calendar (354 days, seasons drift), **solar** calendar (Gregorian, **leap years**), **luni-solar** (adds **Adhika Maasa**). **Indian National Calendar** starts 22 March (Shaka era).
- Festivals follow the Moon, so their dates move. **Artificial satellites** (ISRO) help communication, weather, maps.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** phases aren't the Earth's shadow · the full Moon rises at sunset, the new Moon can't be seen · lunar year = 354 days, solar = 365¼ · 1900 wasn't a leap year, 2000 was · Eid moves ~11 days earlier each year, Diwali stays within a month · solar eclipses only at new Moon, lunar only at full Moon.
