## Probe and ponder

Each chapter in the book opens with **"Probe and ponder"** questions — not for exams, but to get you **wondering**:

- **Why is one side of a puri thinner than the other?**
- Are there more **grains of sand** on all the world's beaches and deserts, or more **stars in our galaxy**?
- Why has nature made such a **vast variety** of leaves and insects?

## Three years, three steps

| Grade | The big idea about science |
|---|---|
| **6** | science **begins with wonder** — simple "Why?" and "How?" questions |
| **7** | science is **always evolving** — each answer opens new questions; ideas change as we learn |
| **8** | science is **INVESTIGATIVE** — ask **focused** questions, **design experiments**, use observations to **improve understanding** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The goal this year: not just to learn new facts, but to learn HOW to FIND new facts.**
> Investigating means: **observe carefully → experiment thoughtfully → explain clearly.**

> [!NOTE]
> **The page numbers have symbols.** Left pages show a **root** — the deep, solid **foundation of knowledge** that keeps us connected to our environment and heritage. Right pages show a **kite** — **curiosity taking flight**. Good science needs **both**: **grounded in careful observation**, yet **free to think creatively**.

## The journey this year

1. **Microorganisms** — an unseen world in a single drop of water: **helpers** (digestion, medicines) and **harmful** ones (infections).
2. **Health** — how **food, exercise, medicines and vaccines** keep us well and fight infection.
3. **Electricity** — its **heating effect** (warmth) and **magnetic effect** (motors, machines).
4. **Forces** — what makes things **speed up, slow down or turn**: why a thrown ball falls back, why brakes stop a car.
5. **Pressure, winds, storms, cyclones** — pressure = how a force is **spread over an area**; a **small pressure difference → a breeze**, a **big one → strong winds, even cyclones**.
6. **Particles** — everything is made of tiny particles: **barely moving in solids**, **moving freely in gases**. This explains **air pressure** and why water **boils at a fixed temperature**.
7. **Elements, compounds, mixtures** — sorting materials: **pure substances**, **elements bonded together**, and **physical combinations** that can be separated.
8. **Solutions** — how **sugar dissolves in tea**.
9. **Light** — reflection from **flat and curved mirrors**, bending through **lenses**: images in a **shiny spoon**, how **glasses** correct sight.
10. **Keeping time with the skies** — the **Moon's phases** (a different part lit each night) gave humans the **first calendars**.
11. **Ecosystems** — every living thing, from tiny insect to whale, **depends on air, water, sunlight and other organisms**.
12. **Our Earth** — **just the right distance** from the Sun for **liquid water**; an **atmosphere** giving **oxygen** and blocking **harmful ultraviolet rays**. Human activity can shift Earth's **temperature** and disrupt **climate** — and **science** must guide our response.

## Investigating like a scientist — the puri puzzle

**Science is everywhere — your kitchen is a lab.** A **puri** or **bhatura** puffs up in hot oil; a **phulka** swells on a flame. **Why? And why is one side thinner?**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Step 1 — Ask a SCIENTIFIC question:** *what things might change the way a puri puffs up?*
>
> **Step 2 — List what you can CHANGE (control):**
> - **thickness** and **size** of the rolled dough
> - **type of flour** (atta, maida…)
> - **temperature** of the oil (boiling hot, hot, not very hot)
> - **how you drop it in** (vertically? sliding at an angle? slowly?)
>
> **Step 3 — Decide what to OBSERVE or MEASURE:**
> - **yes/no answers** — did it puff up?
> - **numbers** — **how many seconds** to puff up?
> - does a **very thick** dough still give a thin side?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Change only ONE thing at a time — keep everything else the SAME.**
>
> To test **oil temperature**, use dough circles of the **same thickness**, dropped the **same way**, and change **only** the oil temperature.
>
> **Why:** if you change two things at once (say, thickness **and** oil temperature) and the puri puffs better, you **can't tell which change caused it**. Keeping the rest fixed makes it a **fair test**.

> [!TIP]
> **Keep notes of everything** — did the oil **splatter, smell or smoke**? Small details often lead to the next question:
> - Do puris puff better from **fresh or stored dough**?
> - What if I **prick a hole** before frying?
>
> **One round of experiments → new questions → the next round.** This is **SYSTEMATIC INVESTIGATION** — how all scientific experiments are done, from the simplest to the most complicated.

> [!NOTE]
> **Even the puffing of a puri isn't completely understood by scientists today!** Everyday things still hold real puzzles waiting to be solved.

## Key words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Investigation** | a planned search for an answer using observation and experiment |
| **Scientific question** | one that can be tested by observing or experimenting |
| **What you change** | the one thing you deliberately vary |
| **What you keep the same** | every other condition, held fixed for a fair test |
| **What you observe/measure** | the result — yes/no, or a number |
| **Fair test** | an experiment where only one thing changes at a time |

## In a nutshell

- Grade 8 science is about **investigating** — finding new facts, not just learning them.
- **Root and kite:** stay grounded in **observation**, let ideas **soar**.
- An investigation: **ask a question → list what you can change → decide what to measure → change ONE thing at a time → record everything → ask the next question.**
- Science is everywhere — even in a **kitchen**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top trap:** changing two things at once — you can't tell which one caused the result. One change per experiment.
