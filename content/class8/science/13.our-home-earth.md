**The concluding chapter.** It gathers everything from the year to answer one question: **why is Earth like no other place we know?**

## Life lives on a very thin skin

**All the mountains, rivers, forests, animals and people exist on just a very thin layer at the surface — the CRUST.**

> [!TIP]
> **If the Earth were the size of an apple, the crust would be as thin as the apple's skin.**
>
> Everything alive, from the tallest mountain to the deepest ocean trench, lives on that skin.

> [!NOTE]
> The mosaic image of India on the chapter's opening page was taken by an **ISRO Earth Observation Satellite**, made by combining nearly **3000 smaller images**. It is a **false colour image** — scientists use different colours to show different kinds of information. Such images let us study plants on land and tiny organisms in the ocean, and detect **ocean temperature, oil spills and wind direction**.

## The planets, and why Venus is the hottest

**Eight planets orbit the Sun in nearly circular orbits:** Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.

- **Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars** — relatively **small and rocky**
- **Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune** — **large, mostly gas**

**All planets get their energy from the Sun, so nearer = hotter and farther = colder.** That holds — **with one striking exception**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Venus is the hottest planet (about 450 °C), even though MERCURY is closer to the Sun.**
>
> **Why:** Venus has a **thick atmosphere almost entirely carbon dioxide**, which **does not let the heat escape**. This is the **GREENHOUSE EFFECT**.

**On Earth too**, gases like carbon dioxide **trap heat by absorbing the radiation the Earth gives off** after being warmed by the Sun.

> [!TIP]
> **The greenhouse effect keeps Earth at just the right temperature.** It is not a bad thing in itself — **without it the Earth would lose heat to space and become too cold.** The danger is having *too much* of it.

> [!WARNING]
> **A plant greenhouse works differently from the atmospheric greenhouse effect.**
>
> - **Atmosphere:** gases **absorb the radiation** the warmed Earth gives off.
> - **Plant greenhouse:** it is simply a **closed space with glass walls** — warmed air can't get out.
>
> Both keep things warm; the **mechanisms are not the same**.

## Four things that make Earth habitable

### 1. Position — the habitable zone

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Earth is at just the right distance from the Sun for water to exist as a LIQUID.**
>
> - **Closer** → too hot, all the water would **evaporate**
> - **Farther** → too cold, all the water would **freeze**
>
> **The range of distances from a star where water stays liquid is the HABITABLE ZONE**, also called the **"Goldilocks zone"** — not too hot, not too cold, just right.

**Liquid water is essential for life to evolve**, as far as we know. (Some microbes survive frozen environments, but that's survival, not origin.)

**Most of Earth's surface is covered with water**, which is why from space it looks blue — the **Blue Planet**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did Mars ever support life?** Mars lies **at the edge** of the habitable zone. Spacecraft and rovers have explored it and **found no proof of life**. But scientists think Mars **may once have had liquid water — perhaps even lakes** — and conditions that could support simple life.
>
> **It also reminds us that science doesn't always have final answers.** As we explore more we may find new clues, or new kinds of life. **Science stays open to change when we learn more.**

### 2. Nearly circular orbit

Most planets, including Earth, orbit in **almost circular** paths. **This keeps sunlight and heat nearly steady all year**, preventing extreme summers and winters in most places.

### 3. Size — just right for an atmosphere

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Earth is the right size to hold on to an atmosphere.**
>
> - **Much smaller** (same density) → **gravity too weak** to hold the gases, which would **escape into space**
> - **Much larger** → **gravity so strong** it might **crush our bones**

**Evidence:** **Mars's atmosphere is 100 times thinner than Earth's**, and **Mercury has no atmosphere at all** — both are smaller than Earth.

**What the atmosphere gives us:**

- **Oxygen** to breathe, needed by almost all life
- **The OZONE LAYER** — some atmospheric oxygen converts to **ozone**, a **three-atom oxygen molecule**, forming a **shield that blocks harmful ultraviolet (UV) rays** which would damage living cells

### 4. The magnetic field

**The Earth behaves like a giant magnet**, believed to arise from **the movement of molten iron in its core**.

**Earth is constantly hit by tiny high-energy particles:**

- **Cosmic rays** — from far across the universe
- **Solar wind** — from the Sun

**These can damage the atmosphere, reduce the ozone layer, and let in more harmful UV.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The magnetic field acts as a protective shield, pushing many of these particles away** — keeping the atmosphere, and hence life, safe.

> [!NOTE]
> **India's Mangalyaan (Mars Orbiter Mission)**, launched by **ISRO in 2013**, carried tools to study Mars's **atmosphere, surface and signs of past water** — helping ask whether Mars was ever suitable for life. It showed the world that **India could do space science with smart, low-cost technology**.

## The four spheres that sustain life

| Sphere | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Atmosphere** | the layer of **gases** around the Earth |
| **Hydrosphere** | all the **water** — ponds, lakes, rivers, springs, seas, oceans, groundwater (about **70% of Earth's surface**) |
| **Geosphere** | the **solid** parts — rocks, soils, minerals |
| **Biosphere** | **all living beings** and the places where they live — land, water and air |

### Air, water and sunlight

```
plants: carbon dioxide (air) + water (soil) + sunlight → FOOD + OXYGEN
animals and plants: oxygen → RESPIRATION
```

**Water's roles:** a **good solvent**; **transports nutrients** from soil to leaves in plants; in animals it **regulates body temperature, aids digestion and ensures hydration**.

**The hydrosphere is home to millions of life forms**, from tiny plankton to giant whales — **many still being discovered**. Fresh water also grows crops and supports people everywhere.

**The water cycle:** water vapour forms **clouds** and brings **rain or snow**, refilling rivers, lakes and underground water. **Rainfall decides which plants and animals can live in a place**, and **moving air shapes weather and rainfall** — which decide farming and water supply.

### Soil, rocks and minerals

**The crust may look hard and lifeless, but it provides almost everything life needs.**

- **Soil** — rich in nutrients like **nitrogen and potassium**, which come from the **slow breakdown of rocks** and the **remains of plants and animals**
- **Minerals** — salt, coal, oil, and metals like iron and copper

**GEODIVERSITY** is the variety of landforms, rocks and soils, along with the processes that shape them. **It creates the unique habitats where different kinds of life can thrive.**

> [!TIP]
> **The non-living parts of nature are not just a background** — they shape the story of life itself.

### Balance

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Life on Earth survives not because of any one thing, but because everything works together in balance.**
>
> Land, air, water and living things **support and affect one another**. Even a small change in one part — **cutting down a forest** — can change **rainfall, soil, air quality and the animals living there**.

## What keeps life from disappearing — reproduction

**If plants and animals didn't reproduce, life would eventually disappear.** Reproduction **maintains the continuity of life**.

### Genes

**Parents pass on instructions telling the offspring how to develop from a single cell.** These are the **genetic material, or genes**, stored **inside every cell**.

> [!TIP]
> **Think of genes as a detailed instruction manual inside each cell.** Some instructions tell the cell how to make blood; others guide bones, muscles or skin. Together they ensure **a calf grows into a cow and a kitten into a cat**.

### Reproduction also allows change

**Small changes creep into the instructions passed down.** Sometimes those changes **help an organism survive better**.

- **Camels** developed **humps to store fat** and survive deserts
- **Some bacteria became resistant to antibiotics** (Chapter 3), helping them persist

**Over many generations, such changes can produce new features — or entirely new kinds of living beings.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **So reproduction does two jobs at once: it keeps each kind CONTINUING, and it lets life ADAPT and change.**

### Asexual reproduction

**A single parent produces new individuals that are EXACT COPIES of itself** (exact in terms of the instructions inside the cell).

**In plants — VEGETATIVE PROPAGATION.** A leaf, stem or root planted in soil grows into a new plant.

**Try it:** a **money plant stem cutting** (in water, so you can watch), the **"eyes" of a sprouted potato**, or a piece of **ginger**. Give them water, air and sunlight, and record how many days roots, stems and leaves take to appear.

**This is how bamboo and sugarcane spread** — which is why you never see their seeds.

> [!NOTE]
> **Microbes and simple animals reproduce asexually too.**
> - **Bacteria and amoeba** divide into **two identical individuals**
> - **Algae** can **regrow from small cut parts**
> - **Hydra** grows **tiny buds** that break off and become new individuals
> - **Planaria**, a flatworm, can **regrow from a fragment of its body** — scientists study it to understand **regeneration** in animals

### Sexual reproduction

**Two parents are involved.** Flowering plants have male and female parts, and even bacteria and yeast have two **"mating types"**.

**GAMETES — the key idea.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **If both parents passed on ALL their genetic material, the child would have double — and it would keep doubling every generation.**
>
> **It doesn't, because each parent makes special reproductive cells called GAMETES that carry only HALF the parent's genetic material.**
>
> When a male and a female gamete join, they form **one new cell with a complete set** — half from each parent.

**Why brothers and sisters look different.** Every gamete carries a **different set of instructions** for eye colour, hair type and so on. **The instructions mix differently every time**, so each child is unique — one may inherit a nose like the mother's, another eyes like the father's.

**In plants:**

```
POLLEN GRAINS (in the anther) = male gametes
OVULES (deep inside the flower) = female gametes
        ↓  pollen carried by wind, insects or animals = POLLINATION
        ↓  gametes combine = FERTILISATION
ZYGOTE → becomes the SEED
the fleshy part around the ovule → becomes the FRUIT
```

**Then birds and animals eat the fruit and drop the seeds far from the parent plant** — which is how a banyan seed can end up sprouting in a crack in a wall after the rains.

**In animals:** gametes are **sperm** (male) and **eggs** (female).

| Group | Where fertilisation happens | Where the embryo develops | How the embryo is fed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fish, frogs** | in **water** — sperm and eggs released into it | in **water** | from the egg's own store |
| **Birds** | **inside** the female's body | **after the egg is laid**, during hatching | from **food packed into the egg**, which must last until hatching |
| **Most mammals** (including humans) | **inside** the female's body | **inside** the mother's body | the **mother's body supplies all food and oxygen** until birth |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why don't dogs lay eggs, or hens give birth to live chicks?**
>
> The two are **different complete strategies for feeding the embryo**, and an animal's whole body is built around one of them.
>
> - **Laying eggs:** the mother must pack **all** the food into the egg in advance; she is free to move but the egg is exposed and vulnerable.
> - **Live birth:** the mother's body **supplies food and oxygen continuously**; the embryo is far safer, but the mother carries the weight and cost.

## Threats to life on Earth

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The three biggest environmental challenges — climate change, biodiversity loss and pollution — are together known as the TRIPLE PLANETARY CRISIS.**

### Climate change

```
burning fossil fuels (coal, oil)
        ↓
releases greenhouse gases — carbon dioxide, methane
        ↓
more heat trapped in the atmosphere = GLOBAL WARMING
```

**Why Earth can't cope.** Normally **trees, plants and ocean plankton absorb carbon dioxide as they grow**. But burning fossil fuels **releases extra carbon that has been locked underground for millions of years** — and **the Earth cannot absorb it fast enough**, so heat builds up.

**Even a small temperature rise can:** melt ice caps · **raise sea levels**, flooding coastal cities · cause **extreme weather** · make many plants and animals **disappear**.

**These long-term changes in temperature, rainfall and weather patterns are CLIMATE CHANGE.**

### Biodiversity loss

**When habitats are destroyed, plants and animals disappear, upsetting ecosystems.**

If grasses vanish, deer and grasshoppers struggle — and **without herbivores, predators like tigers and foxes lose their food too**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Every type of living thing has a role, and losing even a few weakens nature's ability to support life.**

### Pollution

- **Air pollution** from factories, vehicles and burning fuels → **breathing problems, damaged crops, smog and acid rain**
- **Water pollution** from factory, farm and plastic waste → **harms aquatic life and makes water unsafe**
- **Soil pollution** from excess fertilisers and poor waste disposal → **reduces crop yield and spreads harmful substances through the food chain**

> [!WARNING]
> **The margins are narrow.** A little less or more oxygen in the air, a slightly higher or lower Earth temperature, a little less ozone — **any of these could endanger life**.

## What is being done, and what we can do

> [!NOTE]
> **Global agreements:**
> - **Montreal Protocol (1987)** — reduced harmful **chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)**, allowing the **ozone layer to slowly recover**. A genuine success story.
> - **Earth Summit (1992)** — launched international efforts on climate change and biodiversity.
> - **Kyoto Protocol (2005)** and **Paris Agreement (2015)** — committed countries to cut greenhouse gas emissions.
>
> **The Paris Agreement set a goal of limiting global warming to below 1.5 °C — but as of 2025 the world is NOT on track to meet it.**

**Because the four spheres are connected, damage to one affects the others.**

**Protecting the climate:** use **renewable energy** like solar and wind, improve **energy efficiency**, choose **environmentally friendly travel**.

**Preserving biodiversity:** **diverse ecosystems are stronger and more balanced**, and **local communities** can do a great deal by using natural resources sustainably.

**What anyone can do:** **reuse, repair and recycle** clothes and plastic; **save energy and water**; and **learn more, share ideas and encourage others**.

> [!TIP]
> **Sustaining life on Earth needs action at every scale — from local communities to global leaders.** The same scientific principles that ran through this whole book — **observing, measuring, experimenting** — are what let us understand the changes and guide what we do about them.
