## Probe and ponder

- What happens when you add **too much sugar to tea** and it stops dissolving?
- Why do sugar and salt dissolve in **water but not in oil**?
- Why are water bottles **tall cylinders**, not spheres?

## Why does every sip of ORS taste the same?

**ORS** (sugar + salt + water) tastes **the same in every sip** — never salty in one and sweet in the next — because the sugar and salt are **evenly spread** through the water: a **uniform mixture**. But **chalk powder, sand or sawdust** in water are **not** evenly spread — **non-uniform mixtures**.

## Solute, solvent, solution

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SOLUTION = a UNIFORM mixture** — the components can't be seen separately.
> - **SOLUTE** = the substance that **dissolves** (e.g. salt, sugar — the solid).
> - **SOLVENT** = the substance it **dissolves IN** (e.g. water — the liquid).
>
> **SOLUTE + SOLVENT → SOLUTION**
>
> **Two liquids?** The one in the **smaller amount** is the **solute**; the one in the **larger amount** is the **solvent**.
> **A mixture of gases** (like **air**) is also a **solution** — it's uniform.

> [!NOTE]
> **Gulab jamun's chashni** has **lots of sugar** in a **little water** — yet **water is still the solvent** and sugar the solute (a solid dissolving in a liquid: the liquid is the solvent).

## How much can dissolve?

**Activity 9.1:** half a glass of water; add salt **one spoon at a time**, stirring. At first it all dissolves; eventually **some salt stays undissolved** at the bottom.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **UNSATURATED solution** | **more solute CAN still dissolve** at that temperature |
> | **SATURATED solution** | **no more solute can dissolve** at that temperature — extra settles at the bottom |
> | **CONCENTRATION** | **amount of solute in a fixed amount of solution (or solvent)** |
> | **DILUTE / CONCENTRATED** | **less / more** solute — **relative** terms (1 spoon is dilute compared with 2) |
> | **SOLUBILITY** | the **MAXIMUM** amount of solute that dissolves in a fixed amount of solvent (e.g. 100 mL) **at a given temperature** |

> [!TIP]
> **Which is more concentrated: 2 spoons of salt in 100 mL, or 4 spoons in 50 mL?** Compare per 100 mL: 4 spoons in 50 mL = **8 spoons per 100 mL** vs 2 → the **4-in-50 solution is 4 times as concentrated**.

### Temperature and solubility of solids

**Activity 9.2 (demo):** at **20 °C**, add **baking soda** to 50 mL of water till some won't dissolve. **Heat to 50 °C** → the leftover **dissolves**; add more till it won't. **Heat to 70 °C** → it **dissolves again**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **For most solids, SOLUBILITY INCREASES with TEMPERATURE.**
> **Why:** hotter water particles **move faster** and pull more solute particles apart.
> So **a solution saturated at a low temperature becomes UNSATURATED when heated.**
>
> **Too much sugar sitting at the bottom of your tea?** **Warm** the tea (or add more tea).

> [!NOTE]
> **Traditional medicine** in India (Ayurveda, Siddha) uses **water** as the main solvent, and **hydro-alcoholic extracts**, **oils, ghee and milk** as solvents for herbal medicines.
>
> **Asima Chatterjee** used solvents to **extract compounds from medicinal plants**, developing **anti-epileptic and anti-malarial drugs**. She was the **second Indian woman to earn a Doctorate of Science** (after **Janaki Ammal**), the **first woman to win the Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Award** in chemistry, and received the **Padma Bhushan**.

### Gases dissolve too

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Oxygen dissolves in water** — only a **little**, but this **dissolved oxygen sustains all aquatic life**. Gas in water is a **uniform** mixture.
>
> **For GASES it's the OPPOSITE: SOLUBILITY DECREASES as TEMPERATURE RISES.**
> **Cold water holds more oxygen** — good for fish. When water **warms up**, it holds **less oxygen**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Don't mix them up:** heat **helps SOLIDS** dissolve but **drives GASES out**. (A warm fizzy drink goes flat faster.)

## Why do things float or sink?

Rice **husk floats** while **rice sinks** when washed; **oil floats** on water; **sawdust floats**, **sand sinks**. A **wooden stick** and an **iron rod** of the same size — the iron feels **much heavier**. That "heaviness for its size" is **density**.

## Density

**A crowded bus** = high density of people; a nearly empty bus = low. A **dense forest** has trees close together.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **DENSITY = MASS per unit VOLUME**
>
> **Density = Mass ÷ Volume**
>
> - **SI unit: kg/m³.** For liquids and small solids we often use **g/mL** or **g/cm³**.
> - **1 kg/m³ = 1 g/L** (since 1 kg = 1000 g and 1 m³ = 1000 L).
> - **Water ≈ 1 g/mL** at room temperature → **10 mL of water ≈ 10 g**, 100 mL ≈ 100 g.
> - Density **doesn't depend on shape or size** — but **does** depend on **temperature and pressure**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **RELATIVE DENSITY = density of a substance ÷ density of water** (at that temperature) — **a pure number, no units**.
> **Aluminium:** 27 g in 10 cm³ → **2.7 g/cm³** → it's **2.7 times as dense as water**; relative density **2.7**.
>
> **Less dense than water → FLOATS; more dense → SINKS** (density is a big part of the story, though not the only factor — shape matters too, as for boats).

> [!TIP]
> **A 1-litre oil pack weighs only about 910 g.** 1 L of water would be 1000 g → oil's density ≈ **0.91 g/mL**, **less than water** → that's why **oil floats**.

### Measuring mass (Activity 9.3)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Digital balance:**
> 1. Switch on — it should read **zero**; if not, press **TARE / reset**.
> 2. Put a clean **watch glass** on the pan → press **TARE** again (so its mass is ignored).
> 3. Place the object → read its **mass** (e.g. a stone: **16.400 g**).
>
> For a **liquid**, use a beaker instead of the watch glass.

> [!NOTE]
> **Mass (g, kg) ≠ weight (N).** Most balances actually measure **weight** but are **marked in mass units**. (A **two-pan balance** truly compares masses.)

### Measuring volume

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Units:** **1 L = 1 dm³**; **1 mL = 1 cm³ = 1 cc**. SI unit: **m³**.
>
> A **MEASURING CYLINDER** — a tall, narrow, transparent tube marked in mL (5, 10, 25, 50, 100, 250, 500 mL sizes).
> **Smallest reading** = (gap between big marks) ÷ (number of small divisions): 10 mL ÷ 10 = **1 mL** for a 100 mL cylinder. (Typically 0.1 mL for 10–25 mL cylinders, 2 mL for 250 mL, 5 mL for 500 mL.)

> [!TIP]
> **Measuring 70 mL — which cylinder?** A **50 mL** one needs **two steps** (50 + 20) — awkward. A **250 or 500 mL** one does it in one go but is **less accurate** (bigger divisions). → a **100 mL** cylinder is **best**.
>
> **Why are cylinders tall and narrow, not wide like a beaker?** A small volume change makes a **big change in height** in a narrow tube — so you can read it **accurately**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Reading a measuring cylinder:**
> - Water's surface **curves** — the **MENISCUS**.
> - For **water and colourless liquids**, read the **BOTTOM** of the meniscus.
> - For **coloured liquids**, read the **TOP** of the meniscus.
> - Keep your **eye level** with the meniscus. Top up the last bit with a **dropper**.

**Regular solids:** **Volume = length × width × height.** A notebook 25 cm × 18 cm × 2 cm = **900 cm³**.

**Irregular solids — DISPLACEMENT (Activity 9.7):** fill a cylinder to **50 mL**, lower a **stone on a thread** in → level rises to **55 mL**. **Volume of stone = 55 − 50 = 5 mL = 5 cm³** (the water it pushes aside).

> [!TIP]
> **Density of that stone = 16.400 g ÷ 5 cm³ = 3.28 g/cm³** → denser than water → **sinks**.

### Temperature, pressure and density

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Heating → particles spread out → VOLUME goes UP, MASS stays the same → DENSITY goes DOWN.**
> That's why **hot air RISES** (less dense than the cool air round it) — and how **hot-air balloons** fly.
>
> **Pressure:** squeezes **gas** particles closer → **gas density rises**. **Liquids** barely change (nearly incompressible); **solids** even less.

> [!NOTE]
> **Earth's layers** get **denser towards the centre** — crust (lightest) → upper mantle → lower mantle → **outer core (liquid)** → **inner core (solid)** — because **pressure and temperature rise** with depth, packing materials tighter.
>
> **Bamboo rafts** float — bamboo is **light and hollow**. Hollowed **wooden logs** made the first boats.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why does ice float?** Water is **densest at 4 °C**. When it freezes at 0 °C, its particles arrange themselves to **take up MORE space** (it **expands**) — same mass, bigger volume → **lower density** → **ice floats**.
> **Why it matters:** a floating ice layer on lakes **insulates** the water below, which stays liquid and **warm enough for fish** to survive.

> [!TIP]
> **A raw egg sinks in tap water. How do you make it float?** **Dissolve lots of salt** in the water — salt water is **denser**, until it's denser than the egg.
>
> **An unpeeled orange floats; a peeled one sinks.** The **peel has tiny air pockets** — they make the whole orange **less dense** than water. Without the peel, the orange is **denser** than water.

## Worked problems

| Problem | Working | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Stone sculpture: 225 g, 90 cm³ | 225 ÷ 90 | **2.5 g/cm³ → sinks** (> 1) |
| Object: 400 g, 40 cm³ | 400 ÷ 40 | **10 g/cm³** |
| A: 200 g, 40 cm³; B: 240 g, 60 cm³ | A = 5, B = 4 | **A is denser** |
| Clay 120 g, 60 cm³ cube, then flattened | 120 ÷ 60 | **2 g/cm³ — unchanged** (shape doesn't change density) |
| Iron 600 g, density 7.9 g/cm³ | volume = mass ÷ density = 600 ÷ 7.9 | **≈ 76 cm³** |
| 2 L bottle with 500 mL water | 2000 − 500 | **1500 mL more** |

> [!TIP]
> **Rearranging the formula:** **mass = density × volume**; **volume = mass ÷ density**.
>
> **Warming water in a test tube makes it rise up a glass tube** → the water's **volume increased** (same mass) → its **density decreased**.

## Think-it-through questions

1. **True or false — correct the false ones:**
   - *Oxygen is more soluble in hot water.* → **False** — **cold** water.
   - *Sand and water is a solution.* → **False** — it's a **non-uniform mixture**.
   - *Space occupied by an object is its mass.* → **False** — that's **volume**.
   - *An unsaturated solution has more solute than a saturated one.* → **False** — **less**.
   - *The gases of the atmosphere form a uniform mixture.* → **True.**
2. **Most appropriate statement about saturation** → **"No more solute can dissolve in a saturated solution at that temperature."** (Saturated ≠ "can dissolve more"; unsaturated ≠ "maximum"; saturation happens at **any** temperature.)
3. **Oil floats on water** → **water is denser than oil**.

## In a nutshell

- **Solution** = uniform mixture: **solute** dissolves in **solvent**.
- **Unsaturated** (more can dissolve) → **saturated** (no more). **Solubility** = the maximum, at a set temperature.
- Heat **raises** solubility of **solids**, **lowers** it for **gases** (cold water holds more oxygen).
- **Density = mass ÷ volume** (kg/m³, g/cm³); water ≈ 1 g/cm³; **relative density** has no unit.
- Measure mass with a **balance** (tare!), volume with a **measuring cylinder** (bottom of meniscus) or by **displacement**.
- Heating lowers density (hot air rises); **ice floats** (water densest at 4 °C).

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** gases dissolve less in hot water · in sugar syrup water is still the solvent · shape doesn't change density · read the bottom of the meniscus for water · 1 mL = 1 cm³ · relative density has no unit · dilute/concentrated are relative.
