## Probe and ponder

- Can mirrors make images **bigger or smaller**?
- Why do side-view mirrors say ***"Objects in mirror are closer than they appear"***?
- Why do some **reading glasses** have a curved line on them?

## Meena at the science centre

At a science centre, **Meena** looks into a row of **curved mirrors**. In one, her **face looks huge** — and her brother, farther back, appears **upside down**. In another she sees a **tiny version of herself**. But in Class 7, a mirror's image was always the **same size and upright**! A guide explains: these are **spherical mirrors** — curved inwards or outwards.

## Spherical mirrors

**Activity 10.1 — a shiny spoon:**
- **Inner side** (curved **in**) → your face looks **upside down (inverted)**.
- **Outer side** (bulging **out**) → **upright (erect) but smaller**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SPHERICAL MIRRORS** have a reflecting surface shaped like **part of a hollow sphere**.
> - **CONCAVE mirror** — reflecting surface **curves INWARDS** (like the inside of a spoon or a cave).
> - **CONVEX mirror** — reflecting surface **bulges OUTWARDS** (like the back of a spoon).
>
> In diagrams, the **non-reflecting back** is **shaded**.

> [!NOTE]
> Spherical mirrors aren't cut from a glass ball — they're made by **grinding and polishing flat glass** into a curve. A reflective coating (thin **aluminium**) on the **outer** curve makes a **concave** mirror; on the **inner** curve, a **convex** one.
>
> **Telling them apart (Activity 10.2):** lay them face-up and look **from the side** at eye level — the concave one **dips in**, the convex one **bulges up**.

### Images in spherical mirrors (Activity 10.3)

Stand a toy 3–4 cm from a concave and a convex mirror, then slowly move it away.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> | Mirror | Object CLOSE | Object moved FARTHER away |
> |---|---|---|
> | **PLANE** | **erect, same size** | **erect, same size** — always |
> | **CONCAVE** | **erect and ENLARGED** | becomes **INVERTED**; first enlarged, then **smaller and smaller** |
> | **CONVEX** | **erect and DIMINISHED** | **always erect and diminished** (gets slightly smaller still) |
>
> **All three show LATERAL INVERSION** (left and right swapped).
>
> **So the image tells you the mirror:** always same size → **plane**; always small and upright → **convex**; big and upright close up, upside-down farther off → **concave**.

> [!TIP]
> **Walking towards a big concave mirror from far away:** at first you see an **inverted** image that **grows bigger** as you approach; very close, it becomes **erect and magnified**.

### Uses

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **CONCAVE mirrors** (converge light; magnify close up):
> - **reflectors** in **torches** and **vehicle headlights**
> - **dentist's mirror** — an **enlarged view** of teeth when held close
> - main mirror of **reflecting telescopes**
> - **solar cookers and concentrators**
>
> **CONVEX mirrors** (always erect; **wider view**):
> - **side-view (rear-view) mirrors** on vehicles
> - **road-safety mirrors** at sharp bends and junctions
> - **surveillance mirrors** in big shops

> [!TIP]
> **Why "Objects in mirror are closer than they appear"?** A convex side mirror makes everything look **smaller** — and smaller looks **farther away**. The car behind is actually **nearer** than it seems.
>
> **Why use a convex mirror at all, then?** It's **always upright** and shows a **much wider area** of the road behind than a plane mirror. (Assertion and reason both true, reason explains it.)

## The laws of reflection

**Activity 10.4:** pass a torch beam through **one slit of a comb** (the rest covered with black paper) onto a **plane mirror** standing on white paper. Change the angle — the reflected beam moves too. Draw it:

> [!IMPORTANT]
> - **INCIDENT RAY** — the ray falling **on** the mirror.
> - **REFLECTED RAY** — the ray coming **back** from the mirror.
> - **NORMAL** — a line at **90° to the mirror** at the point where the ray hits (the **point of incidence**).
> - **ANGLE OF INCIDENCE (i)** — between the **incident ray and the NORMAL**.
> - **ANGLE OF REFLECTION (r)** — between the **reflected ray and the NORMAL**.
>
> (Rays are drawn as **straight lines with arrows** — light travels in straight lines.)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **LAW 1: ANGLE OF INCIDENCE = ANGLE OF REFLECTION (i = r).**
>
> **LAW 2: The incident ray, the normal (at the point of incidence) and the reflected ray all lie in the SAME PLANE.**
> *(Activity 10.5: let the reflected beam fall on chart paper sticking out over the table edge — **bend** the paper down and the beam **disappears**; flatten it and it's back. The reflected ray stays in the plane of the incident ray and normal.)*
>
> **These laws hold for ALL mirrors — plane, concave and convex.**

> [!WARNING]
> **Angles are measured from the NORMAL, not from the mirror.**
> - A ray along the **normal** has **i = 0° and r = 0°** — it bounces **straight back**. (Not 90°!)
> - **i = 40° from the normal** → the reflected ray makes 40° with the normal, so it makes **90° − 40° = 50° with the mirror**.

## Converging and diverging (Activity 10.6)

Shine **several parallel beams** (many comb slits open) onto each mirror:

> [!IMPORTANT]
> | Mirror | Parallel beams after reflection |
> |---|---|
> | **Plane** | stay **parallel** |
> | **Concave** | come **together — CONVERGE** |
> | **Convex** | spread **apart — DIVERGE** |
>
> **Each ray still obeys i = r** — but because the surface is **curved**, the normal points a **different way at every point**, so the rays end up converging or diverging.

**Activity 10.7 (with an adult):** point a **concave mirror at the Sun** and focus the reflected light on **paper** → a **sharp bright spot** → the paper **smokes and may burn**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Never look at the Sun** or into a mirror reflecting it, and never aim the spot at anyone's face or eyes.

> [!NOTE]
> **SOLAR CONCENTRATORS** use mirrors and lenses to **focus sunlight** — to make **steam for electricity**, for **large-scale cooking**, and in **solar furnaces** hot enough to **melt steel**.
>
> **Bhāskara II's time, 800+ years ago:** astronomers watched the **reflections of stars and planets in shallow bowls of water** through tubes set at the right angles to measure their positions — they seem to have **used the laws of reflection in practice**.

## Lenses

**Activity 10.8:** put a **drop of water** on an oiled clear scale over printed text — the drop's surface **bulges outward**, and the letters under it **look bigger**. The drop is acting as a **simple lens** — like a **magnifying glass**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **LENS = a piece of TRANSPARENT material (glass or plastic) with CURVED surfaces.**
> - **CONVEX lens** — **THICKER in the MIDDLE** than at the edges.
> - **CONCAVE lens** — **THICKER at the EDGES** than in the middle.
>
> **Mirrors REFLECT light; lenses let light PASS THROUGH** — we see **through** a lens, but **in** a mirror.

### What you see through lenses (Activity 10.9)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> | Lens | Object CLOSE | Object FARTHER away |
> |---|---|---|
> | **CONVEX** | **erect and ENLARGED** | becomes **INVERTED**; enlarged at first, then **diminished** |
> | **CONCAVE** | **erect and DIMINISHED** | **always erect and diminished** |
> | flat glass | same size, erect | same size, erect |
>
> **Notice the pattern:** a **convex lens behaves like a concave mirror**; a **concave lens like a convex mirror**.

> [!TIP]
> **A magnifying glass is a CONVEX lens.** Held **close** to text, the letters look **bigger and upright**. Move it **farther** away and the text turns **upside down** and smaller.

### Converging and diverging lenses (Activity 10.10)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Parallel beams through:
> - a **thin flat glass plate** → pass straight through, **unchanged**
> - a **CONVEX lens** → **CONVERGE** → it's a **CONVERGING lens**
> - a **CONCAVE lens** → **DIVERGE** → it's a **DIVERGING lens**
>
> **A convex lens can focus sunlight to burn paper** (Activity 10.11) — just like a concave mirror. (Don't look at the Sun through it!)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Lenses are everywhere:** **eyeglasses**, **cameras** (including phone cameras), **telescopes**, **microscopes** — and your **EYE has a convex lens** that **changes its shape** so you can focus on a book **or** on something far away.

> [!TIP]
> **A pencil behind a glass half-filled with water looks bent, broken or thicker** where seen through the water. The **curved glass of water acts like a lens** — light **bends** as it passes from water and glass into air, so the pencil's image shifts and changes size.

## Think-it-through questions

1. **Light along the normal** → **angle of incidence = 0°** (and reflection 0°) — it bounces straight back.
2. **A tilted mirror, ray along the new normal** → still **i = r = 0°**. **Ray 20° from the normal** → **r = 20°**.
3. **Match:** concave mirror → **reflecting surface curves inwards**; convex mirror → **image always erect and diminished**; convex lens → **object may appear inverted at some distance**; concave lens → **object always appears diminished**.
4. **Identify mirrors from images of a graph sheet:** squares the **same size** → **plane**; **bigger** squares → **concave** (close up); **smaller** squares → **convex**.

## In a nutshell

- **Concave mirror** (curves in): erect and enlarged close up, inverted farther away; **converges** light — torches, headlights, dentists, solar concentrators.
- **Convex mirror** (bulges out): **always erect and diminished**, wide view; **diverges** light — side-view and road-safety mirrors.
- **Laws of reflection:** **i = r**, and incident ray, normal and reflected ray lie in **one plane** — for **all** mirrors.
- **Convex lens** (thick middle): **converging**, magnifier; **concave lens** (thick edges): **diverging**, always erect and diminished.
- Lenses in **glasses, cameras, microscopes, telescopes** and the **eye**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** measure angles from the normal · ray along the normal → i = 0°, not 90° · convex MIRROR diverges but convex LENS converges · magnifying glass = convex lens · side mirrors are convex, headlight reflectors concave · all mirrors show lateral inversion.
