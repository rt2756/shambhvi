## Probe and ponder

- How could **forest loss and changing rainfall** drive **elephants into farms and villages**?
- If you were a **tree in a dense forest**, what relationships would you have with water, sunlight and animals?
- Can **human actions cause natural disasters**?

## Elephants on the move

In **Odisha, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Assam and Chhattisgarh**, elephants often wander into farms and villages. **Why?** When **vegetation is scarce and waterholes dry up**, they come looking for **bananas and sugarcane** — damaging crops and sometimes harming people and animals.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The chain of causes:** changes in **rainfall and temperature** + **cutting trees for roads and buildings** → forests **dry and shrink** → animals **lose their HABITAT** → they move into **human habitats** → **conflict**.
>
> **Wildlife CORRIDORS** — strips of forest **connecting** habitats — let elephants travel **between forests without entering villages**.
>
> **Nature's parts are closely connected** — change one, and others change too.

## Habitats — living and non-living parts

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **HABITAT = the place where an organism lives** — a pond, a forest, a farm, a banyan tree, or even just **tree bark**. It gives **food, oxygen, shelter and space to grow**.
>
> | | Meaning | Pond examples | Forest examples |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **BIOTIC components** | **living** things | fish, frogs, turtles, snakes, dragonflies, snails, ducks, **algae, diatoms, duckweed, lotus** | trees, grass, birds, insects |
> | **ABIOTIC components** | **non-living** things | water, sunlight, air, temperature | soil, air, sunlight, water |

> [!TIP]
> **A fish** gets its **biotic** needs (food — small plants and animals) and **abiotic** needs (**oxygen dissolved in water**) from the pond.
>
> **Sharing one habitat:** a **snake active at night** and a **rodent active by day** live in the same forest but face **different conditions** (cool nights, warm days) — so they **coexist** without clashing.

## Population, community, ecosystem

> [!IMPORTANT]
> | Level | Meaning | Example |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Individual** | one organism | one fish |
> | **POPULATION** | a group of **the SAME kind** of organism in a habitat **at a given time** | all the fish of one kind in the pond |
> | **COMMUNITY** | **different populations** sharing a habitat — all the **biotic** parts together | fish + frogs + plants + microbes in the pond |
> | **ECOSYSTEM** | the **community + the ABIOTIC components**, all **interacting** | the whole pond |
>
> **Individual → population → community → ecosystem** (each bigger than the last). **An ecosystem is NOT part of a community** — it's the other way round.

**Activity 12.2:** mark a **1 m × 1 m** square in the school garden and count each kind of plant or animal in it — e.g. 20 of one plant, 5 of another → their **populations**.

> [!TIP]
> **A habitat with only ONE kind of organism?** They'd all need the **same food, water and space** → fierce **competition** → resources would run out.

> [!NOTE]
> **Pollination:** a flower has a **stalk, sepals (green), petals (coloured)**, and reproductive parts — **carpels (female)** and **stamens (male)**. Stamens release yellow **pollen**; **wind, water, insects, bats and birds** carry it to carpels. **POLLINATION** is needed for **fruits and seeds** to form.

## Does every organism matter? — fish and flowers

**Activity 12.3:** researchers compared **Pond A (with fish)** and **Pond B (no fish)**. Pond A had **more flowering plants producing seeds** around it. Why?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> 1. **Fish eat dragonfly larvae** → **fewer dragonflies** at Pond A.
> 2. Dragonflies eat **bees, flies and butterflies** → with fewer dragonflies, **more pollinators**.
> 3. More pollinators → **more pollination** → **more seeds** in nearby plants (like St John's wort).
>
> **Fish, which never touch the flowers, affect their seeds!** Every organism matters — through **direct and indirect** effects.
> **Overfishing** would upset this whole chain.

## Interactions in an ecosystem

> [!IMPORTANT]
> | Type | Example |
> |---|---|
> | **Biotic ↔ abiotic** | earthworms live in **moist soil**; a fish **lays eggs in water**; microbes in the pond |
> | **Abiotic ↔ abiotic** | **sunlight** raises the day temperature; sunlight **evaporates** water; **wind** makes waves; soil near the pond is **moist** |
> | **Biotic ↔ biotic** | a **frog eats insects**; a **water snake eats fish**; frogs and fish **compete** for insect larvae |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The dependence goes BOTH ways:**
> - **Living on non-living:** plants need **sunlight, CO₂ and water** to make food; **soil** gives nutrients; **air** gives oxygen; **water** for everyone.
> - **Non-living shaped by living:** plants **release oxygen**; **roots hold soil** and prevent erosion; plants **keep soil moist** and **cool the air**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Types of ecosystem:**
> - **AQUATIC** — ponds, rivers, lakes, seas
> - **TERRESTRIAL** — forests, grasslands, deserts, farms, even a single big tree
> - **HUMAN-MADE** — **farms, fish ponds, parks** (need human care)
>
> Ecosystems can be **big or small** and they **overlap and interact** — a river runs through forest, grassland and farmland.

## Who eats whom?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> | Group | Meaning | Examples |
> |---|---|---|
> | **PRODUCERS (AUTOTROPHS)** — *auto* = self, *troph* = food | **make their own food** by **photosynthesis** | **green plants**, trees, grass |
> | **CONSUMERS (HETEROTROPHS)** — *hetero* = other | **depend on other organisms** for food | animals |
> | — **HERBIVORES** | eat **only plants** | **deer, hare**, grasshopper |
> | — **CARNIVORES** | eat **only animals** | **leopard**, tiger, snake, eagle |
> | — **OMNIVORES** | eat **both** | **crow, fox, mouse** |
> | **DECOMPOSERS (SAPROTROPHS)** — *sapro* = rotten | break down **dead matter** | **bacteria, fungi** (mushrooms) |

### Food chains

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **FOOD CHAIN = a simple sequence showing "WHO EATS WHOM"**, arrows pointing **from the eaten to the eater** (the direction energy flows).
>
> - Grass → hare → leopard
> - **Grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → eagle**
> - Millet → mouse → eagle
>
> **Every food chain starts with a PRODUCER** (and ultimately the **Sun**).

### Trophic levels and the pyramid

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **TROPHIC LEVEL** = an organism's **position** in a food chain:
> 1. **Producers** (green plants)
> 2. **Herbivores** (hare, deer)
> 3. **Small carnivores** (frogs)
> 4. **Large carnivores** (tigers, vultures)
>
> **Activity 12.7:** in a crop field, count the **millet plants, mice and eagles** — **lots** of millet, **fewer** mice, **very few** eagles. Stack them with the biggest number at the bottom → a **PYRAMID**.
> **Why?** Each level gets food from the level below, and only a part of that food passes up — so **each level supports fewer** organisms than the one beneath.

### Food webs

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Most animals eat **more than one** thing and are eaten by **more than one** thing (a **mouse** may be eaten by a **snake, owl, fox or hawk**). So food chains **link up** into a network — a **FOOD WEB**.

> [!TIP]
> **In Grass → grasshopper → frog → snake, the frogs disappear:**
> - **grasshoppers INCREASE** (nothing eats them)
> - **snakes DECREASE** (they lose their food)
> - then grass may be **over-eaten** by too many grasshoppers.
>
> **Hares drop due to disease** (in a web with grass, hare, deer, fox, eagle): **grass grows more**; **foxes and eagles** have less food and may **decline** or eat **more deer**.

## Nothing is wasted — decomposers

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **DECOMPOSITION:** **fungi** (like **mushrooms** on dead wood in the rains) and **bacteria** break down **complex substances in dead plants and animals** into **simpler ones**, **returning nutrients to the soil** — which **plants** then use. **Beetles and flies** help break down **dung** (e.g. elephant dung).
>
> **DECOMPOSERS RECYCLE NUTRIENTS — in nature, nothing is wasted.**

> [!TIP]
> **If all decomposers vanished from a forest:** **dead leaves, bodies and dung would pile up**, nutrients would stay **locked inside** them, **soil would lose fertility**, plants would suffer — and so would everything that eats plants.
>
> **An ecosystem with ONLY producers?** Dead plants would never be **broken down** — nutrients wouldn't return to the soil, and growth would eventually stop. Every ecosystem needs **producers, consumers AND decomposers**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Migratory birds** fly thousands of kilometres to India to escape **harsh climates** and find **food**. They **pollinate**, **disperse seeds**, and **eat insect pests** — linking habitats and helping farmers. **Demoiselle Cranes** visit the water body of **Khichan village (Jodhpur district)** every winter.

## One change leads to another

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A cascade:** pollution kills **pond plants** → **less oxygen** in the water → **fish die** → **insects increase** (fewer fish eating them) → insects spread to **farms** → farmers use **pesticides** → **more pollution**.

> [!NOTE]
> **India's frog-leg exports (1980s):** huge harvests of the **Indian bullfrog** (*Hoplobatrachus tigerinus*) → **frogs declined** → **insect pests increased** (frogs eat insects) → farmers used **more pesticides** → harm to **soil, water and human health**. **The Government banned frog-leg exports.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ECOLOGICAL BALANCE:** an ecosystem is balanced when interactions keep **populations and resources stable**. This balance is **dynamic** (always adjusting), and **natural or human-made changes** can upset it.

## Relationships that keep balance

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **COMPETITION** — organisms compete for **food, water, space, sunlight**. This **keeps populations in check**; without it one species could **multiply too much**.
>
> | Relationship | Who gains? | Example |
> |---|---|---|
> | **MUTUALISM** | **both benefit** | **honeybee and flower** — bee gets **nectar**, flower gets **pollinated** |
> | **COMMENSALISM** | **one benefits, other unaffected** | **orchid on a tree** — orchid gets **support**, tree unaffected |
> | **PARASITISM** | **one benefits, other harmed** | **tick on a dog** — tick feeds on **blood**, dog gets **skin irritation** |

> [!NOTE]
> **A. J. T. Johnsingh** — wildlife biologist who pioneered **tracking** animals. His work in **Bandipur National Park (Karnataka)** showed that **tigers and leopards depend on prey** like deer and wild boar — **a healthy prey population is key to predator survival**.

## What ecosystems give us — and the threats

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Ecosystem benefits:** **forests** give fresh **air, fertile soil, food, fibre, timber, medicines**; **aquatic** ecosystems give **water and food**; plus **beauty and recreation**, and **climate regulation**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Sundarbans** — the **world's largest mangrove forest**, where the **Ganga and Brahmaputra** meet (India–Bangladesh).
> - Home to many **endangered** plants and animals.
> - **Slows strong winds and waves** in storms and floods; trees **absorb CO₂** and give oxygen.
> - **UNESCO World Heritage Site (1987).**
> - **Threats:** mangroves cut for **fuelwood and farming**, **illegal hunting**, **over-use**, **industrial waste and sewage**.

> [!TIP]
> **Selvam's village (Cuddalore, Tamil Nadu) suffered less in the 2004 tsunami** — its **mangrove forest** acted as a **natural barrier**, its dense roots and trunks **slowing the waves** before they reached the village.

> [!WARNING]
> **Threats to ecosystems everywhere:** **deforestation**, **over-use of resources**, **invasive species**, **unsustainable land use**, **pollution**, **habitat loss**, **climate change** — damaging forests, rivers, scrublands, **wetlands**, grasslands and coasts.

> [!NOTE]
> **PROTECTED AREAS** — national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, community conserved areas: **Jim Corbett NP** (Uttarakhand), **Manas NP** (Assam), **Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve** (Western Ghats), **Chilika Lake** (Odisha), **Eaglenest WLS** (Arunachal), **Hemis NP** (Leh), **Keibul Lamjao NP** (Manipur), **Pirotan Island Marine NP** (Gujarat).
>
> **Tribal traditions:** the **Nyishi and Mishmi** (Arunachal) treat the **tiger as sacred**; the **Baiga** (Chhattisgarh) worship **Bagesur Dev**, the tiger as **protector of the forest**.

## Farms and ecosystems

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The GREEN REVOLUTION:** after a **food crisis (1950–1965)**, **tractors, machines, synthetic fertilisers and pesticides** boosted production and made India **food secure**.
>
> **But overuse is unsustainable:**
> - **Too much synthetic fertiliser** → kills **friendly soil microbes** and lowers **humus** (organic matter that binds soil) → soil **erodes** and loses fertility.
> - **Pesticides** kill the **natural predators of pests** (like **beetles** that eat pests) → **more pests**; some pests become **resistant**.
> - **Heavy irrigation, repeated ploughing** disturb **earthworms and snails**; **excess groundwater** is pumped out.
> - **MONOCULTURE** (the same crop again and again) → **less biodiversity**, harm to **pollinators**.
>
> **Better:** **organic and natural farming**, compost, **crop rotation**, mixed crops, protecting natural pest predators.

> [!NOTE]
> **Vrikshayurveda** (ancient text) stresses **soil health** — nourishing soil with organic manure like **Kunapa Jala**, a **fermented** liquid fertiliser of animal and plant waste.

> [!TIP]
> **Fewer butterflies in the school garden?** Causes: **pesticide** spraying, **loss of flowering plants** (nectar) and **host plants** (for caterpillars), pollution. **Fix:** plant **native flowering and host plants**, **stop pesticides**, keep a small **water source**.

## In a nutshell

- **Habitat** = biotic + abiotic parts. **Population → community → ecosystem.**
- Interactions: biotic–abiotic, abiotic–abiotic, biotic–biotic; dependence runs both ways.
- **Producers (autotrophs) → consumers (herbivores, carnivores, omnivores) → decomposers (saprotrophs).**
- **Food chain** (who eats whom), **trophic levels** (pyramid), **food web** (linked chains).
- One change **cascades** (frog-leg export, pond pollution). **Competition, mutualism, commensalism, parasitism** keep balance.
- Ecosystems give air, water, food, medicine, protection (**Sundarbans**, mangroves vs tsunami) — protect them; farm **sustainably**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** an ecosystem includes the non-living parts; a community doesn't · arrows go from food to eater · decomposers are bacteria and fungi, not "consumers" · orchid on a tree = commensalism, tick = parasitism · fewer frogs → more grasshoppers, fewer snakes · farmland is a human-made ecosystem.
