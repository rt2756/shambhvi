## Why a new subject?

We've been curious about our surroundings since we were tiny — exploring, poking, asking questions. In the Preparatory Stage that was "The World Around Us". Now, in the **Middle Stage**, that same journey gets a new name: **Science**.

The book is called ***Curiosity*** because curiosity is **the most important thing** you need for science.

## What science is

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Science is a way of thinking, observing and doing things to understand the world we live in and to uncover the secrets of the universe.**
>
> Think of it as a **big adventure**: we **ask questions**, **explore**, and **try to understand how things work**.

- **Science is everywhere** — in tiny **grains of sand** and huge **mountains**, a **blade of grass** and a vast **forest**, the **depths of the ocean** and **outer space**, what's **cooking in the kitchen** and what's happening on the **playground**.
- **Big discoveries often come from unexpected places** — someone noticing something ordinary that nobody had questioned before.
- Questions like *why do stars shine?* or *how does a flower know when to open?* are exactly what science tries to answer.

> [!TIP]
> **Science is like a giant, unending jigsaw puzzle.**
> - Every new discovery **adds a piece**.
> - There's **no limit** — every new piece **raises more questions**.
> - Sometimes we find a piece was put **in the wrong place** and must be **moved**: **new discoveries often change our understanding**.
>
> **Why that last point matters:** a good scientist isn't someone who's never wrong — it's someone willing to **change their mind when the evidence says so**.

## The scientific method — you already use it

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Science is NOT just memorising facts and figures, and not just doing experiments.** It's a **step-by-step process for finding answers to questions**.

**Your pen stops writing.** What do you do?

1. **Observe** — the pen has stopped writing.
2. **Question** — *"Why did my pen stop writing?"*
3. **Guess** a possible answer — *"Maybe the ink is finished."*
4. **Test** the guess — open the pen and look at the refill.
5. **Analyse** the result —
   - Refill **empty** → your guess was **right**. Answer found.
   - Refill **not empty** → your guess was **wrong**. Make a **new guess** (*"maybe the ink has dried up"*) and **test that**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The scientific method: Observe → Question → Guess → Test → Analyse** — and **loop back** if the guess fails.
>
> **Why a wrong guess isn't a failure:** it **rules out one possibility** and points you to the next one. That loop — guess, test, guess again — is exactly how science makes progress.

> [!TIP]
> **What makes a good guess?** It must be **testable** — something you can actually check. "The ink is finished" can be checked by looking. "The pen is sad" can't.

## Everyone can be a scientist

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Scientists** are people who follow the scientific method to solve problems or discover new things. **But anyone who follows the method is working like a scientist.**

Everyday "scientists" in action:

| Person | Observation / question | Possible guesses to test |
|---|---|---|
| **Someone cooking** | Why did the **dal spill out of the cooker**? | too much water? too much heat? |
| **Bicycle repair person** | Why is this **tyre flat**? | where did the air leak out? (dip the tube in water — bubbles show the hole) |
| **Electrician** | Why isn't this **light bulb working**? | is it the **bulb** or the **switch**? (swap the bulb into a working socket to test) |

> [!TIP]
> **Notice the electrician's trick:** change **one thing at a time**. Put the doubtful bulb in a socket you know works. If it lights, the bulb is fine and the problem is the switch. That's a fair test.

## How to be good at science

- **Be curious and observe your surroundings keenly** — this is "the first and foremost thing".
- **Keep asking "how?" and "why?"** — *"To be a wise person, you must be a 'whys' person!"*
- **Remember the world is full of things we don't yet know** — waiting to be explored.
- **You won't find every answer in Grade 6** — and that's fine. This is a journey over the next five years and beyond.
- **Science is rarely done alone.** Scientists all over the world work **together, often in large teams**. If you can't find an answer, **ask your friends** — discovering together is more fun.

## In a nutshell

- **Science** = a way of thinking, observing and doing things to understand the world.
- It's **everywhere** — kitchen, playground, ocean, space.
- It's an **unending jigsaw**: each answer brings new questions, and old pieces may need moving.
- **Scientific method:** observe → question → guess → test → analyse (and loop back).
- **Anyone** who follows the method is working like a scientist.
- **Curiosity + keen observation + teamwork** make good science.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** science isn't memorising facts · a wrong guess is useful, not a failure · a guess must be testable · test one thing at a time.
