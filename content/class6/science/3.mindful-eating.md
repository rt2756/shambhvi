## What is food made of?

At the school's **Traditional Food Festival** ("Eat Healthy, Live Healthy"), nutrition expert **Dr Poshita** says **"Health is the ultimate wealth."** To understand why, look at what food does for us.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **NUTRIENTS** = the components of food that **give energy, support growth, help repair the body, protect it from disease, and keep its functions running**.
>
> **The five major nutrients: carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, minerals.** Food also gives us **dietary fibre** and **water**.

### 1. Carbohydrates — energy

- Miss a meal and you feel **tired and low on energy** — your fuel is running out.
- **Why a marathon runner drinks glucose water:** **glucose gives instant energy**. Glucose is a **carbohydrate**.
- **Sources:** cereals — **wheat, rice, maize, bajra**; vegetables — **potato, sweet potato**; fruits — **banana, pineapple, mango**; **sugarcane**; and **common sugar** is a carbohydrate too.

### 2. Fats — stored energy

- **Why we eat laddoos in winter:** they're made with **besan or wheat flour, ghee, goond (edible gum), nuts and seeds** — rich in **fat**, which gives **energy that keeps us warm**.
- **Sources (plant and animal):** **ghee, butter, oils, curd**; nuts — **groundnut, walnut, almond, cashew, pistachio, coconut**; seeds — **pumpkin, sunflower, sesame**; soya bean.
- **Fat is a source of STORED energy.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Polar bears** build up a thick layer of **fat under their skin**. It's their stored energy for **months of winter sleep (hibernation)**, when they don't eat at all.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Carbohydrates and fats give us energy for everything we do — they're ENERGY-GIVING foods.**

### 3. Proteins — body-building

- **Sportspersons** need extra protein to **build muscles**; **growing children** need the right amount for **growth and development**.
- **Plant sources:** **pulses (tuar dal, moong), beans (kidney beans), peas, gram, soya bean, nuts**, edible **mushrooms** (grow in dark, moist places).
- **Animal sources:** **milk, paneer, eggs, fish, meat**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Proteins help growth and repair — they're BODY-BUILDING foods.**

### 4 & 5. Vitamins and minerals — protection

Two true stories show why we need them:

> [!NOTE]
> **Case 1 — scurvy at sea (1746).** On long voyages, sailors suffered **bleeding, swollen gums**. Scottish physician **James Lind** noticed that sailors who ate **lemons and oranges recovered**. The disease is **scurvy**, caused by lack of **vitamin C** — which citrus fruits supply.
>
> **Case 2 — goitre in the hills (1960s).** Indian scientists found many people in the **Himalayan region and Northern plains** with **swelling at the front of the neck** — **goitre**. The **soil there lacked iodine**, so local food and water did too. The Government had common salt mixed with iodine — **iodised salt** — and the swelling **visibly reduced**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Vitamins and minerals are PROTECTIVE nutrients.** They're needed only in **small amounts**, but they're **essential** to keep us healthy. Lacking one for a long time causes a **deficiency disease**.

| Nutrient | Job | Lack of it causes | Symptoms | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vitamin A** | healthy eyes and skin | **loss of vision** | poor vision, **night blindness**, sometimes full loss of sight | papaya, carrot, mango, milk |
| **Vitamin B1** | healthy heart; helps body functions | **beriberi** | swelling, tingling/burning in feet and hands, trouble breathing | legumes, nuts, whole grains, seeds, milk products |
| **Vitamin C** | helps fight diseases | **scurvy** | **bleeding gums**, slow healing of wounds | **amla, guava**, green chilli, orange, lemon |
| **Vitamin D** | helps the body **absorb calcium** | **rickets** | **soft, bent bones** | **sunlight**, milk, butter, fish, eggs, mushrooms |
| **Calcium** | healthy bones and teeth | bone and tooth decay | weak bones, tooth decay | milk/soya milk, curd, cheese, paneer |
| **Iodine** | helps physical and mental activity | **goitre** | **swelling at the front of the neck** | seaweed, **water chestnut (singhada)**, **iodised salt** |
| **Iron** | an important part of **blood** | **anaemia** | **weakness, shortness of breath** | **green leafy vegetables, beetroot, pomegranate** |

> [!TIP]
> **Why a doctor gave Gourav, with a broken leg, calcium tablets — then vitamin D too:** calcium **rebuilds bone**, and **vitamin D helps the body absorb that calcium**. Without vitamin D, much of the calcium would be wasted.

> [!NOTE]
> **Not all nutrients come from plants or animals.** **Salt** comes from **seawater or rocks**. And **vitamin D** can be **made by our own body in sunlight**.
>
> The **Agariya** community harvest salt from seawater in the **Little Rann of Kutch (Gujarat)**, living **eight months** in the desert's extreme heat. The **Apatani** of Arunachal Pradesh make their own salt, **tapyo**.

### Cooking and washing can lose vitamins

- Cooked vegetables often lose their **bright colour** and **crispness**. **Vitamin C** and some others are **destroyed by high heat** — so it's wise to also eat **fresh fruits and uncooked vegetables**.
- **Washing vegetables after cutting or peeling** washes away some vitamins. **Wash them thoroughly — but BEFORE cutting.**

### Dietary fibre (roughage) and water

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ROUGHAGE (dietary fibre)** gives **no nutrients** — but it's essential: it helps the body **get rid of undigested food** and keeps **stools passing smoothly**.
> **Sources (mainly plants):** green leafy vegetables, fresh fruits, whole grains, pulses, nuts.
>
> **WATER** helps the body **absorb nutrients** and **removes waste** through **sweat and urine**. Drink enough water regularly. (Water also comes from fruits, vegetables, milk, buttermilk, soups.)

> [!TIP]
> **Medu eats biscuits, noodles and white bread, no vegetables — and gets stomach aches and constipation.** He's missing **roughage**. Fix: add **vegetables, fruits, whole grains and pulses**, and drink more water.

> [!NOTE]
> **Eating locally grown, plant-based food** (as far as possible) is good for **your body and for the planet**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Coluthur Gopalan (1918–2019)** started nutrition research in India. He analysed **more than 500 Indian foods**, recommended suitable diets for Indians, and surveyed the nation's nutrition — finding widespread shortages of protein and energy. This led to the **Mid Day Meal Programme (2002)**, now **PM POSHAN**, giving **balanced meals** in government and government-aided schools to millions of children.

## Testing food for nutrients

Some nutrients — **starch, fat, protein** — can be detected with simple tests (others need a well-equipped lab). Try potato, cucumber, bread, boiled rice, boiled gram, crushed peanut, oil, butter, coconut.

| Test | Method | Positive result |
|---|---|---|
| **Starch** (Activity 3.5) | 2–3 drops of **dilute iodine solution** | **blue-black** colour |
| **Fat** (Activity 3.6) | wrap food in **paper** and press; let any water dry | an **oily patch** that light **faintly shines through** |
| **Protein** (Activity 3.7, teacher demo) | paste + water in a test tube; **2 drops copper sulfate**, then **10 drops caustic soda**; shake, wait | **violet** colour |

> [!WARNING]
> **Copper sulfate and caustic soda are harmful.** Don't touch, taste or smell them. If any spills on you, **wash it off with water immediately**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **One food can contain several nutrients.** **Peanuts** test positive for **both protein and fat**.

> [!TIP]
> **Tricky test questions:**
> - **Sugar is a carbohydrate — why doesn't it turn iodine blue-black?** Because iodine tests only for **starch**, and sugar isn't starch. *"All starches are carbohydrates, but not all carbohydrates are starches."*
> - **Iodine spilt on a saree turns blue-black, but not on socks. Why?** The saree was probably **starched** (cotton saris are often stiffened with starch); the socks weren't.
> - **Is this liquid iodine?** Drop it on a slice of **potato** or some **cooked rice** — if it turns blue-black, it's likely iodine.

## A balanced diet

Do you and your grandparents need the same food? No.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Needs vary with age, gender, physical activity, health and lifestyle.**
>
> **A BALANCED DIET** = one with **all the essential nutrients, roughage and water, in the right amounts**, for proper growth and development.

### Junk food

**Activity 3.9** — compare the labels (per 100 g):

| | **Potato wafers** | **Roasted chana** |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | **536 kcal** | 355 kcal |
| Fats | **35.0 g** | 6.26 g |
| Carbohydrates | 53.0 g | 58.58 g |
| **Proteins** | 7.0 g | **18.64 g** |
| **Dietary fibre** | 4.8 g | **16.8 g** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Roasted chana wins**: far **less fat**, more than **double the protein**, and about **3½ times the fibre**.
>
> **JUNK FOODS** have **lots of calories from sugar and fat** but **very little protein, minerals, vitamins and fibre** — e.g. potato wafers, candy bars, carbonated drinks. Eating them often can make a person **obese** and lead to health problems.

> [!TIP]
> **Tasty isn't always healthy, and healthy isn't always tasty** — chips are tasty but junk; bitter gourd is healthy but not everyone's favourite. **Fresh fruit beats fresh juice, and fresh juice beats canned juice**: fresh fruit keeps its **fibre** and vitamins; canned juice often has **added sugar** and loses vitamins.

> [!NOTE]
> **Packaged foods must show nutrition information.** Some foods have extra nutrients added during processing — **fortification** (e.g. **iodised salt**, some **baby foods**). The **Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI)** regulates food quality.

## Millets — nutri-cereals

**Jowar, bajra, ragi and sanwa (barnyard millet)** are **native Indian crops**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Millets** are small grains, part of Indian diets for centuries and popular again because they're:
> - **easy to grow in different climates**
> - rich in **vitamins**, minerals like **iron and calcium**, and **dietary fibre**
>
> That's why they're called **nutri-cereals**. But **millets alone aren't enough** — a balanced diet still needs proteins, fats, fruits and vegetables.

> [!TIP]
> **Odd one out:** *jowar, bajra, ragi, chana* → **chana** (a pulse; the rest are millets). *kidney beans, green gram, soya bean, rice* → **rice** (a cereal; the rest are pulses/legumes rich in protein).

## Food miles — from farm to plate

**The story of a chapati:** a **farmer grows wheat** → **threshing and winnowing** separate the grain → grains are **stored** → **ground into flour and packed** → **transported to a shop** → cooked → **on your plate**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **FOOD MILES** = the **whole distance a food item travels from the producer to the consumer.**
>
> **Why reduce food miles:** it **cuts transport costs and pollution**, **supports local farmers**, and keeps food **fresher and healthier**. Eating **local food** is the easiest way.

> [!WARNING]
> **Don't waste food.** Remember the time and effort of farmers and everyone else who brought it to your plate. **Take only as much as you can eat.**
> *Eat healthy, share, and respect food. Support local producers!*

## In a nutshell

- Traditional food depends on **local crops**, taste, culture and tradition; **cooking practices have changed** over time.
- **Nutrients:** carbohydrates & fats (**energy**), proteins (**body-building**), vitamins & minerals (**protective**); plus **roughage** and **water**.
- **Deficiency diseases:** scurvy (C), rickets (D), beriberi (B1), night blindness (A), goitre (iodine), anaemia (iron).
- **Tests:** iodine → blue-black (starch); oily patch (fat); violet with copper sulfate + caustic soda (protein).
- **Balanced diet:** all nutrients, roughage, water in the right amounts; needs differ person to person.
- **Junk food:** high sugar/fat, low everything else. **Millets** are nutri-cereals.
- **Reduce food miles** and **never waste food**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** sugar doesn't turn iodine blue-black · wash before cutting · vitamin D also comes from sunlight · salt comes from neither plants nor animals · roughage gives no nutrients but is still essential.
