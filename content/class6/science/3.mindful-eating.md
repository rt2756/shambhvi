## Why food varies across India

**The traditional food of a state is usually based on the crops grown there.** India has diverse soils and climates, so different regions grow different crops — and eat accordingly.

| State | Crops grown | Traditional foods |
|---|---|---|
| **Punjab** | maize, wheat, chickpea, pulses | makki di roti, sarson da saag, chhole bhature | 
| **Karnataka** | rice, ragi, urad, coconut | idli, dosa, sambhar, ragi mudde, rasam |
| **Manipur** | rice, bamboo, soya bean | eromba, utti, singju, kangsoi |

Choice of food also depends on **taste preferences, culture and traditions**.

**Cooking (culinary) practices have changed too** — the **chulha** gave way to the **gas stove**, the **sil-batta** to the **electric grinder**. The causes: **technological development, improved transport and better communication**.

## The five nutrients

**Nutrients** = food components that provide energy, support growth, help repair, protect against disease, and maintain bodily functions.

| Nutrient | Job | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbohydrates** | **energy-giving** | wheat, rice, maize, bajra, potato, sweet potato, banana, mango; **sugar and glucose** |
| **Fats** | **stored energy** | ghee, oils, butter, nuts (groundnut, walnut, almond, cashew), seeds (pumpkin, sunflower, sesame) |
| **Proteins** | **body-building** — growth and repair | *Plant:* pulses, beans, peas, nuts, soya, mushrooms · *Animal:* milk, paneer, egg, fish, meat |
| **Vitamins** | **protective** | fruits, vegetables, milk, wholegrains |
| **Minerals** | **protective** | milk, green leafy vegetables, iodised salt |

- **Carbohydrates and fats are the energy-giving foods**; **proteins are the body-building foods**; **vitamins and minerals are the protective nutrients**.
- **Glucose gives instant energy** — which is why a marathon runner drinks glucose water.
- **Laddoos in winter** make sense: besan/wheat flour, **ghee**, nuts and seeds are rich in fat, giving energy that keeps you warm.

> [!NOTE]
> **Polar bears** build up a thick layer of fat under the skin. It's stored energy that carries them through **months of winter sleep (hibernation)** without eating.

## Deficiency diseases

| Nutrient | Deficiency disease | Symptoms | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vitamin A** | loss of vision | poor vision, **night blindness** | papaya, carrot, mango, milk |
| **Vitamin B1** | **beriberi** | swelling, tingling/burning in feet and hands, trouble breathing | legumes, nuts, wholegrains, seeds, milk products |
| **Vitamin C** | **scurvy** | **bleeding gums**, slow healing of wounds | amla, guava, green chilli, orange, lemon |
| **Vitamin D** | **rickets** | soft and bent bones | **sunlight**, milk, butter, fish, eggs |
| **Calcium** | bone and tooth decay | weak bones, tooth decay | milk, curd, cheese, paneer |
| **Iodine** | **goitre** | **swelling at the front of the neck** | seaweed, water chestnut, **iodised salt** |
| **Iron** | **anaemia** | weakness, shortness of breath | green leafy vegetables, beetroot, pomegranate |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Two historical discoveries:**
>
> **Scurvy (1746):** sailors on long voyages suffered bleeding, swollen gums. Scottish physician **James Lind** noticed that those who ate **lemons and oranges recovered** — the citrus supplies the missing **Vitamin C**.
>
> **Goitre (1960s):** neck swelling was common in the Himalayan region and Northern plains. The cause was **iodine missing from the soil**, and so from local food and water. The Government of India began supplementing common salt with iodine — **iodised salt** — and the symptoms visibly reduced.

- **Vitamin D is special:** the body can **make it itself on exposure to sunlight**.
- **Salt** is a nutrient source that comes from neither plants nor animals — it comes from **seawater or rocks**.

## Testing for nutrients

| Test | Method | Positive result |
|---|---|---|
| **Starch** | add 2–3 drops of **dilute iodine solution** | **blue-black** colour |
| **Fat** | wrap in paper and press; let any water dry | **oily patch** that light shines faintly through |
| **Protein** | crush, add water, then **2 drops copper sulfate + 10 drops caustic soda** | **violet** colour |

> [!WARNING]
> Copper sulfate and caustic soda are **harmful chemicals** — don't touch, taste or smell them. If any spills on you, **wash the area with water immediately**.

**One food can contain several nutrients** — **peanuts** test positive for **both protein and fat**.

## Roughage & water

Besides nutrients, the body needs **dietary fibre (roughage)** and **water**.

- **Roughage provides no nutrients**, but helps the body **get rid of undigested food** and ensures **smooth passage of stools**. Sources: green leafy vegetables, fresh fruits, wholegrains, pulses, nuts.
- **Water** helps the body **absorb nutrients** and **removes waste** through sweat and urine.

## Balanced diet

> **A balanced diet has all the essential nutrients, roughage and water in the right amounts for proper growth and development.**

- **Requirements vary** by **age, gender, physical activity, health status and lifestyle** — you and your grandparents don't need the same thing.
- **Cooking destroys some nutrients:** high heat loses **Vitamin C** and others, and **washing cut or peeled** fruit and vegetables loses some too. So include **fruits and uncooked vegetables** — but always **wash produce thoroughly before cutting**.
- **Locally grown, plant-based food** is good for the body **and** for the environment.

### Junk food

**Junk foods are high in calories from sugar and fat, but very low in proteins, minerals, vitamins and dietary fibre** — potato wafers, candy bars, carbonated drinks.

Compare per 100 g:

| | Potato wafers | Roasted chana |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | 536 kcal | 355 kcal |
| Fats | **35.0 g** | 6.26 g |
| Proteins | 7.0 g | **18.64 g** |
| Dietary fibre | 4.8 g | **16.8 g** |

The chana gives **less fat, far more protein and over three times the fibre** — the better choice by every measure that matters. Frequent junk food can make a person **obese** and lead to several health problems.

> [!NOTE]
> **Coluthur Gopalan (1918–2019)** began nutrition research in India, analysing **over 500 Indian foods** for nutritional value and surveying the population's nutritional status. His finding of widespread deficiencies led to the **Mid Day Meal Programme (2002)**, now **PM POSHAN**, providing balanced food in government schools.
