# 11. Nature's treasures

<!-- NCERT Class 6 "Curiosity" Chapter 11 — Nature's Treasures.
     Air, water, sunlight, forests, soil/rocks/minerals and fossil fuels. -->

**Nature's treasures** — air, water, sunlight, forests, soil, rocks and minerals — are needed for our survival and comfort. **Without them, no form of life on Earth is possible.**

## Air

**Air is a mixture of gases** surrounding the Earth.

| Gas | Share |
|---|---|
| **Nitrogen** | **78%** |
| **Oxygen** | **21%** |
| Argon, carbon dioxide and other gases | **1%** |

*(**Percentage** = the number of parts in 100, written **%**.)*

- We breathe in **oxygen**, which the body needs to perform its functions. Hold your breath and you soon feel uncomfortable — the body isn't getting enough.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **We can survive without food or water for a few days, but not without oxygen for even a few minutes.** That single comparison shows how essential it is.

### Wind

**Moving air is called wind** — sometimes fast (a storm), sometimes gentle (a breeze).

- You notice air when leaves rustle, clothes sway on a line, or book pages flutter under a fan.
- Wind makes a **firki (paper pinwheel)** rotate — and a **windmill** works the same way.
- **Windmills** run flour mills, pull water from wells, and **generate electricity**. A **windmill farm** is an area with many windmills generating electricity.

> [!NOTE]
> Leading Indian wind farms: **Muppandal** (Tamil Nadu), **Jaisalmer Wind Park** (Rajasthan), **Brahmanvel** (Maharashtra).

## Water

Used for drinking, cooking, bathing, washing, cleaning, **growing crops** and **industry**.

- **Water covers about two-thirds of the Earth's surface**, but **most of it is in oceans and seas and is saline (salty)** — unfit for domestic, agricultural or industrial use.
- We need **freshwater**, found as **ice sheets and snow**, in **rivers and lakes**, and **underground**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Freshwater is scarcer than it looks.** The water in ice sheets, snow and underground is **difficult to access** — only a **very small fraction**, in ponds, rivers, lakes and wells, is easily available. That's why water must be used carefully.

**Saving water:** turn off taps when not in use · fix leakages · **recycle** water · **harvest** water.

**Pollution:** throwing trash into water bodies, and dumping waste from **homes and industries**, pollutes freshwater sources. **Polluted water is not fit for consumption by living beings.**

### Water harvesting

**Rainwater harvesting** collects and stores rainwater for later use — an **age-old practice in India**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Stepwells** — **Bawadi** in Rajasthan, **Vav** in Gujarat — were built in response to water scarcity. They store **not only rainwater but also water seeping in from nearby lakes, ponds and rivers**, because the trench walls are lined with **stone blocks that allow seepage**.
>
> **World Water Day** is observed on **22 March**.

## Energy from the Sun

> **The Sun is the main source of energy on Earth.**

- **Sunlight helps plants prepare food**; the Sun also gives all living beings **heat and light**.
- Used for **drying** clothes, chillies and raw mangoes, and driving the **water cycle** through evaporation.
- **Solar panels** capture the Sun's energy and **produce electricity**; energy from the Sun is used directly in a **solar cooker** and a **solar water heater**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A cow grazing gets its energy from the Sun — indirectly.** The cow eats grass; the **grass needed sunlight to grow**. So the energy came from the Sun, passed through the plant, into the animal.
>
> **Plants get energy from the Sun and produce food. Animals eat plants and grow. We get food from both.** This whole cycle exists because of the Sun.

## Forests

> **Forests are large areas with dense growth of various types of plants** — herbs, shrubs and trees.

- They are a **natural home for wild animals, birds and insects**, providing them **food and shelter**.
- **In nature, every animal depends on other life forms for survival**, and the **diversity of life forms ensures food for every living being**.
- **Forest cover has been decreasing**, mainly because of human activities such as **large-scale cutting of trees**. It takes **many years to grow a new forest** — so forests must be used responsibly, giving them **time to regenerate**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Van Mahotsav** is a week-long forest festival held across India in **July** — new plants and trees are planted and awareness is raised, to increase the green cover.
>
> The **Chipko movement** began in the **early 1970s in Uttarakhand**. **Local women encircled and hugged trees** to stop them being felled.

**Recycling in nature:** the roots of plants **hold the soil and prevent it being washed away**, while **fallen leaves decay and enrich the soil with nutrients**, which new plants then use to grow.

## Soil, rocks & minerals

**Soil is formed by the disintegration (breaking apart) of rocks by the actions of the Sun, water and living organisms over thousands of years.**

- Soil contains sand, insects, worms, tiny organisms too small to see, and **decayed plants and animals**.
- **Soils from different places have different colours** because they contain different materials. Some suit certain crops; some are good for **making bricks**.
- **Earthworms are natural agents that turn and loosen the soil.**

**Rocks** are used for houses, buildings, temples, roads, dams and table tops — **slate** for roofing, **laterite** as building blocks; **granite, sandstone and marble** are important rocks. Humans have made **hand axes and arrowheads** from rock for thousands of years.

**Rocks are made up of minerals.** Metals such as **aluminium, gold, copper and iron** are extracted from minerals, which are used in aeroplanes, cars, jewellery, cosmetics and electrical/electronic equipment — **a basic mobile phone contains about a dozen minerals** including gold, silver, copper and cobalt.

> [!WARNING]
> **It takes thousands to millions of years to form rocks** — so they must be conserved and used responsibly. The same logic applies to forests and to fossil fuels: **anything that takes ages to form cannot be replaced once used up.**

## Fossil fuels

- **Petrol, diesel and kerosene are obtained from petroleum.** Petrol and diesel are the two most widely used vehicle fuels.
