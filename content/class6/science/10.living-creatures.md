## What makes something living

**No single characteristic is enough** — a car moves but isn't alive; a plant doesn't walk but is. **A living being shows ALL of these:**

| Characteristic | In animals | In plants |
|---|---|---|
| **Movement** | walking, flying, swimming | flowers opening; **Drosera** trapping insects; climbers winding round supports |
| **Nutrition** | eating food | making their own food |
| **Growth** | a child outgrowing old clothes | seedling to full plant |
| **Respiration** | breathing in and out | air in and out through **stomata** |
| **Excretion** | **sweat** (water + salts), urine | **droplets of excess water and minerals** on leaves (grasses, roses) |
| **Response to stimuli** | pulling away from a thorn or a hot cup | **touch-me-not (chhui-mui)** folding leaves; **amla** leaves closing after sunset |
| **Reproduction** | young ones of cats, dogs | seeds, new plants |
| **Death** | eventually dies | eventually dies |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Movement alone doesn't prove life.** A car moves on its own but cannot grow, reproduce, respire or respond — so it is **non-living**. And plants, which don't move from place to place, **are** living because they show every other characteristic (and do show movement of their parts).
>
> **The test is the whole set, not any one item.**

- A **stimulus** is anything or any event that prompts a living being to respond. Stepping on a thorn and touching a hot cup are stimuli.
- **Reproduction** is producing new ones of one's own kind. **It is necessary for the continuity of life.**
- **When a living being cannot show these characteristics despite having all the resources it needs (food, air, water), it is said to be dead.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Drosera** is an **insectivorous** plant — it depends on insects for nutrition. Its saucer-shaped leaves have **sticky-tipped hairs of unequal length**; when an insect enters, the hairs **move inward and trap it**.

## Germination of a seed

**Germination** = when a seed turns into a **sprout**.

**The four-pot experiment**, each pot differing in one condition:

| Pot | Conditions | Air? | Water? | Sunlight? | Germinates? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | sunlight, **no water** | ✅ | ✗ | ✅ | **No** |
| **B** | sunlight, **excess water** (always standing above the soil) | **✗** | ✅ | ✅ | **No** |
| **C** | moist soil, **in the dark** | ✅ | ✅ | ✗ | **Yes** |
| **D** | moist soil, **in sunlight** | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | **Yes** |

> **Germination requires the right amount of WATER and AIR. Sunlight is NOT essential for germination.**

**Why each condition matters:**

- **Water** — it **softens the seed coat** (the seed's outer covering) and lets the tiny **embryo** inside develop into a plant. It also enables the processes needed for growth. **Pot A fails** without it.
- **Air** — seeds use the air in the **spaces between soil particles**, and those spaces also **let roots grow easily**. **Pot B fails** because standing water **fills those spaces and drives the air out** — the seeds drown, despite having plenty of water.
- **Light** — **most seeds do not need light to germinate** (Pot C proves it). But **after germination, sunlight is needed for the seedling's further growth**.

> [!TIP]
> **Pot B is the clever one.** It has *more* water than Pot D and still fails — showing the problem isn't too little water but **too much**, because excess water **excludes the air**.

> [!NOTE]
> There are exceptions: seeds of **Coleus and Petunia require light** to germinate (covering them with soil stops them sprouting), while **Calendula and Zinnia need darkness** and must be well covered.
