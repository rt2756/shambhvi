## Why we need standard units

Five friends measure the same table in **handspans** and get five different answers — because **their handspans are different sizes**.

- A measurement always has **two parts**: a **number** and a **unit**. "13 handspans" = the number 13 and the unit "handspan".
- Body-part units (handspan, foot, fist, finger) **differ from person to person**, so the same length gives different results.

> **We need units for which measurements of the same length made by different people do not differ.**

Countries came together and adopted the **International System of Units (SI units)**.

> [!NOTE]
> India has a rich measurement history: **angula** (finger width), **dhanusa** and **yojana** appear in ancient literature, used for artefacts, architecture and town planning. The **angula** is still used by traditional carpenters and tailors, and **ruled markings that may be scales have been excavated from Harappan Civilisation sites**.

## SI units of length

**The SI unit of length is the metre, symbol `m`.**

`1 km = 1000 m` · `1 m = 100 cm` · `1 cm = 10 mm`

- Use **kilometres** for large lengths (a railway track between cities), **centimetres or millimetres** for small ones (the thickness of a page).
- On a 15 cm scale, the smallest division is **1 mm** — the smallest length you can measure with it. `1 mm = 0.1 cm`.
- The **inch** (`1 inch = 2.54 cm`) is an older unit still used by some people.

> [!WARNING]
> **Writing units correctly:** lowercase (`km, m, cm, mm`), **never** add an "s" for plural, **no full stop** after the symbol, and **leave a space** between the number and the unit — `9.4 cm`, not `9.4cm.`

## Measuring correctly

- **Choose the right instrument:** a 15 cm scale for a pencil; a **metre scale or measuring tape** for a room's height; a **flexible tape** (tailor's tape) for the girth of a tree or your chest — a rigid scale simply can't wrap around them.
- **Place the scale in contact with the object, along its length.**
- **Keep your eye directly above the point** you're reading. Looking from an angle gives a wrong reading.
- **Broken or unclear zero end?** Line the object up with any **full mark** (say 1.0 cm) and **subtract**. Reading 10.4 cm at the other end gives `10.4 − 1.0 = ` **9.4 cm**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Visually challenged students** measure using **scales with raised markings** that can be felt by touch.

### Measuring a curved line

A rigid scale can't follow a curve. Use a **flexible measuring tape**, or lay a **thread** along the curve, then **straighten the thread** and measure it with a metre scale.

## Reference point

Deepa's friends disagree about whether the garden or the school is closer — and **all of them are right**, because each is measuring from **their own house**.

> **When distance is stated with respect to a fixed object or point, that point is called a reference point.**

Measure from the **same** reference point (say the bus stand) and everyone agrees.

- **Kilometre stones** work this way: "Delhi 70 km" then "Delhi 60 km" means Padma's **position** is changing — with **Delhi as the reference point**.

## Motion & rest

> **An object is in MOTION if its position changes with respect to a reference point with time. If its position does not change, it is at REST.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Whether something is moving depends on the reference point you choose.**
>
> Deepa sits in a moving bus. Taking **the bus** as the reference point, the other passengers are **at rest** — their positions don't change. Taking **a building outside** as the reference point, those same passengers are **in motion**.
>
> Both answers are correct. Motion is always **relative to something**.

## Types of motion

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Linear** | moves along a **straight line** | a dropped eraser, a falling orange, a pushed box, a march-past |
| **Circular** | moves along a **circular path** | a merry-go-round, an eraser whirled on a thread |
| **Oscillatory** | moves **to and fro** about a fixed position | a swing, a pendulum, a pressed metal strip springing up and down |

- **Periodic motion** = motion that **repeats its path after a fixed interval of time**.
- **Both circular and oscillatory motion are periodic** — the circular one goes round the same path again and again, the oscillatory one repeats its to-and-fro.
