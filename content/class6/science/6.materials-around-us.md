## Objects & materials

> **Any substance used to create an object is called a material.**

Paper, wood, cloth, glass, metal, plastic, clay — all materials.

- **One object can be made of several materials** — a pen has plastic, metal and ink.
- **One material can make many different objects** — plastic makes bottles, rulers, chairs.
- **We choose a material for an object based on its properties and the purpose** the object must serve.
  - A tumbler must **hold water**, so cloth or paper won't do.
  - Balls for different sports are made of different materials because each sport needs a different **bounce**.

## Classification

> **Arranging objects into groups is called classification.** Objects are classified on the basis of a **common property**.

- Different people may group the same objects differently — by shape, colour, hardness, or material. **All are valid**; the useful question is which property matters for your purpose.
- **Why it's useful:** grouping helps us **study and observe patterns** in the properties of things — which is why a kitchen stores similar utensils together and a grocer keeps spices in one corner and pulses in another.

> [!NOTE]
> The earliest pottery in the Indian subcontinent dates back **7,000–8,000 years** (Lahuradewa in the Ganga plains, Mehrgarh in Baluchistan). From about **4000 BCE** the Sindhu-Sarasvatī (Harappan) tradition developed **wheel-turned pottery**, coloured "slips" and decorative painting — bright red surfaces with black geometric and animal designs. Clay was selected, cleaned, sieved, kneaded, turned on a wheel and **baked in kilns** (baked clay is **terracotta**).

## Properties of materials

### Lustre (appearance)

- **Lustrous** materials have **shiny surfaces** — usually **metals**: iron, copper, zinc, aluminium, gold.
- **Non-lustrous** materials are not shiny — paper, wood, rubber, jute.
- Metals can **lose their lustre** and look dull because of **air and moisture** — which is why lustre often shows best on a **freshly cut surface**.

> [!WARNING]
> **"All that glitters is not gold."** **Not everything shiny is a metal** — some surfaces are made shiny by **polishing or coating** with thin layers of plastic, wax or other materials.

### Hardness

- **Hard** materials are **difficult to compress or scratch**; **soft** materials compress or scratch easily.
- Test by pressing with your hand, or by trying to **scratch** the surface with a metal key.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Hardness is relative, not absolute.** Rubber is **harder than sponge** but **softer than iron**. So "is it hard?" only makes sense as "harder than *what*?"

### Transparency

| Type | You can see | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Transparent** | **clearly** through it | glass, water, air, cellophane paper |
| **Translucent** | objects, but **not clearly** | butter paper, frosted glass |
| **Opaque** | **not at all** | wood, cardboard, metals |

### Solubility in water

- **Soluble** materials **dissolve and disappear** in water — sugar, salt.
- **Insoluble** materials **do not mix** and stay visible however long you stir — chalk powder, sand, sawdust.
- **Liquids too:** some mix completely with water; others **form a separate layer** when left undisturbed (oil).
- **Gases too:** **oxygen dissolves in water**, which is **vital for the survival of animals and plants that live in water**.

**Water can dissolve a large number of materials — which is why it is so important in the working of our body.**

> [!TIP]
> **Making ORS at home:** Oral Rehydration Solution treats dehydration from diarrhoea. Ready-made packets dissolve in **1 litre** of water. Without one, mix **six teaspoons of sugar and half a teaspoon of common salt in one litre of boiled and cooled water**.

### Mass — how heavy

- **Mass** is the property that tells how heavy or light something is: **more mass = heavier**.
- **Mass gives the quantity of matter.** Units: **gram (g)** and **kilogram (kg)**.

### Volume — space occupied

- **The space occupied by a material is its volume.**
- Two identical tumblers with water at different levels hold **different volumes**.
- Units: **litre (L)** and **millilitre (mL)** — the "500 mL" on a bottle is its volume. The **SI unit is the cubic metre (m³)**, and `1 m³ = 1000 L`.

> [!WARNING]
> **Writing units correctly:** `kg` (no space inside, no full stop, **no "s"** — `7 kg`, not `7 kgs`). Litre is a **capital L**, millilitre is **mL** (lowercase m, capital L). Cubic metre is **m³**. Always **leave a space** between the number and the unit.

## What is matter?

> **Anything that occupies space and has mass is called matter.**

- **Mass and volume are the two properties possessed by ALL materials** — not every material is shiny, hard, transparent or soluble, but every single one has mass and takes up space.
- Water, sand, pebbles, a cup — all matter. **Air is matter too**, since it occupies space and has mass.
- **Materials are the types of matter used to make objects.**

> [!NOTE]
> Ancient India classified matter too. **Ayurveda** describes all physical matter using **20 properties (guṇa)** — **ten pairs of opposites** — which are also used to describe living systems, the environment and food (*Aṣhtanga hṛidaya*, Sūtra sthāna 1.18).
