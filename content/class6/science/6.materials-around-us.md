## Ghulan and Sheeta's new class

**Ghulan and Sheeta** come back to school after the summer break. One has a new notebook, the other a new notebook and a pen. Their science teacher, **Madam Vidya**, asks: *how are the things you use every day similar or different? What shape and colour are they? How do they feel? Are some heavier?*

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Every object is made of some MATERIAL** — paper, wood, cloth, glass, metal, plastic, clay, and so on.
> **MATERIAL = any substance used to make an object.**
>
> - **One object can be made of several materials** — a pen: **plastic + metal + ink**.
> - **One material can make many objects** — wood makes tables, doors, pencils.
> - **The same object can be made from different materials** — a tumbler of **glass, steel, copper or plastic**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Old pottery.** The earliest pottery in the Indian subcontinent is **7000–8000 years old** — from **Lahuradewa** (Ganga plains) and **Mehrgarh** (Baluchistan). The **Harappan (Sindhu-Sarasvatī) Civilisation** (2600–1900 BCE) made **wheel-turned**, bright-red pots painted with black designs. Clay was cleaned, sieved, kneaded, shaped on a wheel and **baked in kilns** — baked clay is called **terracotta**.

## Why we choose a material

**Activity 6.3:** what could you make a tumbler from? Glass, steel, copper, plastic, clay — yes. **Cloth or paper — no.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **We choose a material by its PROPERTIES and the PURPOSE of the object.**
>
> **Why not a cloth tumbler?** A tumbler must **hold water**. Cloth lets water soak through — wrong property for the job. **Why not paper cooking pots?** Paper **burns** and goes soggy.

**Activity 6.4 — bouncing balls:** drop a **tennis ball, cricket ball and hand-exercise ball** of the same size from the same height and compare how high they bounce.

> [!TIP]
> **Different sports need different balls** because the game needs a different **bounce, hardness and weight** — a hard, heavy **cricket ball** vs a light, bouncy **tennis ball**. So: **can a cricket ball be used for tennis? No** — the material is wrong for that game.

## Classification

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **CLASSIFICATION = arranging objects into groups on the basis of a common property** (shape, colour, hardness, shine, material…).
>
> **Why bother?** Grouping helps us **find things quickly** and **see patterns** in their properties — the kitchen keeps utensils together, a grocer keeps spices in one corner and pulses in another, a chemist arranges medicines in order.

## Properties of materials

### 1. Appearance — lustrous or not

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **LUSTROUS** materials have a **shiny surface** — usually **metals**: iron, copper, zinc, aluminium, gold.
> **NON-LUSTROUS** materials are **not shiny** — paper, wood, rubber, jute.

> [!WARNING]
> **Metals can lose their lustre.** **Air and moisture** act on the surface and make it **dull** — so the shine is often seen only on a **freshly cut** surface.
>
> **"All that glitters is not gold."** Not every shiny thing is a metal — things can be made shiny by **polishing** or a coat of **plastic or wax**.

### 2. Hard or soft

**Activity 6.5:** press objects with your hands; then try scratching wood, aluminium, stone, iron, candle and chalk with a **metal key**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SOFT** = easily **compressed or scratched** (sponge, eraser, cotton). **HARD** = **difficult** to compress or scratch (stone, iron).
>
> **These properties are RELATIVE** — **rubber is harder than sponge but softer than iron.** "Hard" only means "harder than what you compare it with".

| Object | Hard / soft | Material |
|---|---|---|
| Brick | Hard | Baked clay |
| Pillow | Soft | Cotton / cloth |
| Sweater | Soft | Wool |
| Table | Hard | Wood / metal |

### 3. Can you see through it?

**Hide and seek:** Ghulan hides behind a **wall**, Sheeta behind a **big tree**, Sara behind a **frosted-glass door**. Sheeta's younger brother watches everything through a **glass window**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> | Type | You see… | Examples |
> |---|---|---|
> | **TRANSPARENT** | things **clearly** | glass, **water, air**, cellophane |
> | **TRANSLUCENT** | things, but **not clearly** | **butter paper, frosted glass** |
> | **OPAQUE** | **nothing at all** | wood, cardboard, metals, a wall |
>
> So **Ghulan (wall) and Sheeta (tree)** hid behind **opaque** things; **Sara** behind **translucent** frosted glass (seen only as a blur); the brother looked through **transparent** glass.

> [!TIP]
> **Why are shop and kitchen containers usually transparent?** So you can **see what's inside and how much is left** without opening them.
>
> **Can water be made opaque?** Yes — **mix in something like mud or ink** and you can't see through it any more.

### 4. Soluble or insoluble in water

Ghulan comes in hot and tired; his mother makes **shikanji** — sugar, a pinch of salt and lemon juice in water. The **sugar and salt disappear** as she stirs.

**Activity 6.7:** stir a teaspoon each of **sugar, salt, chalk powder, sand, sawdust** into five tumblers of water.

| Material | Result |
|---|---|
| Sugar | **disappears — soluble** |
| Salt | **disappears — soluble** |
| Chalk powder | stays (makes water milky, settles) — **insoluble** |
| Sand | settles at the bottom — **insoluble** |
| Sawdust | floats — **insoluble** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SOLUBLE** = **disappears (dissolves) completely** when mixed in water.
> **INSOLUBLE** = **does not disappear** even after long stirring.
>
> **Water can dissolve a great many materials** — that's why it's so important in our **body**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Liquids and gases:** some liquids (**vinegar, honey**) mix completely with water; others (**oil**) **form a separate layer**. Some gases dissolve too — **oxygen dissolves in water**, which is how **fish and water plants survive**.

> [!TIP]
> **Make your own ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution)** — for dehydration from diarrhoea: **6 teaspoons of sugar + ½ teaspoon of salt in 1 litre of boiled and cooled water.** It works because the sugar and salt **dissolve completely**.

### 5. Mass — heavy or light

**Activity 6.8:** half-fill three identical cups with **water (A), sand (B), pebbles (C)** and weigh them on a balance.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **MASS** tells how **heavy or light** an object is — **heavier → more mass.**
> **SI unit of mass: kilogram (kg).** Also gram (g). (In everyday talk "weight" is used for mass, because we find it by **weighing**.)

### 6. Volume — space taken up

Madam Vidya tells everyone to keep their **bags on their seats** and sit down — they **can't**, because **the bags occupy the space**. Then two students pour leftover water into **identical tumblers**: one is **half full**, the other **almost full**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **VOLUME = the space occupied by something.** The fuller tumbler holds **more water → more volume**.
> **Units: litre (L) and millilitre (mL)** — the **"500 mL"** on a milk packet or water bottle is the **volume** inside.
> **SI unit of volume: cubic metre (m³).** **1 m³ = 1000 L.**

> [!WARNING]
> **Writing these units:**
> - **kg** — lowercase, **no space** between k and g, no "s": `7 kg`, not `7 kgs`
> - **L** for litre is a **capital** letter; **mL** = small m + capital L, no space
> - always **a space** between the number and unit: `500 mL`, `2 m³`

## What is matter?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Mass and volume are the two properties EVERY material has.**
>
> **MATTER = anything that has MASS and occupies SPACE (volume).**
> - **Mass** = **how much** matter (g, kg).
> - **Volume** = **space** it takes up (L, mL, m³).
>
> Water is matter; sand and pebbles are matter; so is the cup. **Materials are types of matter used to make objects.**

> [!TIP]
> **Is air matter? YES.** You can't see it, but it **takes up space** (a balloon swells when you blow air in) and **has mass**. ("I have mass and volume but you can't see me" → **air**.)

> [!NOTE]
> **Ayurveda's way of classifying.** The *Aṣhṭāṅga Hṛidaya* lists **20 guṇas (properties) — 10 pairs of opposites** — to describe all matter, living things and food, e.g. **guru (heavy) / laghu (light)**, **hima (cold) / uṣhṇa (hot)**, **kaṭhina (hard) / mṛidu (soft)**, **sāndra (solid) / drava (liquid)**, **sthūla (big) / sūkṣhma (small)**. Grouping by properties is an old idea!

## Think-it-through questions

1. **True or false — correct the false ones.**
   - *Wood is translucent; glass is opaque.* → **False** — wood is **opaque**, glass is **transparent**.
   - *Aluminium foil has lustre; an eraser does not.* → **True.**
   - *Sugar dissolves in water; sawdust doesn't.* → **True.**
   - *An apple is matter because it occupies no space and has mass.* → **False** — it **occupies space** and has mass.
2. **Air lets us see each other; a wooden door doesn't. Air is ___, the door is ___.** → **transparent, opaque.**
3. **Material X is rigid and dissolves in water; Y changes shape easily and doesn't dissolve.** → X is **hard and soluble** (e.g. a **sugar cube / rock salt**); Y is **soft and insoluble** (e.g. **clay / dough / sponge**).
4. **Who am I?** lustre → **a metal (aluminium foil)**; easily compressed → **sponge**; hard and soluble → **rock salt / sugar candy**; can't see clearly through me → **butter paper**; have mass and volume but invisible → **air**.
5. **Bins for food waste, broken glass and waste paper?** → choose materials that are **strong, easy to clean** and **won't be cut or soaked** — e.g. **metal or thick plastic** for glass; a washable plastic bin for food waste; a cardboard box or basket for paper.
6. **Chairs:** hard → **iron, wood, stone**; lightweight → **plastic, bamboo**; doesn't feel cold in winter → **wood, bamboo, plastic** (not iron or stone); easy to clean and look new → **plastic, steel**.
7. **Pairs:** soluble — **glucose in water, vinegar in water, honey in water**; insoluble — **mustard oil in water, wheat flour in water**.

## In a nutshell

- Objects are made of **materials**; we choose them by **properties + purpose**.
- **Classification** = grouping by a common property.
- Properties: **lustrous / non-lustrous**, **hard / soft** (relative), **transparent / translucent / opaque**, **soluble / insoluble**, **mass**, **volume**.
- **Matter** = anything with **mass** that **occupies space** — air included.
- Units: mass **kg, g**; volume **L, mL**, SI **m³** (1 m³ = 1000 L).

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** not everything shiny is metal · metals go dull in air and moisture · hard/soft is relative · frosted glass is translucent, not opaque · chalk powder is insoluble · air IS matter · write `7 kg`, `500 mL` (capital L).
