> *Just as winnowing blows the husk away and keeps the grain, the wise keep the good and let go of the bad.* — Kabir

## Malli and Valli's Bharat ki Yatra

**Malli** and his sister **Valli** spend their summer holidays visiting family across India — **Haryana → Ahmedabad → Puducherry → Bhopal → Shillong** — and at every stop they see people **separating** things.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **MIXTURE** = two or more substances mixed together.
>
> **Why separate?**
> 1. to **remove a useless or harmful** part — stones from grain
> 2. to **get a useful** part — butter from curd, salt from seawater
> 3. to **separate two useful** parts — iron nails from sawdust
>
> **Every method works because the parts DIFFER in some property** — size, colour, weight, solubility, magnetism. **Find the difference → pick the method.**

## 1. Handpicking — Haryana

At **Nani's** house, the family pick **small stones and husk** out of grain piled in the courtyard so it's **fit for cooking**. At lunch, Malli picks **whole black peppers** out of his pulao.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **HANDPICKING** = picking out a part **by hand**, based on differences in **size, colour and shape**.
> **Use it when** the part to remove is **small in quantity** and **easy to pick**.

> [!TIP]
> **Why not handpick husk from a big pile of grain?** There's **too much** husk and it's too fine — it would take forever. Handpicking suits only a **few, easily seen** bits.

## 2. Threshing

In the fields, farmers **beat bundles of dried wheat stalks on a wooden log**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THRESHING** = **beating the stalks to separate the grains** from them.
> (Stalks are first **dried in the sun**.) Farmers often **sing folk songs** as they work.

## 3. Winnowing

After threshing, grain is mixed with **heaps of husk**.

**Activity 9.1:** rub **roasted peanuts** between your palms to loosen the skins, then **blow** — the **light skins fly away**, the **heavy peanuts stay**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WINNOWING** = separating **heavier and lighter** parts of a mixture **by wind or blowing air**.
>
> A farmer stands on a **raised platform** and tips grain from a **soop (bamboo tray)** **in the direction of the wind**:
> - **heavy grains** fall **straight down, close by**
> - **light husk** is **blown further away**
>
> **Why it works:** the wind pushes on both, but it can carry the **light** husk much further than the **heavy** grain.

> [!TIP]
> **Valli can't winnow in a closed room** — there's no wind. **Use a fan** or blow air.
>
> **THRESHERS** (machines) do **threshing and winnowing together**.

## 4. Sieving

Before kneading flour for **meethi puri**, Mami **sieves** it to remove **bran**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SIEVING** = separating a **solid–solid** mixture whose particles have **different SIZES**.
> **Small** particles (fine flour) **pass through the holes**; **big** ones (bran, small stones) **stay on the sieve**.
>
> **The holes must be bigger than the small particles but smaller than the big ones** — holes larger than both and nothing is separated.

> [!TIP]
> **At construction sites**, a large sloping sieve separates **pebbles and stones from sand**.

## 5. Evaporation — Ahmedabad

At **Sabarmati Ashram** they learn about the **Namak Satyagraha (Dandi March)**. Where does **namak (salt)** come from? **Seawater**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Getting salt from seawater:** seawater is kept in **shallow pits** in **sunlight and air**. The **water evaporates** completely, leaving a **solid mixture**, which is purified into common salt.
>
> **EVAPORATION** separates a **solid DISSOLVED in a liquid**: the **liquid goes off as vapour**, the **solid is left behind**.

- **Activity 9.2:** drops of salt solution on **black paper** dry to **white salt patches**. (That's also how **white patches** form on dark clothes after summer **sweat** dries.)
- **Activity 9.3:** heat salt solution in a **china dish** until the water boils away — **salt is left**.
- **Sambhar Lake** (Rajasthan) is a source of common salt.
- **Ayurveda** dries **herbs in the shade** to **evaporate** excess water, keeping the useful part.

> [!TIP]
> **Want BOTH the salt and the water back?** Evaporation loses the water to the air. **Cover the boiling solution with a cold lid/plate** — the vapour **condenses** on it and drips off as **pure water**, and salt stays in the dish (evaporation + **condensation**).

## 6. Sedimentation and decantation — Puducherry

**Dada** makes tea. No strainer? **Leave the pan still for a while** — the **tea leaves settle** — then **gently tilt** and pour the tea into a cup.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SEDIMENTATION** = the **heavier, insoluble** part **settling at the bottom** of a liquid.
> **DECANTATION** = **pouring off the liquid** by **tilting** the vessel, leaving the settled part behind.
>
> Also used to **wash rice and pulses**, and to separate **oil from water** (oil forms a separate layer on top).

> [!WARNING]
> **Decantation is NOT complete** — a few tea leaves still get into the cup. For a cleaner separation use **filtration**.

## 7. Filtration

Dada pours the tea **through a strainer** — all the leaves stay in it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **FILTRATION** = separating **insoluble solids from a liquid** by passing it through a **filter** with **pores**.
> - The solid left on the filter = **RESIDUE**.
> - The clear liquid that passes through = **FILTRATE**.
>
> **The key is PORE SIZE** — the pores must be **smaller than the particles** you want to stop.

| Filter | Pores | Stops |
|---|---|---|
| tea strainer | big | tea leaves — **not** mud |
| **cloth** (many layers) | small gaps between threads | some mud — used since **ancient times** |
| **filter paper** | **very fine** | mud — gives **clear water** |
| cotton, sand, charcoal | various | used in **water filters** |

**Activity 9.4:** fold **filter paper** into a **cone** (fold in half, fold again, open one pocket), put it in a **funnel** over a **conical flask**, pour in **muddy water** → **mud stays (residue)**, **clear water drips through (filtrate)**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Tea bags** were first made of **silk** (strong, didn't fall apart in hot water), then **gauze or muslin**, and today mostly **filter paper**.

> [!TIP]
> **A fishing net is a filter** — water drains through the mesh, fish stay. But Otukkam's net also pulls up **plastic bags, broken bottles, wrappers** — and a fish with a **straw** stuck in its mouth. **Stop plastic pollution at the source.**

> [!TIP]
> **Decantation or filtration?** Use **decantation** when the solid is **heavy and settles quickly** (sand, tea leaves) and a little left behind doesn't matter. Use **filtration** when particles are **fine** or must be **completely** removed.

## 8. Churning — Bhopal

At a dhaba, a painting shows a woman **churning curd** with a big **mathni**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **CHURNING** separates **butter from curd** (or **cream from milk**). **Butter is lighter, so it floats to the top**; **buttermilk (chhach)** is left behind.
> A kitchen **mixer/blender** does the same with electricity.

## 9. Magnetic separation — Shillong

A carpenter drops **iron nails into sawdust** and starts handpicking. Malli and Valli hand him a **magnet** — he sweeps it through, and **every nail jumps onto it**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **MAGNETIC SEPARATION** = separating **magnetic** substances (**iron**) from **non-magnetic** ones using a magnet.
> **Recyclers and industries** use **magnets fitted to cranes** to lift **scrap iron** from waste heaps to recycle it.

## Choosing the method — summary

| Method | Property used | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Handpicking** | size, colour, shape (few bits) | stones from pulses |
| **Threshing** | grains stuck on stalks | wheat from stalks |
| **Winnowing** | heavy vs light | husk from grain |
| **Sieving** | particle size | bran from flour, pebbles from sand |
| **Evaporation** | solid dissolved in liquid | salt from seawater |
| **Sedimentation + decantation** | heavy insoluble solid settles / liquids that don't mix | tea leaves, oil & water |
| **Filtration** | insoluble solid vs liquid (pore size) | mud from water |
| **Churning** | lighter butter floats | butter from curd |
| **Magnetic separation** | magnetic vs non-magnetic | iron nails from sawdust |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Mixtures with MORE than two parts need SEVERAL methods, in the right ORDER.**
>
> **Potatoes + salt + sawdust:**
> 1. **Handpick** the potatoes.
> 2. Add **water** — salt **dissolves**, sawdust **floats**/stays insoluble.
> 3. **Filter** → sawdust is the residue.
> 4. **Evaporate** the filtrate → **salt**.

## Think-it-through questions

1. **Handpicking serves the purpose of** → **sorting**.
2. **Churning separates** → **cream from milk** (and butter from curd).
3. **Most essential for filtration** → **pore size**.
4. **True or false — correct the false ones.**
   - *Salt can be separated from salt solution by keeping it in the Sun.* → **True** — the water evaporates.
   - *Handpicking only when one part is small in quantity.* → **True.**
   - *Puffed rice and rice grains — by threshing.* → **False** — **winnowing** (puffed rice is lighter) or **sieving / handpicking**.
   - *Mustard oil and lemon water — by decantation.* → **True** — oil forms a layer on top.
   - *Rice flour and water — by sieving.* → **False** — use **sedimentation + decantation** or **filtration**.
5. **Match:** gram flour + black gram → **sieving**; chalk powder + water → **filtration**; corn + potatoes → **handpicking**; iron powder + sawdust → **magnetic separation**; oil + water → **decantation**.
6. **Nasal hair** → a **filter** — traps dust before air reaches the lungs. **Masks** (cloth) **filter** germs and dust from the air.
7. **Leela's muddy pond water:** let it **settle**, **filter** it through **muslin cloth**, **boil** it ~10 minutes (kills germs), cool and filter again → **fit for drinking**.

## In a nutshell

- Separate to **remove useless/harmful parts** or to **get useful ones**; each method uses a **difference in property**.
- **Handpicking, threshing, winnowing, sieving** — solids.
- **Evaporation** — dissolved solid from liquid. **Sedimentation, decantation, filtration** — insoluble solid from liquid.
- **Churning** — butter from curd. **Magnetic separation** — iron from the rest.
- Complex mixtures → **several methods in sequence**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** threshing = beating stalks; winnowing = wind · decantation is incomplete · filtration can't remove dissolved salt (use evaporation) · residue stays on the filter, filtrate passes through · sieving needs different sizes.
