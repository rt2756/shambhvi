# 9. Methods of separation

<!-- NCERT Class 6 "Curiosity" Chapter 9 — Methods of Separation in Everyday Life.
     Foundation for Class 9's Exploring Mixtures and their Separation. -->

**A mixture is two or more substances mixed together.** Each method below separates a mixture by exploiting a **difference between its components**.

## Choosing the method

| Method | Separates on the basis of | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| **Handpicking** | **size, colour, shape** | the unwanted bits are **few** and easily picked |
| **Threshing** | grain vs stalk | separating **grain from harvested stalks** |
| **Winnowing** | **weight** (heavy vs light) | one component is much **lighter** and can be blown away |
| **Sieving** | **size of particles** | a **solid–solid** mixture whose particles differ in size |
| **Sedimentation & decantation** | **heavier insoluble solid settles** | an insoluble solid in a liquid |
| **Filtration** | **particle size vs pore size** | separating insoluble solids from a liquid **completely** |
| **Evaporation** | **solid dissolved in a liquid** | recovering a **dissolved** solid, e.g. salt from seawater |

## Handpicking

**Picking components out of a mixture by hand** — small stones and husk from wheat or rice, or black peppers out of pulao.

- Works on differences in **size, colour and shape**.
- **Convenient only when the particles to be removed are few** and easy to pick up. Handpicking a mountain of husk would be hopeless.

## Threshing

**Beating harvested stalks to separate the grains from them** — traditionally against a large wooden log.

## Winnowing

> **Separating the heavier and lighter components of a mixture by wind or by blowing air.**

- Threshed grain is dropped from a **soop (bamboo tray)** in the direction of the wind: the **light husk blows away**, the **heavy grains fall straight down**.
- Same idea on a small scale: rub roasted peanuts between your palms, then **blow** — the light skins fly off and the peanuts stay.

> [!WARNING]
> **Winnowing needs moving air.** In a closed room with no breeze, there's nothing to carry the husk away — so it won't work.

> [!NOTE]
> Modern **threshers** do **threshing and winnowing simultaneously**.

## Sieving

**Fine particles pass through the holes; bigger particles stay on top** — flour passes through while bran and small stones remain.

- Used when the components of a **solid–solid mixture have different sizes**.
- **The hole size must be between the two particle sizes.** If the holes are bigger than everything, nothing gets separated.
- Also used at **construction sites** to separate pebbles and stones from sand.

## Sedimentation & decantation

- **Sedimentation** — the **settling down of a heavier insoluble component at the bottom** of a liquid (tea leaves settling in the pot).
- **Decantation** — **removing the liquid by gently tilting/pouring** it off, leaving the settled solid behind.

Used in everyday life for **washing rice and pulses**, and to **separate oil from water** (oil forms a separate layer).

> [!WARNING]
> **Decantation does not separate completely** — a few tea leaves still come through with the poured tea. For a clean separation you need **filtration**.

## Filtration

**Passing the mixture through a filter whose pores are too small for the solid to pass.**

- **Residue** = what stays on the filter. **Filtrate** = the liquid that comes through.
- A **cloth** works as a filter because there are **small pores between the woven threads** — a practice followed since ancient times. **Filter paper** has much finer pores.
- Other filter materials: **cotton, charcoal, sand**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The choice of filter depends on the size of the particles to be removed.** A tea strainer stops tea leaves but lets mud straight through; muddy water needs a **finer** filter such as cloth, and finer still, filter paper.

## Evaporation

**Used to recover a solid that is DISSOLVED in a liquid** — no filter can catch a dissolved solid, because it has broken up completely into the liquid.

**Getting salt from seawater:** seawater (a mixture of salts and other substances dissolved in water) is kept in **shallow pits exposed to sunlight and air**. Over a few days the **water evaporates completely**, leaving the solid mixture behind; common salt is then obtained by further purification.

- Boil a salt solution in a china dish and the water boils away, **leaving the salt behind**.
- The white patches on dark clothes after sweating in summer are the same thing: the **water in sweat evaporates, leaving the salt**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Sambhar Lake** in Rajasthan is one Indian source of common salt.
>
> In **Ayurveda**, roots, leaves, flowers and seeds of medicinal plants are **dried in the shade** — evaporating the excess water while leaving the important part of the medicine behind.
