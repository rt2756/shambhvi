## Biodiversity

**The variety of plants and animals found in a particular region is the biodiversity of that region.**

- **Every member has a role.** Trees give food and shelter to birds and animals; animals spread seeds after eating fruit. **Plants and animals depend on each other.**

## Why we group things

Grouping makes it **easier to understand and study** plants and animals — just as grouping books makes a school bag easier to organise.

- Groupings can be made on **many different bases**: height, hard/soft stem, presence of flowers, what an animal eats, where it lives, how it moves.
- **Different people may group the same things differently** — because they chose a different feature to sort by. Neither is "wrong"; the useful question is *which feature answers your question*.

## Grouping plants

### By stem and height

| Group | Height | Stem | Branches | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tree** | tall | **hard, thick, brown, woody** | start **higher up** the stem | mango |
| **Shrub** | medium | hard, brown, **thinner** than a tree's | branch **close to the ground** | rose |
| **Herb** | short | **soft and green** | — | tomato |
| **Climber** | — | weak — **needs support** to climb | — | money plant |
| **Creeper** | — | weak — **creeps along the ground** | — | pumpkin |

### By leaf venation

**Veins** are the thin lines on a leaf; their pattern is the **venation**.

- **Reticulate venation** — a **net-like** pattern either side of a thick middle vein (hibiscus).
- **Parallel venation** — the veins **run parallel** (banana, grass).

### By root type

- **Taproot** — one **main root** with small side roots off it (mustard, hibiscus).
- **Fibrous roots** — a **bunch of similar-sized thin roots** from the stem's base (grass).

### By seed — and the pattern that ties it all together

Split a soaked seed: each part is a **cotyledon**.

- **Dicot** — seed splits into **two** cotyledons (chickpea).
- **Monocot** — a **single** thin cotyledon (maize).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **These three features travel together:**
>
> **Dicot → reticulate venation → taproot** (chickpea, mustard, hibiscus, sadabahar)
> **Monocot → parallel venation → fibrous roots** (maize, wheat, grass, lemongrass)
>
> **Why this is useful:** you can look at a leaf and predict what its roots and seed look like, without digging the plant up.

## Grouping animals

Animals can be grouped by **how they move** and the **body parts** they use:

| Animal | Movement | Body parts |
|---|---|---|
| Ant | walks | legs |
| Goat | walks, jumps | legs |
| Pigeon | walks, flies | legs and wings |
| Fish | swims | fins |
| Snake | crawls | body |

Also by **what they eat**, **where they live**, size, shape and colour.

## Habitat & adaptation

- **Habitat** = the place where a plant or animal **lives**. It provides **food, water, air, shelter** and everything else needed to survive. Many kinds of plants and animals may **share** one habitat.
- **Adaptations** = the **special features that enable a plant or animal to survive in a particular region**.

**Habitats split into two big groups:**

- **Terrestrial** (on land) — forests, deserts, grasslands, mountains.
- **Aquatic** (in water) — ponds, lakes, rivers, oceans.
- **Amphibians** such as frogs live **both** on land and in water.

### Adaptations worth knowing

| Living thing | Adaptation | What it solves |
|---|---|---|
| **Cactus** (desert) | thick, **fleshy stem** | **stores water** where there is very little |
| **Deodar** (mountains) | **conical** shape, **sloping** branches | lets **snow slide off** |
| **Rhododendron** (windy peaks) | **shorter**, with **smaller leaves** | survives **heavy winds** |
| **Fish** | **streamlined** body, **fins** | moving through water |

**Camels — the same animal, adapted two ways:**

| | Hot desert (Rajasthan) | Cold desert (Ladakh) |
|---|---|---|
| Legs | **long**, with **wide hooves** — walk on sand without sinking | **shorter** — walk easily in mountains |
| Humps | **one** | **two** (they shrink in late winter as the stored food is used) |
| Hair | — | **long hair** from head to neck for the cold |

Camels also **excrete little urine, produce dry dung and do not sweat** — so they **lose very little water** and can go many days without drinking.

> [!TIP]
> **Biodiversity varies from region to region because the conditions vary.** Deserts have little water and swing from very hot days to very cold nights; mountains get heavy snow. Only living things with the right adaptations survive in each — so different places end up with different life.

## Losing habitats

**Damage to a habitat destroys the home, food and resources of everything living there — which causes loss of biodiversity.**

- The **Bengal Tiger**, **Cheetah** and **Great Indian Bustard** have all declined in India through habitat loss caused by human activities.
- **Project Tiger** (1973) was started to protect the Bengal Tiger; the **Cheetah Reintroduction Project** began in 2022.

> [!NOTE]
> **Janaki Ammal (1897–1984)** — botanist who documented and preserved India's plant biodiversity, headed the Botanical Survey of India, and played a key role in the **Save Silent Valley** movement, a 10-year public campaign that stopped a dam across the Kunthipuzha river in Kerala.
>
> **Salim Ali (1896–1987)** — the **"Birdman of India"**, who documented birds, their routes and habitats across India, helped preserve Keoladeo National Park (Bharatpur) and Ranganathittu Bird Sanctuary (Mandya), wrote a 10-book series on the birds of the subcontinent, and received the **Padma Vibhushan** in 1976.
