> *"Trees stand in the Sun and give shade to others. Their fruits are also for others. Likewise, good people bear hardships and bring welfare to others."* — a Sanskrit wise saying (*subhashita*)

## A nature walk

After a night of rain, science teacher **Madam Sulekha** invites two guests for a **nature walk**: **Dr Raghu**, a scientist from a nearby research laboratory, and **Maniram chacha**, an elder from the community who can **mimic bird calls** and identify plants and animals brilliantly.

Their advice for the walk:
- carry a **notebook, pen and water bottle**
- **notice the smells**, and **listen to the sounds**, not just look
- **respect every living thing** — observe **without disturbing**; **don't pluck** leaves or flowers (collect only **fallen** ones)

The students see **grasses, bushes and big trees**; **birds** on branches; **butterflies** going flower to flower; **monkeys** leaping between trees. Dr Raghu points out that **each bird has its own unique chirp** — one small example of **diversity in nature**.

## What the students record

**Plants (Activity 2.1)** — stems, leaves, flowers differ:

| Plant | Stem | Leaves |
|---|---|---|
| **Common grass** | soft and thin | one leaf at a time, **alternately** from different points on the stem |
| **Tulsi** (holy basil) | hard and thin | **pairs of leaves facing opposite ways** |
| **Hibiscus** (gudhal) | hard | — (pinkish-purple / red flowers) |
| **Neem** | hard and thick | — |

So plants vary in **height**, **hard or soft stems**, **leaf shape and arrangement**, and **flower colour, shape and scent**.

**Animals (Activity 2.2)** — where they live, what they eat, how they move:

| Animal | Lives | Eats | Moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crow** | trees | leaves, seeds, insects | flies and walks |
| **Ant** | nest in soil / burrow | — | walks (6 legs) |
| **Cow** | — | grasses, leaves | walks |

Some animals live **on land**, some **on trees**; **fish** live **in water**; **frogs** live **both on land and in water**. They eat many different foods and move in many different ways.

## Biodiversity

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **BIODIVERSITY** = the **variety of plants and animals found in a particular region.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Every member of a region's biodiversity has a role — plants and animals depend on each other.**
> - **Trees** give **food and shelter** to birds and other animals.
> - **Animals spread seeds** after eating fruits — helping new plants grow.

> [!TIP]
> **Activity 2.2:** everyone closes their eyes, thinks of one plant and one animal, and draws them on the board. Count them up — and remember there are **far more** kinds than the whole class could draw. That's biodiversity.

## Why we group living things

**Grouping books and notebooks** makes your school bag easier to organise. Grouping living things does the same for **studying** them.

**Activity 2.3** — groups of students sort picture cards of plants and animals. **Different groups sort the same cards differently!**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why groupings differ:** each group chose a **different feature** to sort by — **height**, **flowers or no flowers**, **hard or soft stem**, **what an animal eats**, **where it lives**, **colour**, **how it moves**.
>
> **None of them is "wrong".** The useful question is: *which feature helps answer my question?*
>
> **Why group at all:** grouping by **similarities and differences** makes plants and animals **easier to understand and study**.

## Grouping plants

### 1. By height and stem (Activity 2.4)

Check: **how tall** (shorter than you / your height / taller)? **Stem colour** (green/brown)? **Bendable or stiff**? **Where do branches start** — close to the ground or high up?

| Group | Height | Stem | Branches start | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tree** | **tall** | **hard, thick, brown, woody** | **higher up**, away from the ground | mango, neem |
| **Shrub** | medium | **several** brown woody stems, hard but **thinner** than a tree's | **close to the ground** | rose, hibiscus, tulsi |
| **Herb** | **short** | **soft, green**, tender | — | tomato, mint, coriander |

**Plants with weak stems:**
- **Climbers** — need **support** to climb (money plant, bottle gourd).
- **Creepers** — **creep along the ground** (pumpkin, watermelon).

> [!TIP]
> **Tree vs shrub — the giveaway is where the branches start.** A shrub is bushy right from the ground; a tree has a clear trunk before branching. So if Raj says "hibiscus is a shrub", Sanjay could ask: *Is its stem hard and woody? Does it branch close to the ground? How tall is it?*

### 2. By leaf venation (Activity 2.5)

The thin lines on a leaf are **veins**; their **pattern** is the **venation**.

| Venation | Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Reticulate** | a **net-like** pattern either side of a thick **middle vein** | hibiscus, tulsi, mango |
| **Parallel** | veins **run side by side** | banana, grass, wheat, maize |

### 3. By roots (Activity 2.6)

Carefully dig out a few small herbs with a **khurpi (trowel)** (wet the soil first so roots don't break), wash the roots — and **replant** the herbs afterwards.

| Root type | What it looks like | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Taproot** | **one main root** with small side roots | mustard, hibiscus |
| **Fibrous roots** | a **bunch of similar-sized thin roots** from the base of the stem | common grass |

> [!TIP]
> **A radish** is a fat **taproot** (we eat the main root). So its leaves will show **reticulate** venation.

### 4. By seeds (Activity 2.8)

Soak **chickpea (chana)** and **maize** seeds for 2–3 days, peel off the seed coat, and look:

- **Chickpea** splits into **two** parts. Each part is a **cotyledon** → **DICOTYLEDON (dicot)**.
- **Maize** has **one** thin cotyledon → **MONOCOTYLEDON (monocot)**.

### The pattern that ties it together

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **These features travel together:**
>
> | | Seed | Leaf venation | Roots | Examples |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | **Dicots** | **two** cotyledons | **reticulate** | **taproot** | chickpea, mustard, hibiscus, sadabahar, kidney beans |
> | **Monocots** | **one** cotyledon | **parallel** | **fibrous** | maize, wheat, grass, lemongrass |
>
> **Why it's useful:** look at a **leaf**, and you can **predict** the roots and seed — without digging the plant up. (Activity 2.7: saplings of lemongrass, marigold, sadabahar… check venation and roots before planting.)

## Grouping animals

**Activity 2.9:** how does each animal move, and with what body parts?

| Animal | Movement | Body parts |
|---|---|---|
| Ant | walks | legs |
| Goat | walks, jumps | legs |
| Pigeon | walks, flies | legs and wings |
| Housefly | walks, flies | legs and wings |
| Fish | swims | **fins** |
| Snake | crawls | whole body |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Animals move in many ways — fly, run, crawl, walk, hop, jump, swim — using different body parts (wings, legs, fins).** Movement is one basis for grouping animals; so are **shape, size, structure, colour, food and habitat**.

> [!TIP]
> **A duck's webbed feet** — skin joining its toes — work like **paddles**, so it can **swim**. A pigeon's separate toes are for **perching and walking**.

## Plants and animals in different surroundings

**Do surroundings shape living things?** (Activity 2.10)

| Desert | Mountains | Ocean | Forest |
|---|---|---|---|
| camel, cactus | deodar, rhododendron | fish, whale | lion |

The plants and animals of one kind of region are **different** from those of another. Why?

### Desert plants — cactus

**Alex** saw **cacti with thick, fleshy stems** in the **Rajasthan** desert.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Deserts have very little water**, and a **hot desert** is **very hot by day and very cold at night**. Plants and animals there must survive both.
>
> **The cactus's fleshy stem stores water** — so it can last through long dry spells.

### Mountain trees — deodar

**Maya** saw **deodar trees** in the **Himalayas of Himachal Pradesh** — **cone-shaped** with **flexible, sloping branches**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why:** cold mountains get **frequent snowfall**. A cone shape and sloping branches let **snow slide off** instead of piling up and **breaking the branches**.

### Camels — one animal, two deserts

| | **Hot desert (Rajasthan)** | **Cold desert (Ladakh)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Legs** | **long**, with **wide hooves** | **shorter** |
| **Why** | wide hooves spread the weight so they **don't sink into sand** | short legs make it easier to walk in **mountainous** land |
| **Humps** | **one** | **two** |
| **Why** | stores food for times of scarcity | food is scarce in winter; the humps **shrink in late winter** as stored food is used |
| **Hair** | — | **long hair from head to neck** against the **cold** |

> [!NOTE]
> **Camels save water too** (Kashi from Rajasthan): they **pass little urine**, their **dung is dry**, and they **don't sweat**. Losing so little water, they can go **many days without drinking**.

### Rhododendrons — one plant, two places

- In the **Shola forests of the Nilgiris**, rhododendrons are **shorter with smaller leaves** — to survive **heavy winds on mountain tops**.
- **Pema** from **Sikkim** has seen **taller** rhododendrons in her mountains.
- **So even one kind of plant can show different features in different regions**, to suit local conditions.

### Ocean life

**Sagar** saw **huge whales and colourful fish** at the **Andaman and Nicobar Islands**. A fish's **streamlined body** helps it **swim** easily; its **fins** help it move.

## Adaptation and habitat

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ADAPTATIONS** = the **special features that enable a plant or animal to survive in a particular region.**
> (The deodar's cone shape and the rhododendron's height are adaptations to the mountains; the camel's hooves and humps to the desert.)
>
> **HABITAT** = the **place where a plant or animal lives**. It gives them **food, water, air, shelter** and everything else they need. **Many kinds of plants and animals can share one habitat.**
> - sea turtle → sea/ocean · camel → hot or cold desert · rhododendron → mountains

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why biodiversity differs between regions:** the **conditions** differ — water, temperature, snow, wind. **Only living things with the right adaptations survive in each place**, so each place ends up with its own set of life.

### Terrestrial, aquatic — and both

| Habitat | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Terrestrial** | **on land** | forests, deserts, grasslands, mountains |
| **Aquatic** | **in water** | ponds, lakes, rivers, oceans |
| **Both** | **amphibians** live in water and on land | **frog** |

> [!TIP]
> **Sorting animals by habitat** (from the book): **aquatic** — dolphin, whale · **terrestrial** — horse, sheep, squirrel, earthworm, pigeon · **both** — frog, crocodile, tortoise (many tortoises and turtles spend time in water and on land).

## When habitats are damaged

What would happen to a **goat with no grass**? A **fish with no water**?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Damaging a habitat destroys the homes, food and other resources of the plants and animals living there — leading to LOSS OF BIODIVERSITY.**

- The **Bengal Tiger**, **Cheetah** and **Great Indian Bustard** have declined in India because of **habitat loss caused by human activities**.
- **Government action:** **Project Tiger (1973)** for the Bengal Tiger; the **Cheetah Reintroduction Project (2022)**; the Great Indian Bustard's habitats declared **Protected Areas** in **Gujarat, Rajasthan and Maharashtra**.
- **Cutting forests** for a growing population's needs harms habitats, soil, rainfall and wildlife. **Planting trees, protecting forests and using resources carefully** help.

> [!TIP]
> **Ask your elders** which plants, birds and insects they used to see often as children but rarely see now. Those changes usually mean a **habitat was damaged**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Sacred groves** are **undisturbed patches of forest**, big or small, found **all over India**. They're **protected by the local community** — no cutting trees, no harming animals. Home to many plants (including **medicinal** ones) and animals, they're a **community-protected treasure of biodiversity**.

## People who protected India's biodiversity

> [!NOTE]
> **Janaki Ammal (1897–1984)** — botanist who documented and preserved India's **plant biodiversity**. As head of the **Botanical Survey of India** she started programmes to record India's plants. She played a key role in the **Save Silent Valley** movement.
>
> **The Save Silent Valley Movement** — a moist **evergreen forest** in **Palakkad, Kerala**, threatened by a **hydroelectric dam** on the **Kunthipuzha river**. For **10 years** ordinary people — many not even living nearby — fought with **awareness programmes, letters to editors, newspaper articles, seminars, petitions and court appeals**. **The forest was saved.**

> [!NOTE]
> **Salim Ali (1896–1987)** — the **"Birdman of India"**. He travelled across India studying **bird diversity**, listing birds and recording their **travel routes and habitats**, and worked to conserve bird-rich regions like **Keoladeo National Park (Bharatpur, Rajasthan)** and **Ranganathittu Bird Sanctuary (Mandya, Karnataka)**. He wrote a landmark **10-book series** on the birds of the Indian subcontinent and received the **Padma Vibhushan (1976)**.

## Think-it-through questions

1. **Wheat vs kidney bean plants — roots and venation?** → **Wheat** (monocot): **fibrous** roots, **parallel** venation. **Kidney bean** (dicot): **taproot**, **reticulate** venation.
2. **A mountain goat vs a goat from the plains.** → Both are goats, but the mountain goat has **thick fur** against cold and **strong legs and hooves** for steep rocky slopes — **adaptations** to its habitat.
3. **Group cow, cockroach, pigeon, bat, tortoise, whale, fish, grasshopper, lizard by a new feature.** → For example: **can fly** (pigeon, bat, cockroach, grasshopper) vs **can't fly** (cow, tortoise, whale, fish, lizard). Many groupings are possible.
4. **Flowchart: plant → has leaves? yes → reticulate venation? yes → A; no → B.** → A could be **hibiscus/mango** (dicot); B could be **grass/wheat/banana** (monocot).

## In a nutshell

- **Biodiversity** = the variety of plants and animals in a region; its members depend on each other.
- **Grouping** by similarities and differences makes study easier; groupings can use **different features**.
- Plants: **trees, shrubs, herbs** (+ climbers, creepers); **reticulate/parallel** venation; **taproot/fibrous** roots; **dicot/monocot** seeds.
- **Dicot = reticulate + taproot · Monocot = parallel + fibrous.**
- Animals can be grouped by **movement** and body parts used.
- **Adaptations** help living things survive; **habitat** = where they live — **terrestrial, aquatic**, or both (**amphibians**).
- **Habitat damage → loss of biodiversity.** Project Tiger, cheetah reintroduction, protected areas, sacred groves help.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** different groupings can all be right · shrubs branch near the ground · dicot ↔ reticulate ↔ taproot · camel humps store food, not water · adaptations suit one region — the same plant can differ between regions.
