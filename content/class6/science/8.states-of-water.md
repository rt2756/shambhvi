## Ice and water are the same substance

Ice feels hard and can be held; water flows and splashes — yet **leave an ice cube on a table and it turns into water**.

> **Ice, water and water vapour are three STATES of the same substance.**

## The three states

| | **Ice** (solid) | **Water** (liquid) | **Water vapour** (gas) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | keeps its **own** shape | takes the **shape of the container** | **no fixed shape** |
| **Flows?** | **No** | **Yes** | Yes |
| **Spreads?** | **No** | Yes, over a surface | **Spreads out into the entire available space** |
| **Volume** | fixed | **fixed** | fills whatever it's in |

- **Water vapour exists even at room temperature** — it is **invisible**, and present in the air around us.
- Other substances have three states too: **wax, oil, ghee**. Solids: stones, wood, glass. Liquids: milk, oil. Gases: oxygen, carbon dioxide.
- **Why you can smell food from outside the kitchen:** the smell **spreads through the air** — gases spread into all the space available.

## Evaporation

> **The conversion of water into its vapour state is called evaporation.**

It happens **continuously, even at room temperature**.

**Everyday examples:** puddles disappearing, wet utensils drying, clothes drying, a mopped floor drying, sweat drying on your body, hand sanitiser disappearing as you rub it.

**The steel-plate test:** put a spoonful of water on a steel plate. It disappears — but **nothing seeps through** to the other side. So the water didn't leak away; it **became water vapour**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Water vapour is invisible.** What you see rising from a hot pan as "steam" is water vapour **some of which has already condensed into tiny water droplets** — the droplets are what make it visible.

### What makes evaporation faster

| Increase this | Effect | Everyday evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Exposed surface area** | **faster** | water in a flat plate dries sooner than the same amount in a bottle cap |
| **Temperature / sunlight** | **faster** | clothes dry faster on a hot sunny day; a cap in sunlight dries before one in shade |
| **Movement of air (wind)** | **faster** | clothes dry faster on a windy day |
| **Humidity** (water vapour already in the air) | **SLOWER** | clothes dry slowly on a rainy day |

- **Humidity** = the amount of water vapour in the air. **Rainy days are more humid**, so evaporation is slower.

## Condensation

> **The conversion of water vapour into its liquid state is called condensation.**

**It happens when water vapour in the air touches a cold surface.**

**Examples:** droplets on the outside of a glass of iced water · **dew drops** on plants in the morning · water drops on the inside of a plate covering a boiling pan.

**Proving the droplets come from the air, not from leaking:** put the glass of iced water, covered, on a **digital weighing balance** and watch for 30 minutes. **The mass increases**, and the water level inside doesn't drop. So the water on the outside was **gained from the air** by condensation — it cannot have come from inside.

> [!TIP]
> That's a nicely designed experiment: if water were **seeping out**, the total mass would stay the **same** (it just moved). Mass **going up** proves water arrived from **outside** the system.

> [!NOTE]
> **Atmospheric Water Generator (AWG)** machines produce drinkable water by **cooling humid air** so its vapour condenses — exactly the glass-of-iced-water effect, put to work.

## Melting & freezing

- **Melting** — solid → liquid (**supply heat**). Ice melts to water; wax and coconut oil melt too.
- **Freezing** — liquid → solid (**cool it**). Water in a freezer becomes ice; coconut oil solidifies in winter.

**The full set of changes:**

- **solid → liquid: melting** · **liquid → solid: freezing**
- **liquid → gas: evaporation** · **gas → liquid: condensation**

**Water and other substances change their states on heating or cooling.**

## Cooling effect

**Evaporation causes cooling.**

**Why water in a matka (earthen pot) stays cold:** water **seeps through the porous surface** of the pot and **evaporates** — and evaporation takes heat away, cooling the water left behind. A stainless steel pot isn't porous, so this can't happen.

**Other examples:**
- **Sprinkling water on a floor or roof** in summer cools it.
- **Sitting under a fan feels cool** — the moving air makes **sweat evaporate faster**, and that evaporation cools you.
- **Sanitiser feels cold** on your hands as it evaporates.
