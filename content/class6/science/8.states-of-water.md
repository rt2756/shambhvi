> *"If it does not rain well, even the mighty ocean will be drained."* — Thirukkural

## Ice and water — same or different?

On a hot afternoon, **Aavi and Thirav** drink **shikanji** with ice. Thirav says ice and water must be **different substances** — ice is hard and can be held; water can't. Aavi says they're **the same**. Thirav adds: water turns to ice in the freezer, but *maybe something gets added in the freezer*.

**Activity 8.1:** leave an **ice cube in a cup** on the table. It **turns into water** — nothing added, nothing taken away.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Ice and water are two forms — two STATES — of the SAME substance.**
> They **behave** differently (water flows and splashes; ice doesn't), but they are the same thing.

## Water's disappearing act — evaporation

After a rainy morning there are **puddles** in the playground; by evening **some of the water has gone**. Thirav thinks the **soil soaked it up**. But water also vanishes from **washed utensils** — can it seep through steel?

**Activity 8.2:** put a tablespoon of water on a **steel plate**. Check underneath at intervals until it's all gone. **It never seeps through** — yet it disappears.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The water has turned into an invisible GAS — WATER VAPOUR** (the gaseous state of water).
>
> **EVAPORATION = conversion of water into its vapour state.**
> It happens **all the time, even at room temperature** — not only when water boils.

Examples: **wet clothes drying**, a **mopped floor drying**, **sweat** drying, water **sprinkled on a hot dosa pan** vanishing, **hand sanitiser** disappearing as you rub it.

> [!NOTE]
> **Water vapour is invisible.** The white "**steam**" you see over a hot pan or kettle is really **tiny droplets of water** formed as some vapour cools — that's what makes it visible.

> [!TIP]
> **So where did the puddle go?** **Both** ways — some **seeped into the ground**, and some **evaporated**.

## Another mystery — condensation

**Activity 8.3:** cold water + ice cubes in a glass tumbler. After 5 minutes: **tiny water droplets appear on the OUTSIDE** of the glass. They join into bigger drops, and the glass feels cold and wet.

Where do they come from? Aavi and Thirav argue it through:
- *"Water seeped out through the glass."* → But the **water level inside didn't fall**.
- *"Maybe the drop is too small to notice."* → Use a **tall, narrow** container — a tiny fall would show.
- *"Test with room-temperature water"* — does that sweat too?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **CONDENSATION = conversion of water vapour into its liquid state.**
> **Water vapour in the air touches a COLD surface → cools → turns back into water droplets.**
>
> The same thing makes **dew drops on plants** (more in the morning, after the cool night) and the **drops under a lid** on a pot of boiling water.

### Proving it (Activity 8.4)

Put the covered tumbler of ice water on a **digital balance** and read it every 5 minutes for 30 minutes. **Mark the water level.**

- The **mass goes UP**; the **level inside does NOT go down**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Why this proves condensation:** if water were **seeping out**, nothing would be **added** — the mass couldn't increase, and the level would fall. **Extra mass from outside** can only be **water vapour from the air** condensing on the cold glass.
>
> **Fair test:** repeat with a glass of **room-temperature water** — **no** droplets, **no** gain. Only the **cold** surface makes vapour condense.

> [!NOTE]
> **HUMIDITY** = the **amount of water vapour in the air** — reported daily in newspapers. **Atmospheric Water Generators (AWG)** make drinking water by **cooling humid air** so its vapour condenses — the same as your sweating tumbler!

## The three states of water

| Property | **Ice** (solid) | **Water** (liquid) | **Water vapour** (gas) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | **fixed** — keeps its shape in any container | **none** — takes the container's shape | **none** |
| **Volume** | fixed | **fixed** (stays constant) | fills **all** available space |
| **Flows?** | no | **yes** | yes |
| **Spreads?** | no | yes (volume constant) | **spreads out through the whole space** |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Water vapour exists even at room temperature**, invisible in the air around us — from drying clothes, mopped floors and so on.
>
> **Other substances have these states too:** wax, oil, ghee. **Solids:** stone, wood, glass. **Liquids:** milk, oil. **Gases:** oxygen, carbon dioxide — and the **smell of cooking** spreading from the kitchen shows how a gas **spreads through the air**.

## Changing states

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **HEATING** changes ice → water → vapour. **COOLING** changes vapour → water → ice.
>
> | Change | Name | How |
> |---|---|---|
> | solid → liquid | **MELTING** | heat (ice out of freezer; candle wax near a flame) |
> | liquid → solid | **FREEZING** | cool (water in the freezer; **coconut oil turns solid in winter**) |
> | liquid → gas | **EVAPORATION** | heat / at any temperature |
> | gas → liquid | **CONDENSATION** | cool (a cold surface) |

```
            melts                 evaporates
  SOLID  ───────────▶  LIQUID  ───────────────▶  GAS
  (ice)  ◀───────────  (water) ◀───────────────  (vapour)
            freezes               condenses
```

## What makes evaporation faster or slower?

**Activity 8.7 — exposed area:** same amount of water in a **bottle cap** and on a **plate**, side by side. **The plate dries first.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **What we changed:** the **exposed area**. **What we measured:** time to evaporate. **What we kept the same:** amount of water, place, time of day.
>
> **This is a FAIR TEST — change only ONE condition**, keep the rest the same, so you know **which** condition caused the difference.

**Activity 8.8 — sun vs shade:** two identical caps with equal water — one in **sunlight**, one in **shade**. The **sunlit** one dries first.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Evaporation is FASTER with:**
> 1. **larger exposed area** — more water surface touches the air (spread clothes out)
> 2. **higher temperature** — sunlight dries clothes faster
> 3. **more air movement (wind)** — clothes dry faster on a windy day
> 4. **lower humidity** — dry air takes in vapour easily
>
> **Rainy days** are **humid** — the air already holds **lots of vapour**, so clothes dry **slowly**.

> [!TIP]
> **Why does a fan dry clothes even though it blows "cool" air?** Evaporation needs heat, but the fan **carries away the moist air** next to the cloth and brings drier air — so more water can evaporate.

## The cooling effect of evaporation

Aavi's mother buys a **matka** (earthen pot) instead of a steel pot. Its water is **cold** — why?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Water seeps through the tiny pores of the clay and EVAPORATES from the outside. Evaporation takes heat away, so the water inside cools.**
> A **steel pot** has no pores — no seeping, no evaporation, no cooling.
>
> **EVAPORATION CAUSES COOLING.**

Other examples:
- sitting under a **fan** — it helps **sweat evaporate**, cooling you
- **sprinkling water** on the floor or roof in summer
- rubbing **sanitiser** on your hands feels cold
- a **surahi** keeps drinking water cool in summer
- a **wet hand** feels colder than a dry one when you blow on both
- **natural grass** feels cooler than **plastic grass** — real grass **gives off water** that evaporates

**Activity 8.9 — pot-in-pot cooler** (no electricity!):
1. Put a layer of **sand** in a **large earthen pot**.
2. Stand a **smaller pot** in the centre; fill the gap with **more sand**.
3. **Pour water into the sand**; cover the small pot with a **lid or wet jute sack**.
4. Wait **4–5 hours**. Keep **vegetables and fruit** inside; keep the sand **moist**.

> [!TIP]
> **How it works:** water in the sand **evaporates through the outer pot**, taking heat from the inner pot — food stays **fresh for days**.

## How clouds give us rain

> [!IMPORTANT]
> 1. **Water vapour is lighter than air**, so it **rises** (like a gas balloon).
> 2. **Higher up, air gets cooler and cooler.**
> 3. At a certain height the vapour **condenses into tiny droplets**, usually **around dust particles**.
> 4. These droplets float as **CLOUDS**.
> 5. Droplets **join** into bigger drops; when **too heavy**, they fall as **RAIN** — or under special conditions as **hail or snow**.

**Activity 8.10 — cloud in a bottle:** a little water in a 1-litre plastic bottle, lid tight, **squeeze and release** for 2–3 minutes. Then add a **small burnt piece of newspaper** and repeat — now a **haze (cloud)** forms.
**Why:** the smoke gives **tiny dust particles for the vapour to condense around**.

## The water cycle

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WATER CYCLE = the circulation of water between the Earth's surface and the atmosphere.**
> Water from **oceans, lakes, rivers** **evaporates** → vapour rises → **condenses** into **clouds** → falls as **rain, hail or snow** → collects in rivers, lakes, **groundwater** → flows back to the **oceans**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Only a small part of Earth's water is fit for use** — most is in the **oceans** and can't be used directly. Demand rises with **population**, causing **shortages**. **Use water wisely** and keep water bodies **free of pollution** — *"water is our responsibility before it is our right."*

## Think-it-through questions

1. **Best description of condensation?** → **conversion of water vapour into its liquid state.**
2. **Where is evaporation important?** → **water colours** (the paint dries) and an **ink pen** (the ink dries).
3. **Liquids other than water that evaporate:** **sanitiser, perfume, petrol, nail-polish remover, spirit.**
4. **Drain sludge left in heaps for 3–4 days before it's moved?** → its **water evaporates**, so it gets **lighter** (cheaper to transport) and **drier** (safer to handle).
5. **Water as a solid in nature?** → **snow, ice on mountains and glaciers, hail.**
6. **A two-wheeler seat hot from the sun?** → **sprinkle water / wipe with a wet cloth** — the water **evaporates and cools** it.

## In a nutshell

- **Ice, water and water vapour** are three **states** of the same substance.
- **Evaporation** (liquid → vapour) happens at any temperature; **condensation** (vapour → liquid) happens on cold surfaces.
- **Melting** (solid → liquid), **freezing** (liquid → solid); heating/cooling changes state.
- Evaporation is faster with **more area, more heat, more wind, less humidity**.
- **Evaporation causes cooling** — matka, surahi, sweat, pot-in-pot cooler.
- Vapour rises, cools, condenses around dust → **clouds** → **rain/hail/snow** — the **water cycle**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** visible "steam" is droplets, not vapour · droplets on a cold glass come from the air, not through the glass · evaporation happens without boiling · rainy days dry clothes slowly (humid) · a matka cools because water evaporates through its pores.
