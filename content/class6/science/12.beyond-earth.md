## Stars over Nubra

**Yangdol** and her twin brother **Dorjay**, 11, live in **Nubra, Ladakh**. Their favourite sight is the **night sky** — thousands of stars, because the weather is almost **cloudless** and there's almost **no air or light pollution**. Their elders tell how certain stars helped **caravans** crossing Nubra **find their way** long ago.

**Activity 12.1:** join bright stars with imaginary lines to make shapes, name them, and invent a story. Everyone's patterns and stories come out **different** — and that's what ancient people did too.

## Stars and constellations

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **STARS shine with their OWN light.** Some look bright, some dim.
>
> **CONSTELLATION** — originally a **group of stars forming a pattern** (an animal, an object, a character from a story). Today it means a **defined region of the sky** containing such a pattern. In the early 20th century the **International Astronomical Union (IAU)** divided the whole sky into **88 constellations**.
>
> **Why the patterns mattered:** they made stars **easy to recognise** — and before modern technology, even before the **magnetic compass**, they helped **sailors and travellers find directions**. They're still a **backup in emergencies**.

| Pattern / star | What to know |
|---|---|
| **Orion** | the **hunter**; **three stars in a row** in the middle = his **belt** |
| **Canis Major** | Orion's **dog** — contains **Sirius**, the **brightest star in the night sky** |
| **Taurus** | the **bull** Orion battles |
| **Big Dipper** | in **Ursa Major** — Indian name **Saptaṛiṣhi** |
| **Little Dipper** | in **Ursa Minor** — contains the **Pole Star** |
| **Pole Star (Polaris)** | Indian name **Dhruva tārā**; stays nearly **still** in the **north** |

> [!NOTE]
> **Nakṣhatra** in Indian astronomy = a star or a group of stars: **Ārdrā** = **Betelgeuse** (in Orion); **Kṛittikā** = the **Pleiades** (in Taurus); **Rohiṇī** = **Aldebaran** (in Taurus).
>
> **Regional stories:** tribes in **Central India** see four stars of the Big Dipper as **"grandmother's cot"** with **three thieves** (the other three stars) stealing it. **Konkan fishermen** see the four as a **boat** and the last three as its **neck**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The POLE STAR appears nearly STATIONARY in the NORTH.** Everything else seems to wheel around it — so it **shows north** in the **Northern hemisphere**.
> **It is NOT visible from the Southern hemisphere.**

## Watching the night sky

> [!WARNING]
> **LIGHT POLLUTION = too much artificial light at night.** With city lights, **smoke and dust**, only a few stars are seen. **Tall buildings and trees** block the view too. **Villages and dark open areas** show many more stars. **Dark sky reserves** protect dark skies for research.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Preparing for sky-watching:**
> - always go **with an adult**, to a **dark open place** away from lights, buildings and trees
> - pick a night with **no Moon and no clouds** (the Pole Star is **not very bright**)
> - use a **sky-map app** (e.g. **Sky Map, Stellarium**) or printed star charts; carry a **compass** and notebook
> - **wait about half an hour** so your **eyes adjust to the dark**

### Finding the Pole Star (Activity 12.2)

> [!TIP]
> Around **9 p.m. in summer**, look **north** for the **Big Dipper**. Take the **two stars at the end of its cup** and imagine a line through them **towards the north**. Go about **five times the gap between them** — you reach a **not-very-bright star: the Pole Star**.

### Finding Orion and Sirius (Activity 12.3)

> [!TIP]
> Orion is best seen in India **December to April after sunset**. First find the **three bright belt stars in a short straight line**. Follow that line **towards the east** → it leads to the very bright **Sirius**.

## Our Solar System

### The Sun

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The SUN is a STAR — the one closest to us.** An **extremely hot, spherical ball of gases** giving out huge amounts of energy, which is why it glows.
> - About **100 times the Earth's diameter**.
> - About **150 million km** away. This distance is called **1 astronomical unit (au)** — a handy unit for distances in the Solar System.
> - The **main source of energy** on Earth: **heat** keeps the right temperature for life; **light** lets plants make food and oxygen; it drives **seasons, weather, the water cycle and winds**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **If the Sun is a star, why does it look so big and why can't we see stars by day?**
> - **Size:** the Sun is **much closer** than other stars. Other stars are **so far away** they look like **points** — even ones **bigger** than the Sun.
> - **Daytime:** the Sun is **so bright** that it drowns out the light of other stars. They're still there; they **fade at dawn** and **appear at dusk** as the Sun's glare goes.

> [!NOTE]
> **Next-nearest star: Proxima Centauri**, about **269 000 au** away — 269 000 times as far as the Sun.
> In India the Sun is worshipped as **Sūrya**.

### Planets

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **REVOLUTION** = moving **around the Sun**.
> **PLANET** = a **large, nearly spherical** object that **revolves around the Sun**.
> - The **Earth** takes about **one year** for one revolution.
> - It also **rotates on its axis** — one rotation takes about **24 hours = one day**. Other planets rotate too.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The eight planets, nearest the Sun first:**
> **Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune**
>
> | Group | Planets | What they're like |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Inner four** | Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars | **smaller**, **solid rocky surfaces** |
> | **Outer four** | Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune | **much bigger**, mostly **gas and ice**, with **flat rings** of dust and rock |

> [!TIP]
> **Remember the order:** **M**y **V**ery **E**ager **M**other **J**ust **S**erved **U**s **N**oodles.

| Planet | Nickname / Indian name | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Venus** (Śhukra) | **Morning Star / Evening Star** — but **not a star!** | shines brightly at **dawn and dusk** |
| **Mars** (Mangala) | **Red Planet** | **reddish soil** |
| **Earth** (Pṛithvī) | **Blue Planet** | mostly **covered with water** |
| Mercury (Budha), Jupiter (Bṛihaspati / Guru), Saturn (Śhani) | | |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Farther from the Sun → generally COLDER** (planets get most of their energy from the Sun).
> **But an ATMOSPHERE can TRAP HEAT** — that's why **Venus is hotter than Mercury**, even though Mercury is closer to the Sun.

> [!NOTE]
> **Pluto** lies beyond Neptune and is **smaller than our Moon**. It was once called a planet, but in **2006 the IAU** redefined "planet", and Pluto and similar small objects are now **dwarf planets**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Spotting planets:** **Venus** is the easiest — after the **Sun and Moon**, it's the **brightest object in the sky**. Look **east before sunrise** or **west after sunset**. **Mercury, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn** are also visible to the naked eye.
> **Planets vs stars:** both look like dots, but **stars TWINKLE a lot**; planets don't.
>
> **Binoculars and telescopes** make objects look **brighter and larger** and show **dim objects** you can't see by eye.

### Moons — natural satellites

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SATELLITE** = an object that **moves around a planet** (more generally, around a much larger object — so Earth is a satellite of the Sun).
> **MOONS are NATURAL SATELLITES.** **Earth has 1 Moon; Mars has 2**; Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune have **many**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Our Moon:**
> - about **3,84,000 km** away — our **nearest neighbour in space**
> - goes round the Earth in about **27 days**
> - about **a quarter of Earth's diameter**
> - **hardly any atmosphere**; no water or life
> - covered in **craters** — bowl-shaped dents made by **asteroids and rocks hitting it**
>
> **Why do craters last so long?** With **no air, water or life**, there's no **wind, rain or plants** to wear them away.

> [!NOTE]
> **India's Moon missions:** **Chandrayaan-1 (2008)**, **Chandrayaan-2 (2019)**, **Chandrayaan-3 (July 2023)** — its **Vikram lander** carrying the **Pragyan rover** soft-landed on **23 August 2023**, making India the **first country to land near the Moon's south pole**. **23 August = National Space Day.** **Chandrayaan-4** is planned to bring back Moon **soil and rock samples**.

### Asteroids and comets

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ASTEROIDS** — small, **rocky, irregular-shaped** objects (about **10 m to 500 km**). Most revolve around the Sun **between Mars and Jupiter** — the **asteroid belt**.
>
> **COMETS** — made of **dust, gases, rocks and ice**. As a comet nears the Sun, its frozen material **evaporates** and forms a long **TAIL**. Moving away, it grows **dim** and vanishes from view. Many return **periodically**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Halley's Comet** appears every **76 years** — last seen in **1986** (so next in **2061**).
> Indian names: **Dhūmaketu** (Sanskrit), **Pucchya-Taro** (star with a tail), **Zendya-Taro** (star like a flag). Comets were once feared as bad luck — now we know they're just **icy-rocky visitors**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE SOLAR SYSTEM = the Sun + eight planets + their moons + smaller objects (asteroids, comets).**
> **The Sun** is the **largest and heaviest** member and makes **almost all its energy**. Everything else **shines by REFLECTING sunlight**.

## The Milky Way and the Universe

> [!IMPORTANT]
> On a **moonless night** from a dark place you can see a **faint band of light** stretching **north to south** — our home galaxy, the **MILKY WAY (Ākāśha Gangā)**.
> **A GALAXY has millions to billions of stars.** **Our Solar System is part of the Milky Way.**
>
> There are **many more galaxies** beyond it. Scientists search for life on **exoplanets** (planets round other stars) — **no evidence of life has been found yet**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Indian Astronomical Observatory, Hanle (Ladakh)** — one of the **highest observatories in the world**, on **Mount Saraswati**. It has the **Himalayan Chandra Telescope**, named after Nobel laureate **Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar**. The **Hanle Dark Sky Reserve** was notified in **December 2022**; local people are trained as **Astronomy Ambassadors**. **Why Hanle?** **Cold, dry, high and cloudless**, with **very little light pollution**.

## Think-it-through questions

1. **Match:** satellite of Earth → **Moon**; red planet → **Mars**; constellation → **Orion**; "evening star" → **Venus**.
2. **Riddle:** M (in MAN, not CAN) · A · R (in RAT, not CAT) · S (in SUN, not FUN) → **MARS**.
3. **Not a member of our Solar System?** → **Sirius** (a star far beyond it).
4. **Not a planet of the Sun?** → **Pluto** (a dwarf planet).
5. **Brighter: Pole Star or Sirius?** → **Sirius** — the brightest star in the night sky; the Pole Star isn't very bright.
6. **Watch the Big Dipper every 2–3 hours** → it **appears to move round the Pole Star**, which **stays nearly still** (because the Earth **rotates**).

## In a nutshell

- **Stars** shine by their own light; **constellations** are sky regions with star patterns (**88**); patterns helped **navigation**.
- **Pole Star** — nearly still in the **north**; find it from the **Big Dipper**. **Sirius** — brightest star, found from **Orion's belt**.
- **Sun** = our nearest star, ~**150 million km (1 au)**, ~**100 ×** Earth's diameter.
- **Planets:** Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars (rocky) · Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune (gas/ice giants, rings). Earth: **1 year** round the Sun, **24 h** rotation.
- **Moon:** ~3,84,000 km, ~27 days per revolution, ¼ Earth's size, **craters**, no air.
- **Asteroids** (belt between Mars and Jupiter), **comets** (tails near the Sun).
- **Solar System** is part of the **Milky Way galaxy**, one of many in the Universe.

> [!WARNING]
> **Top traps:** Venus is a planet, not a star · Pluto is a dwarf planet · Venus is hotter than Mercury (atmosphere) · the Pole Star is dim, Sirius is brightest · stars twinkle, planets don't · the Moon has no light of its own · the Pole Star isn't visible from the Southern hemisphere.
