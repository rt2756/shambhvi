<!-- ===== The building ===== -->

In Bela's Building of Fun, how are the floors numbered, and what number is the ground floor?

> [!ANSWER]
> The ground floor (the "Welcome Hall") is **Floor 0** — the reference point.
>
> Floors **above** it are **positive** (`+1, +2, …`, reached by pressing `+`); floors **below** it are **negative** (`−1, −2, …`, reached by pressing `−`).

---

Is **0** a positive or a negative number?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Neither.** It is the dividing point that everything else is measured from — we don't write a `+` or `−` in front of it.

---

You are on Floor **+2** and press **−3**. Where do you end up? Write the expression.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Starting Floor + Movement = Target Floor**
>
> `(+2) + (−3) = ` **−1** — you end up on Floor −1 (the Toy Store).

---

Evaluate: **(a)** `(+4) + (−3)` · **(b)** `(−1) + (+2)` · **(c)** `(−1) + (+1)` · **(d)** `0 + (−2)`

> [!ANSWER]
> **(a) +1** · **(b) +1** · **(c) 0** · **(d) −2**
>
> Each is a starting floor plus a movement — count up for `+`, down for `−`.

---

Gurmit is on Floor **−1** and wants to go down two floors, but presses `+` **twice** and then `−` **three** times. Where does he end up?

> [!ANSWER]
> The button presses combine: `(+2) + (−3) = −1` — a net movement of **one floor down**.
>
> From Floor −1: `(−1) + (−1) = ` **−2**.

---

<!-- ===== Inverse ===== -->

Basant presses **+3** by mistake on the ground floor. What cancels it? What is this called?

> [!ANSWER]
> Press **−3**: `(+3) + (−3) = 0` — back to the ground floor.
>
> **−3 is the inverse of +3** (and +3 is the inverse of −3). **A number and its inverse always add to 0.**

---

Write the inverses of: **+4, −4, −3, 0, +2, −1**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **−4, +4, +3, 0, −2, +1**
>
> Note **0 is its own inverse** — `0 + 0 = 0`.

---

<!-- ===== Comparing ===== -->

Should we write **−3 < −4** or **−4 < −3**? Explain using floors.

> [!ANSWER]
> **−4 < −3.**
>
> Floor −4 is **below** Floor −3 — four floors down is lower than three floors down. Lower floor means smaller number.

---

Fill in `<` or `>`: **(a)** −2 ☐ +5 · **(b)** −5 ☐ −3 · **(c)** 0 ☐ −4 · **(d)** −10 ☐ −12

> [!ANSWER]
> **(a) −2 < +5** · **(b) −5 < −3** · **(c) 0 > −4** · **(d) −10 > −12**
>
> **The trap is (b) and (d):** the bigger-looking digit gives the *smaller* number once it's negative, because further below ground is lower.

---

Why is **every negative number less than every positive number**?

> [!ANSWER]
> All negative-numbered floors are **below** Floor 0, and all positive-numbered floors are **above** it.
>
> So every negative is below 0, every positive is above 0 — and anything below 0 must be below anything above it.

---

<!-- ===== Subtraction as movement ===== -->

Write the rule for finding **which button to press**, and explain where it comes from.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Target Floor − Starting Floor = Movement needed**
>
> **Why:** we know `Starting + Movement = Target`. If the movement is the unknown, that's `Starting + ? = Target` — and finding a **missing addend** is exactly what subtraction does. So `? = Target − Starting`.

---

Complete: **(a)** `(+4) − (+1)` · **(b)** `(+4) − (−3)` · **(c)** `(−1) − (+2)` · **(d)** `(−2) − (−2)`

> [!ANSWER]
> **(a) +3** — from Floor +1 up to +4 is 3 floors up.
>
> **(b) +7** — from Floor −3 up to +4: 3 floors to reach 0, then 4 more.
>
> **(c) −3** — from Floor +2 down to −1 is 3 floors down.
>
> **(d) 0** — you're already there; no movement needed.

---

Complete: `(0) − (−2)` and `(+3) − (−3)`.

> [!ANSWER]
> `(0) − (−2) = ` **+2** — from Floor −2 up to 0 is 2 floors up.
>
> `(+3) − (−3) = ` **+6** — from −3 to 0 is 3, then 3 more to reach +3.

<!-- ===== Integers ===== -->

What are **integers**? How many are there?

> [!ANSWER]
> The **positive and negative numbers together with zero**: `… −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, …`
>
> **Infinitely many** — just as `+1, +2, +3, …` never ends, `−1, −2, −3, …` never ends either. They run forever in **both** directions.

---

<!-- ===== Number line ===== -->

On the number line, which direction is positive, and how do you tell which of two numbers is bigger?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Right is positive (forward), left is negative (backward).**
>
> **The number further RIGHT is always bigger.** That single rule handles every comparison: `−5 < −3` because −5 sits further left.

---

Is **2 > −3**? Is **−2 < 3**? Explain both.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Yes to both.**
>
> 2 lies to the **right** of −3 → `2 > −3`. And −2 lies to the **left** of 3 → `−2 < 3`.
>
> Any positive beats any negative, since positives are right of 0 and negatives left of it.

---

Starting at **9**, you want to reach **3**. How far and in which direction? Write both the addition and the subtraction statement.

> [!ANSWER]
> Move **6 steps backward**, i.e. **−6**.
>
> Addition: `9 + (−6) = 3` (Starting + Movement = Target).
>
> Subtraction: `3 − 9 = −6` (Target − Starting = Movement).

---

Evaluate: **(a)** `−5 + 0` · **(b)** `7 + (−7)` · **(c)** `−10 + 20` · **(d)** `10 − 20`

> [!ANSWER]
> **(a) −5** — adding 0 changes nothing.
>
> **(b) 0** — a number plus its inverse.
>
> **(c) +10** — from −10, moving 20 right passes 0 and lands at 10.
>
> **(d) −10** — from 10, moving 20 left passes 0 and lands at −10.

---

Use an unmarked number line to evaluate **85 + (−60)**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Mark 0, go **85 to the right**, then **60 back to the left**.
>
> `85 − 60 = ` **25**
>
> The unmarked line is handy exactly because you don't need every number drawn — just the rough positions.

<!-- ===== The two big rules ===== -->

Evaluate **(+2000) − (−200)** and say what shortcut it reveals.

> [!ANSWER]
> From Floor −200 up to Floor +2000: press **+200** to reach 0, then **+2000** more → **+2200**.
>
> Notice `(+2000) + (+200)` is also **+2200**.
>
> **The rule: subtracting a negative is the same as adding the positive.** Removing a debt is the same as gaining money.

---

Evaluate: **(a)** `+105 − (−55)` · **(b)** `−99 − (−200)` · **(c)** `+1500 − (−1500)`

> [!ANSWER]
> Use "subtracting a negative = adding the positive":
>
> **(a)** `105 + 55 = ` **160** · **(b)** `−99 + 200 = ` **101** · **(c)** `1500 + 1500 = ` **3000**

---

Rewrite each as an **addition**: `(+7) − (+5)` · `(−3) − (+8)` · `(+8) − (−2)`

> [!ANSWER]
> Replace the number being subtracted with its **inverse** and add:
>
> `(+7) + (−5) = +2` · `(−3) + (−8) = −11` · `(+8) + (+2) = +10`
>
> **Why it always works:** break the journey at zero. Going from 2 to −3 is "2 → 0" (movement −2) then "0 → −3" (movement −3), totalling `−3 + (−2) = −5` — the same answer as `−3 − 2`, with no subtraction left in it.

<!-- ===== Token model ===== -->

What is a **zero pair**, and why can you add or remove them freely?

> [!ANSWER]
> One **positive (green)** token together with one **negative (red)** token — their value together is **0**.
>
> Because a zero pair is worth nothing, putting some in or taking some out **doesn't change the total value** of the collection. That freedom is what makes the model work.

---

The lift attendant's pocket holds **5 positive and 3 negative** tokens. Which floor is he on?

> [!ANSWER]
> Cancel the zero pairs: 3 positives pair off with the 3 negatives, leaving **2 positives**.
>
> `(+5) + (−3) = ` **+2** — Floor +2.

---

Use tokens to find **(+5) + (−8)**.

> [!ANSWER]
> Lay out 5 positives and 8 negatives. **Five zero pairs cancel**, leaving **3 negatives**.
>
> `(+5) + (−8) = ` **−3**

---

Use tokens to find **(+5) − (+6)** — there are only 5 positives to start with.

> [!ANSWER]
> You can't remove 6 positives from 5 — so **add one zero pair** first (free, since it's worth 0).
>
> Now there are **6 positives and 1 negative**. Remove the 6 positives → **one negative left**.
>
> `(+5) − (+6) = ` **−1**

---

Use tokens to find **(+4) − (−6)**. How many zero pairs are needed?

> [!ANSWER]
> Start with 4 positives — but there are **no negatives to remove**.
>
> Add **6 zero pairs** (one for each negative you must take away) → now 10 positives and 6 negatives. Remove the 6 negatives → **10 positives left**.
>
> `(+4) − (−6) = ` **+10**
>
> This is the "subtracting a negative = adding the positive" rule, proved with tokens: `(+4) + (+6) = +10` ✓

---

Evaluate with tokens: **(a)** `(−8) − (−4)` · **(b)** `(−3) − (+5)`

> [!ANSWER]
> **(a)** 8 negatives, remove 4 negatives → **4 negatives left** → **−4**.
>
> **(b)** 3 negatives, but 5 positives must be removed — add **5 zero pairs** → 5 positives and 8 negatives → remove the 5 positives → **8 negatives left** → **−8**.
