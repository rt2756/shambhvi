<!-- ===== Compass and circle ===== -->

You mark every point that is **4 cm** from a point P. What shape appears, and what are P and the 4 cm called?

> [!ANSWER]
> A **circle**.
>
> **P is the centre**, and the fixed distance **4 cm is the radius**.

---

What is true of **every** point on a circle?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Every point on a circle is exactly the same distance from the centre** — that distance being the radius.
>
> That *is* the definition of a circle, and it's exactly what a compass enforces: the tip stays fixed while the pencil stays a set distance from it.

---

How do you set a compass to a radius of **4 cm**, and how do you then draw the circle?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Open the compass against a ruler** until the gap between the metal tip and the pencil point measures 4 cm.
>
> Then **keep the tip fixed** on P and swing **only the pencil** all the way round.

---

Besides drawing circles, what is a compass most useful for in constructions?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Copying an exact distance from one place to another.**
>
> Once set, it carries a length anywhere on the page without you re-measuring — which is why constructions using a compass come out far more accurate than ones using repeated ruler measurements.

---

<!-- ===== Properties ===== -->

State the **two properties** of a rectangle and the **two** of a square.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Rectangle:** **R1)** opposite sides are equal · **R2)** all angles are 90°.
>
> **Square:** **S1)** **all** sides are equal · **S2)** all angles are 90°.

---

What is the single difference between a square and a rectangle?

> [!ANSWER]
> The **sides**. A rectangle needs only its **opposite** sides equal; a square needs **all four** equal.
>
> Both demand all angles 90°. So **every square is a rectangle**, but not every rectangle is a square.

---

Is it possible to draw a 4-sided figure with **all angles 90°** but **opposite sides unequal**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No — impossible.**
>
> Once all four corners are right angles, the figure is forced to close with **opposite sides equal**. Try it: draw three right angles and the fourth side has no choice about its length.
>
> So R2 automatically drags R1 along with it.

---

A square piece of paper is **rotated** so it sits like a diamond. Is it still a square?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Yes.**
>
> Check the definition: are all sides still equal? **Yes** — rotating doesn't change lengths. Are all angles still 90°? **Yes** — rotating doesn't change angles.
>
> Both properties hold, so it's still a square. **Judge a shape by its sides and angles, never by how it sits on the page.**

---

<!-- ===== Naming ===== -->

Rectangle ABCD can also be called BCDA and DCBA. Why are **ABDC** and **ACBD** not valid names?

> [!ANSWER]
> A valid name lists the corners **in order of travel around the shape**, starting at any corner and going either way.
>
> **ABDC** and **ACBD** **jump across** the figure instead of walking round its boundary, so they don't describe the shape's path.

---

Which of these is **not** a name for square PQRS: **PQSR · SPQR · RSPQ · QRSP**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **PQSR** is not valid.
>
> Going P → Q → S skips R and cuts across the square. The other three (SPQR, RSPQ, QRSP) each start at a different corner but still travel around the boundary in order.

---

<!-- ===== Constructing ===== -->

List the steps to construct a **square PQRS of side 6 cm**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **1.** Draw `PQ = 6 cm`. **2.** Draw a **perpendicular** to PQ through **P**. **3.** Mark **S** on it with `PS = 6 cm`. **4.** Draw a perpendicular to PQ through **Q**. **5.** Mark **R** with `QR = 6 cm`. **6.** Join **RS**.

---

After that construction, what should you **check** — and why is it a good test?

> [!ANSWER]
> Measure `RS` (should be **6 cm**) and `∠R` and `∠S` (both should be **90°**).
>
> **Why it's a good test:** you never *set* those three measurements — they came out right on their own. If they're correct, the earlier steps must have been accurate. If they're not, something slipped.

---

Why keep the compass at the **same setting** throughout a square's construction?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because **every side of a square is the same length**, so one setting marks them all.
>
> Re-measuring each side with a ruler introduces small errors, and those errors accumulate until the figure won't close properly.

---

You need a rectangle of sides **4 cm and 6 cm**. How does the construction differ from the square's?

> [!ANSWER]
> The same steps, but with **two different lengths**: draw the 6 cm side, raise perpendiculars at both ends, then mark **4 cm** up each one and join.
>
> You'll need **two compass settings** here (4 cm and 6 cm) instead of one — the only real difference.

---

<!-- ===== Diagonals ===== -->

What is a **diagonal**? How many does a rectangle have, and how do their lengths compare?

> [!ANSWER]
> A line joining **opposite corners**.
>
> A rectangle has **two**, and **they are equal in length**.

---

A diagonal of a rectangle splits a 90° corner into **50° and 40°**. What does that tell you about the rectangle?

> [!ANSWER]
> That it is **not a square** — the two parts are unequal.
>
> (They correctly add to `50 + 40 = 90°`, the whole corner.)

---

When does a diagonal split the corners into **two equal parts**? What is each part?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Exactly when the rectangle is a SQUARE** — and each part is then **45°**.
>
> **Why:** the diagonal's tilt depends on how the two adjacent sides compare. In a long thin rectangle it leans towards the longer side, giving unequal parts. Only when the adjacent sides are **equal** does it sit symmetrically and halve the right angle.

---

Construct a rectangle with one side **4 cm** and a diagonal of **8 cm**. How do you find the fourth corner?

> [!ANSWER]
> Draw the **4 cm** side, then raise a **perpendicular** at one end (the corner must be 90°).
>
> Now **set the compass to 8 cm** and, placing the tip at the **far end** of the 4 cm side, swing an arc. **Where the arc crosses the perpendicular is the corner** you need.
>
> **Why it works:** the compass finds every point exactly 8 cm away; the perpendicular carries every point that makes the corner a right angle. The one point satisfying **both** is where they cross.

---

<!-- ===== Rhombus ===== -->

Is there a 4-sided figure with **all sides equal** that is **not** a square?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Yes — a rhombus.** All four sides equal, but the angles are **not** 90°.
>
> **What it shows:** equal sides alone (S1) isn't enough. A square needs **both** equal sides **and** right angles. Lean a square over and it keeps its equal sides while losing its right angles — becoming a rhombus.

---

Why is it worth constructing figures with **ruler and compass** rather than just measuring with a ruler?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because the compass **guarantees exactness** rather than approximating it.
>
> A circle drawn with a compass has *every* point at precisely the right distance; a length carried by compass is copied exactly. Ruler measurement introduces a small error at every step, and those errors add up until the figure fails to close.
