## The compass draws a circle — and why

Mark a point **P**, then mark every point that is **4 cm away from P**. Those points form a **circle**.

- **P is the centre**; the fixed 4 cm distance is the **radius**.
- **Every point on a circle is exactly the same distance from the centre** — that's the whole definition, and it's exactly what a compass enforces: the tip stays put, the pencil stays a fixed distance away.

**Setting a radius:** open the compass **against a ruler** so the gap between the tip and the pencil is the length you want. Then keep the **tip fixed** and swing only the pencil.

> [!TIP]
> **A compass is a distance-copier, not just a circle-drawer.** That's its real power in every construction below: it moves an exact length from one place to another without you re-measuring.

## Squares & rectangles — the defining properties

| | Properties |
|---|---|
| **Rectangle** | **R1)** opposite sides equal · **R2)** all angles 90° |
| **Square** | **S1)** **all four** sides equal · **S2)** all angles 90° |

**A square is a rectangle with the extra demand that all four sides — not just opposite pairs — are equal.**

> [!WARNING]
> **A four-sided figure with all angles 90° but unequal opposite sides is impossible.** Once all four corners are right angles, the opposite sides are forced to be equal — so R2 drags R1 along with it.

### Rotating changes nothing

Turn a square on the page and it is **still a square**: rotation doesn't alter any **side length** or any **angle**, and those are the only things the definition mentions.

> [!TIP]
> A "diamond" is just a square that's been turned. Don't judge a shape by how it sits — check its **sides and angles**.

### Naming a quadrilateral

The corner letters must go **in order of travel around the shape**, starting anywhere and going either way.

- Rectangle ABCD: **ABCD, BCDA, CDAB, DABC** and the reverses **ADCB, DCBA, CBAD, BADC** are all valid.
- **ABDC and ACBD are NOT** — they jump across the shape instead of walking round it.

## Constructing a square of side 6 cm

1. Draw `PQ = 6 cm` with the ruler.
2. Draw a **perpendicular** to PQ through **P**.
3. Mark **S** on that perpendicular with `PS = 6 cm`.
4. Draw a perpendicular to PQ through **Q**.
5. Mark **R** with `QR = 6 cm` — the compass, still set to 6 cm, does this instantly.
6. Join **RS**.

**Check when you finish:** `RS` should measure **6 cm**, and `∠R` and `∠S` should both be **90°** — even though you never set them. They come out right automatically, which is the sign the construction was sound.

> [!TIP]
> **Why keep the compass at the same setting?** Every side of a square is the same length, so one setting marks them all. Re-measuring with a ruler each time invites small errors that make the figure fail to close.

## Diagonals

The lines joining **opposite corners** are the **diagonals**.

- **In a rectangle, the two diagonals are equal in length.**
- A diagonal **splits each of the two opposite corners into two smaller angles**. In general those two parts are **unequal** — e.g. 50° and 40°, together making the 90° corner.
- **The diagonal splits the corners into two EQUAL parts (45° and 45°) exactly when the rectangle is a SQUARE.**

**Why:** the diagonal cuts the rectangle into two triangles whose legs are the two adjacent sides. The angle it makes depends on how those sides compare — a long, thin rectangle tilts the diagonal towards the long side. Only when the adjacent sides are **equal** does the diagonal sit symmetrically and split the right angle evenly.

> [!TIP]
> **Constructing from a side and a diagonal:** you know one side and the corner is 90°, so draw the side, raise the perpendicular, then **set the compass to the diagonal's length** and swing an arc from the far end of the side. Where it crosses the perpendicular is the missing corner — the compass finds the point that is the right distance away.

## A four-sided figure with equal sides that isn't a square

**Yes — a rhombus.** All four sides equal, but the angles aren't 90°.

This shows **S1 alone is not enough**: a square needs **both** equal sides **and** right angles. Leaning a square over keeps its sides equal while destroying its angles.
