## Why negative numbers exist

The counting numbers 1, 2, 3, … then **0**, then fractions between them — but the number line so far is really only a **number ray**: it starts at 0 and goes right forever.

**Are there numbers to the left of 0?** Yes — and thinking about a building with floors below the ground is the easiest way to see them.

## Bela's Building of Fun

The lift has a **`+` button (go up)** and a **`−` button (go down)**. The entrance is the **ground floor = Floor 0**.

- Floors **above** ground: **positive** — `+1, +2, +3, …`
- Floors **below** ground: **negative** — `−1, −2, −3, …`
- **0 is neither positive nor negative** — it's the reference point everything is measured from.

**A number does two jobs at once:** `+3` is both the *floor number* of the Book Store **and** the *number of floors you move* when you press `+3`.

### Addition = movement

> **Starting Floor + Movement = Target Floor**

- From Food Court (+1), press +2 → `(+1) + (+2) = +3` (Book Store).
- Press the wrong buttons and they still combine: press `+2` then `−3` → `(+2) + (−3) = −1`, one floor down.

### Inverse — the way back to zero

Press `+3` by mistake? Press `−3` to cancel it: `(+3) + (−3) = 0`.

**−3 is the inverse of +3, and +3 is the inverse of −3.** A number and its inverse always add to **0**.

## Comparing integers

**Lower floor = smaller number.**

- `+3 < +4` (Floor 3 is below Floor 4) — and `−4 < −3`, because Floor −4 is **below** Floor −3.
- **Every negative number is less than 0; every positive number is greater than 0.**

> [!WARNING]
> **The big trap: −5 is SMALLER than −3**, even though 5 looks bigger than 3. The minus sign means *below ground* — and 5 floors down is lower than 3 floors down. Compare *positions*, not digits.

## Subtraction = which button to press

> **Target Floor − Starting Floor = Movement needed**

**Why:** we know `Starting + Movement = Target`. If the movement is what's unknown, `Starting + ? = Target`, and finding a missing addend **is** subtraction. So `? = Target − Starting`.

- From +2 to +5 → press `+3` → `(+5) − (+2) = +3`
- From +3 to −1 → press `−4` → `(−1) − (+3) = −4`
- From −2 to −1 → press `+1` → `(−1) − (−2) = +1`
- From −2 to +2 → press `+4` → `(+2) − (−2) = +4`

## Integers

Positive and negative numbers **together with zero** are the **integers**:

`… −4, −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, …`

They go on forever in **both** directions — just as `+1, +2, +3, …` never ends, neither does `−1, −2, −3, …`

## Back to the number line

Turn the "infinite lift" on its side and it **is** the number line, now complete on both sides of 0.

- **Right = positive (forward)** · **Left = negative (backward)**
- **Bigger numbers are always to the right.** So `2 < 5`, `−3 < 2`, `−5 < −3`.
- We usually drop the `+` and write positives as plain `1, 2, 3, …`

**Moving along it:** from 5 to 9 you travel **+4** → `5 + 4 = 9`. From 9 to 3 you travel **−6** → `9 + (−6) = 3`. From 3 to −2 you travel **−5** → `3 + (−5) = −2`.

> [!TIP]
> **The unmarked number line (UNL):** draw a line marking only **0**, then sketch roughly where the numbers fall. Perfect for big numbers — `85 + (−60)` becomes a picture: go 85 right, then 60 back, landing on **25**.

## Two rules that make everything easier

### 1. Subtracting a negative = adding the positive

`(+2000) − (−200) = +2200` — and `(+2000) + (+200)` is also `+2200`.

**Why:** to get from Floor −200 up to Floor +2000 you press `+200` to reach zero, then `+2000` more — a total of `+2200`. Removing a debt is the same as gaining money.

### 2. Any subtraction can be turned into an addition

**Replace the number being subtracted with its inverse, and add instead.**

| Subtraction | Same as |
|---|---|
| `(+7) − (+5)` | `(+7) + (−5)` |
| `(−3) − (+8)` | `(−3) + (−8)` |
| `(+8) − (−2)` | `(+8) + (+2)` |
| `(+6) − (−9)` | `(+6) + (+9)` |

**Why it works:** break the journey at zero. Going from 2 to −3 is "2 → 0" (a movement of −2) followed by "0 → −3" (a movement of −3). Total `−3 + (−2) = −5` — the same as `−3 − 2`, but with no subtraction anywhere.

## The token model

Green **positive** tokens and red **negative** tokens.

- **A positive and a negative token make a ZERO PAIR** — together they're worth 0, so you may add or remove zero pairs freely **without changing the value**.

**Adding:** lay out the tokens, **cancel every zero pair**, and read what's left.
`(+5) + (−3)` → three pairs cancel → **+2** left.
`(+5) + (−8)` → five pairs cancel → **−3** left.

**Subtracting:** take away the tokens named.
`(+5) − (+4)` → remove 4 positives → **+1**.

**Not enough tokens to remove?** Put down as many **zero pairs** as you need first — it's free, because each is worth 0.

- `(+5) − (+6)`: only 5 positives, need to remove 6. Add one zero pair → now 6 positives and 1 negative → remove 6 positives → **−1** left.
- `(+4) − (−6)`: no negatives to remove! Add **6 zero pairs** → now 10 positives and 6 negatives → remove the 6 negatives → **+10** left.
  - Notice this confirms rule 1: `(+4) − (−6) = +10 = (+4) + (+6)` ✓

> [!TIP]
> The token model, the lift, and the number line are **three pictures of the same thing**. Use whichever makes a particular question obvious — they can never disagree.
