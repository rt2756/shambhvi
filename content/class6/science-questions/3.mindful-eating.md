<!-- ===== Regional food ===== -->

Why does traditional food differ so much between Indian states?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because **the traditional food of a state is usually based on the crops grown there**, and India has **diverse soils and climates** — so different regions grow different crops.
>
> Choice is also shaped by **taste preferences, culture and traditions**.

---

Punjab eats makki di roti and Karnataka eats ragi mudde. Connect each to its local crops.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Punjab** grows **maize** — makki di roti is made from maize flour.
>
> **Karnataka** grows **ragi** — ragi mudde is made from ragi.
>
> A neat illustration of food following farming.

---

Name two ways cooking practices have changed, and why.

> [!ANSWER]
> The **chulha** (traditional stove) → the **gas stove**; the **sil-batta** (stone grinder) → the **electric grinder**.
>
> **Why:** technological development, improved transportation and better communication.

---

<!-- ===== Nutrients ===== -->

Name the **five** major nutrients.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins and minerals.**

---

Which nutrients are **energy-giving**, which is **body-building**, and which are **protective**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Energy-giving: carbohydrates and fats.**
>
> **Body-building: proteins** — they help **growth and repair** of the body.
>
> **Protective: vitamins and minerals** — they protect the body from diseases.

---

Why does a marathon runner drink **glucose water** during a race?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Glucose provides instant energy.** It is a **carbohydrate**, and carbohydrates are a primary energy source.
>
> During and after hard exercise the body needs energy quickly, and glucose supplies it faster than food that must first be digested.

---

Why are **laddoos** traditionally eaten in winter?

> [!ANSWER]
> Laddoos are made with besan or wheat flour plus **ghee**, nuts and seeds — all **rich in fat**.
>
> **Fat is a source of stored energy**, and that energy helps keep the body warm through the cold.

---

Give two **plant** and two **animal** sources of protein.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Plant:** pulses, beans, peas, nuts, soya bean (and edible mushrooms).
>
> **Animal:** milk, paneer, eggs, fish, meat.

---

Why do growing children and sportspersons need plenty of protein?

> [!ANSWER]
> Proteins are the **body-building** nutrient — they support **growth and repair**.
>
> Children are actively growing, and sportspersons need to **build and repair muscles**, so both need more of it.

---

How does the fat under a **polar bear's** skin help it?

> [!ANSWER]
> It is **stored energy**, which supports the bear through its **months-long winter sleep (hibernation)** — letting it survive without eating.

---

<!-- ===== Deficiency diseases ===== -->

Name the deficiency disease and the missing nutrient: **bleeding, swollen gums**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Scurvy**, caused by lack of **Vitamin C**.
>
> Cured by citrus fruits — **lemons, oranges**, and also amla, guava and green chilli.

---

How was the cure for scurvy discovered?

> [!ANSWER]
> During a voyage in **1746**, the Scottish physician **James Lind** observed that sailors who **ate lemons and oranges recovered** from bleeding and swollen gums.
>
> Long voyages meant no fresh fruit, hence no Vitamin C — a natural experiment that revealed the cause.

---

What is **goitre**, what causes it, and how did India tackle it?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Swelling at the front of the neck**, caused by a deficiency of **iodine**.
>
> In the 1960s it was common in the Himalayan region and Northern plains, because the **soil there lacks iodine** — so local food and water lacked it too.
>
> **The fix:** the Government of India supplemented common salt with iodine, creating **iodised salt**. Consumption of it visibly reduced the symptoms.

---

Match the deficiency to the disease: **Vitamin A · Vitamin B1 · Vitamin D · iron**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Vitamin A → loss of vision** (poor vision, night blindness).
>
> **Vitamin B1 → beriberi** (swelling, tingling or burning in feet and hands, trouble breathing).
>
> **Vitamin D → rickets** (soft and bent bones).
>
> **Iron → anaemia** (weakness, shortness of breath).

---

Which vitamin can the body **make for itself**, and how?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Vitamin D** — the body produces it naturally on **exposure to sunlight**.
>
> (It can also be eaten, in milk, butter, fish and eggs.)

---

Salt is a source of minerals. Does it come from plants or animals?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Neither.** Salt is obtained from **seawater or from rocks**.
>
> A useful reminder that not every nutrient comes from a living source.

<!-- ===== Food tests ===== -->

Describe the test for **starch** and its positive result.

> [!ANSWER]
> Put **2–3 drops of dilute iodine solution** on a small piece of the food.
>
> A **blue-black colour** indicates the presence of **starch**.

---

Describe the test for **fat**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Wrap the food in paper and press** it (without tearing). If the food contains water, let the paper **dry** first.
>
> An **oily patch** — one that light **shines faintly through** when held up — indicates **fat**.
>
> Drying first matters: a wet patch could be mistaken for an oily one.

---

Describe the test for **protein**, and the safety precautions.

> [!ANSWER]
> Crush the food to a paste, put about half a teaspoon in a test tube, add water and shake. Add **2 drops of copper sulfate solution** and **10 drops of caustic soda solution**, shake and leave a few minutes.
>
> A **violet colour** indicates **protein**.
>
> ⚠️ **Precautions:** these chemicals are **harmful** — don't touch, taste or smell them, and if any spills on you, **wash the area with water immediately**.

---

Peanuts test positive for both fat and protein. What does that tell you?

> [!ANSWER]
> That **a single food can contain more than one nutrient**.
>
> Most foods are mixtures — which is why a varied diet supplies many nutrients at once, and why no single food is "the" source of anything.

---

<!-- ===== Roughage and water ===== -->

What is **roughage**, and why does it matter if it provides no nutrients?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Dietary fibre** — it provides **no nutrients at all**, but is still essential.
>
> It helps the body **get rid of undigested food** and ensures the **smooth passage of stools**.
>
> Sources: green leafy vegetables, fresh fruits, wholegrains, pulses and nuts.

---

Give two functions of **water** in the diet.

> [!ANSWER]
> It helps the body **absorb nutrients** from food, and it **removes waste** from the body through **sweat and urine**.

---

<!-- ===== Balanced diet ===== -->

What is a **balanced diet**?

> [!ANSWER]
> A diet that has **all the essential nutrients, roughage and water in the right amounts** for the proper growth and development of the body.

---

Do you and your grandparents need the same diet?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No.** The type and amount of nutrients needed **varies with age, gender, physical activity, health status and lifestyle**.
>
> A growing child needs more protein for growth; an elderly person's needs are different again.

---

Why should some fruits and vegetables be eaten **uncooked**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Cooking destroys some nutrients** — **Vitamin C** and others are lost to high heat.
>
> **But always wash produce thoroughly before eating** — and note that washing **already-cut or peeled** fruit and vegetables also loses some vitamins, so wash **before** cutting.

---

<!-- ===== Junk food ===== -->

What is **junk food**, and why is it a poor choice?

> [!ANSWER]
> Food that is **high in calories from sugar and fat** but contains **very low amounts of proteins, minerals, vitamins and dietary fibre** — potato wafers, candy bars, carbonated drinks.
>
> Eating it frequently can make a person **obese** and lead to several health problems.

---

Per 100 g, potato wafers give 536 kcal, 35 g fat, 7 g protein, 4.8 g fibre; roasted chana gives 355 kcal, 6.26 g fat, 18.64 g protein, 16.8 g fibre. Which is the better choice?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Roasted chana**, on every measure that matters:
>
> **Less fat** (6.26 g vs 35 g) · **far more protein** (18.64 g vs 7 g) · **over three times the fibre** (16.8 g vs 4.8 g) · and fewer calories.
>
> The wafers deliver mostly **fat and calories** with little of the nutrients the body actually needs — the definition of junk food.

---

Who was **Coluthur Gopalan**, and what did his work lead to?

> [!ANSWER]
> **(1918–2019)** — he initiated nutrition research in India, analysed **more than 500 Indian foods** for nutritional value, and led surveys of the population's nutritional status.
>
> His finding of **widespread deficiencies** in protein, energy and other components led to the **Mid Day Meal Programme (2002)**, now the **PM POSHAN** initiative, which provides balanced food in government and government-aided schools.
