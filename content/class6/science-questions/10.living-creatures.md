<!-- ===== Characteristics ===== -->

List the characteristics shown by **all living beings**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Movement, nutrition (need food), growth, respiration, excretion, response to stimuli, reproduction** — and eventually **death**.
>
> **Absence of any of these** indicates the thing is non-living.

---

A car moves on its own. Is it living?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No.** Movement alone is **not enough**.
>
> A car cannot **grow, reproduce, respire, excrete or respond to stimuli** — it fails every other test. **A living being must show the whole set of characteristics, not just one.**

---

Plants don't move from place to place. Does that make them non-living?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No.** They show **every other characteristic** — growth, nutrition, respiration, excretion, response, reproduction.
>
> And they **do move**, just not from place to place: **flowers open**, **climbers wind** around supports, and insectivorous plants **trap insects**.

---

Describe how **Drosera** moves, and why it does so.

> [!ANSWER]
> Drosera is an **insectivorous** plant — it depends on insects for **nutrition**.
>
> Its **saucer-shaped leaves** carry many **hair-like projections of unequal length with sticky ends**. When an insect enters the saucer, the **hairs move inward and trap it** with their sticky ends.

---

How do **plants respire**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Through tiny pores called **stomata** on the surface of their leaves, which let air move **in and out**.
>
> **All living beings respire** — plants included.

---

Give an example of **excretion** in animals and in plants.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Animals:** **sweat** — the white patches on shirts in summer are the water and salts the body removes as waste. **Urine** is another excretory product.
>
> **Plants:** they excrete **excess water and minerals** as small **droplets on their leaves** — seen on grasses and roses.

---

What is a **stimulus**? Give two examples with the body's response.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Anything or any event that prompts a living being to respond.**
>
> **Stepping on a thorn** → you instantly pull your foot away. **Touching a hot cup of tea** → you jerk your hand back.

---

Give two examples of plants responding to stimuli.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Touch-me-not (mimosa, chhui-mui, lajjalu)** — **folds its leaves when touched**.
>
> **Amla** (Indian gooseberry) — its leaves **fold together after sunset** ("sleeping leaves"). The stimulus here is the **change in light**.

---

What is **reproduction**, and why is it necessary?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The process of producing new ones of one's own kind.**
>
> **Why necessary: for the continuity of life.** Without it, every kind of living being would end with the present generation.

---

When is a living being said to be **dead**?

> [!ANSWER]
> When it is **not able to exhibit the characteristics of living beings**, **despite having all the resources needed to be alive** — food, air and water.
>
> The qualifier matters: lacking food doesn't make something dead, failing to show the characteristics *while having* food does.

---

Why is comparing something with **yourself** a useful way to decide if it's living?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because **you are a certain example of a living being**, so you can check the thing against each characteristic you know you have.
>
> Ask: does it grow as I do? respire? reproduce? respond? A car fails all of these, so it's non-living.

---

<!-- ===== Germination ===== -->

What is **germination**?

> [!ANSWER]
> When a **seed turns into a sprout**.

---

In the four-pot experiment, which pots **germinate**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Pot C** (moist soil, in the dark) and **Pot D** (moist soil, in sunlight).
>
> **Pot A** (no water) and **Pot B** (excess standing water) do **not**.

---

Why do the seeds in **Pot A** fail?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No water.**
>
> Water is needed to **soften the seed coat** so the tiny **embryo** inside can develop, and to enable the processes necessary for growth. Without it nothing can start.

---

Pot B has **plenty of water** — more than Pot D. Why do its seeds still fail?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because the **excess standing water fills the spaces between the soil particles and drives out the air**.
>
> Seeds need **air** for germination, and they get it from those spaces. With the air excluded, the seeds fail — **drowned, not thirsty**.
>
> This is the cleverest pot in the experiment: it shows the problem isn't too little water but **too much**.

---

Compare **Pot C and Pot D**. What does the comparison prove?

> [!ANSWER]
> Both have air and moist soil; they differ only in **light** — C is in the dark, D in sunlight. **Both germinate.**
>
> → **Sunlight is NOT essential for germination.**

---

State the conditions required for germination, and say what sunlight *is* needed for.

> [!ANSWER]
> Germination requires the **right amount of water and air** — not sunlight.
>
> But **after germination, sunlight is required for the further growth of the seedling**.

---

Besides supplying air, why do the **spaces between soil particles** matter?

> [!ANSWER]
> They also **allow the roots to grow easily** through the soil.
>
> Tightly packed, waterlogged soil obstructs roots as well as excluding air.

---

Are there seeds that **do** need light to germinate?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Yes — exceptions exist.** Seeds of **Coleus and Petunia require light**; covering them with soil stops them sprouting.
>
> The reverse also happens: **Calendula and Zinnia need darkness** and must be covered with sufficient soil.
>
> So "most seeds don't need light" is a general rule, not a universal one.

---

Is a **seed** living or non-living?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Living.** A seed contains a tiny living **embryo**, and given the right conditions — water and air — it **germinates, grows** and eventually **reproduces**.
>
> It appears inactive, but so does a sleeping animal; being dormant is not the same as being non-living.
