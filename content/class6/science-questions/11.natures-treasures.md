<!-- ===== Air ===== -->

Give the **composition of air** by percentage.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Nitrogen 78%** · **Oxygen 21%** · **argon, carbon dioxide and other gases 1%**.
>
> (**Percentage** means the number of parts in 100 — so out of 100 parts of air, 78 are nitrogen.)

---

Why is it difficult to **hold your breath** for long?

> [!ANSWER]
> The air we breathe in contains **oxygen**, which the body needs to **perform its functions**.
>
> Holding your breath means the body **stops getting enough oxygen**, so you soon feel uncomfortable and have to breathe.

---

Compare how long we can survive without **food or water** versus without **oxygen**.

> [!ANSWER]
> We can survive **a few days** without food or water — but **not even a few minutes** without oxygen.
>
> That contrast shows just how essential oxygen is.

---

What is **wind**? Give two ways you can notice air even though you can't see it.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Moving air is called wind** — fast in a storm, gentle as a breeze.
>
> You notice it when **leaves rustle**, **clothes sway on a line**, or **book pages flutter** when a fan is switched on.

---

What makes a **firki** rotate, and what machine works the same way?

> [!ANSWER]
> **The wind** makes it rotate.
>
> A **windmill** works on the same principle — wind rotates its wings.

---

Give three uses of windmills, and say what a **windmill farm** is.

> [!ANSWER]
> Windmills can **run flour mills**, **pull up water from a well**, and **generate electricity**.
>
> A **windmill farm** is an area with a **large number of windmills** using wind energy to generate electricity — such as **Muppandal** (Tamil Nadu), **Jaisalmer Wind Park** (Rajasthan) and **Brahmanvel** (Maharashtra).

---

<!-- ===== Water ===== -->

Water covers about two-thirds of Earth's surface. Why is there still a shortage?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because **most of that water is in oceans and seas and is saline (salty)** — unfit for domestic, agricultural or industrial use.
>
> We need **freshwater**, and most of *that* is locked in **ice sheets, snow and underground**, which are **difficult to access**. Only a **very small fraction** — in ponds, rivers, lakes and wells — is easily available.

---

Where is freshwater found?

> [!ANSWER]
> In **ice sheets and snow**, in **rivers and lakes** on the surface, and **underground**.

---

Give four ways to save water.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Turn off taps** when not in use · **fix leakages** · **recycle** water · practise **water harvesting**.

---

How do humans **pollute** freshwater sources, and why does it matter?

> [!ANSWER]
> By **throwing trash** into water bodies and **dumping waste from homes and industries** into them.
>
> **Why it matters: polluted water is not fit for consumption by living beings** — so pollution effectively destroys part of an already small freshwater supply.

---

What is **rainwater harvesting**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Collecting and storing rainwater** in large quantities for later use — in buildings, homes, residential societies and schools.
>
> It is an **age-old practice in India**.

---

What are **stepwells**, and how do they collect more than just rainwater?

> [!ANSWER]
> Traditional water-harvesting structures — **Bawadi** in Rajasthan, **Vav** in Gujarat — built in response to water scarcity.
>
> Their trench walls are **lined with blocks of stone that allow seepage**, so they store **not only rainwater but also water seeping in from nearby lakes, ponds and rivers**.

---

When is **World Water Day**?

> [!ANSWER]
> **22 March**, every year.

---

<!-- ===== Sun ===== -->

Why is the Sun called the **main source of energy** on Earth?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Sunlight helps plants prepare food**, and the Sun provides all living beings with **heat and light**.
>
> **Plants get energy from the Sun and produce food; animals eat plants and grow; we get food from both.** The whole cycle depends on the Sun.

---

A cow is grazing in a field. One student says it gets its energy from the **grass**, another from the **Sun**. Who is right?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Both — but the Sun is the ultimate source.**
>
> The cow eats grass, so it gets energy from the grass directly. But the **grass needed sunlight to grow** in the first place.
>
> So the energy **originated in the Sun**, was captured by the plant, and passed to the cow. (Note the third student's claim — that the cow gets energy from *standing* in the Sun — is wrong; that isn't how animals take in energy.)

---

Give three uses of energy from the Sun in daily life.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Drying** — clothes, chillies, raw mangoes. **Solar panels** capturing energy to produce **electricity**. Direct use in a **solar cooker** or **solar water heater**.
>
> It also drives **evaporation** in the water cycle, and lets plants make food.

---

<!-- ===== Forests ===== -->

What is a **forest**, and what does it provide to animals?

> [!ANSWER]
> A **large area with dense growth of various types of plants** — herbs, shrubs and trees.
>
> It is a **natural home** for wild animals, birds and insects, providing them **food and shelter**.

---

Why is the **diversity** of life forms in a forest important?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because **in nature every animal depends on other life forms for survival** — and the **diversity of life forms ensures food for every living being**.
>
> Remove some kinds and others lose their food supply.

---

Why is forest cover decreasing, and why must forests be used responsibly?

> [!ANSWER]
> Mainly due to **human activities like large-scale cutting of trees**.
>
> **It takes many years to grow a new forest or restore a lost one** — so forests must be preserved and used responsibly, giving them **enough time to regenerate**.

---

What was the **Chipko movement**?

> [!ANSWER]
> A famous effort to save forests that began in the **early 1970s in Uttarakhand** (then part of Uttar Pradesh).
>
> **Local women actively participated**, **encircling and hugging the trees** to protect them from being felled.

---

What is **Van Mahotsav**?

> [!ANSWER]
> A **week-long forest festival** celebrated across India in **July**.
>
> New plants and trees are **planted** and **awareness about respecting forests** is raised, with the aim of **increasing the green cover**.

---

Explain the "recycling in nature" seen on a forest floor.

> [!ANSWER]
> The **roots of plants hold the soil** and **prevent it from being washed away**.
>
> **Fallen leaves decay and enrich the soil with nutrients**, and that soil is then **used by new plants and trees to grow**.
>
> Nothing is wasted — what the forest drops, it feeds back to itself.

<!-- ===== Soil, rocks, minerals ===== -->

How is **soil formed**, and how long does it take?

> [!ANSWER]
> By the **disintegration (breaking apart) of rocks** through the actions of the **Sun, water and living organisms**.
>
> It takes a **long time — thousands of years**.

---

Why do soils from different places have **different colours**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because they **contain different materials**.
>
> Soil holds sand, insects, worms, tiny unseen organisms, and decayed plants and animals — and the mix varies from place to place.

---

How do **earthworms** help the soil?

> [!ANSWER]
> They are **natural agents that turn and loosen the soil**.
>
> Loosened soil has more spaces between its particles — which supplies **air** to seeds and roots, and lets **roots grow easily**.

---

Name three rocks and give a use for each.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Slate** — used for **roofing**. **Laterite** — used as a **building material, like bricks**. **Granite, sandstone and marble** — construction of buildings, temples, roads, dams and table tops.
>
> Humans have also made **hand axes and arrowheads** from rock for thousands of years.

---

What are rocks made of, and why do minerals matter?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Rocks are made up of minerals.**
>
> Important metals — **aluminium, gold, copper and iron** — are **extracted from minerals**, which go into aeroplanes, cars, jewellery, cosmetics, and electrical and electronic equipment.
>
> Even a **basic mobile phone contains about a dozen minerals**, including gold, silver, copper and cobalt.

---

Why must rocks and minerals be used **responsibly**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because it takes **thousands to millions of years to form rocks**.
>
> **Anything that takes that long to form cannot be replaced once it's used up** — the same reasoning that applies to forests and fossil fuels.

---

<!-- ===== Fossil fuels ===== -->

Which fuels are obtained from **petroleum**, and which two are most used in vehicles?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Petrol, diesel and kerosene** are obtained from petroleum.
>
> **Petrol and diesel** are the two most widely used vehicle fuels.
