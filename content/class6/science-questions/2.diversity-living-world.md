<!-- ===== Biodiversity ===== -->

What is **biodiversity**?

> [!ANSWER]
> The **variety of plants and animals found in a particular region**.

---

Give two examples showing that plants and animals **depend on each other**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Trees provide food and shelter** to birds and other animals.
>
> **Animals spread seeds** after eating fruits, helping new plants grow in new places.
>
> Each member of a region's biodiversity has a role — remove one and others are affected.

---

<!-- ===== Why group ===== -->

Why do we **group** plants and animals?

> [!ANSWER]
> Grouping makes it **easier to understand and study** them, by organising them according to their **similarities and differences**.
>
> The same reason you group books in a school bag — finding what you need becomes far quicker.

---

Two classmates group the same set of plants differently. Is one of them wrong?

> [!ANSWER]
> **No.** They simply **chose different features** to sort by — one might use height, the other presence or absence of flowers.
>
> Both groupings are valid. The real question is **which feature is useful for the question you're asking**.

---

<!-- ===== Plant groups ===== -->

Distinguish between a **herb, a shrub and a tree**, giving an example of each.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Herb** — short, with a **soft green** stem (tomato).
>
> **Shrub** — medium height, **hard brown** stems that **branch close to the ground** (rose).
>
> **Tree** — tall, with a **hard, thick, woody** stem whose **branches start higher up** (mango).

---

What are **climbers** and **creepers**?

> [!ANSWER]
> Both have **weak stems**.
>
> **Climbers need support** to climb upward (money plant); **creepers spread along the ground** (pumpkin).

---

What are **veins** and **venation**? Name the two types with examples.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Veins** are the thin lines on a leaf; the **pattern** they make is the **venation**.
>
> **Reticulate** — a **net-like** pattern on both sides of a thick middle vein (**hibiscus**).
>
> **Parallel** — the veins **run parallel** to each other (**banana, grass**).

---

Describe the two types of **root system**, with an example of each.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Taproot** — one **main root** with smaller side roots branching off it (**mustard**, hibiscus).
>
> **Fibrous roots** — a **bunch of similar-sized thin roots** arising from the base of the stem (**grass**).

---

What is a **cotyledon**? Distinguish **dicots** from **monocots**.

> [!ANSWER]
> A cotyledon is one of the parts a seed splits into.
>
> **Dicot** — the seed splits into **two** cotyledons (**chickpea**).
>
> **Monocot** — the seed has a **single thin** cotyledon (**maize**).

---

A plant's leaves show **parallel venation**. Predict its root type and its seed type.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Fibrous roots** and a **monocot** seed (one cotyledon).
>
> **Why you can predict it:** these three features travel together — **monocot → parallel venation → fibrous roots**. Wheat, maize, grass and lemongrass all follow it.

---

You dig up a plant and find a **taproot**. What do you expect of its leaves and seeds?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Reticulate venation** and a **dicot** seed (two cotyledons).
>
> Examples: chickpea, mustard, hibiscus, sadabahar.
>
> This link is genuinely useful — **look at a leaf and you can predict the roots without digging the plant up**.

---

<!-- ===== Animals ===== -->

Name the body parts used for movement by: **ant · pigeon · fish · snake**.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Ant — legs** (walks) · **pigeon — legs and wings** (walks and flies) · **fish — fins** (swims) · **snake — its body** (crawls).

---

Give three features **other than movement** that could be used to group animals.

> [!ANSWER]
> **What they eat**, **where they live** (their habitat), and their **shape, size or colour**.

---

<!-- ===== Habitat & adaptation ===== -->

What is a **habitat**? What does it provide?

> [!ANSWER]
> The **place where a plant or animal lives**.
>
> It provides **food, water, air, shelter** and everything else needed for survival. **Many kinds of plants and animals may share the same habitat.**

---

Define **adaptation**.

> [!ANSWER]
> The **special features that enable a plant or animal to survive in a particular region**.

---

Name the two broad habitat groups with two examples each. Where do frogs fit?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Terrestrial** (on land) — forests, deserts, grasslands, mountains.
>
> **Aquatic** (in water) — ponds, lakes, rivers, oceans.
>
> **Frogs are amphibians** — they live **both** in water and on land.

---

How does a **cactus** survive in the desert?

> [!ANSWER]
> Its **thick, fleshy stem stores water** — vital where very little water is available.
>
> Deserts are also very hot by day and very cold at night, so only living things that tolerate both extremes survive there.

---

Why is a **deodar** tree conical with sloping branches?

> [!ANSWER]
> It grows in **extremely cold mountains with frequent snowfall**.
>
> The conical shape and sloping branches let **snow slide off easily**, instead of piling up and breaking the branches.

---

Rhododendrons in the Nilgiris are **short with small leaves**, but taller in Sikkim. Why?

> [!ANSWER]
> On the Nilgiri mountain tops they face **heavy winds** — being short with small leaves helps them survive it. Sikkim's conditions are less punishing, so they grow taller.
>
> It shows that **even the same kind of plant can show different features in different regions**, each suited to where it grows.

---

Compare the **legs** of a hot-desert camel and a cold-desert camel.

> [!ANSWER]
> **Hot desert (Rajasthan):** **long legs with wide hooves** — spreading the weight so they **don't sink into the sand**.
>
> **Cold desert (Ladakh):** **shorter legs** — better for **walking in mountainous regions**.

---

How many **humps** does each camel have, and what are they for?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Hot-desert camel: one hump.** **Cold-desert camel: two.**
>
> Humps **store food** for times when food is scarce. The two humps of the Ladakhi camel **shrink in late winter** as the stored food gets used up. It also grows **long hair** from head to neck against the cold.

---

How can a camel survive many days without drinking?

> [!ANSWER]
> It **loses very little water**: it excretes only **small amounts of urine**, its **dung is dry**, and it **does not sweat**.
>
> Water conservation, not water storage, is the trick.

---

Why does biodiversity vary from one region to another?

> [!ANSWER]
> Because the **conditions vary**.
>
> Deserts have little water and swing from very hot days to very cold nights; mountains get heavy snow and wind. Only living things with **the right adaptations** survive in each place — so different regions end up with different life.

---

<!-- ===== Habitat loss ===== -->

What happens when a habitat is damaged?

> [!ANSWER]
> Plants and animals **lose their homes, food and other resources** — which leads to **loss of biodiversity**.

---

Name three Indian species that have declined through habitat loss, and two projects protecting them.

> [!ANSWER]
> The **Bengal Tiger**, the **Cheetah** and the **Great Indian Bustard**.
>
> **Project Tiger** (started **1973**) protects the Bengal Tiger; the **Cheetah Reintroduction Project** (**2022**) aims to restore the cheetah.

---

Who was **Salim Ali**, and what is he remembered for?

> [!ANSWER]
> The **"Birdman of India" (1896–1987)**. He travelled across India documenting birds, their **travel routes and habitats**, and identified regions of high bird diversity to conserve.
>
> He helped preserve **Keoladeo National Park** (Bharatpur, Rajasthan) and **Ranganathittu Bird Sanctuary** (Mandya, Karnataka), wrote a landmark **10-book series** on the birds of the Indian subcontinent, and was awarded the **Padma Vibhushan** in 1976.

---

Who was **Janaki Ammal**, and what was the **Save Silent Valley** movement?

> [!ANSWER]
> **Janaki Ammal (1897–1984)** was an Indian botanist who documented and preserved India's plant biodiversity and headed the **Botanical Survey of India**.
>
> **Save Silent Valley** was a 10-year public campaign — using awareness programmes, newspaper articles, seminars, petitions and court appeals — that stopped a **hydroelectric dam across the Kunthipuzha river** in Palakkad, Kerala, saving a moist evergreen forest of rich biodiversity.
>
> Notably it was led largely by **people who didn't even live near the forest**.
