# 🧊 Cubes (1 to 15)

The **cube** of a number is that number **times itself three times**: `n³ = n × n × n`.
It's called a "cube" because `n × n × n` is how many little cubes fill an **n × n × n
cube** — for example `3³ = 27` fills a 3×3×3 cube.

| n | n³ = n × n × n |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 8 |
| 3 | 27 |
| 4 | 64 |
| 5 | 125 |
| 6 | 216 |
| 7 | 343 |
| 8 | 512 |
| 9 | 729 |
| 10 | 1000 |
| 11 | 1331 |
| 12 | 1728 |
| 13 | 2197 |
| 14 | 2744 |
| 15 | 3375 |

> [!TIP]
> **Build a cube from its square:** you already know `n²`, and `n³ = n² × n`. Example:
> `7³ = 7² × 7 = 49 × 7 = 343`. If you can square a number, you're just one multiply away
> from its cube.

> [!NOTE]
> Cubes grow **much** faster than squares — by 15 the cube is already **3375**. Anchor on
> the round ones: `5³ = 125` and `10³ = 1000`.
