🔢 Numbers

Numbers & place value

  • Place value grows ×10 each step: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten-thousands, lakh, ten-lakh, crore.
  • Indian system commas: 12,34,567 (groups of 3, then 2, 2). International: 1,234,567 (groups of 3).
  • Face value = the digit itself · place value = digit × its place (in 5,43,210 the 4 means 40,000).
  • Compare: more digits = bigger; same number of digits → compare from the left.
  • Round / estimate to the nearest 10/100/1000 — look at the next digit, 5 or more rounds up (482 → 480). Successor = +1, predecessor = −1.
  • Roman numerals: I = 1, V = 5, X = 10, L = 50, C = 100, D = 500, M = 1000. Repeat to add (XX = 20); a smaller symbol before a bigger one subtracts (IV = 4, IX = 9, XL = 40, XC = 90). V, L, D are never repeated or subtracted.
⚠️ Common mistakesIndian commas group 3 then 2,2 (12,34,567) — not in 3s · place value is not the same as face value · IV = 4 (not IIII).

Whole numbers

  • Whole numbers: 0, 1, 2, 3, … (natural numbers + 0). Smallest is 0; there is no largest.
  • Every whole number has a successor; every one except 0 has a predecessor. Show them on a number line.
  • Properties: commutative (a + b = b + a, a × b = b × a), associative, distributive a × (b + c) = a×b + a×c.
  • Identities: a + 0 = a · a × 1 = a · a × 0 = 0.
⚠️ Common mistakes0 is a whole number but not a natural number · subtraction and division are not commutative.

Multiplication & division

  • Multiply: factor × factor = product. By 10/100/1000 → add 1/2/3 zeros.
  • Divide: dividend ÷ divisor = quotient, with a remainder. Steps: divide → multiply → subtract → bring down.
  • Check: divisor × quotient + remainder = dividend.
  • The remainder is always less than the divisor · 0 ÷ any number = 0 · dividing by 0 is not allowed.
⚠️ Common mistakesThe remainder must be smaller than the divisor · don't forget to bring down each digit.

Factors & multiples

  • Factor = divides exactly (factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12). Multiple = times-table value (multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, …). Factors are finite, multiples are infinite.
  • Prime = exactly 2 factors · composite = more than 2 · 1 is neither · 2 is the only even prime. Even ends 0/2/4/6/8, odd ends 1/3/5/7/9.
  • Divisibility: 2 → even; 3 → digit-sum ÷ 3; 4 → last 2 digits; 5 → ends 0/5; 6 → by 2 and 3; 8 → last 3 digits; 9 → digit-sum ÷ 9; 10 → ends 0; 11 → alternating digit-sum.
  • Prime factorisation: write as a product of primes (36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3).
  • HCF = biggest common factor · LCM = smallest common multiple · HCF × LCM = the two numbers multiplied.
⚠️ Common mistakes1 is not prime · for ÷ 3 and ÷ 9 add the digits (don't just look at the last digit) · factors divide, multiples multiply.

Integers

  • Integers: …, −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, … (negatives, zero, positives). 0 is neither + nor −.
  • On the number line, further right = bigger; every negative < every positive; −2 > −5.
  • Add: same sign → add and keep the sign; different signs → subtract and keep the bigger one's sign.
  • Subtract: add the opposite — 4 − (−3) = 4 + 3 = 7.
⚠️ Common mistakes−3 > −8 (it's nearer 0 on the right) · two minuses make a plus: 4 − (−3) = 7.

➗ Fractions & decimals

Fractions

  • A fraction is part of a whole; in 3/4, 3 = numerator, 4 = denominator.
  • Proper (num < den) · improper (num ≥ den) · mixed (1 2/3). Convert improper ↔ mixed (11/4 = 2 3/4).
  • Equivalent: × or ÷ top and bottom by the same number (1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6). Simplest form: divide both by a common factor.
  • Like (same denominator) → add/subtract numerators · unlike → make a common denominator (LCM) first. Compare like by numerators, unlike using the LCM.
  • "of" means ×: 1/2 of 10 = 5.
⚠️ Common mistakesAdd only with a common denominator (1/2 + 1/3 ≠ 2/5) · simplify by dividing, not subtracting.

Decimals & money

  • The point splits whole and part: 4.73 = 4 + 7/10 + 3/100 (tenths, hundredths, thousandths).
  • Compare: line up the point and compare place by place; 0.5 = 0.50.
  • Add / subtract: line up the decimal points.
  • Fraction ↔ decimal: 1/2 = 0.5, 3/4 = 0.75; 0.25 = 25/100 = 1/4.
  • Money: 100 paise = ₹1; write amounts with 2 decimal places (₹12.50).
⚠️ Common mistakes0.5 > 0.45 (line up the point, don't count digits) · keep 2 places after the point for paise.

🧮 Algebra

Algebra

  • A variable is a letter for an unknown number (x, n); a constant is a fixed number.
  • An expression joins numbers and variables with + − × (e.g. 2x + 5).
  • Rules as formulas: perimeter of a square = 4s; the nth term of 2, 4, 6, … is 2n.
  • Simple equation: 2x + 3 = 11 → 2x = 8 → x = 4 (do the same to both sides).
⚠️ Common mistakesx + x = 2x (not x²) · keep the equation balanced — do the same to both sides.

Ratio & proportion

  • Ratio compares two amounts by division, written a : b (same units). Equivalent ratios work like equivalent fractions.
  • Proportion: a : b = c : d (2 : 3 = 4 : 6); the product of the extremes = the product of the means.
  • Unitary method: find the value of 1, then multiply (5 pens ₹50 → 1 pen ₹10 → 8 pens ₹80).
⚠️ Common mistakesMake the units the same before writing a ratio · order matters (2 : 3 is not 3 : 2).

📐 Geometry

Lines, shapes & angles

  • Basics: point, line, line segment, ray; intersecting and parallel lines.
  • An angle is two rays from a point (the vertex), measured in degrees ° with a protractor: acute < 90°, right = 90°, obtuse 90°–180°, straight = 180°, reflex 180°–360°.
  • 2D shapes: triangle (3 sides), square & rectangle (4), pentagon (5), hexagon (6), circle. Triangles — by sides (equilateral / isosceles / scalene), by angles (acute / right / obtuse).
  • Quadrilaterals: square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram, trapezium. Circle: centre, radius, diameter (= 2 × radius), chord, circumference.
  • 3D shapes: cube, cuboid, cylinder, cone, sphere — count faces, edges, vertices (a cube has 6, 12, 8). A net folds up into a solid.
⚠️ Common mistakesA right angle is exactly 90° · diameter = 2 × radius · a square is a special rectangle (all sides equal).

Perimeter, area & volume

  • Perimeter = distance around (add all sides), in cm or m: square = 4 × side, rectangle = 2 × (l + b).
  • Area = surface covered, in square units (cm², m²): square = side × side, rectangle = l × b — or count squares.
  • Volume = space filled, in cubic units (cm³): cuboid = l × b × h.
⚠️ Common mistakesPerimeter is in cm, area is in cm² (squared) · keep all lengths in the same unit first.

Symmetry & patterns

  • A line of symmetry folds a shape into two matching mirror halves; a shape can have 0, 1, 2, … or infinite lines.
  • Square → 4 lines · rectangle → 2 · equilateral triangle → 3 · circle → infinite.
  • Patterns: numbers (2, 4, 6, …), shapes, and repeating or growing patterns.
⚠️ Common mistakesA rectangle has 2 lines of symmetry, not 4 (its diagonals don't match up).

📏 Measurement & data

Measurement & time

  • Length: 10 mm = 1 cm · 100 cm = 1 m · 1000 m = 1 km.
  • Weight: 1000 mg = 1 g · 1000 g = 1 kg. Capacity: 1000 mL = 1 L.
  • Big unit → small unit: × · small → big: ÷.
  • Time: 60 sec = 1 min · 60 min = 1 hour · 24 hours = 1 day. 24-hour clock: 1 pm = 13:00.
⚠️ Common mistakes1 km = 1000 m (not 100) · make units the same before adding.

Data handling

  • Data = collected information; organise it in a table with tally marks (in groups of 5).
  • Pictograph: a symbol stands for a number (read the key). Bar graph: bar heights show the amounts (read the scale). Always label the axes.
⚠️ Common mistakesAlways read the key / scale before reading or drawing a graph.
